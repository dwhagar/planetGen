What a planet or moon is made of in bulk, which stored values record it, and how its mass, radius, density and gravity follow from it. The code is the authority; each module named here opens with a docstring that gives its sources and limits, and the designs are bulk-composition.md and composition-spine-contracts.md. The air is in atmosphere-data-model.md; column types and versions are in database-schema.md.

The answer to "does the composition drive the mass, density and gravity?" Yes, for every body the generator makes. Before the composition build (main at 8.0.1635) a rocky body's density was a uniform draw for its class, its composition one fixed text per class, and no rock, ice, metal or gas fractions existed. Now the composition is drawn first, from the host star's abundances and the body's origin, and the radius, density and gravity follow from it. No class letter is read by the draw.

1. The order of the work

physics/planets.generate_planet_properties:

  1. Kind and mass. A planet draws its kind (terrestrial or giant) and its mass from the formation law at its distance (planet_kind.draw_formation); a moon's mass comes from its parent's moon size law; a caller may give a mass, a radius or both (kept, and checked against tuning.GIVEN_MASS_KG_BOUNDS and GIVEN_RADIUS_KM_BOUNDS).
  2. A terrestrial body (one the formation law does not make a giant, up to bulk_draw.MAX_MASS_EARTH = 20 Earth masses) draws its composition and takes its radius from it (bulk_draw.draw_terrestrial).
  3. A giant draws its heavy elements, radius and interior layers (giant_draw.draw_giant).
  4. The volume, the surface gravity (planets.calculate_surface_gravity, G M / R^2 in Earth gravities), the radiogenic content and the interior heat follow. The air, the water and the ground come after (see the atmosphere page and physics/surface_composition.py).

Mass, radius and gravity are never drawn apart: the mass and the composition say the radius (or a given radius says the mass), the density is the mass over the volume of the radius, and the gravity is G M / R^2.

2. What is stored

On planets and moons alike (schema v144, DB.69; every column is NULL on a row saved before it, which bulk_fractions reads back as None):

Column Unit What it is
metal_mass_fraction share of the body's mass The iron-rich core, light elements included
rock_mass_fraction share The silicate mantle and crust, or silicon carbide and graphite when carbon_rock
water_mass_fraction share H2O in every phase
ice_mass_fraction share Every other ice (CO2, NH3, CH4, CO, N2, H2S, CH3OH, organics) as one
envelope_mass_fraction share Hydrogen and helium gas
rock_feo_fraction, rock_al2o3_fraction share of the rock FeO and Al2O3
rock_mg_si_ratio molar ratio The rock's Mg/Si
rock_na2o_fraction, rock_k2o_fraction, rock_tio2_fraction, rock_cr2o3_fraction, rock_mno_fraction, rock_nio_fraction, rock_p2o5_fraction share of the rock The seven minor oxides (GEN.1362, mantle-oxides.md); NULL for a carbon rock
envelope_helium_fraction, envelope_z_fraction share of the envelope Helium (Y) and heavy elements (Z)
porosity share of volume The void share of a small body (NULL where the structure solver decides)
carbon_rock flag The rock is silicon carbide and graphite
composition_origin drawn or inverse Composition first, or worked back from a given mass and radius
composition text The phrase written from the fractions (section 6)
mass_kg, radius_km, density_g_cm3, gravity_g kg, km, g/cm3, Earth gravities The results; density = mass / (4/3 pi radius^3)

The five shares of the whole body (metal, rock, water, ice, envelope) are not negative and add up to 1. A giant's interior adds interior_kind (gas_giant, ice_giant, brown_dwarf), core_mass_earth, dilute_core_mass_earth, metallization_pressure_gpa, helium_depletion and has_helium_shell (v154, physics/giant_interior.py); the layer masses add up to the body's mass and the layers hold exactly the heavy-element mass and natal helium (tested to 1e-9). Where a body formed relative to its star's snow line is born_beyond_snow_line (v132), and the star's own snow line stars.snow_line_km. The ground (v159) is a separate set of ground_* columns, drawn from the bulk fractions and described in physics/surface_composition.py.

3. How a terrestrial body's composition is drawn

physics/bulk_draw.py (GEN.428), in this order (the order is part of the generator epoch):

  1. The star sets the dry ratios. The host star's abundances (stellar_composition) give Fe/Mg, Si/Mg, Al/Mg, Ca/Mg, Ni/Mg and C/O. A rocky body takes all the star's Mg, Si, Ca and Al into its rock as oxides and the iron its mantle does not hold as FeO into its metal core (dry_partition). An Earth-like star gives a core mass fraction near 0.3 with no draw; an iron-rich or iron-poor star moves it, the observed trend [R1146, R3916].
  2. What the star cannot say is drawn, from RANGES and the tables, each with its source in the module: the kind of rock (ROCK_KIND_TABLE: silicate, or silicon carbide and graphite for C/O of 0.8 or more, [R0455, R4469]); the water share, log-uniform over the range of the body's origin (tuning.DRY_WATER_MASS_FRACTION 1e-6 to 1e-3; a water world, WATER_WORLD_SHARE 0.03 of bodies, 0.01 to 0.1; an icy body beyond the snow line ICY_WATER_MASS_FRACTION 0.05 to 0.5); for an icy body the other ices from the comet volatiles; the rock's FeO (2 to 18 percent), the core's light elements (3 to 12 percent), a small icy body's porosity (0 to 0.3), a carbon body's graphite over rock (5 to 40 percent); then the iron branch of a rocky planet (IRON_BRANCH_TABLE: its iron follows the star's with a scatter, or is enriched to a core mass fraction of 0.5 to 0.8).
  3. Radius from mass. bulk_structure.structure gives the radius the composition holds the mass at. A caller that gave a radius gets the mass the composition needs for it, by bisection; a body too dense to reach its radius below 20 Earth masses is set to the radius of 20 (MAX_MASS_EARTH). A body below SMALL_BODY_MASS_KG (1e21 kg) is a heap with a porosity and its mass is its volume times the mixing density.
  4. A body given both a mass and a radius goes the other way (composition_origin = inverse): they say the core mass fraction (bulk_structure.cmf_from_mass_radius, at most MAX_CMF 0.9), as an observer's route does.
  5. A moon that holds its origin (moon_composition, GEN.466) takes its shares from it: a disk moon of a giant (ice rising with the formation distance past the disk's ice line, [R3922]; the moon system of [R0643]), an impact moon (little metal, no ice, [R2724]), a captured body, a ring moon or the partner of a dwarf planet. Every weight of the origin table is open (no source), so the rows a moon supports share the probability equally. Below the taper's end of the compaction law (compaction.TAPER_END_KG, GEN.442) its mass follows from its radius by the tapered brittle law and its grain density; heavier, the structure solver decides.

Small bodies (belts, comets, icy bodies, rings) draw their composition first from rows of the materials table and derive their density from it with physics/compaction.py (small_body_composition.py, GEN.442: the Bus and DeMeo taxa of the Solar System's one belt are flagged stand-ins).

4. How the radius follows: the structure model

physics/bulk_structure.py (GEN.429), pure functions with no draws. The radius is at the 1 bar level (Boss, 1:24 AM EDT 2026-10-17). One reference solver and three fast forms:

  • layered_structure, the reference: a two-layer integrator extended with a water layer on top whose density is the 300 K isotherm of liquid water (IAPWS-95 through the NIST WebBook, to 950 MPa), the ice VI, VII and X reference points of the materials table, and the polytrope of [R3992] above 1 TPa. Hydrostatic balance in s = m^(1/3), fourth-order Runge-Kutta, shooting on the central pressure to 1 bar at the surface. Checked against a scipy reference built from the full mantle tables (0.20 percent in radius, 0.28 in the moment of inertia, 0.93 in the core-mantle pressure over 90 cases) and, with water, within 1.5 percent of [R4881] from 0.5 to 50 Earth masses. Validated 0.1 to 20 Earth masses.
  • Dry rock and iron, radius_fit_earth: 0.1 to 20 Earth masses, core mass fraction 0 to 0.8; at most 0.40 percent (rms 0.10) against the solver.
  • Water-bearing, water_world_radius_earth: 0.3 to 20 Earth masses, water fraction 0 to 1; rms 1.2 and at most 3.6 percent.
  • Small differentiated bodies, small_body_radius_earth: 1.5e-4 to 0.3 Earth masses; rms 0.57, at most 2.0 percent.
  • An envelope on a core, envelope_radius_earth: [R2741] Eqs. 1 and 3 at the 20 mbar level plus ln(50) scale heights for 1 bar; 1 to 20 Earth masses, envelope 0.01 to 20 percent; rms 6.3 percent against their tables at 1 Gyr. The valley of the radius distribution (physics/radius_valley.py, GEN.509) tunes the starting envelope against the Kepler population.

A body whose envelope is more than GIANT_ENVELOPE_FRACTION (0.5) of its mass, or that has over 20 Earth masses of core, is a giant and outside this module.

A giant (giant_radius.py, giant_draw.py, GEN.460): the envelope's metallicity is a multiple of the star's, the heavy-element mass follows [R0710] (M_Z = M_core + f_Z (M - M_core) with its scatter, at most MAX_HEAVY_FRACTION 0.9 of the mass), and the radius is interpolated from the cool-start tables of [R1429] (17 to 3,594 Earth masses) plus the inflation of [R4029] above a threshold flux (an empirical median, a stand-in for the thermal evolution the project does not have). Where the tables do not hold the body (an ice giant, a giant under 17 Earth masses, a moon) the anchored mass-radius relation of planets.sample_giant_radius_km is used. The density of a giant is bounded by tuning.GIANT_DENSITY_BOUNDS_G_CM3 (0.05 to 60) and a terrestrial body's by bulk_draw.DENSITY_BOUNDS_G_CM3 (0.6 to 25).

5. The materials table

physics/materials.py (GEN.427): the one table of every element, mineral, ice, organic and alloy a body can contain, 161 rows from physics/data/materials.json, which scripts/build_materials_table.py builds from the research parts' tables (E materials, B's phases and iron rows, C's ice phases) and scripts/materials_data.py ([R3992: Tables 1 to 3], the Handbook of Mineralogy, PubChem). A row has its formula, molar mass (kg/mol, from the element table), grain density (g/cm3 at ambient conditions), phase points, latent heats, band albedos and, where it has one, the equation-of-state constants of the model GEN.426 chose, each number with its source and a source state (verified, partly verified with its limit, or not reached). A value with no source is None; none is kept from memory. The gate says which classes can hold a row: any for every planet and moon class and small-body kind (135 rows), planetary for the planet and moon classes only (26 rows: the high-pressure silicate phases, high-pressure ices, core iron alloys and hydrogen-helium envelopes). The loader refuses a file without its sources, a row without a source and state, a formula it cannot read, an unknown gate, and a missing equation-of-state constant. The 84 asteroid components, the taxa and the class composition texts all resolve to rows of it, and a test fails when one does not. The licence of the data is in DATA-LICENSE.md.

6. The words

physics/composition_text.py (GEN.430) writes the composition phrase of every planet and moon from the stored fractions, so the words and the numbers cannot disagree: each material the body holds at least 1 percent of is named with the level its share falls in (mostly from 0.5, a large share of from 0.2, a moderate share of from 0.05, a small share of from 0.01), largest first, for example "mostly silicate rock, with a large share of iron-rich metal and a small share of water". A porosity of 0.1 or more adds "loosely packed".

7. The migration and the sweep

Migration 0144 adds the columns, all NULL: a row made before it has no composition to read back (it needs its star's abundances and the draws), so the regenerated galaxy of the generator epoch replaces it. A stopped run can be started again.

tests/test_sweep_bulk.py (TEST.210) runs the rules of tests/audit/sweep_bulk.py over bodies of every class: fractions-sum, detail-ranges, porosity-range, density-from-mass-radius, gravity-from-mass-radius, radius-from-structure (to 1e-6 for a planet and 4 percent for a moon, whose mass follows the tapered brittle law; a body with an envelope is left out because its flux and age are not stored), denser-than-grains and text-materials. Its last test breaks each rule and checks the sweep reports it.

8. Limits

The Fortney tables do not inflate, so the inflation is the Sestovic median (a stand-in, marked in the gap plan); the water-world share and the origin weights of moons are open; the small-body taxa kernel is the Solar System's one belt and is flagged. These stand-ins are registered with their tier and tweak level in stand-in-values.md.
