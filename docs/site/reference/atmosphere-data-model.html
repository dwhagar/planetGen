What a planet or moon stores about its air, which code makes each value, and where the numbers behind it come from. The code is the authority; every module named here opens with a docstring that gives its sources and limits, and the designs are in docs/design/composition/ (the colour chain's plan is atmosphere-colour.md). The colour itself is described in atmosphere-colour.md; the columns' types and versions are in database-schema.md. Planets and moons store the same values in the same columns; a moon's starlight is its planet's.

1. The order of the work

physics/planets.calculate_atmospheric_conditions makes the air of a body in this order:

  1. Albedo. The Bond albedo is drawn from the range of the body's regime (climate_draws.albedo_range), and the temperature with no air is T_e = ((1 - A) S / (4 sigma))^(1/4), never below the 2.725 K background.
  2. The air and the temperature together (physics/surface_air.solve, designed in surface-pressure-supply-law.md): the mixture draw, the pressure the interior has degassed less the sinks, the freeze-out of what the temperature condenses, the density and scale height of what is left, and the greenhouse that gives the temperature, repeated until the pressure and the temperature stop moving.
  3. Storing the air (physics/atmosphere.store_air): the partial pressures, the mantle redox and the words of atmosphere.
  4. Water and ground (hydrosphere, surface_composition), then cloud, haze and dust (physics/aerosols.generate_aerosols).
  5. Later in the body's generation: the ozone column and the doses (physics/radiation.generate_dose) and the colour (physics/body_colour).

A body with no air, or whose air is lost, has the temperature the starlight alone gives, no gas rows, and NULL density, molar mass and scale height.

2. What is stored

A column that a row made before its version does not have is NULL, and the page shows nothing for it.

3. The gas table

physics/gases.py is the one constants table of the gases and volatile compounds the chain reads: 119 rows, loaded from physics/data/gases.json, which scripts/build_gas_table.py builds from the tables of the research parts (atmosphere-composition-parts, parts 01 to 03). A row holds the formula (the key everything cites), name, CAS number, molar mass (kg/mol, summed from the element table; the research's own figure is kept for the audit), the infrared class (ir, cia, weak, none), the volatility class, the lowest photolysis threshold and its channel, whether it absorbs near ultraviolet, triple, boiling and critical points, ideal-gas heat capacity at 100, 300 and 1000 K, the latent heats, and where it has them the Rayleigh cross sections at 450, 550 and 650 nm, the Henry constant, the Lennard-Jones constants that give the viscosity, the infrared bands and the ultraviolet bins.

Every row says where it comes from (source) and how far it is checked (state: verified, partly verified with its limit, or open). The loader refuses a file or row without those fields, a duplicate formula, a formula it cannot read, and a molar mass of the research part that is more than 0.01 g/mol from the element sum. It has no weights and no class gate: the recipes that weight the gases (physics/atmosphere_recipes.py) cite its rows by formula. Units: kg/mol, K, Pa, J/(mol K), kJ/mol, cm2 per molecule, mol/(kg bar), uPa s. A number the research does not give is None.

4. The mixture and how the values agree

The mixture is a plain dict of gas formula to partial pressure in Pa (physics/mixture.py); every reader goes through partial_pressures_pa, so the storage could change underneath (since DB.71 it is the child tables). Mole fractions and the mean molar mass follow from it. The rule that holds the stored values together:

  • the partial pressures add up to atmospheric_pressure_pa;
  • atm_molar_density is the mole-fraction mean of the gases' molar masses;
  • atm_density is P M / (R T) and scale_height_km is R T / (M g) at the stored temperature and gravity;
  • surface_temperature_k is the formula on the stored bond_albedo and greenhouse_factor;
  • the ozone follows the oxygen and the star, and the aerosol depths follow their rules;
  • every gas the text names is in the table and the mixture.

tests/test_sweep_atmosphere.py (TEST.209) checks these over planets of every class and zone round three host stars and the bodies of generated systems, with the rules of tests/audit/sweep_atmosphere.py (pressure-sum, mean-molar-mass, air-density, scale-height, temperature, ozone, aerosol-airless, aerosol-cloud, aerosol-haze, aerosol-dust, text-gases); its last test breaks each rule in turn and checks that the sweep reports it.

How the mixture is drawn. physics/atmosphere_recipes.py draws the regimes (mantle redox, volatile delivery, outgassing, retention, envelope, giant, brown dwarf, hot Jupiter, lava world, trace gases). physics/mixture_draw.draw_mixture turns them into mole fractions by chemistry and not by a per-class template: the element budget, the temperature and pressure of the layer and the oxygen fugacity go into the equilibrium solver (physics/gas_equilibrium.py, on the NASA Glenn thermodynamics of physics/species_thermo.py). A class letter only gates which recipe rows apply. The family is chosen by what the recipe drew (mixture_rocky, mixture_giant, mixture_lava, mixture_escape, mixture_evolved, and so on). The draws follow the recipe draw in a fixed order; changing the order changes every body and is a generator epoch.

5. Ozone

physics/ozone.py. The column in Dobson units (1 DU = 2.687e16 molecules/cm2) is the fifth-degree polynomial of Kozakis et al. 2022 (A&A, Table 3) against the log10 of the oxygen mixing ratio, one for each of five hosts (G0V, Sun, K2V, K5V, M5V), valid for 0.01 to 150 percent of the present oxygen level and held at its ends outside it. The oxygen level at another pressure or gravity is (p_O2 / g) over Earth's. Between two hosts the column is the linear mix in temperature; cooler than the M5V row takes it; hotter than G0V scales the G0V curve to the F7V and F0V columns of [R3845, R3846] (a labelled stand-in). The draw multiplies the median by a structure factor log-uniform on 0.85 to 1.71, which spans the published models. Below 0.01 percent of the present level (abiotic oxygen) the column is 0.01 to 0.1 percent of the same host's full column, and 15 percent for an F host ([R1137], second-hand). No oxygen gives none. A loss event that kept a share of the air scales the column by that share (column_after_air_loss).

6. Cloud, haze and dust

physics/aerosols.py, three things that scatter besides the gas:

  • Cloud. The air is cooled along a dry adiabat to the skin temperature T_s / 2^(1/4); a condensable gas forms a cloud where its partial pressure reaches saturation. The most condensate that can fall out gives the depth limit, tau = 3 Q_ext m_c / (4 rho_p r_eff) with Q_ext = 2; the droplet radius is log-uniform on 1 to 20 micron, the depth log-uniform from 0.1 to its limit, cloud_cover 0 without a condensate else uniform on 0.1 to 1. Clouds are grey, albedo 1, asymmetry 0.85.
  • Haze (stand-in). Above a CH4/CO2 ratio of 0.1 methane photolysis makes a haze, supply limited by diffusion with K_zz log-uniform on 1e6 to 1e10 cm2/s and a yield log-uniform on 0.01 to 1; the depth is the particles' mass flux over their Stokes fall speed with the radius log-uniform on 0.05 to 0.3 micron and the Titan tholin density (1000 kg/m3). The extinction goes as lambda^-a with a from 0.97 to 2.34 and the absorption follows the Khare tholin index. The Titan laws are a test target, never a preset.
  • Dust (stand-in). A dry solid surface lofts dust above the threshold friction velocity; u*/u*_t is log-uniform on 0.3 to 3, the depth log-uniform on 0.5 to 1.5, grey, radius 1.2 to 1.8 micron, asymmetry 0.673 +- 0.081, and the single-scattering albedo of the [R4743] spectra (digitised, a labelled stand-in).

The draws always use the same number of random numbers (four for the cloud, four for the haze, two for the dust, one for aerosol_draw), whether or not the body has the aerosol, so one body's draws never shift another's.

7. Rayleigh scattering and the colour

physics/rayleigh.py: the optical depth of the scattering is tau_R(lambda) = sum_i N_i sigma_i(lambda), with N_i = p_i N_A / (m g) and sigma = A lambda^-4 (1 + B / lambda^2)^2 fitted to the gas table's three values (450, 550, 650 nm). Dry Earth air gives 0.0970 at 550 nm against 0.09721 for [R0575: Table 4]. The cross sections are good to 2.5 to 4 percent for N2 and Ar, 7 for CO2 and 10 to 18 for CO, CH4 and N2O [R4138]. The colour model (physics/atmosphere_colour.py) is described in atmosphere-colour.md; it reads the stored ozone column, the aerosol depths and the cloud cover, and a stand-in ground albedo until the ground's reflectance is stored.

8. Limits

The infrared band and wing opacities are stand-ins until real line data are in (physics/infrared_opacity.py); the stored water vapor is taken as it is, with no condensation above the surface; the closure's pressure exponent is 2 for every air. The haze yield is a labelled placeholder (stand-in AT.n7) and the dust and haze spectra come from similar materials (stand-in AD2), both in stand-in-values.md; the ozone fit for a star hotter than G0V is labelled in the gap plan (gap-filling-plan.md).
