Boss (2026-10-07 11:47Z) asked for the tiered structure of where stars of different types sit in the galaxy, and for the kinds of phenomena with similar restrictions, added to the density and type draws. This note is the review of 2026-10-16 turned into a table: which placement rules the code already has, and which three population-dependent rules for the type draws are still missing, each with a way to calculate it and, for two of them, the constants Boss supplied on 2026-10-16.

Nothing here is a fixed catalogue. Every number a rule uses comes from the sector profile (galaxy/profile.py), which is a function of the galaxy shape, the cell's address and the galaxy seed. A rule below either reads a profile field or names the physical law that turns profile fields into the number. Where a law needs a constant from the literature the note says so, marks it for Boss's research, and the code reads it from tuning.py with its source named.

Informs: GEN.103, GEN.269, GEN.270, GEN.271, GEN.272, GEN.273, GEN.275. Status: 3.1 and 3.3 built (GEN.103); 3.2 dropped by Boss (2026-10-17, 10:07 AM EDT, GEN.373); check TEST.196 queued. Design dated 2026-10-16. Evidence tags: [S] read from the repository, [R] recalled and unconfirmed, [C] computed here.

1. What decides where a thing goes

A cell's profile carries nine populations (profile.populations, each with a density, a share of the cell's stars, an age window, a metallicity peak, a velocity dispersion and a rotation rule) and a kind_factors map. Two kinds of rule sit on top of them, and the first is complete.

Layer Question it answers Where it lives Status
Density How many systems does the cell hold? galaxy/density.relative_density; profile.expected_systems built
Population mix Which populations are they from? galaxy/density.population_densities; profile.mix, profile.populations built (GEN.234)
Age and metallicity How old, how metal rich? tuning.STELLAR_POPULATION_AGE_RANGES_GY; profile._fe_h; bright_stars.py reads populations[...].fe_h built (GEN.158, GEN.234)
Per-kind rate factors How much of a phenomenon does the place scale? profile.kind_factors_at; run_sector.py multiplies phenomenon_rate_per_star built (GEN.236)
Scatter placement Where do the single objects go? phenomenon_scatter.py, one weight per band and longitude bin built (GEN.278)
Type draws by population Does the place change how many stars a system holds and how far apart they sit? generation/multistar.py not built (section 3)

The cluster and satellite populations (GEN.160, GEN.237) are placed by the component field, not by a gate: their density is the components' systems in the cell's cube.

3. Left: three rules for the type draws

multistar-and-compact-systems.md section 6.6 names three effects of the population on a system's stars. 3.1 and 3.3 are built (GEN.103: generation/place_terms.py, StarSystem._companion, wide_binary.py); 3.2 is not, and the scatter's member trees (multistar.py) read the star's mass and nothing about the place. Each rule below is a function of fields the profile already holds. The constants are the only things to look up, and they sit in tuning.py with a source.

3.1 Close pairs rise as metallicity falls

[R3109] corrected five surveys for their selection biases and found one consistent trend: the intrinsic fraction of solar-type primaries (about 0.6 to 1.5 solar masses) with a companion inside 10 AU (periods under 10^4 days) is 53% +-12% at [Fe/H] -3.0, 40% +-6% at -1.0, 24% +-4% at -0.2 and 10% +-3% at +0.5 [S: the abstract, read through search results; the paper's full text could not be opened from this environment]. [R0223] found the same direction in APOGEE: metal-poor stars ([Fe/H] below about -0.5) are two to three times as often in multiples as metal-rich ones (above about 0). [R0281] reproduces it in radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, where low opacity lets the disc fragment more and the companions migrate inside 10 AU [R].

Rule (Boss, 6:12 to 6:15 PM EDT, decided): the close-companion chance follows one pooled line in [Fe/H], read at the star's own [Fe/H] (PopulationProfile.fe_h, peak and scatter, drawn per population by the cell), for every primary mass, with no fade. F is the straight line between the four points above, held flat past the ends; F(0) is 20%. The factor F(fe_h) / F(0) multiplies the odds p / (1 - p) of the close share the generator already has (Boss, 7:19 PM EDT, on a card: the odds form, not the chance), and nothing else moves:

  • Built in GEN.448 (it was two rolls, "has a companion" then a coin flip between close and wide): the chance of a companion, BINARY_SYSTEM_PROBABILITY_BY_SPECTRAL_CLASS[class] (O 0.90, early B 0.84, mid B 0.76, B 0.65, A 0.55, F 0.47, G 0.44, K 0.40, M 0.26; GEN.449), is shared between the close range (0.05 to 0.25 AU), the inner range (0.25 to 10 AU) and the outer range (10 AU and out) by Moe and Di Stefano's companions per decade of period (generation/companion_classes.py; multistar-and-compact-systems.md 4). The points describe companions inside 10 AU, so the close and inner ranges together are p_in0 = P_binary(class) * (their share) (G 0.126, O 0.41 before the place's cut), and the outer range is p_out.
  • New: odds_in = p_in0 / (1 - p_in0) * F(fe_h) / F(0), so p_in = odds_in / (1 + odds_in); p_out is unchanged (answer 3: no dependence); one roll then picks close, wide or single from p_in (split between the close and inner ranges as before the factor), p_out and the rest, with p_in + p_out held to at most 1: where the sum would pass 1 the outer share is cut to 1 - p_in, the one place the share beyond 10 AU moves with [Fe/H], an exception to answer 3 that the wide-flatness check skips. The place then keeps the part of each range under its widest axis (3.3); what it drops is lost from the chances, not moved to another range.
  • Why the odds: the chance form lets a massive star's close share run to 1 (an O star at [Fe/H] -3.0 would have a companion inside 10 AU every time), against the O and early-B surveys that find no change within about 30%; the odds form never passes 1 and moves a star that is already likely to have a companion less. It uses no constant beyond the four points (a fade above 2 solar masses, which Boss declined, or a per-mass ceiling would).
  • A G dwarf inside 10 AU then gets 0.28, 0.22, 0.15 and 0.07 at [Fe/H] -3.0, -1.0, -0.2 and +0.5, against 53, 40, 24 and 10% (+-12, 6, 4, 3) in the paper [C]: the shape follows the line (the odds ratios equal F / F(0)), the level is about 0.6 of it (0.52 to 0.67 at the four points). The cause is not settled [R]: Table 13's 0.15 companions inside log P 3.7 are a count per primary, and the generator's chance is the fraction of stars with one. An O star inside 10 AU goes from 0.41 at [Fe/H] 0 to 0.54 at -0.7 (+31%).

The factor is a function of the star's own [Fe/H] and nothing else, so no population is named.

GEN.705: the tree draw (multistar.close_trend, draw_member_tree) applies the factor. The star-count table is the Sun's neighbourhood (F(0)); the primary's share of companions inside 10 AU is the part of its period law under the period of 10 AU (_inside_share); that part's chance goes through close_binary_trend.scaled_close_chance, the outside part keeps its chance (it gives way only past 1), the counts of 2 to 7 stars keep their proportions, and the root pair's period draw gives the inside range the matching odds (_Builder.period_days). A system build passes the primary's [Fe/H] (Placed.fe_h), as the scatter does.

What the sources limit:

  • Which stars the four points describe. Field solar-type dwarfs and subgiants, pooled over the disc and halo samples the five surveys hold; they are not four populations. The -3.0 end rests on the Carney-Latham halo sample and the +0.5 end on Kepler and APOGEE, so the line between them is an interpolation of a pooled trend, not a measurement per population. Boss chose the pooled line (answer 1) over a per-population one, which no source gives.
  • Mass: all masses, which the sources do not support above about 1.5 solar masses. The points are for 0.6 to 1.5 solar masses. Moe, Kratter and Badenes argue the close fraction of massive stars does not change with metallicity [R]; the BLOeM survey [R4551] finds early-B stars at the Small Cloud's metallicity ([Fe/H] about -0.7) more often close than in the Milky Way, so early B may follow the trend while O stars do not. Boss's decision is to apply the line to every mass, on the odds, and let the check (3.1b) show where the numbers leave the published ranges. What the line does to a massive star is in the table below: the plain factor would have pushed O stars below [Fe/H] about -0.7 past the observed ceiling (0.77 at -0.7 against 0.69 +-0.09), which is why Boss chose the odds form.
  • Wide pairs. Answer 3: no metallicity dependence, so the rule moves the close share only. [R1235] say the wide fraction is flat in [Fe/H] beyond about 250 AU and anticorrelated below about 200 AU; [R2062] find it peaks near [Fe/H] 0 and falls to both sides for 10^3 to 10^4 AU, and [R3298] say the sign depends on separation range and temperature. Wide pairs start at 0.25 AU (WIDE_BINARY_SEPARATION_MIN_AU, GEN.448); the outer range, 10 AU and out, carries no trend, and the inner range carries the close-binary one, so the 10 to 200 AU anticorrelation is left out; that is Boss's rule.
  • Iron or alpha. [R2965] find the close fraction follows the alpha abundance more steeply than iron, with a high alpha abundance suppressing it at most [Fe/H]. The generator has no alpha abundance. Used with [Fe/H] alone the rule would give the thick disc more close pairs than a high-alpha sample shows. [R: from the abstract only]

3.1b The check that the numbers act as expected (TEST.196)

Boss (6:15 PM EDT): "build into that a check to see if the numbers act as expected." It is TEST.196, owner Core 4, in the census harness next to TEST.186 (Core 3's; ask before editing census.py). It fills seeded sectors across the [Fe/H] range, counts the share of primaries with a companion inside 10 AU by primary-mass bin and [Fe/H] bin, and compares each bin to the expected range below plus the binomial allowance of its sample (three standard errors), and shows the wide share is flat in [Fe/H]. It fails the run when a bin with a published range is outside it, prints every bin (also the ones with no range), and writes the table to the audit report. The ranges are reference values for the check and are never read by the draw.

3.2 Massive multiples sit in young regions

Observed: high-mass stars are far more often in multiples than low-mass ones, and the most massive sit in clusters and associations. The mass dependence is already in BINARY_SYSTEM_PROBABILITY_BY_SPECTRAL_CLASS and hosts.STAR_COUNT_PROBABILITIES (the star-count table); the place dependence is not. The prevalence argument of draw_member_tree already scales the chance of five or more stars, so the place only has to set it.

Rule: prevalence is the young share against the solar circle's, clamped to 0 to some cap, where the young share is the profile's populations["young"].share over the reference profile._reference_young_share(shape) that kind_factors_at already uses. That makes the knob 1 where a cell looks like the solar circle, above 1 in arms and associations and 0 in the halo. The cap on the rise is the one constant (a design default for Boss, since the five-or-more column is a tail). The five-or-more columns are the ones that scale, which is how the knob works today.

3.3 Wide pairs survive less where it is dense

Two limits cut the widest axis a pair can keep. Both are functions of the cell and the second also of the stars' age.

The encounter limit. Passing stars and clouds unbind soft pairs [R0229, R1880]. [R2166] restate the 1985 result: the semi-major axis at which half of the pairs of age t survive is

a_1/2 = 0.002 * (M_1 + M_2) * sigma / (G * rho_2 * t)

with sigma the dispersion of the relative velocity between the pair and the perturbers (3 sigma^2 is their mean-square relative velocity; Jiang and Tremaine take 29 km/s one-dimensional for the Sun's neighbourhood and "choose the relative velocity dispersion to be 2^(1/2) times this, so sigma = 40 km/s"), t the pair's age and rho_2 = the integral of n(M) M^2 dM, the second moment of the perturbers' mass function (units of solar masses squared per cubic parsec; their equation 47 gives 0.029 for rho_2 and 0.045 solar masses per cubic parsec for the mass density rho_1, so the mass-weighted perturber mass rho_2 / rho_1 is 0.64 solar masses) [S: read in the paper, 2026-10-17]. The constant 0.002 is Bahcall, Hut and Tremaine's, reproduced through equation (1). The same source gives its worked value: 3.1 x 10^4 AU for M_1 + M_2 = 2, sigma = 50 km/s, rho_2 = 0.03 and t = 10 Gyr, which I reproduced to 31,300 AU [C]. It is linear in 1/rho_2 and 1/t, which is what the code needs: a_1/2 follows the cell's density and the star's age with nothing stored by place. In the solar neighbourhood (a one-dimensional sigma of 30 km/s, rho 0.08 solar masses per pc^3, the default mass-weighted perturber mass of 1.19 solar masses below) it gives 1.4 x 10^4 AU for two solar masses at 10 Gyr, just under Boss's 2 to 5 x 10^4 AU and Jiang and Tremaine's 50% survival near 30,000 AU, because the generator counts the remnants they leave out (see GEN.447 below).

GEN.447 (built, 2026-10-17). Two corrections to the first version of this limit, which read sigma as the population's own dispersion and used 0.4 solar masses for the perturber mass: place_terms.RELATIVE_VELOCITY_FACTOR (root 2) multiplies the population's one-axis dispersion (the profile's three-axis total over root 3, GEN.284), and place_terms.mass_weighted_perturber_mass_solar computes <M^2> / <M> from the generator's own initial mass function (tuning.IMF_BREAKS_SOL, IMF_SLOPES), aged to each population's age range at a steady rate (tuning.STELLAR_POPULATION_AGE_RANGES_GY), with the remnants of what has left the main sequence: stars up to the turn-off where the main-sequence lifetime of [R2055] (ms_lifetime.mass_for_lifetime_gy) reaches the age, white dwarfs from the WHITE_DWARF_PROGENITOR_SOL range by the initial-final relation (WD_IFMR_*), neutron stars and black holes from the windows of initial mass the generator draws them from (remnant_progenitors, explosion-islands.md) above the white dwarf range, the last two uniform over their mass ranges as the generator draws them. A cell takes the share-weighted mix of the populations it holds (from_profile). The two corrections go opposite ways: root 2 makes the limit 1.41 times longer, and the perturber mass, 1.44 solar masses for the whole disk against the 0.4 used before, makes it about 3.6 times shorter, so the limit is about 2.5 times shorter than the first version's. For the stars alone, aged over the disk's 10 Gyr of star formation, the mass-weighted mean is 0.568 (GEN.927; 0.553 on the Kroupa function), 12% under Jiang and Tremaine's 0.644 (the check in tests/test_place_terms.py); the rest of the 1.44 is remnants. The black holes do most of it: 0.18% of the stars formed (those above 20 solar masses) leave a 12.5 solar mass hole, which holds about 2.2 times the second moment of all the stars. That is the initial mass function's arithmetic (it gives about 1e8 holes for 1e11 stars, the order of the Galaxy's estimated number), but it rests on every such hole staying in the disk; kicks that eject holes would lower it, and Jiang and Tremaine count observed stars only. The follow-up question for Boss and TEST.196 is whether the perturbers should include the dark remnants; the stars-only value is remnants=False.

GEN.554 (2026-10-21): the black holes follow the failed share. The neutron stars and black holes of the cohort now come from the windows of initial mass of the failed share of the middle draw at solar metallicity (13.6% of the 8 to 150 solar mass stars, explosion-islands.md; the natal kick of natal-kick.md ejects 16% of the neutron stars, which lets the cap on the black hole share rise), not from the old 20 to 100 solar mass range (29% of them). The perturber mass of the whole disk falls from 1.44 to 1.01 solar masses (the stars alone stay 0.553), the encounter limits lengthen by 1.4 times (the table of limits by place below is the new one), and the black holes' count falls to 7e7 for 5e10 solar masses of stars, under the published 1e8 to 1e9 (the shares a galaxy can draw, up to 45%, give 2.4e8 and are inside it). The figures below this paragraph (1.44, 175, the 0.61 share, the 0.29 of core-collapse stars) are the GEN.541 check as it was made.

GEN.927 and GEN.928 (2026-10-30): the mass function is Chabrier's, and the placed stars are its primaries. The single-star function is now the [R0705] log-normal below one solar mass and a 2.3 power law above it (docs/design/galaxy/stellar-mass-function.md), which has fewer very light stars than the Kroupa broken line: the mass-weighted perturber mass of the stars alone rises from 0.553 to 0.568 and of the whole disk from 1.01 to 1.19, the black holes' count for 5e10 solar masses of stars from 7e7 to 1.0e8 (the lower end of the published 1e8 to 1e9, so the gap the test used to record is closed), and the mean mass of a system from 0.50 to 0.52 solar masses (the placed stars are the primaries, each system's companions are the rest of the stars). The encounter limits shorten by 15% (the table below).

The tidal limit. A pair of total mass M has a Jacobi radius of (G M / (4 Omega^2 - kappa^2))^(1/3), and 4 Omega^2 - kappa^2 = 4 A (A - B) from Oort's constants. Boss's text wrote the last form with 2 A (A - B), which does not match its own first form; with A = 15.3 and B = -11.9 km/s/kpc the correct one gives 4 Omega^2 - kappa^2 = 1.75 x 10^-30 s^-2 (his "about 2 x 10^-30") and 1.73 pc for 2 solar masses, which matches Jiang and Tremaine's 1.7 pc; the 2 A (A - B) form would give 2.18 pc [C]. Section 6.6 of multistar-and-compact-systems.md takes this form from the galactic potential at the cell, the one the orbital update uses. 4. How the rules reach the draw

Built. The close-pair factor reads the primary's own [Fe/H] (primary_star.metallicity_fe_h), so it needs nothing from the cell. The wide-pair limit needs the cell's density and dispersion: place_terms.from_profile(profile) builds a PlaceTerms once per cell (FillContext.place_terms), FillContext.apply and the pre-placed bright stars put it on SystemConfig.PLACE_TERMS, and a system with none takes the Sun's neighbourhood (tuning.SOLAR_NEIGHBOURHOOD_*). StarSystem._companion rolls none, close or wide in one draw and WideBinaryPair draws its axis up to wide_pair_limit_au. It is a function of the cell, so the same seed gives the same stars wherever a run lands (the GEN.39 rule), and it stays out of the stellar evolution constants. Section 3.2 would need the same record to carry the young-share prevalence.
