Status (2026-10-29): a measurement report (PERF.31, #1179), a dated snapshot of one build box on the old queue. Where it says a scatter is one job whose chunks are tasks, that was replaced by PERF.86 (built): each scatter kind is its own stage of a run and its units are RQ jobs on the bulk queue. The timings were not re-measured.

Measured with planetgen benchmark --workers 1,2 --profile on the lane's build box (4 cores, 16 GB, MariaDB 10.11 on the same box, Redis local). Galaxy: seed ...BEEF, plan to ring 12 (a small galaxy: 2,041 layers), then galaxy --ring 12 --limit 2. Two runs of the 1-worker case agreed within 5%.

Re-run with python -m planetgen.cli.generate benchmark --workers 1,2 --profile --output report.json (about 15 min). It makes and drops its own scratch databases.

Where a 1-worker run goes (138 s)

Stage Seconds Share
Plan: scatter the phenomena 23.7 17%
Plan: scatter the massive stars 10.6 8%
Plan: scatter the bright stars 8.2 6%
Fill: generate the 2 sectors 48.4 35%
Fill: save the sector paths (settle) 33.4 24%
Fill: link neighbours 4.5 3%

The database ran at about 980 statements per second (311 inserts/s, 180 updates/s, 179 commits/s).

Findings, largest first

  1. Two workers make the plan scatter 12 times slower (plan 47 s -> 594 s; bright-star scatter 8 s -> 282 s, massive stars 11 s -> 206 s, phenomena 24 s -> 101 s). The server answered 542,000 statements against 135,000, so the extra cost is queue and database round trips per layer (2,041 small layers), not computation. The fill itself does speed up with two workers (generate 48 s -> 30 s). Cause not yet isolated; the poll constants in queue/work.py and a task per layer are the places to look.
  2. Save the sector paths costs 33 s for 2 sectors (24% of the run and flat with workers). In the profile settle_after_run is 80 s of 227 s and integrate_path (17,284 calls) is 54 s.
  3. Generating a sector: nearest_neighbors makes 1.8 million distance_to calls for 1,916 systems (41 s of 227 s in the profile); a spatial grid would cut this by a large factor.
  4. Inserting a sector is 40 s of the profile (insert_sector), 17% of it.
  5. The scatter stages scale with layers, not objects: bright-star scatter visits 2,041 layers and places 0 objects (8 s).

The profiler slows the run 2.5 times, so read its seconds as shares of the run, not as wall time.

Not measured

Default-scale galaxy; MySQL 8.4; more than 2 workers. The profile is of the fill only, not the plan.

Save the sector paths, after PERF.80

Measured by re-saving the paths of the benchmark galaxy's two sectors (sector_paths.settle_sectors, 17,284 bodies: 1,916 star systems and the rogue planets and comets; the two sectors and their neighbours) on the kept scratch database, 4 cores, MariaDB 10.11, an otherwise idle machine:

Seconds
Before (main) 44.7
After, 1 worker 20.0
After, 2 workers 13.3
After, 4 workers 10.8

The saved paths are the same to the last bit: a hash over every path and knot of the 17,284 bodies is identical before and after, at every worker count.

What changed:

  • The cell test. integrate_path asked geometry.sector_address_at (a ring, a layer and a slot, each worked out afresh, ring_sector_count three times over) at every step and 40 more times to bisect where the path leaves the cell: 43% of its time. geometry.sector_cell_test repeats the same arithmetic with the cell's numbers worked out once and stops at the first of ring, layer and slot that is not the cell's; a test holds it equal to sector_address_at == address on thousands of points including the cell's faces. ring_sector_count and ring_master_count are cached.
  • Smaller costs. The bisection builds only the position of the Hermite curve it tests; the integrator unpacks its masses to (x, y, z, mu) once per path instead of computing G * mass at every step for every mass.
  • One statement batch per sector. A body's path took a lookup, a delete, an insert and a knot insert; a sector's paths are now saved with one lookup per 500 bodies, one insert of the path rows and one of the knots.
  • Worker processes. galaxy passes its worker count to settle_sectors, which hands a crowded sector's bodies (250 per task) to that many spawned processes; loading, saving and the commit stay in the calling process, and the paths and their order do not depend on the count. What remains at 4 workers (about 10 s) is that serial part: the sector and neighbour query and the inserts.

After PERF.79 (scatter layers in chunks)

The plan's three scatter passes now send their layers to the workers in at most 8 chunks per worker instead of one task per layer (WorkQueue.submit_each). Same benchmark (ring 12, seed ...BEEF, 2 sectors), machine otherwise idle:

workers plan phenomena massive bright objects
1 38.5 s 18.4 s 8.8 s 6.7 s 52,517
2 24.2 s 8.2 s 6.0 s 5.4 s 52,517

Before the change two workers took 593.9 s for the plan (about 12 times slower than one). The object counts match between one and two workers, so the rows are the same. At the time of this change the scatter was one job on the RQ queue and the chunks were tasks of that job (WorkQueue.submit_each). That model is gone (PERF.86, built by PERF.95 to PERF.100 and PERF.106, PERF.118, PERF.121, PERF.122): each scatter kind is its own command and its own stage of a run, cut into units of shells of equal expected work (about 512, never more than 4,000, the same at any worker count), and each unit is an RQ job on the bulk queue carrying JSON; the timings above are of the old queue and have not been re-measured (see redis-and-the-work-queue.md, "As built").

Writing the phenomenon rows (PERF.73)

At the default scale the phenomena pass writes about 68 million rows, so the write is most of its time. Measured on 1,000,000 synthetic rows of the real shape (40 layers of 25,000, same machine, MariaDB 10.11, default buffer pool, one connection, commit per statement batch):

way of writing rows per second notes
driver executemany, 10,000 per statement (before) 37,000
same, 1,000 or 50,000 per statement, or one commit per layer 37,000 to 40,000 batch and commit size do not matter
same, unique_checks/foreign_key_checks off 37,000 the table has neither
multi-row INSERT text built in the store instead of the driver's per-value escaping 49,000 the driver's escaping alone is about 9 s of the 27 s
built text and the two secondary indexes dropped, then built once at the end 60,000 (79,000 for the write, 4 s to build the indexes) what the scatter does now
LOAD DATA LOCAL INFILE, indexes dropped 89,000 needs local_infile on the server and the client; not used

So the scatter now writes 1.6 times faster than before on this table, and the 68 million rows go from about 31 minutes of database time to about 19 plus the index build. The rows are the same (a test stores numpy numbers, NULLs and quoted names both ways). A scatter that dies half way leaves the indexes off but also no recorded seed, so no sector fill uses the table until the next scatter clears and rebuilds it. LOAD DATA stays an option if the host's server allows it.

PERF.87 and PERF.90: placement as arrays

SpaceSector._random_position tests a candidate against all massive neighbours with one NumPy comparison; points within 1e-9 of a boundary fall back to math.dist, so accept and reject decisions match the old loop. Two tests prove it (a fill compared with the old loop, and a borderline check). Benchmark, 2 sectors at ring 12, 1 worker, idle machine: the sector fill went from 68.0 s to 64.0 s (the plan stages are untouched and moved by noise, 40 to 45 s). _ring_centerline_edges joins ring_master_count and ring_sector_count in the cache.

PERF.89: population densities as arrays

density.population_densities_array gives the four population densities for every angle bin of a ring in one call; bright_stars._ring_bins and layer_expected_stars use it. NumPy's exp, log, cos and arctan2 are not bit-for-bit the maths library's: about 12% of values differ in the last digit, so the tests hold the array form to 1e-12 relative of the one-point form rather than to the last digit (Math Research asked for the last digit; flagged). A bin weight moving by 1e-16 can change a star's placement only if a random draw lands within 1e-16 of a bin edge. Benchmark bright-star stage, 2 sectors at ring 12: 8.5 s to 7.5 s (the other plan stages moved by up to 5 s between runs, so this is within noise at benchmark scale; the default-scale galaxy has far more bins).

DB.24: the nebula field table

planetgen plan writes nebula_field inside the phenomena stage (one task per first-index slab of 50 pc field cells, through the work queue). Benchmark (2 sectors, ring 12): the phenomena stage stays at 18.8 s on one worker and 12.3 s on two, as before the table, so the build is small at that scale. The table has no object IDs for clouds no sector has stored: working an ID out costs a nebula shape (interior_centroid over the fit grid, about 112 ms), which for every drawn cloud would be about 30 hours of CPU at the default scale. A save sets uid and is_stored on the row of each cloud it stores.

PERF.92: per-object timing

Each generated object is timed through the class's constructor (object_timing.track), so no generator has timing code of its own. Measured on this machine: 3.3 microseconds per object with a unit active and nothing when none is. A 25-system sector with planets builds about 740 objects in about 0.3 s, so the records cost about 0.8% of generation time (inside the 1% the item asks for; a whole-sector A/B run was lost in the noise, at plus or minus 15%, so the per-object cost is the figure to trust). Draw counts and memory per object are not recorded: counting draws would add a call to the random wrapper's hot path, which costs more than the timing itself. The plain sector command (unplaced sectors) is not timed; every galaxy path is.

PERF.88: the sector-path integrator on numba

physics/field_kernel.py holds the integrator (mass selection, kick-drift-kick steps, exit bisection and the spline simplification) as one numba-compiled call, and sector_path.integrate_path calls it. Measured on this machine (planetgen-env, an idle box): 3,000 random bodies of one sector (edge 4 pc, 300 masses within 6 pc of its centre, speeds of about 30 km/s) against the Python integrator it replaced, each with the body's own mass excluded:

Per path
Python loop (before) 838 us
field_kernel.integrate (after) 40 us (21 times faster)

The first call in a process loads the compiled code from numba's cache (about 0.3 s; the first ever compile is about 6 s). On 300 random cells of the grid (core, caps and interior bands, one to five masses) the knots were bit-identical to the Python integrator's, so saved paths do not change. The end-to-end "save the sector paths" figure of the benchmark fill was not re-run here; the research note measured 34.2 s to 13.4 s for the same change, the rest being database writes.
