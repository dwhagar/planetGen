How the code uses these numbers

  • Every kind in PHENOMENON_DENSITY_PC3 becomes a rate per star: density / 0.14 * PHENOMENON_RATE_SCALE[kind] (phenomenon_rate_per_star). All scales are 1.0.
  • generate_sector_phenomena counts the sector's stars (a binary counts two, sector_star_count) and draws each kind's count from a Poisson distribution with mean rate * stars. This is the "per star" form the model below asks for.
  • A kind's rate per star is scaled by the place it is rolled in: the sector profile's kind_factors ("Rates by place" below, GEN.236). The mean number of a kind in a sector is rate * stars * factor; a kind with no entry in kind_factors is scaled by star count only. (The research's gas-density scaling for supernova remnants, n_* * rho_gas, is the supernova-remnant factor.) Correction (GEN.47, checked against the code 2026-10-09): molecular clouds in a galaxy are now scaled by gas density, GMC_GAS_DENSITY_EXPONENT is read by galaxy/nebula_field.py; GMC_ARM_FILLING_FACTOR is still defined and read by nothing.
  • Correction (GEN.100): in a galaxy, black holes, neutron stars, planetary nebulae, supernova remnants and hypervelocity stars are not rolled per sector. They are drawn galaxy-wide at the same per-star rates and placed by generation/phenomenon_scatter.py right after the plan; a sector's fill only builds what was placed there.
  • Runaway and hypervelocity stars are flags on ordinary systems (star_systems.runaway_class), not phenomenon rows. The hypervelocity chance scales as (8000 pc / r_GC)^2.
  • Interstellar comets kept as rows use a design rate, "comet": 0.007 per pc³ (0.05 per star), not the research density. The real population (1e15 per pc³, INTERSTELLAR_DEBRIS_DENSITY_PC3) is shown only as a computed count on the sector page (queryDb.interstellar_debris_count).
  • Asteroid fields have rate 0; the type stays for hand-made fields and facilities.

The model

Treat each object type as a Poisson process in space. Its local number density n_i (objects per pc³) scales with the local stellar density n_*, normalized to the solar neighborhood value n_*0 ≈ 0.14 pc⁻³ (0.10-0.14 in the literature):

n_i(r) = n_i0 * (n_*(r) / 0.14)

The chance of at least one object of type i in a volume V is 1 - exp(-n_i V), and the count is Poisson with mean n_i V.

Because a sector's system count already follows n_* V, a density that scales with n_* is the same as a fixed rate per star: n_i0 / 0.14. So the existing per-sector Poisson draw in generate.generate_sector_phenomena keeps working; only the rates change (and "per star system" should become "per star", or be divided by the mean stars per system, about 1.3 with binaries).

Three types don't scale with n_* alone:

  • Giant molecular clouds scale with gas density, ∝ ρ_gas^1.4 (Schmidt-Kennicutt), and are extended: a filling factor of about 0.01-0.02 inside spiral arms.
  • Supernova remnants scale with n_* · ρ_gas.
  • Hypervelocity stars scale with r_GC⁻² (distance from the galactic center), since Sagittarius A* ejects them.

Galaxy totals behind these (for scale): 25-100 billion brown dwarfs, 1e7-1e8 runaway stars, 1e3-1e4 hypervelocity stars, ~1e9 isolated neutron stars, ~1e8 isolated black holes, ~20,000 planetary nebulae, ~1,000 detectable supernova remnants. Intracluster ("hostless") supernovae are 5-20% of supernovae in rich galaxy clusters; they happen between galaxies, so they have no place in one galaxy's generator.

Rogue planet mass bins (GEN.2, GEN.45)

Low-mass rogues dominate: disk scattering ejects small bodies while giants stay bound. Draw a bin by its per-star rate, then a mass inside it.

Since GEN.45 both the rates and the draw inside a bin follow one mass function, dN/dlogM ∝ M^-0.65 from 0.1 M⊕ to 13 Mjup (Boss's research of 2026-10-01), normalized to the same 6.5 rogues per star (ROGUE_PLANET_MASS_FUNCTION_SLOPE, ROGUE_PLANET_RATE_PER_STAR). The research writes it dN/dM ∝ M^-0.65; read per unit mass, that would make about 87% of rogues gas giants, against its own aim of keeping them rare, so it is read per logarithm of mass.

(Pre-retune figures; the GEN.100 retune of 2026-10-09 sets terrestrial rogue planets to 5.8 per star.)

That is about 95% terrestrial and 5% giants (past ROGUE_PLANET_GAS_GIANT_MASS_THRESHOLD_JUPITER, 10 M⊕, the core mass that holds an envelope; GEN.1133 moved it from 16). The hand-set rates before it (5, 1, 0.25 and 0.25 per star, a log-uniform mass in each bin) gave 91% and 9%. Above 13 Mjup is a brown dwarf, its own row in the density table.

A steeper law doesn't fit both ends: dN/dlogM ∝ M^-0.96 (the [R4254, R4255] slope) normalized to 0.25 per star above 1 Mjup gives about 575 per star above 0.1 M⊕, a hundred times the terrestrial estimate.

Checks

  • Comet separation column (GEN.659). The old research table gave 1e11-1e14 pc⁻³ (1e-4 to 1e-2 AU⁻³, a spacing of 45 to 4.5 AU). The papers say more: Do, [R1124] find about 0.2 AU⁻³ for objects like '[R2159] about 0.1 AU⁻³ (1 pc³ = 8.8e15 AU³), so 7e14 to 1.8e15 pc⁻³, a spacing n^(-1/3) of about 2.2 to 1.7 AU. The constant takes 1e15, the middle of that range, for objects of about 100 m and up.
  • Hypervelocity stars. With n ∝ r_GC⁻², the count inside radius R is 4π n0 r0² R. Using 5e-9 pc⁻³ at 8 kpc gives about 30,000 inside 8 kpc and about 400,000 out to 100 kpc, against the stated 1e3-1e4 total. A total of 1e4 out to 100 kpc implies about 1e-10 pc⁻³ at 8 kpc. Use 1e-10 unless Boss prefers the table's figure.
  • Supernova remnants. ~1,000 detectable remnants over a disk of about 7e11 pc³ is about 1.5e-9 pc⁻³; the table's 1e-8 implies about 7,000. The generator before 7.18.0 used 2,000 galaxy-wide. The code now uses 1e-8, which counts faint, undetectable remnants too.
  • Molecular cloud density (recommendation, 2026-10-09). 5e-6 pc⁻³ is the top of the table's range and, with the GEN.47 field, puts 3% to 46% of the volume inside a cloud (13% of arm sectors at the solar circle) against about 0.5% to 1% observed; the catalogues (about 8,100 to 9,700 clouds, 1,064 massive ones) give 1.2e-8 to 1.4e-8 pc⁻³. About 1e-7 pc⁻³ for class M, with the small dark classes as their own rows, would match. Boss chose the 5e-6 research value, so this is a recommendation only (derivation in nebula-and-asteroid-field-classes.md, "The molecular cloud field").
  • Neutron star pulsar fraction (recommendation, 2026-10-09; pre-retune: the GEN.100 retune of 2026-10-09 sets 2% of neutron stars pulsing, and NEUTRON_STAR_PULSAR_CHANCE no longer exists in src/). The catalogue above gives about 5e8 isolated neutron stars. NEUTRON_STAR_PULSAR_CHANCE = 0.7 makes 3.5e8 of them pulsars, against roughly 1e5 beamed radio pulsars (an active fraction of about 1e-4 to 1e-3), because radio emission ends after about 1e7 years and an isolated star has no companion to recycle it. The constant is a gameplay choice in the code today; the physical value and an age rule are in anomalies.md.
  • (Pre-retune values; the GEN.100 retune of 2026-10-09 sets terrestrial rogue planets to 5.8 per star, neutron stars to 0.4% and black holes to 0.05%, see "Retune of 2026-10-09" below.) Terrestrial (0.78 pc⁻³ = 5.6 per star), Jupiter-mass (0.004 = 0.028 per star, under the 0.035 limit), brown dwarfs (0.03 = 1 per 4.7 stars), runaways (1.5%), neutron stars and black holes (0.5-0.7% and 0.05-0.07% of stars, matching the galaxy totals) are self-consistent. The generator used 0.5% and 0.1% before the retune of 2026-10-09 instead, the shares the mass-and-age star model leaves behind (star-fix study, 2026-09-30), so isolated remnants and the star census agree.

Generation cost

Measured on main at 2026-09-30 (default args, 10 sectors): a sector builds about 10 systems in about 0.1 s, roughly 650 body rows (stars, planets, moons). Building one rogue planet object takes about 0.13 ms.

At the research rates a local-density sector adds about 50 rogue rows (45 terrestrial, 2 giant, 2 brown dwarfs, plus ~1 sub-Neptune class per star under the default bins, so nearer 60). That is about 8-10% more rows and well under 1% more generation time. Storage and speed are not the problem. What changes is what a sector looks like: rogues would outnumber systems about five to one in the Contents table and on the Sector Map.

Comets can't be rows: ~6e16 per sector. Neutron stars and everything rarer are fine as rows; most sectors simply get none.

Why it works this way

  • Per star, not per system or per volume. A sector's system count already follows local stellar density, so a fixed rate per star gives the density scaling for free and keeps the old per-sector Poisson draw. A binary counts as two stars, which is why the rate is per star, not per system (section "The model").
  • Full rates. Boss chose the research rates at full strength on 2026-09-30. PHENOMENON_RATE_SCALE exists so a kind can be dialed down (for example 0.1 for rogue planets) without changing its research value.
  • Mass bins for rogue planets. One power law cannot match both the terrestrial and the Jupiter-mass measurements (it overshoots the terrestrial count a hundredfold), so the rates are binned ("Rogue planet mass bins").
  • Hypervelocity 1e-10. The table's 5e-9 implies 30 to 40 times more hypervelocity stars than its own galaxy total ("Checks").
  • Neutron stars 0.5% and black holes 0.1% of stars (pre-retune; 0.4% and 0.05% after the GEN.100 retune of 2026-10-09, and the black hole density has been metallicity-dependent since GEN.563, black-hole-density.md). These were the shares the mass-and-age star model (7.23.0) leaves behind, so isolated remnants and the star census agree.
  • No free asteroid fields. They disperse in 1e6 to 1e7 years.
  • Comets as a figure, not rows. About 6e16 per sector cannot be stored.

Alternatives not taken

  • Scaling clouds and remnants by gas density. The constants are in place for it, but the reason it was not wired up is not recorded. (Clouds were wired up later by GEN.47; remnants still are not.)
  • Supernova remnants at the detectable-only density (1.5e-9). The code chose 1e-8; the comment says it "counts faint remnants too". No further reason is recorded.

Rates by place: the populations and the kind factors (GEN.236)

The per-star rates above are the solar neighbourhood's. Every sector carries a profile (galaxy/profile.py, GEN.235; see intergalactic-and-halo-contents.md section 5.3) that says which stellar populations it holds, and kind_factors_at turns those populations and the gas into one factor per kind, each in 0 to 1, that multiplies the kind's rate per star. A halo cell, a cluster or an old satellite therefore makes no H II region and no core-collapse remnant, and the disk's young, gas-rich places make the most.

The populations (profile.POPULATIONS): young, intermediate and old (the thin disk by age, with the young open clusters of tuning.OPEN_CLUSTER_POPULATIONS counted with the disk), bulge, halo, cluster, satellite (with satellite_young and satellite_intermediate), nuclear_cluster and nuclear_disk. Each has an age window, [Fe/H] peaks, a velocity dispersion and a rotation rule.

Kind Factor
brown-dwarf 1 everywhere (every population makes them)
black-hole (stellar mass) the share of the place's massive stars that failed to explode and made their remnant, from the populations' shares, ages and [Fe/H] peaks (remnant_density.place_factor_from_peaks, GEN.563): a metal-poor place makes more
neutron-star what the black holes leave of the massive stars that made their remnant, less the galaxy's unbound share (GEN.554): a metal-rich place makes more
supernova-remnant the gas fraction (nebula_field.gas_factor, capped at 1), times the share of stars that are not in a cluster; 0 in the halo and in clusters. Not zeroed in a superbubble: the hot cavity is where the explosions went off. The Type Ia share of the remnants is a profile field, type_ia_share (GEN.319): 1 where no young population is
planetary-nebula the share of the place's stars that are not young against the solar circle's, capped at 1 (GEN.319): 1 in the disk (within 3%) and in every older place
molecular-cloud the cold gas fraction times the young share against the solar circle's
nebula (emission and reflection) the cold gas fraction; the host star's own age is the young share here
rogue-planet, comet the planet occurrence at the place's [Fe/H] against the disk's (metallicity.planet_occurrence_factor), averaged over the populations

A kind not in the table is not scaled by place. The neutron stars and black holes the galaxy lost to a kick (ejecta_field, GEN.238) are not part of the stars' rate: the fill adds them to the cell's own draw at a density that falls as 1 / r^2. The galaxy-wide scatters (generation/phenomenon_scatter.py) draw the black holes, neutron stars, planetary nebulae and supernova remnants with the same factors, band by band.

The stars a nebula needs (GEN.320, GEN.321, GEN.241)

A nebula of a class that has stars gets them as real systems, inside its bounds, from seeds that depend on the nebula or the star alone, never on the order sectors are filled in (generation/nebula_contents.py). Whether a hot star has a nebula, and of which class, is rolled on a stream of the star's own (hosted_seed), so a star the scatter pre-placed and the same star made in a fill agree. A planetary nebula's white dwarf is made with it, and PLANETARY_NEBULA_COMPANION_CHANCE of them gain a close companion. A Bok globule (P) or a star-forming core (Q) gets its protostars from the cloud's own seed, built by whichever sector holds each point. The cluster behind an H II region of class D or E is its ionizing members, the stars of 5.5 solar masses and up still alive, placed in a Plummer sphere of half-mass radius 1 pc (D) or 2 pc (E) clipped to the nebula's radius; only these are real, and the rest of the cluster stays the sector's background density. A supernova remnant keeps its neutron star or black hole through the remnant object itself.

Wolf-Rayet stars and the ring nebula (GEN.243)

A star of at least tuning.WR_MIN_INITIAL_MASS_SOL (25 solar masses at the Sun's metallicity, rising as Z^-0.5, so 35 at half and 56 at a fifth of it) spends the last WR_PHASE_MYR (0.3 Myr, at most half its supergiant window) of its life as a Wolf-Rayet star, in place of the end of the supergiant window of stellar_evolution.evolve_star: yerkes class W, a present mass of 0.4 to 0.6 of the initial mass, a luminosity log-uniform over 1e5 to 1e6 solar, and a subtype by the share of the phase gone (WN 60%, WC 30%, WO 10%, the last only from 40 solar masses; WR_TEMPERATURE_RANGE_K and WR_SUBCLASS_RANGE give the temperature and the subclass, so the text reads WN6 Blue Wolf-Rayet Star). No Wolf-Rayet star is rolled from a rate: the bright and mass passes place the massive stars and draw their state, which gives about 1,180 in a Milky Way-like disk against the 1,200 expected, and none in a population older than the phase allows. Their companions follow the subtype (WR_COMPANION_CHANCE: 0.52 for WN, 0.90 for WC and WO, read by the system builder; the wolf-rayet-binary row of the phenomena catalogue). A Wolf-Rayet star can light a class X ring nebula (family emission, radius 6.5 to 114 ly, a thin swept-up shell around a hollow interior, host_only: only a Wolf-Rayet star makes one, so a nebula drawn with no host is never X); the host rule in NEBULA_HOST_RULES rolls it from the star, and NEBULA_CLASS_MAX_AGE_MYR["X"] caps the host's age at 10 Myr. The rest of the design and the numbers are in the intergalactic design note, section 5.11.

Retune of 2026-10-09 (Boss: central observed values)
