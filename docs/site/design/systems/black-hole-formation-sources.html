2. What each source says (quoted numbers)

2.1 [R3327] (1.5D general-relativistic collapse, ~700 runs, >100 progenitors)

  • Outcome set by the bounce compactness xi_2.5 (mass 2.5 Msun divided by radius in 1000 km at bounce). Quoted: "progenitors with bounce compactness xi_2.5 >~ 0.45 most likely form BHs without explosion" (LS220 equation of state, neutrino-mechanism assumption, no multi-dimensional effects).
  • Share, quoted from section 5: "Using a Salpeter initial mass function (IMF; alpha = 2.35, Mmin = 8.0 Msun, and Mmax = 150.0 Msun) we estimate that ~15% of all progenitors form BHs without explosion" (low metallicity, where mass loss is negligible). "Depending on the particular mass-loss prescription, we predict a BH fraction of 0%-7% for solar-metallicity stars." The Fig. 13 caption: the fractions are for massive stars above 8 Msun, "under the assumption that stars with 8 Msun <~ M <~ 14 Msun explode robustly".
  • Mass and metallicity dependence: at 10^-4 solar "BHs form within <~ 1 s of bounce for MZAMS >~ 30 Msun", and possibly also 20 to 25 Msun. Solar ([R4765] set): BHs only in "~23-25 Msun and ~35-45 Msun"; above that "strong O-star mass loss" leaves "bare, low-mass, low-compactness carbon oxygen cores in the most massive progenitors that are unlikely to make BHs". With weaker Wolf-Rayet mass loss ([R2689] set A, factor ~2 lower) the 60 to 120 Msun stars keep 17 to 30 Msun and xi_2.5 ~ 0.6 to 0.9, so they would form BHs. Quoted: mass loss is "the single most important unknown parameter".
  • Remnant mass at formation: the proto-neutron star collapses at a maximum baryonic mass of "2.1 to 2.5 Msun" (LS180 equation of state); thermal support raises the maximum by 10 to 25 percent over the cold neutron star. This is the birth mass; the black hole then grows by whatever falls in (the progenitor mass, section 2.2). Rotation raises the PNS maximum by about 5 to 10 percent.
  • Does NOT support: any share beyond 0 to 15 percent, any rotation-free claim for real stars (binaries, rotation and 3D effects are left out; the authors say so), any fallback BHs (explicitly neglected in Fig. 13).

2.2 [R4251] (1D neutrino-powered explosions, solar metallicity, 9 to 120 Msun, Salpeter IMF)

  • Quoted abstract: "Many progenitors with extended core structures do not explode, but become black holes, and the masses of exploding stars do not form a simply connected set." Section 6: "there were islands of 'explodability' in a sea of black hole formation". Stars "above 35 Msun lost their envelopes and a few ... exploded as Type Ibc, but most became black holes". "BH formation ... most, but not all of the stars above 20 Msun and some below 20 Msun".
  • Share: Table 4 gives SN% (IMF-weighted fraction of stars that explode and leave a neutron star; "One minus this fraction is the percentage that became black holes", including the few fallback BHs) for four central-engine calibrations: W15 66, W18 67, W20 55, N20 74. So the black-hole share of stars from 9 to 120 Msun is 34, 33, 45 and 26 percent (W15, W18, W20, N20): range 26 to 45 percent. The fallback part is small, see below.
  • Explosion part: the same table gives the share of explosions from stars above 20 Msun as 8, 9, 3 and 13 percent (and above 30 Msun: 2, 2, 0, 5), "typically 95% of the stars that did explode were SN IIp with masses less than 20 Msun".
  • Black hole masses (Table 4 columns, IMF-weighted averages, gravitational mass set equal to baryonic mass): helium core only 7.69, 8.40, 9.05, 9.23 Msun (text: "7.7-9.2"); whole presupernova star 13.2, 13.3, 13.6, 13.8 Msun (text says "13.3 - 13.8", the table shows 13.2 for W20: a one-digit mismatch inside the paper). The abstract rounds to "about 9" and "about 14". The authors prefer the helium-core case as "in better agreement with observations" and note the whole-star case is an upper bound.
  • Fallback BHs are rare in this grid: "W15 series yielded only one black hole ... 4.7 Msun ... 60 Msun"; W18 two (3.2 and 6.2 Msun); strongest engines a few at 4.1 to 7.3 Msun; none for W20. So the 2 to ~4 Msun mass gap is reproduced; neutron stars average 1.40 Msun gravitational (range 1.23 to 1.77).
  • Does NOT support: other metallicities (solar only), rotation, binaries, a single shared value (four calibrations disagree by 19 points), or a metallicity trend.

2.3 [R1466] (semi-analytic remnant maps for population synthesis)

  • Two engines: "delayed" (continuous remnant masses) and "rapid" (sharp neutron star to black hole step, ~2 to 5 Msun gap). Fits of remnant baryonic mass against initial mass M and metallicity Z (relative to solar): eq. 5 (delayed, 11 to 30 Msun) = 1.1 + 0.2 exp((M-11)/4) - (2.0+Z) exp(0.4(M-26)); eq. 6 rapid; eq. 7 and 8 (30 to 50 Msun); eq. 9 (above 50 Msun, solar): 1.8 + 0.04 (90 - M) below 90 Msun. Quoted: "Below ~25-30 Msun, the results are fairly insensitive to the metallicity". "The maximum BH mass increases with decreasing metallicity, as mass-loss becomes weaker." Gravitational mass of a BH: M_rem = 0.9 M_rem,bar (eq. 14), "the gravitational masses are likely to be ~10% lower" than baryonic.
  • Direct black hole formation is defined by the fallback fraction reaching 1 (eq. 11 delayed: carbon-oxygen core M_CO >= 7.6 Msun; eq. 16 rapid: 6.0 <= M_CO < 7.0 and M_CO >= 11.0 Msun). Quoted caveat: the fallback ranges are "estimated from core collapse models of [R1468] and the analysis of [R1467]".
  • Rapid engine black hole mass distribution "peaks at roughly 7-8 Msun"; the delayed engine puts 33 percent (solar) or 17 percent (0.1 solar) of remnants above 2 Msun into the 2 to 5 Msun gap, the rapid engine 18 percent / below 1.5 percent (quoted as ".18%" in the extraction; read the published text before using it).
  • No shares are published. Pair-instability is explicitly left out.
  • Own computation (indicative, not a source value, transcription risk): integrating a Salpeter IMF over 8 to 150 Msun with my transcription of eqs. 5 to 9 and counting a remnant above 2.5 (or 3.0) baryonic Msun as a black hole gives about 22 to 30 percent of stars in 8 to 150 Msun at solar, 0.1 solar and zero metallicity for both engines. It lands inside the range of the two other sources; it is wind-free at zero metallicity so it is an upper-side estimate.

Observation (all text read):

  • [R3274] ([R3274], LBT, 11 yr, 27 galaxies): failed supernova fraction (of core-collapse) f = 0.16 +0.23/-0.12 at 90% confidence for one clear candidate; upper limit f < 0.226 if all candidates are rejected; they note it matches "~10-30 per cent" estimates from the missing red supergiants and the Galactic BH mass function ([R2404], cited only through Neustadt, not opened).
  • [R0621] ([R0621], PTF/ZTF, 231 galaxies, zero detections): 95% upper limits of 0.61, 0.33, 0.26, 0.23 of the core-collapse rate at absolute magnitude -11, -12, -13, -14.
  • Together the observations allow the same 0 to ~0.4 range and exclude nothing in the theoretical 5 to 45 percent window.

4. Summary for GEN.563

  1. Verified failed-supernova / direct-collapse share f, as a fraction of stars born with initial mass 8 (or 9) up to 120 or 150 Msun, Salpeter IMF in every source:
    • solar metallicity: 0-7% (O'Connor and Ott), 5-8% and 16-21% (Ebinger, two calibrations), 26-45% (Sukhbold, four calibrations), ~22-28% (own computation from Fryer's fits). Widest sourced range at solar: 0 to 45%.
    • low metallicity (1e-4 solar or zero): ~15% (O'Connor and Ott), 18-20% and 27-32% (Ebinger). Range 15 to 32%; no source gives a lower share than solar at low Z except [R2773]'s rotation case (abstract-level).
    • observed (failed supernovae as share of core collapses): 0.16 (0.04 to 0.39) at 90% (Neustadt); < 0.23 to < 0.61 at 95% (Byrne and Fraser).
    • Under the project rule (widest range wins when sources are in doubt), the usable verified range is 0 to 45%, with the dependence "more at low metallicity" supported by Ebinger and O'Connor and Ott, and the Sukhbold engines alone covering 26 to 45%.
  2. Initial-mass and metallicity dependence: not a cut. Islands of failure from ~20 Msun (some below; [R1286]'s introduction, says islands of black-hole cases reach down to 15 Msun), mostly failure above ~35 Msun at low Z; at solar Z strong winds make the most massive stars explode as Ib/c or leave low-mass remnants unless Wolf-Rayet loss is weak. In M_CO terms: windows at ~6 to 7 Msun and above ~13 Msun, both moving down with lower Z (Maltsev, Tables 3 and 4).
  3. Remnant mass of a failed supernova: baryonic birth mass 2.1 to 2.5 Msun at collapse (O'Connor and Ott), then the black hole holds either the helium core (IMF-weighted 7.7 to 9.2 Msun, Sukhbold) or the whole star (13.2 to 13.8 Msun); gravitational mass is 0.9 times baryonic (Fryer eq. 14) or the baryonic mass less neutrino losses up to ~0.3 Msun (O'Connor and Ott, quoted through Sukhbold). Low metallicity gives heavier holes (Fryer, Ebinger). The rapid engine of Fryer peaks at 7 to 8 Msun.
  4. Conversion to the generator's per-star share (own computation, [R2498] IMF 0.08 to 150 Msun, broken power law 1.3 / 2.3): stars of 8 to 150 Msun are 0.637% of stars (9 to 120: 0.540%; 20 to 100: 0.174%, which reproduces the 0.17% in GEN.563). So the black hole share of stars, including only failed supernovae, is f x 0.64% = 0.0% to 0.29% (f of 5 / 15 / 26 / 45% gives 0.032 / 0.096 / 0.166 / 0.287%), i.e. 0 to 4.0e-4 per pc^3 at 0.14 stars per pc^3. The present constant (7e-5 per pc^3, 0.05%) corresponds to f = 7.8%: inside the verified range, near its low end, at the top of the O'Connor and Ott solar band and inside Ebinger's solar standard calibration. No source supports a single central value; the choice is between keeping 7e-5 labelled "inside the verified 0-45% range" and moving it to a derived f(Z) (that would be a GEN.563 decision, with Boss's rules on stand-ins).
  5. Caveats the item should carry: (a) the shares are of core-collapse events of single, non-rotating stars; binaries and rotation change them ([R2773, R4866], Maltsev Case A/B/C); (b) fallback black holes are not failed supernovae: rare in Sukhbold, large in Fryer's maps, so the total black hole share is at least f; (c) the kick-escape share is not in these sources (direct collapse is expected to give low kicks; the multi-star note already uses progenitor >= 40 Msun as the direct-collapse flag, which none of the sources contradicts for low Z but which is not a verified threshold at solar Z where 23 to 25 Msun also fail); (d) the per-star conversion counts stars born, while the density counts living stars plus remnants; low-mass stars survive so the difference is small but it is not computed here.
