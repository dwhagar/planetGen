2. The numbers

Yarkovsky drift (BR1). da/dt scales as 1/D for a large body ([R4568], section 2.1). The best measured case is Bennu: D = 493 m, da/dt = -18.95 +/- 0.10 e-4 au/My at a mean distance of 1.10 au; Golevka (D = 280 m) -4.52 e-4. So da/dt x D = 9.3e-4 au km/My at 1.1 au. Scaled to a belt with the flux (1/a^2, a stand-in that ignores the thermal-parameter dependence), a 1 km body at 2.5 au drifts 1.8e-4 au/My, the "order 1e-4" of the design note. A 40 km body drifts 0.05e-4 au/My, 0.2 au in 4.5 Gy: bodies above about 40 km do not move, as the design says.

Chaotic zone (BR2). The zone of overlapping first-order resonances round a planet of mass ratio mu is delta a / a = 1.3 mu^(2/7) ([R4727], read here in [R3226], who confirm 1.35 mu^0.278 by numerical integration at zero eccentricity). Mean-motion resonances stay a_res = a_giant (q/p)^(2/3).

Loss in unstable resonances (BR3). Dynamical chaos is the main loss for bodies of 10 km and more in the present belt. The loss history follows a logarithmic law, established by 1 My after the planets reached their orbits and holding to 4 Gy: about 50 percent of the large asteroids lost since then, equal amounts (about 30 percent of the present number) in 10 to 200 My and in 0.2 to 4 Gy [R3093]. The design's "lost in 1 to 10 My" is the resonance clearing at the start, not the later tail.

Early depletion (BR4). The primordial belt held about 1 Earth mass (within a factor of a few, from the disk that built the terrestrial planets), against 4.0e-4 now (Pitjeva), three orders less [R3150]. The Grand Tack depletes it by "a factor of several hundred"; of the 1.3e-3 Earth masses of implanted S-type bodies at gas removal (efficiency 0.07 percent), 50 to 90 percent is lost in the instability and a further factor 2 to 3 by chaotic diffusion [R4603]. The belt left by the first event is about 4 times the present one; half of that goes in the later instability and half by resonances [R3150]. Raymond and Izidoro print no single fraction. For the generator: a Grand Tack type event leaves 2e-3 to 1e-2 of the belt (BR4); an instability alone removes 50 to 90 percent (BR5).

Planet or not (BR6, BR7). Soter: mu = M / m (m the mass of the other bodies in the zone), a body with mu above 100 is a planet; the Solar System has a gap, Earth 1.7e6 times Earth mass over the near-Earth objects, Ceres 1/3. Margot: the mass that clears an orbit in the main-sequence lifetime is M_clear = 1.9e-4 C^(3/2) (a / 1 au)^(9/8) (M* / Msun)^(5/2) Earth masses, with C = 2 sqrt 3, and Pi = M / M_clear above 1 is a planet (Jupiter 4.0e4, Mars 54; the dwarf planets are under 1). Stern and Levison's Lambda = k M^2 / P has a gap of five orders of magnitude between Mars and the largest asteroids and Kuiper belt objects. Margot's constant in the design note, 807, is 1 / (1.9e-4 C^(3/2)) = 816 with the printed digits.

Round (BR8, BR9). The potato radius, where a solid body turns spherical, is about 300 km for rock and about 200 km for ice [R2703]: 3.4e20 kg for rock at 3 000 kg/m3 and 3.3e19 kg for ice at 1 000. The design's band for a fully relaxed hydrostatic body (ice 1e20 to 1e21 kg, rock 1e21 to 1e22) is from Bierson and Nimmo and Thomas, which were not reached for this table; it stays a stand-in band (BR9) and the potato radius is the lower, "roughly round" bound.

The top of the size law (BR10, BR11). A young icy disk grows planets of at most about 1 750 km radius, nearly independent of disk mass, stellar mass and age, holding at most 3 to 4 percent of the initial solids [R2324]: a test of the top end of the order statistic. The Kuiper belt: cold classical bright-end slope alpha1 = 1.5 (+0.4 -0.2), break H_B = 6.9, hot alpha1 = 0.87 (+0.07 -0.2), H_B = 7.7 [R1439], both with a faint-end slope near 0.2; masses 0.01 (hot) and 3e-4 (cold) Earth masses. [R0362]: the classical belt holds about 0.010 Earth masses, mostly in bodies near 100 km, its largest about 2 percent of its mass; the excited population's largest holds tens of percent and is about Pluto's mass. [R3539]: the main belt (4.008 +/- 0.029) e-4 Earth masses; the Kuiper belt with its 31 largest objects (1.97 +/- 0.03) e-2, the 31 largest being about 40 percent of it.

White dwarfs (BR12 to BR14). Planets inside about 5 au do not survive the giant branch; any beyond 5 au survive and widen as the star loses mass (a grows as M_initial / M_final). When mass is lost, two-planet systems that were marginal become unstable: of the unstable pairs about 8 percent collide, 92 percent do not, and of those about 40 percent settle with a planet on a closer, more eccentric orbit [R1031]; about 2 percent of young white dwarfs should show a disrupted-planet signature. [R2415] (cooling ages 20 to 200 My): 56 percent of 85 DA white dwarfs show heavy elements, at least 27 percent are accreting now (up to 50 percent), earlier surveys 20 to 30 percent (Zuckerman, 2ND-HAND); accretion rates a few 1e5 to a few 1e8 g/s.

Debris disks round Sun-like stars (BR15). 17.1 (+2.6 -2.3) percent of 275 F to K stars have an excess [R4081], 20.2 +/- 2.0 in DUNES and 29 +/- 9 percent among exoplanet hosts [R2894]. Most disks have a fractional luminosity near 1e-5 at blackbody radii of 7 to 40 au (about 40 au for real grains); the present Kuiper belt sits near the median, "half of stars have either depleted their Kuiper belts or had a lower planetesimal formation efficiency".

Where belts form (BR16). [R2074]: when the pebble flux exceeds a critical value (low viscosity, alpha under 1e-3), silicate dust piles up inside the snow line and rock-rich planetesimals form there. Qualitative; the snow line stays the generator's own snow_line_au.

Size laws of the ledger (BR19 to BR21). physics/belt_ledger.py (GEN.1061) holds two piecewise cumulative laws: the hot Kuiper belt's (q 1.0 and 4.35 about a break of 121 km, [R1439], BR19) and the rocky belt's (q 1.3 below 5 km, 3.0 to 200 km, 4 above: [R2108] for the first two, a stand-in for the top, BR20). Ivezic's 2.3 and 4 are differential indices, so the cumulative exponents are 1.3 and 3.0 (corrected in small-body-size-laws.md 1.4). A stretch of q = 3 holds equal mass in every doubling, so the rocky law must steepen above the measured range; the break at 200 km makes 10 to 20 bodies above 1e19 kg at the main belt's mass (the Solar System has about 20), a count chosen to land there and so tier T9, tweak L3.

Cut rule (BR17, BR18). The cut values of Boss's rule (1e19 kg and 1e15 kg, 90 percent of the belt mass, 300 per belt) are stand-ins from the needed output, registered at tier T9.
