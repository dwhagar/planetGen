The physics inputs the habitability index needs beyond atmosphere species: how active each star is, whether a planet has a magnetic field, how much ionizing radiation and UV reaches its surface, and what water and ocean it holds. This is the research behind docs/TODO.md items GEN.86, GEN.87 and GEN.88, the spin dependency GEN.104, and the reuse of rogue-planet-surface.md. It carries the still-valid formulas of Boss's uploaded documents "Naturally Occuring Ionizing Radiation.md" (Rad), "Chemical Habitability.md" (Chem), "Planetary Habitability Index.md" (PHI-4) and "Mathematical and Algorithmic Implementation of the Planetary Habitability Index.md" (Math), and names each correction to them (section 6). Atmosphere retention and class definitions are in atmospheres-retention-and-classes.md; the score structure and tiers are in habitability-index.md.

Informs: GEN.84, GEN.86, GEN.87, GEN.88, GEN.89, GEN.104, rogue-planet-surface.md Status: research, 2026-10-09; decisions marked "Boss" are his, everything else is a recommendation. Sections 2 and 3 built in GEN.86 (schema v72: physics/activity.py, physics/magnetism.py); the stagnant-lid factor and the induced field of a moon with an ocean are not modelled yet. Section 5 built in GEN.88 (schema v74: physics/hydrosphere.py, which rogue_surface now uses); the class water fractions, the ice-moon eccentricity draw and the 220 K vacuum-ice limit are planetGen defaults, and the stagnant-lid buffer rule is not modelled.

Evidence tags: [S] seen in a search result (URL under Sources), [C] computed (inputs shown), [R] recalled from memory or a design default, unconfirmed. The research environment could only read search-result text, not the papers; the [R] items are listed under Evidence notes.

1. Summary

  1. Stellar activity needs four numbers per star (2.1): saturated L_X/L_bol (7.4e-4), saturation age t_sat, a decay exponent and a flare rate at 1e33 erg. The Rad doc's "L_XUV/L_bol about 1e-3" is the X-ray band only; total XUV of a saturated star is 3.3e-3 (G) to 7.3e-3 (0.1 Msun) [C]. Habitable-zone planets of M dwarfs get 1,000 to 2,000 times Earth's present XUV for billions of years; an exposure index gives the tiers (2.3).
  2. Magnetic fields follow core size, density and convective power, nearly independent of rotation while the dynamo stays dipolar. A tidally locked M-dwarf planet is probably multipolar (dipole about 0.15, standoff 0.53x). Code finding: only moons are tidally locked in generate_orbital_motion_properties; an M-dwarf-zone planet gets a random 10 to 1,400 h spin but is locked within 15 kyr (0.1 Msun) to 3.4 Myr (0.3 Msun) [C]. GEN.104 must fix this before GEN.86.
  3. Surface dose is dominated by column mass. Mars surface 0.64 mSv/day dose equivalent; Earth's cosmic dose 0.39 mSv/yr, 2.4 being the total natural background [S]. A fitted calculator (4.2) matches within 10 percent.
  4. Hydrosphere: the rogue-planet ice-shell function reproduces Europa and Enceladus [C], so reuse is sound with four fixes (5.3). The main one is a high-pressure-ice cap: a liquid ocean cannot exceed about 70 to 110 km at Earth gravity, so ocean_depth_km up to 855 km is not liquid to the bottom.
  5. Add a UV/ozone flag and an optional galactic-hazard modifier to GEN.87 (4.5).

2. Stellar activity (GEN.86)

2.1 X-ray and XUV evolution

  • Saturation level. [R4780] (824 stars): log(L_X/L_bol) = -3.13 +/- 0.08, saturation at Rossby number 0.13, unsaturated slope -2.70 [S for slope and Rossby number; -3.13 is [R]]. Jackson, Davis and Wheatley 2012 (717 stars): the saturated ratio falls from about 10^-3.1 (late K) to 10^-4.3 (early F) [S], so "10^-3 for all stars" holds only for K and M.
  • M dwarfs saturate longer. [R4668]: H-alpha active lifetimes rise from about 0.8 Gyr (M0) to about 8 Gyr (M7) [S]; an upper bound for X-ray saturation.
  • Spread at fixed age is real. Tu et al. 2015: a solar-mass star stays saturated about 10 Myr if it starts slow, 300 Myr if fast [S]; draw a per-star offset of 0.3 to 0.5 dex.
  • Decay. Skumanich spin-down gives L_X ~ t^-1.35 to -1.5 [C], too shallow for the Sun today. Exponent 2.0 for M >= 0.5 Msun gives 3.5e-7 for the Sun now and 2.1e-5 at 0.6 Gyr (Hyades about 1e-5 [R]); fully convective stars decline slowly ([R4781] [R]), so use 1.0 below 0.3 Msun.
  • XUV from X-ray. log L_EUV(100 to 920 A) = 4.80 + 0.860 log L_X (erg/s) [R: as used by [R0711]]. It gives L_XUV(Sun now) = 1.5e28 erg/s, 5.3 erg/cm2/s at 1 AU against Ribas's 4.6 [R], and L_XUV ~ t^-1.29, matching the -1.2 to -1.3 band exponents of Ribas et al. 2005 [S].

Defaults [C: design defaults calibrated as above; the t_sat column is the design's first estimate: generation takes the saturation age from [R2183] by mass and rotation percentile (GEN.493, host-activity-tuning.md section 4) and retunes the exponents from 0.45 Msun up (1.35, 1.8, 1.45, 1.45) to keep the same present-day level]:

2.3 Effects, exposure index and ozone flag

  • Erosion. Energy-limited loss Mdot = eps pi F R^3 / (G M K). With eps = 0.1 and 6.26e7 J/kg to lift water, an Earth-size planet at S = 1 loses 4.5 ocean-equivalents by 5 Gyr, yet Earth kept its oceans because the cold trap limits real loss [C]. Use it as a relative stress index, and as a real estimate only past the moist-greenhouse limit. Scale by (R/R_E)^3 / (M/M_E).
  • Ozone. [R4000]: with protons, a large flare removes 94 percent of ozone over two years on an unmagnetized planet, recovering over about 50 years; [R2014]: 90 percent loss in five years for repeated Proxima-type flaring [S].
  • Ozone-loss flag [R, design default]: flare irradiation index FI = N33 / d_AU^2 (Earth 0.003; Proxima b 2,100). Flag when FI * f_B >= 300, f_B = 1 (no dipole), 0.3 (magnetopause under 3 R_p), 0.1 (above), so that Proxima b without a field is flagged and Earth is not.

2.4 Flares

  • Sun-like stars. [R2807]: one flare above 1e34 erg per 800 years on solar types, mostly faster rotators than the Sun [S]. [R3318]: slow rotators once per 2,000 to 3,000 years [S].
  • Proxima. 1e33 erg about 8 per year (MOST, extrapolated), at least 5.2 (Evryscope), 3 (TESS) [S]; use 3 to 8.
  • Slope of the frequency distribution 1.4 to 2.4, 1.99 +/- 0.07 for low-activity M dwarfs, no change with age [R1007] [S]. Draw alpha uniformly in 1.8 to 2.2. Flaring fractions [R1726]: about 30 percent of mid to late M, 5 percent of early M, under 1 percent of F, G, K [S].

3. Planetary magnetic fields (GEN.86)

3.1 The scaling law

[R3355] fitted 145 numerical dynamos; the time-averaged dipole moment scales with buoyancy flux as F^(1/3) in the dipolar regime and predicts solar-system moments to about 15 percent [S via a citing paper]:

M_dip = 4 pi r_o^3 gamma sqrt(rho / (2 mu0)) (F D)^(1/3),   gamma about 0.2   [R: recalled form]

(r_o outer-core radius, rho core density, D shell thickness, F buoyancy flux in m2/s3.) Check [C]: r_o 3480 km, rho 11,000 kg/m3, D 2260 km, F D = 1.4e-6 gives 7.8e22 A m2 (Earth).

Rotation: the [R0784] law Lo = 0.92 sqrt(f_ohm) Ra_Q*^0.34 [S] with Ra_Q* ~ Omega^-3 gives B ~ Omega^-0.02, so field strength does not depend on rotation in the dipolar regime (a derivation here). Rotation sets the local Rossby number Ro_l: dipolar below about 0.12, multipolar above, with Ro_l ~ Omega^-1.23 [R]. A hard limit would fall at P about 30 hours for Earth, too sharp (Mercury, 59 d, is dipole-ish), so use a soft switch (3.5).

GEN.966 builds this law as a pure module, planetgen/physics/dynamo.py: the dipole moment of a region from the power that drives it, its shell and the spin (field), the local Rossby number from the same power (local_rossby), a dynamo's lifetime from the history of its power (lifetime) and a giant's power from its heat (giant_power_w). Tested on Earth (7.8e22 A m2 from 1.15 TW), Jupiter (1.6e27 against 1.5e27), Ganymede (1.0e20 against 1.3e20), Saturn (six times its field: a stable helium layer), Mercury (22 times its field: a stable layer on a thin shell; the module takes the attenuation as an argument) and a dead Mars (no power, no field). GEN.967 stores its results in place of the defaults of 3.5. The stand-ins are the registry rows DY1 to DY4 (gap plan 6.30).

3.2 Measured moments

Dipole moments in A m2 (M = 4 pi R^3 B_eq / mu0, [C from recalled B_eq]): Earth 7.8e22 | Mercury 2.8e19 | Ganymede 1.3e20 | Jupiter 1.56e27 | Saturn 4.6e25 | Uranus 3.8e24 | Neptune 2.1e24. 3.4 Magnetosphere standoff

Pressure balance with a dipole: R_mp = [mu0 f0^2 M^2 / (8 pi^2 p_sw)]^(1/6), f0 = 1.16 (other papers 1.3), p_sw the total wind pressure [S]. For Earth (M 7.768e22, p_sw 2.24 nPa) this gives 9.76 R_E [C], matching the empirical R/R_E = 9.75 (M/M_E)^(1/3) (P/P0)^(-1/6). A tenfold wind pressure shrinks R_mp only 1.47x [S].

p_sw = 2.24 nPa * (Mdot per area / solar) / d_AU^2, with Mdot/area ~ F_X^1.3 (Wood-type [R]), capped at 10x solar for G and K and 1x for M under 0.4 Msun (Proxima's is about 0.2 solar per area [R]). Earth-dipole planet at S = 1 [C]:

1.0, young / 4.5 Gyr 10x / 1x 6.6 / 9.7
0.6 54x 5.0
0.4 36x 5.4
0.2 176x 4.1
0.1 870x 3.2

With the multipolar factor 0.15 the radius drops by 0.15^(1/3) = 0.53, to 1.7 to 3.5 R_p for locked planets, matching [R4919]'s 1.5 to 4.0 (locked) and 3 to 8 (1-day rotators) [S]. CMEs raise p_sw by 10 to 1,000 transiently [R].

3.5 Generator rules (design defaults [R])

For a rocky planet with mass M (Earth masses), age, rotation period P, core mass fraction c (Earth 0.325) and lid regime:

  1. The core decides (GEN.838). The interior heat model keeps a core temperature beside the mantle's (physics/core_heat.py, docs/design/composition/interior-heat.md section 3.3); the dynamo runs while the heat the core loses across the core-mantle boundary, and the gravitational energy of its inner core's growth, exceed the heat it conducts down its own adiabat, in a liquid shell of at least a quarter of the core's radius. No metal, no dynamo. Mercury (0.055 Me, a core of 70 percent of its mass) keeps its observed field whatever the model says: its iron-sulfide core melts lower than the pure iron the model has.
  2. Lid. The lid regime acts on the mantle's temperature, and so on the base of the mantle the core is measured against; the dynamo lifetime draw and the stagnant-lid factor of GEN.86 and GEN.177 are gone.
  3. Strength (GEN.967). The moment is the dipole of the buoyancy-flux scaling of the liquid shell (physics/dynamo.py, section 3.1): dynamo.field of the power (the core model's, times magnetism.CORE_POWER_SCALE so its Earth is the scaling's Earth), the shell's radii (the core's and its inner core's), the core's density and the spin, times a log-normal of 0.07 dex (the law's fit). Mercury-like (M 0.05 to 0.1, c over 0.4): 3e19 * LN(0.5 dex), a stand-in.
  4. Rotation switch. Ro_l is the local Rossby number of the liquid shell (Ra*^0.41, about 0.09 (P / 24 h)^1.23 at Earth's power); f_dip = 0.15 + 0.85 / (1 + (Ro_l / 0.12)^4). A locked M-dwarf planet has f_dip about 0.15. The stored moment is the dipole's, so it already holds f_dip (over Earth's own); nothing downstream multiplies it by a share again.
  5. Giants. The field is made in the convecting layer of the stored interior (the metallic envelope of a gas giant, the ionic water of an ice giant's mantle) and driven by a tenth of the heat the giant radiates (physics/giant_dynamo.py, physics/giant_heat.py). A giant with no placed layer has no field.
  6. Moons. A moon's core is run through the same thermal model and scaling as a planet's (its heat includes the tide); a moon with a running core has a field, any other none; flag an induced field if a subsurface ocean exists.
  7. Output fields: magnetic_moment_a_m2, dipole_class (none, weak under 0.01 Earth, Earth-like, strong over 5 Earth, multipolar), magnetopause_rp (the standoff in radii of the body itself, R_planet in the unit registry: Earth's 9.75 Earth radii scaled by the field and the wind, then divided by the body's own radius, GEN.843).

Lid regime, as built (GEN.177, GEN.721, GEN.838). The regime is drawn once per planet from mobile_lid_chance: a liquid surface ocean (hydrosphere == "surface ocean") is required, from half an Earth mass up, with a 0.6 chance for 0.5 to 5 Earth masses and 0.3 above (the literature on super-Earth plate tectonics is split, so the chance falls rather than rises). Every other rocky planet is stagnant: Venus, Mars, dry worlds, ice-covered oceans and Hycean worlds. It is stored (lid_regime) and read by the mantle's heat flux law, so a stagnant lid keeps the base of the mantle hotter, which slows the core's cooling; it no longer shortens the dynamo by a factor.

Induced fields, as built (GEN.178). Rule 6's flag became a number: magnetism.generate_induced_field gives an ocean moon (ice-covered ocean, surface ocean or hycean, with a liquid layer) the dipole its ocean induces in its parent's turning field. The parent's equatorial dipole field at the moon's orbit, mu0 M f_dip / (4 pi a^3) (Jupiter at Europa: 520 nT), reaches the moon as 0.4 of its strength at the synodic period (1 / |1/P_rot - 1/P_orb|, 11.2 h for Europa); the ocean answers as a thin conducting shell with |A| = x / sqrt(1 + x^2), x = omega mu0 sigma h R / 2 (Europa: 0.9997, Galileo's fit about 0.97), and the moment is 2 pi R^3 A B / mu0: 3.9e18 A m2 for Europa, 103 nT at its equator, and 6.5e17 A m2 (4.6 nT) for Callisto. sigma is 2.75 S/m for a neutral ocean ([R1785]'s Europa), 0.5 ice-sealed, 5 chloride brine, 3 soda and 10 acid sulfate. No draw is made. A moon whose parent turns with it (tidally locked) sees no change and gets 0.

Induced fields of cores and of a planet in its star's field, as built (GEN.981). Part (b) of the induction: a metal core answers a changing field as a homogeneous conducting sphere (magnetism.sphere_response: F = 1 - 3/z^2 + 3 cot(z)/z, z = (1 - i) a / delta), which is 1 for any core many skin depths across (Earth's core at a day's period is 20 000 of them, Europa's at the 11 h synodic period 6 000) and omega mu0 sigma a^2 / 15 for a thin one; the moment is 2 pi a^3 |F| B / mu0 for the core's radius a (core_heat.Core.radius_m) with a conductivity of 1e6 S/m, solid or liquid. A moon's core feels the field its ocean lets through and adds to the ocean's moment. A rocky planet, which has no parent, is excited by its star's field at its orbit: 5 nT at 1 AU for the Sun, scaled by the square root of the wind's pressure over Earth's (wind_pressure_pa, so a flaring dwarf star's planets feel a stronger field), half of it perpendicular to the spin, turning at the planet's synodic rotation; its ocean (a surface ocean counted by the share of the surface it covers) and its core answer, and the moment is stored as induced_moment_a_m2, 5e17 A m2 for a dry Earth-mass planet at 1 AU against the dynamo's 8e22. A giant has none. The numbers it adds are small beside a dynamo, and matter for a body whose dynamo has stopped (a Mars, a Venus, a Moon) and for a moon of a giant.

3.6 Where the dipole points (GEN.1350)

The size of a field was stored; its direction was not. physics/magnetic_geometry.py draws, from a stream of the body's own (nothing of the run's stream moves), the angle between the dipole's axis and the spin axis (dipole_tilt_deg, folded into 0 to 90 degrees) and whether the dipole is off centre (dipole_offset_class, centred or offset, over 0.15 radii) for a planet, moon or rogue planet with a field:

Dynamo Tilt Offset Cases
dipole-dominated rocky core, gas giant Rayleigh, scale 6 degrees centred Earth 11, Jupiter 9.6, Ganymede 4, Saturn under 0.1
the iron-rich small body of the Mercury stand-in Rayleigh, scale 6 degrees offset Mercury under 3 degrees, 0.19 radii
multipolar core (the dipole under half the field) isotropic offset
ice giant (thin conducting shell over a stable core) [R4196] isotropic offset Uranus 59 degrees and 0.3 radii, Neptune 47 and 0.55

A neutron star stores magnetic_inclination_deg: isotropic at birth, then tan(alpha) falls as exp(-t / 7 Myr) [R5143]; a recycled millisecond pulsar is isotropic again. A star stores surface_dipole_field_gauss, the polar field of its large-scale dipole: a cool dwarf with a convective envelope (below the Kraft break, mass 0.08 to 1.3) has B = 1.5 G (Ro / 1.76)^-1.40 with a 0.35 dex scatter, capped at 5 kG ([R4544, R5144]; Ro = P_rot / tau_conv, [R4780]); an Ap or Bp dwarf has the polar field chemically_peculiar draws for its kind; any other star has none. The stand-ins are DP1 to DP7 (registry 6.212). The Sensor view reads these columns (UX.286).

The sea-level point is 0.34 mSv/yr (UNSCEAR 0.32) and the 10 km point 46 (aviation 47.9); the earlier curve was 1.5 to 2.7 times low from 100 to 1036 g/cm2.
  2. Magnetic effect on GCR (GEN.453): f_B is the ratio of the runs of [R0187] with a dipole of M Earth moments to those with none, at 100, 200, 500, 700 and 1036 g/cm2 (0.31, 0.37, 0.51, 0.61 and 0.52 for M = 1), log-linear in M between the runs and in ln X between the columns; the flat 0.6 was right only near sea level. The field enters as the equivalent moment Rc0 / 14.34 GV, with the Stormer cutoff Rc0 = 14.34 GV (M_dip f_dip / 7.768e22) (R_E / R_p)^2 (moment over radius squared; the cube was wrong, and 14.9 was 4 percent high). The runs reach 10 Earth moments and 100 to 1036 g/cm2; outside them the nearest ratio is taken. f_sol, 0.5 to 1.64 of the mean (Mars), is a seeded draw. The "two or three orders of magnitude" Rad gives applies to solar particles, not GCR.
  3. Solar and stellar particles (SEP): free-space annual dose = 20 mSv/yr * (N33 / N33_sun)^0.5 / d_AU^2 [R, design]; the square root keeps Proxima b from receiving hundreds of Sv/yr. Surface dose = free dose * exp(-p_cut(X) / R0) * polar-cap fraction. p_cut is the rigidity of the proton whose CSDA range in air equals X (NIST PSTAR, physics/proton_range.py; the power law R = 0.0022 E^1.77 was 5 times too long at 10 GeV); R0 = 0.2 GV is calibrated on the 1956 ground-level event [R]. Polar-cap fraction 1 - sqrt(1 - sqrt(R_eff / Rc0)), R_eff = max(0.5 p_cut, 0.2 GV) [R]. GEN.444 replaces this with the Band spectrum.
  4. Crust and radon (replaced by GEN.459, 2026-10-23): the gamma dose in air over the surface rock and the radon of its uranium, from the body's stored potassium, thorium and uranium (physics/radiogenic.py, docs/design/composition/radiation-model.md R15, R16 and section 6); the former 0.48 mSv/yr * a_rad * H(age) and the flat radon term are gone.
  5. Shielding by ground or water: the GCR(X) curve with X = rho * depth: 1 m of regolith (1.5 g/cc) is X = 150, 10 m of water X = 1000 [R]. Muons set the floor.

4.3 Heliosphere compression (GEN.75 link)

The GEN.87 text raises GCR dose in a compressed heliosphere. The effect is real but small: about 2 to 3x in free-space dose equivalent, and none above X of about 200 g/cm2, where primaries above 1 GeV dominate [R]. Recommended: helio_mult = 1 + 1.5 * exp(-X / 100) * min(1, compression), where compression runs 0 to 1 as the heliosphere shrinks from its open-space radius to under 1 AU. compressed_heliosphere_radius in generation/star.py already returns the radius; use R / R_open.

4.5 Galactic hazards and UV

  • Supernovae. [R1530]: a core-collapse supernova within about 8 pc roughly doubles biologically active UV by ozone loss; the rate within 8 pc at the Sun is about 1.5 per Gyr [S]. Rule: lethal events per Gyr within radius r = 1.5 * (r / 8 pc)^3 * (Sigma_SFR(R) / Sigma_SFR(8 kpc)); near the Galactic centre about 250x higher [R/C]. [R1649]: the inner Galaxy sterilizes more planets, yet a habitable planet is still 10x likelier there than in the outer Galaxy [S]. [R2702]: the Galactic habitable zone is an annulus 7 to 9 kpc wide [S].
  • Gamma-ray bursts. [R4340]: a burst within 2 kpc (100 kJ/m2 over 10 s) destroys 35 percent of ozone globally; a 50 percent cut triples UVB [S]. Quoted radii vary (1.4 to 14 kpc); [R4177]: GRBs dominate beyond about 2 kpc, supernovae inside [S]. Rule: ozone-loss flag at gamma fluence >= 30 to 100 kJ/m2. Built (GEN.189): [R3026] put a beamed burst within about 2 kpc at one per 0.5 Gyr at the Sun [S], so activity.GRB_LETHAL_RATE_AT_SUN_PER_GYR = 2 per Gyr; long bursts need low-metal massive stars ([S]), so the rate falls off outward with the supernova scale length (1.45 kpc) but is not raised toward the metal-rich centre.
  • AGN and quasars. No source found. A 1e44 erg/s X-ray source on for 1 Myr delivers 2.7e5 J/m2 at 3 kpc [C], so a luminous nucleus within a few kpc destroys unshielded ozone. Built (GEN.189) as a design estimate, not a measurement: the Fermi bubbles and the X-ray echoes of the Galactic Centre clouds (Su et al. 2010; [R3573] [S]) show Seyfert-like activity in the last few Myr, so activity.AGN_EPISODES_PER_GYR = 10 (1e44 erg/s for 1 Myr every 100 Myr) within AGN_LETHAL_RADIUS_PC = 2.9 kpc, where one episode delivers 30 kJ/m2 (1e44 erg/s * 3.15e13 s / (4 pi r^2); 30 kJ/m2 = 3e7 erg/cm2). Confidence is low; the episode rate is the unsettled number.
  • UV. Blackbody 200 to 300 nm flux relative to the Sun: 2.8x (F0), 0.54 (K0), 0.043 (M0), 0.0011 (M6) [C]; real M dwarfs have chromospheric UV well above that, so floor it at 0.05 to 0.2 of the Sun's [R]. Surface DNA-weighted UV relative to Earth = (UV_rel * S) * (O3 / O3_Earth)^-1.6, clamped near 1e3 at O3 = 0 (exponent from "a 50 percent ozone cut triples UVB" [S]).

4.6 Dose limits and tiers

  • Humans: ICRP 20 mSv/yr averaged over five years (occupational), 50 mSv in any one year, 1 Sv acute sickness, LD50/30 of 4 to 5 Sv (2.5 to 4.5 Gy depending on care) [S for LD50; limits R].
  • Microbes: D10 (dose for 90 percent kill) E. coli about 0.3 kGy; spores 2.1 kGy; Deinococcus radiodurans 10.4 kGy on average [S]; tardigrade LD50 1.1 to 6.2 kGy [S]. Rad's acute-survival figures are consistent (a different metric); use D10 5 to 10 kGy for Deinococcus in any biology table.
  • Rad's and PHI-4's bands, written numerically: Blue below 50 mSv/yr, Green 50 to 100, Yellow "extreme: subsurface", Red above 10 Gy/yr. There is a gap between 100 mSv/yr and 10 Gy/yr (Mars surface 236 mSv/yr, the Moon 520). This research proposed Blue 20 mSv/yr or less (ICRP), Green up to 100 mSv/yr, Yellow (human) 0.1 to 1 Sv/yr, Yellow (microbial) 1 to 10 Gy/yr, Red over 10 Gy/yr. GEN.84 first settled on Blue under 0.05 Sv/yr, Green to 0.1, Yellow to 10 Sv/yr; GEN.148 built this section's proposal for a person's tiers (habitability-index.md 2): Blue to 20 mSv/yr, Green to 100 mSv/yr, Yellow to 1 Sv/yr, Red beyond (50 mSv/yr stays the single-year limit that starts M_rad), and L_rad reads absorbed dose in Gy/yr (3.06 Sv per Gy).
  • The 10 Gy/yr threshold is Eigen's, about the origin of replicating molecules, not the survival of extremophiles, which tolerate 1,000x more acute dose.

4.7 As built (GEN.87)

planetgen/physics/radiation.py runs at the end of planets.generate_spin (after the air, water and field exist) and sets surface_dose_msv_yr = dose_gcr_msv_yr x dose_helio_mult + dose_sep_msv_yr + dose_ground_msv_yr + dose_belt_msv_yr (GEN.983: a moon in its parent's trapped-particle belts, 0 for a planet), uv_surface_index and ozone_loss_flag on every rocky planet and moon (a gas giant has none). Choices the sections above leave open:

  • X = P0 / g of the stored pressure and gravity; the dipole is the moment times f_dip (magnetism.body_dipole_share).
  • dose_helio_mult is 1 at generation. A system's containment is found after generation, so store.refresh_containment (and an edit) re-derives it from the cloud's compressed_heliosphere_radius, with compression = ln(R_open / R) / ln(R_open) capped at 1, and rewrites the total.
  • The crust's dose reads the body's stored potassium, thorium and uranium (GEN.459); radon needs air and some land (land_fraction), with no pressure gate.
  • Ozone: O3 / O3_Earth = sqrt(pO2 / 21.2 kPa) (none under 0.02 kPa).
  • ozone_loss_flag is set when ozone exists and either the star's lethal_event_rate_per_gyr (1.5 x exp((8 kpc - R) / 1.45 kpc), at most 250 times that) exceeds 10 (one lethal event per 100 Myr) or particles give 100 mSv/yr at the ozone layer (X = 10 g/cm2). The rate adds gamma-ray bursts and nuclear-activity episodes to the supernovae (GEN.189, section 4.5).

5. Hydrosphere and ocean chemistry (GEN.88)

5.1 Inventory, depth and land

Earth's ocean mass is 1.4e21 kg = 2.34e-4 of the planet, mean depth over the sphere 2.74 km [C]. Mantle water is perhaps 1 to 10 ocean masses and unmeasured [R].

Land fraction. [R0914]: a tectonically active planet of any mass keeps continents if its water mass fraction is below about 0.2 percent; super-Earths store more water in the mantle, whose capacity is the largest unknown [S]. Since GEN.1147 the cover is a two-level hypsometry (Cowan and Abbot's idea): the surface is ocean crust (60 percent of it, at -4.5 km with a spread of 1.0 km) and continental crust (40 percent, at +0.45 km with 0.9 km), relief scaled as g_E / g; the global water layer D = w M / (rho_w A) fills the lowest ground to a level and f_ocean is the share of the surface under it. The four numbers are stand-ins (row HY2) tuned only so that Earth's 2.74 km layer covers 0.71. The mantle never holds more than its minerals can store (GEN.1365: the upper mantle, the transition zone of wadsleyite and ringwoodite and the lower mantle, rows MR1 to MR3; 4.8 Earth oceans for the Earth). The old law min(1, sqrt(2 D / L)) went from no land to all ocean over a factor of 16 in the water; the new one covers 0.27 at 0.2 km, 0.50 at 0.64 km, 0.59 at 1.3 km, 0.71 at 2.7 km and everything at 5 km. The table below is the old law.

Water fraction w Earth gravity 2.2 g (5 Me, 1.5 Re) Earth gravity, buffer x4
1e-5 0.15 0.33 0.07
1e-4 0.47 1.00 0.24
2.3e-4 (Earth) 0.72 1.00 0.36
1e-3 1.00 1.00 0.75
2e-3 1.00 1.00 1.00

Frozen bodies (GEN.833). A body whose surface is ice holds its water as ice through its outer layers, so the share of it the ice covers (hydrosphere.frozen_cover, stored as ocean_fraction for the states ice and ice-covered ocean) is this law applied to all the water it holds at ice's density (917 kg/m3), not to the part a liquid ocean would have had after the mantle buffer. Its relief is L = 10.5 km at most: observed icy relief is a few to 20 km on bodies of any gravity (Enceladus, Callisto, Iapetus), because ice is strength-limited, so below Earth's gravity it does not grow as 1 / g the way a rock basin's does. A 200 km moon with a fifth of its mass as water is all ice-covered; with a thousandth it is covered in part.

Generator: f_land = 1 - f_ocean with a buffer log-uniform 1 to 8; a stagnant-lid planet takes buffer 1. ROGUE_WATER_MASS_FRACTION_RANGE (1e-4 to 0.1, log-uniform) makes about 68 percent of water-rich rogues exceed Earth by 4x and a third exceed 1 percent.

5.2 Ocean depth before high-pressure ice

Replaced by GEN.461 (2026-10-24). The depth of a water layer is not its mass over 1,000 kg/m3: at a tenth of an Earth mass of water the layer is 746 km deep at 11 GPa, not 1,047 km, and the table below (a fixed 1,050 kg/m3 and [R4585]'s linear liquidus) is 5 to 25 percent too deep. physics/water_layer.py integrates the column from the surface down with the density of the 300 K isotherm of water (IAPWS-95 liquid, then the ice VI, VII and X reference points of the materials table), the gravity of the mass below the level, and the area that grows toward the centre; it reproduces design part C 2.4's integrated table within 1 percent for 0.0001 to 0.5 of an Earth or 5 Earth masses (its two x = 0.2 rows were recomputed from the code by DOC.79 on 2026-10-25 and are test targets like the rest). The liquid stops at the liquidus of the bottom temperature, the melting curves of ice III, V and VI recomputed from the Journaux splines (part C 2.3: 210 MPa at 251.2 K, 350 at 256.2, 620 at 272.1, 800 at 286.8, 1000 at 300.4, 1500 at 327, 2000 at 347, 2200 at 353.8); below it the water is hp_ice_km. At Earth gravity the deepest liquid at 280 K is 65 km and at 300 K 88 km (the old table said 73 and 111); a column is sealed from about 5e-3 of the mass. The old table, for the record:

The pressure at the bottom of the water is stored (water_base_pressure_pa). An Earth-size ocean of 100 km needs a water mass fraction of about 1 percent. 5.3 Reusing the rogue-planet functions

The conductive-lid function ice_shell_thickness_km(flux, T_top, T_base) (D = (A/F) ln(T_base / T_top), A = 567 W/m) reproduces measured shells at plausible fluxes [C]:

Body T_top Flux used Result Reported
Europa 100 K 0.02 to 0.05 W/m2 11 to 28 km 3 to 30+ km; Juno 29 +/- 10 km [S]
Enceladus 75 K 0.02 to 0.05 15 to 37 km 20 to 25 km mean, under 5 km at the south pole [S]
Ganymede 110 K 0.003 to 0.01 52 to 172 km 25 to 150 km [S]

Tidal heating for generator draws: E = (21/2) (k2 / Q) G M_p^2 n R^5 e^2 / a^6 (Peale) [R]. With k2/Q = 0.015 (Io's need) Io gives 9.3e13 W (observed about 9e13) but Europa 7e12 W (estimates 1e11 to 1e12), so use k2/Q = 3e-4 for ice (Europa 1.4e11 W). Enceladus gives 4.5e8 W against about 1.6e10 W observed, so allow a resonance boost of 1 to 50x for small icy moons [C]. The radiogenic flux of a Europa-mass body is 0.0078 W/m2 [C].

Four fixes before sharing the functions with star systems (all verified in src/planetgen/physics/rogue_surface.py):

  1. High-pressure ice cap. rogue_surface_conditions returns ocean_depth_km up to 855 km (10 percent water). Cap the liquid depth by the 5.2 table and report the rest as hp_ice_km; for a deep layer the bottom is ice VI/VII and the "ocean in contact with rock" assumption (the has_liquid_water flag and the chemistry classes) is false.
  2. Units. ice_shell_thickness_km is ice thickness, but water_layer_ depth_km is water-equivalent (1,000 kg/m3). The comparison shell_km >= depth_km and the subtraction overstate the ocean by about 9 percent (ice 917 kg/m3). Divide the shell by 1.09 when comparing.
  3. Melting at the base. The base is fixed at 273.15 K. Pressure lowers it by 0.0074 K/bar (about -10 K for a 100 km shell); salinity and ammonia lower it 2 to 100 K [R]. base_temperature_k is already an argument.
  4. Convection and geometry. Shells thicker than about 20 to 30 km convect and the conductive formula overestimates [R]; on small bodies the lid is a large fraction of the radius (Enceladus 20 km of 252 km). Both are within the formula's spread.

water_boiling_k caps at the critical point (647 K, 22 MPa), which is correct; above it compressed liquid is stable up to the VI/VII melting curve, which the function ignores. Move the ice-shell, boiling and water-depth functions into a shared module (for example physics/hydrosphere.py) that both rogue_surface and star-system planets import.

Carbonate-silicate buffering [R4596]: weathering rises about as pCO2^0.3 and about 15 percent per degree [S]. On a stagnant lid it is supply-limited and can fail, though burial and decarbonation may restore a feedback with windows of roughly 1 to 5 Gyr [S]. Rule: a stagnant lid shortens the habitable window to 1 to 5 Gyr and pushes the ocean to soda or acid sulfate.

5.5 Hycean link

[R2804] [S]: massive oceans under H2-rich air, up to 2.6 R_E and 10 Me; inner edge at T_eq up to about 500 K for late M, no outer limit. rogue_surface's hydrogen-envelope branch already puts the surface on a dry adiabat and checks the boiling curve at envelope pressure. Add hycean to the regimes when an ocean sits under an H2 envelope, below the boiling curve at that pressure and shallower than the 5.2 limit. Deeper ones are ice-sealed (atmospheres-retention-and-classes.md 4.3).

5.6 Aquifers (GEN.654)

A world with no liquid at its surface (hydrosphere dry, ice or vapour) can hold liquid water in the pores of its crust, where the heat flow puts the temperature between the freezing point and the hottest water life can use. The model is physics/aquifers.py, one column of crust, no draw:

  • The geotherm is T(z) = T_surface + q z / k with the stored internal heat flux q (plus an icy moon's tide) and k = 2 W/m/K (tuning.AQUIFER_CONDUCTIVITY_W_M_K; [R0824], at second hand). The 273.15 K level lies at (273.15 - T_surface) k / q (about 4 km on a Mars with 30 mW/m2); on a warm surface water boils away until the hydrostatic pressure over it exceeds its vapour pressure.
  • The water-bearing layer ends where the geotherm reaches 122 C (tuning.AQUIFER_LIFE_MAX_K, [R4295]) or where the pores close: porosity falls as 0.2 exp(-z/K), K = 2.82 km scaled by Mars's gravity over the body's (tuning.AQUIFER_POROSITY_*), and is gone under 0.01.
  • The surface reservoir (the water that is not in the mantle, after the early runaway took its share, GEN.639), spread as a layer over the whole surface, fills the pores from the bottom of the layer up. A small inventory leaves a deep water table and a thin layer; a large one fills the liquid range to its top. A layer under 10 m is none.

Two numbers are stored (aquifer_depth_km, the depth to the top of the layer, and aquifer_thickness_km; NULL for a world with none). They change the score in four places, all in habitability_world.py: the solvent is water (so the likelihood of microbial life is not cut to 0), the habitat dose is the ground's (life underground sees the rock's dose only), the energy is the chemical power of the heat flow (no light reaches it), and the Water rung is Yellow (AQUIFER_WATER_SCORE 0.6) when the top of the layer is within tuning.AQUIFER_REACH_KM (5 km, a drill's reach), Red when it is deeper (the life is out of reach, so it is no food either). The life draw caps such a world at abiogenesis (life.stage_ceiling): chemotrophs, so the Microbial score can rise and Flora, Fauna and Sentient stay 0. The Martian parameters are DESIGN stand-ins read at second hand (the papers could not be opened); they are labelled in tuning.py and DATA-LICENSE.md until a verified source replaces them.
