Boss, 7:54 PM and 8:22 PM EDT 2026-10-24: every object that has a day or night gets its day and night time shares, a moon with the extra night its planet's shadow adds, and the planet says how many hours of daylight it has. The module is src/planetgen/physics/daylight.py; it draws nothing and has no stand-in value, it reads the stored rotation period, orbital period, spin axis, orbit planes and the star's radius.

1. What is stored

Column Meaning
day_share_equator share of the time the star is up at the equator, averaged over a circular year (NULL when the world turns once an orbit)
day_share_mean the same averaged over the whole surface
parent_shadow_share a moon only: share of its time in its planet's shadow
solar_day_hours hours from one noon to the next (NULL when the world turns once an orbit)
daylight_hours the solar day times day_share_equator
polar_circle_deg latitude beyond which the star stays up or down for at least a day

The night share is one minus the day share.

2. The rules

  • The star is up while its upper limb is above the horizon. Its angular radius is alpha = asin(R / d); at a latitude phi and a declination delta the half day arc has cos H = (-sin alpha - sin phi sin delta) / (cos phi cos delta) and the share is H / pi.
  • The year is circular (the model has no eccentricity), so sin delta = sin e sin lambda, e the angle between the spin axis and the normal of the orbit round the star (a moon's: its spin axis against its planet's orbit normal). A tilt past 90 degrees is a retrograde spin and gives the same seasons.
  • The surface mean is exact: a sphere is lit over (1 + sin alpha) / 2 of its area at every instant, whatever the tilt.
  • The solar day is 1 / |1/P_rot -/+ 1/P_orb|. A world with P_rot = P_orb has no day; its day side is lit all the time, the terminator half of it, the night side never.
  • The polar circle is 90 - e - alpha degrees.
  • A moon is in its planet's shadow while the centre of the star is behind the planet's disc as the moon sees it (theta = asin(R_planet / a)). With beta the angle between the star and the moon's orbit plane that is a share arccos(cos theta / cos beta) / pi of an orbit when beta < theta, and the year turns beta through sin beta = sin(Theta) |sin lambda|, Theta being the angle between the moon's orbit normal and its planet's. Eclipses come in seasons: an inclined orbit has none outside them, but the star crosses its plane twice a year, so no circular orbit is free of them. Both day shares of the moon are multiplied by one minus that share.

3. Checks (hand work, in tests/test_daylight.py)

Earth: 50.15 percent at the equator, 12.04 h of daylight in a 24 h solar day, polar circle at 66.3 degrees. Venus's solar day is 116.75 Earth days. Io in Jupiter's shadow 5.3 percent of its orbit (2.2 h of 42.5 h); the Moon 0.05 percent of the time (about four hours a year, in eclipse seasons of about 43 days).

4. Stand-ins and limits

None are values. Limits: circular orbits; the star is a disc of its radius at the parent's distance (a circumbinary host uses its primary's radius when it has one, otherwise a point source); no refraction; a moon locked to its planet loses the extra night on its near side only, which the mean does not show.
