1. Why

A frozen-over ocean is not a dark one. Most of the Arctic Ocean's photosynthesis already happens in water under more than 85 percent ice cover, and perennially ice-covered lakes keep oxygen-making mats going year round under 3 to 5 m of ice. The refuge term of GEN.1723 (the oxygen that life makes where light and any liquid water meet) therefore has to count lit water under a lid, as the ocean's area times the light that gets through, and not as zero or as a pool. This module is the law for the light that gets through.

2. The law

T = (1 - albedo) x exp(-k_ice x h_ice) x exp(-k_snow x h_snow)

T is the share of the photosynthetically active light (400 to 700 nm) at the surface that reaches the water. It is the two-layer Beer-Lambert law of [R5264] (equation 3.1) with the reflection at the top that [R5265] take off before they fit the same law to a lake.

Snow is the main absorber, 30 times stronger per metre than sea ice and about 100 times stronger than clear lake ice, so the snow depth on a lid decides most of T.

3. Checks against measurements

Case Law Measured
Lake A, 1.97 m of ice, snow removed [R5265] 8.5 percent 5.7 to 6.6 percent
Lake A, 2 m of ice and 0.5 m of wind-packed snow (k 2.1, albedo 0.97) 0.55 percent 0.45 percent
Antarctic pack ice in spring, 0.5 m ice and 0.2 m snow of albedo 0.8 [R5264] 0.03 percent under 0.1 percent

The thin lid of a glacier melt lake (16 cm of ice) gives 33 percent against 21 to 23 percent in the one paper that measured it; that paper also says under 10 percent, a conflict the research could not settle.

4. How thick a lid still lets life work

The floor of GEN.1722 (0.04 umol photons m-2 s-1 as a daily mean, net growth) under an Earth-bright sky (a daily mean of about 400 umol m-2 s-1 in the lit season) is T of about 1e-4. The thickest lid that keeps that: bare sea ice about 6.6 m; bare clear lake ice 23 m at k 0.32 and 30 m at k 0.25; snow alone about 0.23 m; 0.1 m of snow takes about 2.4 m of sea ice from the depth. A world's own star and orbit set the surface light, so these depths move with them. They are examples only.

5. What it leaves out, and what it needs

  • The coefficients are for the Sun's spectrum. Ice absorbs least at 400 to 500 nm and more toward the red, so a red star's light falls faster under a lid.
  • The top 10 to 20 cm of sea ice attenuates more than the inside; it is folded into the albedo and the bulk k. Ice algae on the underside shade themselves (k 20 to 50); melt ponds let more through. Neither is counted.
  • The thickness of the ice and the snow is the growth law's: physics/ice_growth.py (GEN.1728, ice-and-snow-growth.md) gives a world's lid from its mean surface temperature, the swing of its year and its starlight; this law only reads the thickness it is handed.

6. Reuse

The generator's own ice albedos (climate_branches.ICE_COVERED_ALBEDO, surface_air.ice_albedo_law) are Bond albedos of a whole world, clouds included, so they are not the albedo of the ice surface to photosynthetic light and are not reused; the stellar spectrum module is the caller's source of surface light (biogenic_oxygen takes it there already). Nothing else in the code holds an ice or snow light law.
