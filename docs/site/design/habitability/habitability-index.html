  • "Planetary Habitability and Speculative Xenobiology.md" (the master doc)

  • "Atmospheric Toxicity.md"

Boss (2026-10-03 05:38Z): "Create a habitability index based on the pressure, temperature, composition, etc..." and "Refactor all planetary classes to better align with our habitability index system and the research for the different kinds of life, etc."

The research of 2026-10-09 behind the inputs is in two further notes:

  • atmospheres-retention-and-classes.md: when a planet keeps an atmosphere, the hot- and cold-zone classes (GEN.91), the class windows and life stage against time habitable (GEN.92).
  • activity-magnetism-radiation-hydrosphere.md: stellar XUV and flares, planetary magnetic fields, surface dose, water, oceans and ocean chemistry (GEN.86, GEN.87, GEN.88).

Status: the scores (section 2) are settled and built (GEN.84); the rest is research, 2026-10-09, and decisions marked "Boss" are his, everything else is a recommendation. Informs: GEN.83 to GEN.92.

1. What the code has today

  • Per planet: class and zone, radius, mass, density, gravity, orbit, rotation period, surface temperature (grey body times a per-class greenhouse factor), surface pressure, atmosphere density and scale height. atmosphere and composition are free text. Pressure scales with gravity linearly (_atmosphere_retention_factor, "a starting point, not derived"); there is no escape, temperature or age term.
  • Missing: partial pressures of any gas, ocean or land fraction, pH, salinity, magnetic field, radiation dose, mantle redox, ozone, humidity.
  • Per star: type and class, mass, radius, temperature, luminosity, age, habitable zone, heliosphere radius. No XUV or flare activity. The habitable zone is a fixed pair of fluxes (1.1 and 0.53) for every star, and a main-sequence star's luminosity is constant for its life.
  • Spin: a rocky planet is born spinning at the rate and tilt the giant impacts that built it leave ([R2431], GEN.731: a median day of 2 to 3 hours, an isotropic axis), a moon log-uniform from 8 to 48 h, a gas giant uniform from 8 to 20 h; the tide of what it orbits then slows the spin for the age of the star (tidal_spin), to a lock (one turn per orbit), to a spin-orbit resonance such as Mercury's 3:2, or not at all.
  • "Habitable" is the PHI-6 rating, not a class (Boss, 2026-10-23, GEN.643): a world is habitable when its overall PHI-6 colour is Blue or Green (habitability.is_habitable, the one function the API's habitable flag, the system page chips, the per-system count hab_count and the description text all call). The API serves the class flag separately as habitable_class (the "Habitable class" chip). A request for a habitable world (+habitable_world) raises the odds of a body that meets its conditions (generation/habitable_request.py, GEN.1130) and steers by no class list or score, so the world it gives may not score Blue or Green; StarSystem.habitable_class_count is a report column only, and StarSystem.habitable_request says whether the finished system met the request's conditions ("aim") or not ("unmet"; GEN.1248: it is answered from the same habitable_request.count the draws use, and there is no fallback).
  • Rogue planets already have internal heat flux, a surface regime, ice-shell thickness and ocean depth (rogue_surface.py); nothing in a star system has these. A terrestrial rogue is scored from its surface too (rogue_habitability.py, rogue-planet-surface.md).
  • Nebulae: a system's surrounding_cloud is set only when it is loaded from the database, so generation can't see it.
  • Life is decided by the conditions of the finished body (the life classes of physics/life_classes.py), never by its class letter: the list HABITABLE_PLANET_CLASSES is gone (GEN.1387), and the page shows the timeline of any body that has one.

2. The scores (settled, GEN.84)

Boss chose the structure on 2026-10-07 (17:11Z): PHI-4's domains and colour tiers for display, with the Xenobiology doc's three scores as the numbers behind them. GEN.207 (2026-10-15) made the four domains six: Chemistry split into Air and Water, and a long-term Suitability was added. planetgen/physics/habitability.py is the reference maths: every constant below is named there with its source, and GEN.89 stores what it computes for every planet and moon.

PHI-6 (display, for a human visitor; called PHI-4 until GEN.207 made the four domains six). Each domain's variables have bands; a variable scores 1 in Blue, falls linearly to 0.75 at the Green edge, 0.4 at the Yellow edge and 0 at the outer Red edge (in log10 for pressures and doses). A domain scores its worst variable, and its tier is Blue at 1, Green from 0.75, Yellow from 0.4, Red below. The six domains are Pressure, Temperature, Air Chemistry (is the air breathable: oxygen, CO2, CO, H2S, SO2), Water Chemistry (is the water drinkable: pH, water activity, chaotropicity; a world with no liquid water scores 0, Red), Radiation and Suitability. PHI-6 (still stored as phi4) is their geometric mean held under the colour gates below.

Variable Blue Green Yellow Red
Pressure, kPa 50 to 250 14.3 to 400 6.3 to 10,000 beyond (0 a decade out)
Temperature, C 0 to 31 -20 to 45 -50 to 122 beyond (0 at -120, 200)
Wet bulb under 31 C 31 C or more makes Blue or Green Yellow
Inspired O2, F(P - 6.3) kPa 8 to 50 under 8 (a mask works) 50 to 160 over 160
CO2, kPa under 0.93 to 2.0 to 5.0 over 5.0
CO, H2S, SO2 under the chronic limit to ten times the acute limit beyond
Water pH (Water) 6 to 8.5 5 to 9.5 1 to 11.5 beyond
Water activity 0.90 or more 0.75 or more 0.605 or more below
Chaotropicity, kJ/kg to 73.8 over 73.8
Dose, Sv/yr under 0.02 to 0.1 to 1 over 1 (0 at 1000)

Where the numbers come from: 6.3 kPa is the Armstrong limit; 14.3 kPa is the least pressure at which a pure-oxygen mask still gives the 8 kPa of inspired O2 consciousness needs (6.3 + 8); 400 kPa is where nitrogen narcosis starts; 31 C wet bulb is the uncompensable limit (Vecellio et al. 2022); 122 C is the limit of carbon-based life; gas limits are the Atmospheric Toxicity table's chronic and acute values, and a filtering respirator (NIOSH APF 10) makes Yellow out to ten times the acute limit; CO2 can't be filtered, so its Yellow ends at the acute 5 kPa (scrubbers); 0.605 and 73.8 kJ/kg are the water-activity and chaotropicity limits for cell division; the dose bands are ICRP's (GEN.148, Boss's defaults of 2026-10-10): Blue to the 20 mSv/yr five-year average, Green to 100 mSv/yr, Yellow to 1 Sv/yr (the threshold of radiation sickness; shelter or shielding needed) and Red beyond, which closes the gap the PHI-4 doc left between 100 mSv/yr and 10 Gy/yr. 50 mSv in any one year is ICRP's single-year limit and where M_rad starts charging for shielding. The Eigen threshold of 10 Gy/yr belongs to L_rad (microbes), not to a person's tiers.

Ultraviolet in the Radiation domain (GEN.445). The Radiation score is the lower of the dose's and the ground ultraviolet's. The UV is the DNA-damaging (Setlow) dose at the ground as a multiple of Earth's (World.uv_index, from the stored uv_surface_index, which radiation.generate_dose derives from the two-stream spectrum under the world's own air, ozone, haze and cloud; radiation-model.md section 7). Blue below 3 times Earth's (where sun-cover clothing starts), Green to 30 (a full-cover suit), Yellow to 300 (the unfiltered light of a world with oxygen and no ozone layer, 280 to 356 times Earth's: shelter from the sun), Red beyond, 0 at 3000 (protection_limits rows uv_cover_index, uv_suit_index, uv_lethal_index, uv_outer_index; the last two are engineering edges from the research table, not published limits). A world with no UV index scores on its dose alone. A body with no stored dose reads the cosmic-ray tables of the radiation model (World.bare_cosmic_ray_dose_sv_yr); the earlier second curve is gone.

Habitable request fallback and food (Boss, 6:54 PM EDT 2026-10-23). A request for a habitable world aims for Blue or Green overall; if it must, it accepts Yellow as technically habitable so long as the sustainability factor (read as Suitability) is at least Yellow. Food comes from the Flora and Fauna scores: Flora is always good food, both are better, Fauna includes all animal life (GEN.653).

The four life scores (Boss, 6:14 PM EDT 2026-10-23). Microbial, Flora, Fauna and Sentient, each 0 (none) to 9 (abundant), from the evolutionary stage and the surface conditions, shown as four small icons with a digit next to the PHI-6 icons. They replace the microbial, complex-life and human-operability scores (phi_bio, phi_cpx, phi_tech; GEN.649); section 12 has the rules, and /help/habitability says them to the reader (DOC.77).

Habitable defined (Boss, 6:02 PM EDT 2026-10-23). A world is habitable when its overall score is Blue or Green; Yellow and Red are not. One predicate (GEN.643) serves the sector counts, the markers, the lists, the search and the descriptions. Boss also wants the PHI-6 tied into the planet classification; the proposal is GEN.644. Decided (Boss, 6:09 PM EDT): classes are conditions-based and assigned after the body is complete, from the atmosphere composition and pressure, the surface temperature, the terrestrial and the gas composition (GEN.645 to GEN.648).

The gates (GEN.207; Boss, 2026-10-11 and 2026-10-14). Importance, most first: Radiation, Temperature, Pressure, Air, Water, Suitability. The colour gates come first, then the geometric mean inside them (habitability.overall_score):

  • Radiation and Temperature cap the overall colour at their own: Yellow caps at Yellow, Red at Red.
  • Pressure: Red makes the overall Red and Yellow caps it at Yellow, unless a full suit is needed anyway (Temperature, Radiation or Air Red), when it changes nothing about the overall (it is still listed among the reasons for the suit).
  • Air in Yellow or Red caps the overall at Yellow; Red air alone cannot make it Red. Water likewise.
  • Suitability Red drops the overall to Yellow, never to Red.
  • Blue overall needs all six Blue.

The ones that cannot make the overall Red (Air, Water, Suitability, and Pressure when a suit is needed anyway) enter the mean no lower than 0.4 (the Yellow edge), so a dead one does not pull the mean to 0.

Suitability (GEN.207) is long-term survival on the surface: the lowest of three rungs, water within reach, food and shelter, each read off Exoplanetary Habitability.md Table 2, then held under a ceiling the other five set (habitability.suitability_ceiling): Red if Radiation, Temperature, Pressure or Water is Red (a world with no liquid water has Red Water and so Red Suitability); Yellow if any of the five is Yellow or Air is Red; Green only if the five are Green or Blue; Blue only if all five are Blue.

Rung Blue (1) Green (0.9) Yellow Red
Water within reach liquid at the surface and Blue Water liquid at the surface ice, or an ocean under ice (0.6) water in minerals (0.25), vapour only (0.2), none (0)
Food (native life) a full biosphere of forests and animals (1) the first plants and animals (0.85) microbial mats and plankton (0.5) none (0.2); life only in an ocean under ice counts as none
Shelter the surface dose is Blue Green Yellow (caves or burial) Red (deep lava tubes or buried habitats)

The food stage is the latest milestone of the planet's evolutionary timeline (abiogenesis to complex cells are microbial, multicellularity is the first plants and animals, a technological civilization is a full biosphere), or microbial for life with no timeline (a moon, a world outside the habitable zone) and none when no life chemistry was drawn. The numbers are planetGen's, one per tier, to be tuned with sample worlds. Life is drawn after the score, so generation scores again once it is known (habitability_world.rescore).

Equipment: ideal (called "shirtsleeve" until Boss renamed it, 2026-10-10 08:08Z); breathing mask (low O2 or pressure below Blue); mask with scrubber (a gas above its chronic limit); environment suit (pressure below Green, any domain Red, a temperature past the Green heat or cold edge, or oxygen above the chronic limit, which is where the description asks for one); full life support with radiation hardening (Radiation Red). The kit is about the air, the cold or heat and the radiation; Water Chemistry and Suitability are about drinking and eating and do not change it.

PHI_bio (microbial life) = the Liebig blend of L_solv, L_chem, L_ener and L_rad: L_min^0.65 · (L_solv · L_chem · L_ener · L_rad)^(0.35/4) (GEN.148; section 7.1 has the measurements against the Master doc's table; section 3 item 1 records the product it replaced).

  • L_solv = solvent factor · f(aw) · exp(-0.5 ((chi - 73.8) / 10)^2) past 73.8 kJ/kg; f(aw) = 1 / (1 + exp(-50 (aw - 0.605))). Solvent factor: water 1, liquid hydrocarbons 0.2, sulfuric acid 0.1, no stable liquid 0.
  • L_chem = (prod over C, N, H of tanh(X_j / 0.25 Earth))^(1/3) · P / (P + 0.1 umol/L).
  • L_ener = log10(Phi / 1e-6) / log10(10 / 1e-6), clamped to [0, 1], Phi the best energy flux in W/m^2 (light, chemistry or radiolysis).
  • L_rad = exp(-0.5 ((D - 10) / 100)^2) above 10 Gy/yr, D the absorbed dose where the solvent is (under the ice for an ice-sealed ocean). D is in Gy/yr; the person's bands are in Sv/yr, and the two are different quantities (Mars: 0.077 Gy/yr, 0.236 Sv/yr, so about 3.06 Sv per Gy for a cosmic-ray dose; habitability.SV_PER_GY). The maths keeps them as two numbers by hand because astropy 6.0.1 has no Gy or Sv unit (and astropy is no longer a runtime dependency).

Microbial dose tolerance (D10, the dose for a 90 percent kill; for any biology table and for LETHAL_HABITAT_DOSE_GY_YR): E. coli about 0.3 kGy, bacterial spores 2.1 kGy, Deinococcus radiodurans 5 to 10 kGy (10.4 kGy on average; use 5 to 10), a tardigrade's LD50 1.1 to 6.2 kGy (activity-magnetism-radiation-hydrosphere.md 4.6). L_rad's 10 Gy/yr is Eigen's threshold for the origin of replicating molecules, a thousand times under these. A compact object's planet is rated at the Deinococcus figure, 10 kGy/yr, so it keeps no life.

PHI_cpx (animal life) = PHI_bio · exp(-(pCO / 0.01)^2) · exp(-(pCO2 / 2.0)^2) · f(pO2), with f = 1 for ambient O2 from 8 to 50 kPa and a half Gaussian outside (sigma 2 kPa below, 20 kPa above).

Phi_tech (human operability with equipment) was the Xenobiology document's third number (the geometric mean of a pressure, a thermal, a water-supply and a radiation-shielding factor). Boss removed it on 2026-10-23 (GEN.649): the equipment tier and the six domains already say what a person needs, and the number was shown beside two life scores it had nothing to do with. Its four factors, the constants behind them (sigma_P, the cold floor, the water-source lookup, the shielding exponent) and the column are gone.

Surface dose, until GEN.87 adds stellar particles and magnetic fields: D = 0.45 exp(-X / 22) + 0.05 exp(-X / 200) Sv/yr from cosmic rays, plus 2 mSv/yr from the ground, X = P0 / g in g/cm^2. This fits the Moon (0.50), Mars (about 0.25 at 16 g/cm^2), a 0.05 bar world (about 0.08) and Earth (2.4 mSv/yr). Inside a nebula the cosmic-ray part doubles (section 6).

3. How the documents' problems were settled

  1. PHI_bio was first built as the product of its four likelihoods, not their geometric mean: each is a requirement, and the mean forgave a missing solvent (the doc's dune world computed to 0.34 against its stated 0.05). GEN.148 replaced the product with the Liebig blend (7.1), which keeps that gate (a term at 0 gives 0) and lands within 0.1 of the Master doc's stated value for every archetype.
  2. The undefined constants are fixed above: k_w 50, X_ref a quarter of Earth's inventory, K_P 0.1 umol/L (the doc's 1.0 scored Earth's ocean 0.70), L_ener log-scaled from 1e-6 to 10 W/m^2 (no single Phi_ref in tanh fits both sunlight and radiolysis), solvent factors 1 / 0.2 / 0.1 / 0, and the surface dose formula.
  3. L_rad keeps 10 Gy/yr, read in Gy (not Sv); Mars's 0.077 Gy/yr (0.236 Sv/yr) then scores 1, not the table's 0.65. Mars is held back by its solvent and chemistry instead.
  4. One threshold each: CO chronic 0.005 kPa, acute 0.1, and T_metazoa's 0.01 scale; CO2 chronic 0.93, Green 2.0, acute 5.0; the mask band is 14.3 to 400 kPa (Green), with 6.3 to 14.3 kPa needing a counterpressure suit (Yellow).
  5. Other stars (GEN.86, GEN.87): F stars act like G with a shorter saturated phase (about 50 Myr); A, B and O stars have no flare term but a high UV flux; white dwarfs and giants use their own luminosity and age; binaries sum both stars' fluxes at the planet.
  6. Nebulae: section 6.

Values are stored in their own columns, not JSON, so they can be searched.

Rows 1 and 2 are built (GEN.85, schema v70, planetgen/physics/atmosphere.py). The redox mean is IW+3.5 + 2 log10(M / M_earth) with a sigma of 1 [D], reduced at or below IW and oxidized from IW+2.5. Each class's mix is a template read from its old atmosphere text and its solar-system analogue, scattered log-normally. A reduced mantle under anoxic air moves 60 percent of CO2, SO2 and H2O to CO and CH4, H2S and H2 (15 percent for intermediate). No gas exceeds its vapour pressure at the surface temperature (the pN2_cap of the retention document's 3.3, applied to every gas), and the surface pressure drops by what freezes out. Helium, ammonia and sodium are a class's unstored balance. The H inventory for the water-loss clock is read from the stored partial pressures (H2, H2O, 2 CH4, H2S) where that clock is built. An H2-dominated mix (x_H2 over 0.5) is never called "reducing" in the text, so the sub-Neptune and Hycean classes can reuse it once GEN.90 adds them.

5. Classes and life

Known conflicts between today's classes and the research:

  • N (Venus analog, about 737 K and 9.2 MPa) carries life with no stage cap; the thermal ceiling for life is 122 C (395 K). E (about 399 K) is above it too.
  • K (about 611 Pa) and L (about 2 kPa) are below the Armstrong limit (6.3 kPa); L has land animals at a tiny O2 partial pressure.
  • P (about 9.5 kPa, 204 K) can reach civilization; the research gives sub-baric snowballs minimal native life.
  • Classes whose atmosphere names O2 imply life made it, whatever their life stage.
  • No class is an Earth-size world with air that life never processed. Planned class Z (lifeless temperate world) is that world; the research's "photochemically stalled CO" and "oxidized dense CO2" worlds are its likely forms.

Candidate new classes from the research: a dense-CO2 outer-edge world, a hydrogen-rich (Hycean) super-Earth in the cold zone, a desiccated post-runaway super-Earth in the hot zone (the "S and V in the other zones" Boss asked for), an ice-sealed deep waterworld, a soda world and a hyperbaric N2/Ar world.

Life, and how far it gets, should follow the scores rather than the class, and its chemistry should follow the atmosphere, radiation and solvent rather than the star's letter alone.

Built first (GEN.206, 2026-10-14). The descriptions now follow the scores, in generation/life.py (life_ceiling, apply_life_data):

  • phi_bio below tuning.HABITABILITY_LIFE_SCORE_MIN (0.005, what shows as 0.00): the world carries no life chemistry, speed, spectrum or timeline. The class texts claim no life (GEN.221 took "bacterial life", "simple life" and "with vegetation" out of F, G and L), so nothing in them needs replacing.
  • phi_cpx below the same line with microbial life allowed: the timeline stops at "photosynthesis" (get_evolutionary_timeline(..., max_stage=), the lower of this and the class ceiling PLANET_CLASS_MAX_LIFE_STAGE).
  • A body with no scores (a gas giant, or one saved without them) keeps the old class rules. Of 31 bodies of a habitable class in 120 generated systems, 14 score no microbial life, 6 allow microbial life only and 11 are unlimited, so fewer worlds carry life than before (a class used to be enough).

The chemistry itself (which pigment, which solvent) still follows the star's letter; that, and a stage that follows the scores rather than the star's age, are left.

6. Nebulae

None of the documents cover nebulae. The only link they support is cosmic ray flux, raised for a system inside a dense cloud by its compressed heliosphere. A separate feasibility study covers disk photoevaporation near O and B stars, 26Al and 60Fe heating from supernova enrichment, extinction of starlight and cosmic rays near remnants, and turns the results into generation rules (nebula-and-asteroid-field-classes.md).

7.2 Alveolar oxygen behind the mask band

Alveolar PAO2 = x_O2 (P - 6.3) - 5.3/0.8 kPa at 37 C (respiratory quotient 0.8) [C]:

Total P (kPa) PAO2, pure O2 PAO2, air Altitude equivalent
101.3 88.4 13.3 sea level
50 37.1 2.5 about 5,700 m
33.7 20.8 -0.9 Everest summit
21 8.1 -3.6
18.8 5.9 -4.0 40,000 ft
15 2.1 -4.8
11.6 -1.3 -5.5 50,000 ft
6.3 -6.6 -6.6 Armstrong line

Pure oxygen needs at least 18.9 kPa total for PAO2 = 6 kPa, 20.9 kPa for 8 kPa, and 26 kPa to match sea-level air (13.3 kPa) [C]. Aviation practice agrees: 100 percent oxygen holds an "equivalent 10,000 ft" up to about 40,000 ft, positive-pressure breathing above that, a pressure suit above about 50,000 ft, ebullism at 62,000 to 63,000 ft [S: litfl, Wikipedia "Pressure suit"]. The mask-only band therefore starts near 20 kPa, not 6.3.

Section 2 starts the mask band at 14.3 kPa (6.3 plus the 8 kPa of inspired O2 consciousness needs). The alveolar equation, which also subtracts the CO2 the lungs add, puts a pure-oxygen mask's useful floor nearer 19 to 21 kPa. The settled 14.3 kPa stands until GEN.89's tests show a case where the difference matters.

7.3 Five equipment tiers (superseded)

Superseded by the survival equipment line of GEN.655 (physics/equipment.py, docs/design/survival-equipment-fields.md): the one numbered tier is gone, and the table below is kept as the research it was built from, not as a rule. One numbered tier taken from the worst condition, a pure function of dry ambient values [C].

Tier Name Conditions (all must hold)
0 Ideal 50 <= P_tot <= 250 kPa; 16 <= pO2 <= 50; pCO2 < 0.5; pCO < 0.005; pH2S < 0.001; pSO2 < 0.0005; T_dry -20 to 45 C and wet-bulb < 31 C (ordinary weather clothing); dose <= 50 mSv/yr
1 Mask Any of: 20 <= P_tot < 50 (O2-enriched mask; pO2 of air too low); 8 <= pO2 < 16; pO2 50 to 160 (diluent mask); pCO2 0.5 to 1; 250 < P_tot <= 400; T 45 to 90 C, or -20 to -50 C with insulation; wet-bulb 31 to 35 C; dose 50 to 100 mSv/yr
2 Mask and scrubber Any of: pCO2 >= 1; pCO >= 0.005 (Hopcalite); H2S or SO2 above the chronic limit; 400 < P_tot <= 1,000 kPa (heliox or trimix rebreather); pO2 < 8 with P_tot >= 20 and a hostile base gas
3 Pressure suit P_tot < 20 kPa (6.3 to 20: gas-pressurised or mechanical-counterpressure suit plus helmet; below 6.3: full suit); P_tot > 1 MPa (atmospheric diving suit); pO2 > 160; T 90 to 120 C (liquid-cooled garment) or -50 to -120 C (heated suit); wet-bulb > 35 C; dose 0.1 to 1 Sv/yr (shielded habitat, limited surface time)
4 Full life support P_tot > 7 MPa (beyond the tested human record, Hydra X at 7.11 MPa); T < -120 or > 120 C; corrosive acids (H2SO4, HF, Cl); dose > 1 Sv/yr (radiation hardening); no usable atmosphere and no suit-compatible thermal range

The tier is the highest any condition demands. The cold range of -50 to -120 C, which PHI-4 leaves unassigned, is tier 3 here. Differences from section 2: the dose rows here are stricter than a rule that passes tier 0 below 1 Sv/yr (which would put Mars at 0.24 Sv/yr and the Moon at 0.52 at ideal), and the pressure-suit band here is under 20 kPa where section 2 uses 14.3.

Sources behind the thresholds: aviation practice (100 percent oxygen holds an equivalent 10,000 ft to about 40,000 ft, a pressure suit above about 50,000 ft, ebullism at 62,000 to 63,000 ft) [S: litfl, Wikipedia "Pressure suit"]; NASA's spacecraft CO2 limit of 3 mmHg (0.4 kPa) over one hour [S: NASA OCHMO technical brief]; atmospheric diving suits at 1 atm inside to about 300 m [R]; the human hyperbaric record, Hydra X at 7.11 MPa.

8. The score of every planet and moon (GEN.89, built)

planetgen/physics/habitability_world.py turns a rocky body's stored values into the World that section 2's maths reads and stores 19 columns on planets and moons (schema v76; a gas giant has none). It runs last in a body's generation and again when refresh_containment changes the dose, from the row alone. Every rule below is a planetGen default, not a source's number.

Input Rule
Pressure, temperature, gravity As stored (atmospheric_pressure_pa, surface_temperature_k, gravity_g).
Gases Each of O2, CO2, CO, H2S and SO2 as its partial pressure over the total; the balance gas only counts toward the total.

Equipment. The survival equipment line (GEN.655, physics/equipment.py, docs/design/survival-equipment-fields.md) lists what a human visitor needs to stand on the surface, in four slots (body protection, air filter or scrubber, air supply, radiation protection), each item required or recommended, and only what is needed: a world that needs nothing shows no line. It replaces the old equipment_tier (0 to 4), which is gone. The gear follows the physics wherever that is stricter than the score's bands; the PHI-6 scores are not changed by it. Its stored levels are the eq_* columns, and the name and the short code (B4p F100 S2 [R2]) are derived from them. Each domain stores its score (phi4_<domain>) and its colour (tier_<domain>: 0 Blue to 3 Red); hab_note lists the domains short of Blue.

Compact hosts. A planet of a pulsar, a non-pulsing neutron star or a black hole is rated as if its surface took 1000 Sv/yr, the outer Red edge of the radiation domain: Red radiation, a hardened habitat required, microbial and complex life 0, and a hab_note naming the host. The rule is by the host's class, so an X-ray binary (a normal star with a compact companion; none is generated yet) will need its companion's class tested where generate and the dose refresh find the host. The dose refresh leaves such a system's scores alone.

10. What a species can live in (POP.12, built without storage)

A species' environment limits are section 9's table with some rows replaced (planetgen/physics/species_limits.py): overrides, {limit name: value}, empty for the human limits. habitability.bands(overrides) builds the pressure, temperature, dose and oxygen bands from them, and habitability.phi4, core_scores, equipment.compute and the domain functions take those bands (the human HUMAN by default), so the six domains are scored by the same code with the species' edges. Nothing else about the score changes: the Water and Suitability rungs, the toxic gases and PHI_bio are the human visitor's.

Generated from the homeworld (Boss's default of 2026-10-15: every species passes its own homeworld). species_limits.generated(world) leaves a band alone when the human limits already hold the homeworld inside Blue with a margin (tuning.SPECIES_LIMIT_MARGIN). Past it, the side the homeworld lies beyond moves as a whole, so the Green and Yellow widths stay the human ones: pressure edges scale by the factor that puts the homeworld 1.5 times inside the Blue edge (low side or high side), temperature edges move by the degrees that put the cycle's extreme 10 degrees inside, the wet-bulb limit sits 2 degrees above the homeworld's, all the dose edges scale so the homeworld's dose is a third of the Blue edge, and the inspired oxygen limits follow the homeworld's air (the minimum a half of it, the chronic limit a quarter above it, the acute limit in the human ratio). A species with an Earth-like homeworld has no overrides.

What could live where. species_limits.assess(world, overrides, tech_level) scores the six domains. A species could live there unaided when no domain is Red. It could live there with equipment when Water is not Red and its tech level reaches the highest of the required items its limits ask for (equipment.tech_needed; tuning.SPECIES_EQUIPMENT_TECH gives a level per item); a species with no tech level has no kit. A base brings its own food and shelter, so Suitability does not count for the equipped list. The lists are db/species_fit.py: species_for_body and species_for_system (the species that could live on a body), and bodies_for_species (the worlds of a system or sector one species could live on), through GET /api/planets|moons/<id>/habitable-species, /api/systems/<id>/habitable-species and /api/species/<id>/habitable-worlds/...; the system page shows the first as "Species that could live here" and the species page shows its limits.

Storage. species_fit.limits_for_species is the one function that says what a species' limits are. It generates them from the homeworld now; DB.30 adds the columns, and species_fit.stored_overrides then returns the stored values, which win over the generated ones. A species whose homeworld has no habitability score has the human limits. POP.13 (the species editor) will write the stored values and should run species_limits.validate on them.

10.1 A species is only placed where it can live (POP.14)

species_fit.check_placement(conn, species_id, kind, body_id, habitat=False) is the one check every way of putting a species on a planet or moon calls before it writes. It passes when the species could live there unaided (no Red domain against its own limits). With habitat true, for a placement that is a habitat or a vessel and brings its own kit, it also passes when the species could live there with the equipment its tech level can make (section 10). Otherwise it raises PlacementError, whose message names the failing conditions in plain words with no numbers ("The Terrans could not live on Kepler II: it is too hot; the radiation is far beyond what it can survive.") and whose failing lists the Red domains. A gas giant fails (no surface). A body with no habitability score cannot be judged and passes.

A species on its own homeworld is a native there: its Suitability is not judged (assess(..., native=True)), because the water and food of its world are its own. Any other placement is judged on all six domains.

Today a species has one placement, its homeworld, made by the population pass (population.scan_life_worlds). It calls species_fit.check_homeworld first and makes no species on a world whose own limits cannot hold it (its water is undrinkable); the planet is passed over like any other without a civilization. A later settlement (POP.11) and the species editor (POP.13) call check_placement before they write, and the editor runs it on save and shows the failing domain. Facilities are not placements of a species: a facility's affiliation says who runs it, not who lives there.

python3 -m planetgen.cli.species_check is the consistency report: it lists every species placed outside its limits (exit 1) or says all are inside (exit 0). While the limits are generated from the homeworld they follow it, so the report finds nothing; it matters once the limits are stored (DB.30, POP.13) and one is edited or its world changes. A species it lists is fixed by regenerating its limits or the placement; there is no tolerance flag.

11. Where the rating is shown, and the zone (DOC.75)

The in-app text for all of this is /help/habitability (under the Help menu, UX.180; every chip links to its section, UX.181; a body's symbols and life scores link to the Habitability and Life groups of its own page, UX.197) (web/lib/habitability_ref.py, templates/habitability.html); its numbers are read from the code named in sections 2 and 9, so it cannot disagree with the score. The seven rating symbols (overall first, then the six domains; UX.101) are drawn on the Planets and Moons lists (/lists/planet, /lists/moon, a Habitability column that is not sortable; UX.100), under the title of a planet or moon page, in the rows of a system page and in the Search panels. A body with no score shows none; a gas giant has none. The four life scores (0 to 9, the cluster of life shapes beside the symbols) are described on the same page and in GEN.649. The sector colouring (MAP.143), the habitable-world markers (MAP.219, MAP.220) and the radiation terms (GEN.445) are added to the page as each lands.

The habitable zone is a guide, not a rating. Its inner edge is the runaway greenhouse flux and its outer edge the maximum greenhouse flux of [R2447: Table 1], each a polynomial in the star's temperature minus 5780 K, valid from 2600 to 7200 K (a star outside takes the nearest end's edges); for the Sun they are 0.95 and 1.68 AU (physics/orbits.calculate_habitable_zone, GEN.146). The planet-mass dependence of the inner edge and the early runaway history are GEN.639.

12. The four life scores (GEN.649, built)

Boss, 2026-10-23: the line "Microbial 0.00 . Complex life 0.00 . Human operability 0.52" becomes four whole numbers, Microbial, Flora, Fauna and Sentient, each 0 (none) to 9 (abundant), "based on the evolutionary path and surface conditions", and the page shows them as small icons with a number beside the PHI-6 symbols. physics/life_scores.py makes them; they are stored on every scored planet and moon (life_microbial, life_flora, life_fauna, life_sentient, schema v150, NULL for a gas giant or a body with no score) and replace the stored phi_bio, phi_cpx and phi_tech. The two likelihoods remain as in-memory values (they decide, in life.life_ceiling, what the life draw may claim); they are not stored.

The path is the latest milestone the body's timeline reached (life_stage: abiogenesis, photosynthesis, complex cells, multicellularity, technological civilization). Life with a chemistry but no timeline (a moon, or a world outside the habitable zone) is microbial and nothing more, as habitability_world.native_life already said. A body with no life chemistry, or a timeline that has reached no milestone, scores 0 on all four.

The surface is the likelihood: ceil(9 x PHI_bio) for microbial life, ceil(9 x PHI_cpx) for complex life, never below 1 once the likelihood clears tuning.HABITABILITY_LIFE_SCORE_MIN (0.005), 0 under it.

Score Needs Value
Microbial any milestone from PHI_bio
Flora photosynthesis from PHI_bio but at most 3 until multicellularity (mats and algae); from multicellularity the larger of that and PHI_cpx's step
Fauna multicellularity from PHI_cpx, never more than Flora (animals eat plants)
Sentient technological civilization Fauna's step times the share of 10 Myr (LIFE_SCORE_SENTIENT_FULL_GYR) the civilization has had since its milestone, at least 1

The thresholds (3 for simple flora, 10 Myr, the ceil step) are DESIGN values, in tuning.py, to be changed by a sweep or by Boss. What the sweep of 150 systems (2,556 scored bodies) showed on 2026-10-23: 44 bodies carry a life chemistry, 38 of those have a timeline (21 reached a technological civilization, 15 photosynthesis, 2 abiogenesis), 41 score above 0 Microbial; Sentient equals Fauna on nearly every civilization world because the generator places a civilization's milestone more than 10 Myr before "now" on almost all of them.

Who has a path (Boss, 2026-10-23). Every planet and moon, in any zone, that has a life chemistry gets an evolutionary path ("moons always get an evolutionary path if appropriate for them, using the same classifications as planets"); the class's own life chemistries and stage ceiling, and the habitability index's ceiling (life.life_ceiling), are what make it appropriate. Before this only an ecosphere planet that was not a moon had one.

Food comes from Flora and Fauna (Boss, 2026-10-23). The Suitability food rung is no longer read from the stage of the timeline: life_scores.food_stage turns the three scores into the old rungs. Flora is always good food (any Flora above 0 is Green, 0.85), Flora with Fauna is better (Blue, 1.0), Fauna is all animal life and counts as food on its own (Green); microbes alone stay Yellow (0.5) and no life Red (0.2); microbes that live only under an ice lid (or in a deep aquifer) are out of reach as before, but flora and fauna are food wherever the world scores them (Boss, 2026-10-24: a world with Flora 5, Fauna 5 and a civilization had no food because its ocean is under ice, GEN.765). The Suitability colour is still held under the five core domains' ceiling.
