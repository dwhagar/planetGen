What is drawn

The field has three useful scalars at every point, and the map offers each as a mode (one at a time, switchable in the map Menu):

Mode Quantity Unit Reads as
Pull the size of the acceleration vector, |g| m/s^2 where gravity is strong
Well depth the potential, Phi m^2/s^2 how deep the wells are
Tidal strength (shown as Stretch) the size of the gradient of g (the tidal tensor's largest eigenvalue) 1/s^2 where space is stretched; Lagrange points show as saddles

The gradient mode is what Boss called the gradients of the vector field. Lagrange and saddle points (where g is nearly zero but the gradient is not) are marked.

How it is computed

  • Zones. A sector (edge 4 pc, DEFAULT_SECTOR_EDGE_PC) is cut into equal zones; the default is 16 per edge (0.25 pc), a setting. The field is sampled at each zone's centre.
  • Sources. Every point mass in the sector and its neighbours: one per star system (its stars, planets and moons together at the system's place, which changes the field at a zone's size by under 0.1 percent; MAP.386, db/gravity_sources.py), stand-alone black holes and neutron stars (the same table the orbital update reads); rogue planets, comets and asteroid fields are below the tolerance and left out. The System Map's orbital-plane version reads the bodies themselves (MAP.169). The galaxy's own smooth potential (GEN.115) is the background, joined by the rule in "The smooth galaxy and the generated stars" below (MAP.383). Plummer softening keeps a zone that holds a source finite.
  • Near and far. Sources close to a zone are summed exactly; distant ones are summed as one point per cell (mass and centre of mass) of a hierarchy with 0.5 pc leaf cells, the way a tree code does (MAP.385, galaxy/gravity_tree.py; the MAP.151 region is the data window and holds no masses). The cost study (PERF.72) fixes the split, the opening angle (0.3 to 0.7, chosen to a pair budget) and checks it against the exact sum; the kernel is galaxy/gravity_kernel.py (MAP.384).
  • Cache. One file per sector in a cache directory (galaxy/gravity_cache.py, MAP.387), keyed by a stamp of the rule, the settings, the potential and, for every sector within 4 edges, its modified_at and its rows' count and latest modified_at. The orbital update (GEN.105) writes the rows it moves, so a moved star changes the stamp of the grids that reach its sector and of no other, and the next read works only those out again. The file holds the local field (generated stars less the smooth galaxy's own stars, 16^3 zones, ten float32 values each: the pull, the potential and the tidal tensor); the reader adds the smooth galaxy at the zone. The job is db/gravity_grid.py (it imports numba, so a worker runs it; the web app reads the files and never imports it). Derived data: never part of the galaxy, no migration.
  • Slices and volumes. The map shows one slab (a plane slice) at a time and can show the whole volume as transparent layers; on the System Map it is the orbital plane.

The smooth galaxy and the generated stars (MAP.383)

The smooth potential (galaxy/potential.py, GEN.115) already holds the mass of the stars the generator draws: its disks, bulge and halo are fitted to the same density model. Adding the generated stars on top of it counts that mass twice. Measured on generated blocks (reports/map383-background-rule-2026-11-05.md), the double count is small in the pull (0.02 percent of the smooth pull at the solar circle, 0.3 percent in the bulge) and in the potential, and large in the tidal strength: the tidal norm is off by about 36 percent at the median and 67 to 80 percent at the 95th percentile, because the smooth tidal trace is the mean stellar density (4 pi G rho) and a sum of point masses has none between the stars.

The rule (points 1 to 4 and 6 are MAP.383's; point 5 is a recommendation MAP.167 confirms):

  1. Field = smooth galaxy + generated stars - the smooth galaxy's own stars in the same sectors. The last term is each generated sector's share of the smooth model's stellar mass, spread through the sector as a cube and softened by the zone edge, and summed by the same tree as the generated stars. Equivalent: the smooth field plus the fluctuation of the generated stars about their mean.
  2. The mass subtracted is the smooth potential's own stellar density (the Poisson density of its disk and sphere terms, closed forms), not the density model's expected mass. The two differ by 15 to 19 percent where measured (solar circle: potential 0.026 against model 0.031 Msun per pc^3; bulge: 3.68 against 3.10) because the potential's disks and spheres are stand-ins (GP1 to GP3). Using the potential's own mass changes the tidal norm by 5 to 7 percent against subtracting the model's, and removes that mismatch from the map instead of showing it as stars that are not there. GEN.1285 (galaxy/stellar_table.py) closes the mismatch at its source: the potential's stellar density, pull and potential now include a table of the density model's difference from the profiles, so the potential's own stellar mass is the model's to about 2 percent and the subtraction here and the density model agree. The cache stamp reads the table, so files made before it are made again.
  3. A sector that is not generated adds nothing and subtracts nothing (its generated and subtracted mass would be equal), so the near region needs no expected-mass cells for holes.
  4. Dark halo, gas, black hole and every other smooth term stay in the background untouched.
  5. The map has two readings of each quantity. The total field (the rule above) and the local field (generated stars minus their mean). In the pull the local field is 0.03 to 0.09 percent of the total, so a total-pull map shows the galaxy and none of the stars; the tidal local field is as large as the smooth one (100 to 130 percent). The Pull and Well depth modes therefore draw the local field by default with the total as a switch; Tidal strength draws the total.
  6. A generated sector holds less than the model expects in the sample measured (see the report: 85 percent of the systems and 78 percent of the mass in a 125-sector solar-circle block, 98 and 95 percent in one bulge sector), so the local field shows a small real deficit. The rule leaves it visible on purpose; the generator side is filed as its own bug.

Colours

A perceptual, colour-blind-safe ramp on a log scale, with the same rule as the Galaxy Map tint: density of the effect sets transparency and no fill goes above 50% opacity, so stars and routes stay readable. The legend states the unit under the Customary or metric choice (UX.36 number formatting).

The size of a gradient measure comes from the orbital thresholds (planned)

Boss (2026-10-10 22:14Z): "add note to TODO for the future plan of gravitational map, use the preset minimum movement values from the orbital system to build the size of each gradient measure."

The orbital update system already fixes, per scale, the smallest movement that counts (THRESHOLDS_M in physics/position.py, section 3 of orbital-updates.md, pinned by test_update_due.py):

Scale Preset minimum movement In metres Objects
Galactic 0.01 mpc (about 2 AU, 3.1e8 km) 0.01 * PARSEC_M / 1000 stars, black holes, neutron stars, stand-alone bodies
System 0.01 AU (about 1.5e6 km) 0.01 * AU planets, companion stars
Planetary 100,000 km 1.0e8 moons and other satellites

The plan, to be settled in GEN.198, GEN.199 and PERF.72:

  • Step of a gradient measure. The tidal tensor and the size of the gradient are taken by differencing the field over a step. That step is the preset minimum movement of the scale being drawn: galactic for a sector grid, system for a System Map grid, planetary for a planet or moon neighbourhood. Below it the orbital system itself says an object has not moved, so a finer difference measures nothing the rest of the galaxy can see.
  • Size of a zone. A zone edge is a whole number of those steps, so the gradient of a zone is the difference of two samples that are a real number of steps apart. The default sector grid (16 zones of 0.25 pc over a 4 pc edge) is 250 mpc, which is 25,000 galactic steps. A zone is never finer than one step; a setting that asks for finer zones is refused.
  • When a zone changes. A source that has not moved by its own threshold has not moved in the orbital system, so it does not change the grid: the cache (GEN.199) is thrown away only for the zones a source left or entered once it passes its threshold, which is the same event that sets its next update due time (GEN.106).
  • One set of numbers. The grid reads the thresholds from the same constants as the orbital update, not a copy, so changing a threshold there changes the gradient step and the cache rule together.

The Sector Map layer (MAP.168). The View menu's Gravity row (off at first; show=gravity in the address, with gravity=pull|depth|tidal and gslab=<1..16>|all) draws one slab of the sector's 16 x 16 x 16 grid as colored zones, or every slab thinly. The numbers are the total field (Boss, 2026-11-05). The smooth galaxy is 99.9 percent of the pull and its slope across a sector is 700 to 900 times the stars' wells in the depth, so colors over the total are one ramp: the colors come from field=centered (the smooth galaxy held at the sector's center), stretched over the sector's own 2nd to 98th percentile on a log scale (at most 4 decades below the top), and the Key says so and gives the smooth galaxy's level and its change across the sector. A sector with no variation draws nothing and says that. The saddle points show as small white diamonds that can be hovered. No stars only switch (Boss, 2026-11-06). Measured on real sector grids in reports/gravity-range-2026-11-06/.

The wording (UX.94). The reader sees Pull (how hard gravity tugs on a ship at a spot), Well depth (how far down in a gravity well the spot lies) and Stretch (how much gravity pulls a ship apart, nose from tail; the tidal strength, tidal in the address and the API), each with a one-line explanation under the buttons and as the button's tip. Numbers are written the way the site writes them (numberformat.js): the pull through the acceleration unit ladder, so it follows the Metric or Customary choice (meters or feet per second squared), the depth and the stretch in their scientific units to three figures. Both maps' Map help explains the modes, what the colors cover (the view's middle 96% of values on a log scale), the Hill spheres and what a saddle point is (a spot where pulls cancel, so a ship could hover, still stretched). No item code is in any of the words.

The System Map layer (MAP.169). The same row on the System Map draws the orbital plane of the scene on show: the point-mass field of the stars, planets and moons drawn in it (each softened by its own radius; the galaxy's pull is below what a system can show), in the same three modes, colors and Key. The map is a log radial diagram (or one linear scale in a moon system), so the picture is turned back into the kilometres each pixel stands for before the field is worked out (pixelToKm), and drawn only between the first and last orbit, where the scale stands for a distance. The Hill sphere of each planet (by its star, or both stars of a close pair) or moon (by its planet), d (m / 3M)^(1/3), is outlined and mapped forward point by point; the five Lagrange points of each such pair (physics/lagrange_geometry.py's geometry, repeated in systemgravity.js for the page) are marked, L4 ahead along the stored motion. The markers carry data-masskg, data-vxkms/data-vykms and the scene data-hostkg.
