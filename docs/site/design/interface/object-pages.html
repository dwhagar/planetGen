Every star, planet, moon, asteroid belt and comet has a page of its own at /object/<kind>:<Object ID> (web/item_pages.py). The system page and the sector page list their contents as summary trees whose rows link to those pages. This note says how the parts fit and the rules a change has to keep.

The page

In order: the Location block (galaxy, sector, system, the star the body orbits and, for a moon, its planet), links to the previous and next object at the same level (the planets that share a star in their order out, a planet's moons, the system's stars, the belts or comets of a star), the rendered example (star, planet and moon), the property sheet, an "About" panel (the description's paragraph, a life world's dominant species, the facilities the body hosts) and "Belonging to it", the summary tree of the body's children. A planet or moon also shows its PHI-6 symbols and habitability chips under the title, and an admin has that body's Admin menu in the action bar (Regenerate, Change class, Delete; the page takes the POST).

The region (not built, UX.231)

The at-a-glance block will say "in Vang Zarh Region" for a sector or object whose position lies in a charted region (a 9-block with a region_stats row, ../galaxy/named-regions.md), and nothing when it lies in none. Until UX.231 the pages say nothing of regions.

The property sheet

describe/sheet.py is the one registry of what a page shows about a body: per kind (star, planet, moon, belt, comet and the eight standalone phenomenon kinds, UX.175) an ordered list of PropertySpec(key, label, group, quantity, column, solar). The page (item.html) and GET /api/objects/<ref>/sheet both read it, so the website, the API and the tests agree. A value the row does not hold is returned as "not stored", never left out, so an empty line means no data and not a bug.

A new column needs a sheet row. tests/test_property_sheet.py walks every column of stars, planets, moons, asteroid_belts, comets and the eight phenomenon tables (from db.models.metadata, with the unit from db/column_units.py) and fails for a column that is neither a PropertySpec of its kind nor on that kind's NOT_SHOWN list with a reason (the ids, links and bookkeeping a reader never needs). A lane that adds a column to one of those tables adds its sheet row, or its NOT_SHOWN reason, in the same pull request.

Phenomena

A nebula, asteroid field, black hole, neutron star, supernova remnant, rogue planet, interstellar comet or quasar keeps its own page, /phenomenon/<type>/<id> (web/system_pages.py, phenomenon.html): its view, a curated data table with the derived values (Einstein radius, galactic speed) and class links, the Location block, what a nebula or remnant holds, and since UX.175 an "All stored properties" panel that is the same property sheet as a body's, served by the same API. Not yet on the phenomenon pages: the paragraph text (the phenomenon description is built from a freshly generated object and cannot yet be rebuilt from a stored row), neighbour links and the tree. Facilities, star clusters and regions are not object kinds with an Object ID yet, so they have no page of their own.

The summary tree

web/lib/objecttree.py draws the rows and static/objecttree.js makes them live. A row is a toggle button, the kind's mark, the name (a link to the object's page), a summary line (star: spectral type, role, mass, luminosity; planet and moon: class, distance, period and the PHI-6 symbols; belt: density and range; comet: kind, activity and period; system: single or binary and the star types) and, for a row with children, the counts below it. Rules:

  • No link sits inside a button or a <summary> (assistive technology reads that as a nested control). The toggle is its own button, hidden until the script runs; without script every level is simply listed and a row whose children are not on the page holds a link to its own page.
  • A level loads when its row opens, from GET /object/<ref>/tree (the children API, db/children.py, keyset paging, 50 rows at a time) and a longer level ends in a "Show more" button. A system's stars are drawn open onto their own bodies.
  • The keys are the WAI-ARIA tree pattern: Up and Down, Right (open, then first child), Left (close, then the parent), Home and End; one name is in the Tab order at a time.
  • The sector page's Contents table puts the same tree under a system's name (static/datatable.js calls objecttree.js's embedTree); an opened row is kept across the table's redraws.

The rendered example

web/lib/bodyview.py builds the look record of the body (web/maps/bodylook.py, the record the System Map paints from) and static/bodyview.js paints it with solidpaint.js or starpaint.js and shades it as a lit sphere on a 2D canvas, with the atmosphere's limb glow or a star's corona. A giant, which the painters do not cover yet, is its tinted disc; a belt and a comet have no example yet. The caption is look_note, a stand-in until the colour account of UX.158 replaces it. See body-rendering.md.

Links

Every link to a body is /object/<ref>: the Document references (item_pages._ref_link), the search results (system_pages.object_url), the map popups' View button (systemnav.js), and the tree rows. The old /system/<id>#planet-12 anchors, body_anchor, static/bodyanchor.js and the row-highlight styles are gone; tests/test_item_pages.py fails for any source that links a body to a system anchor.

Tests

test_property_sheet.py (the sheet), test_object_children.py (the children API), test_object_tree.py and test_item_pages.py (markup and pages), test_object_page_walk.py (every kind on a generated database), test_web_browser_object_tree.py (the tree in Chromium, with axe-core), test_web_browser_bodyview.py (the picture) and test_web_a11y.py (every page at phone and desktop width).
