Superseded in part by MAP.258 to MAP.262 (2026-10-24): the map is no longer cut into cube tiles. /galaxy/tiles, viewport.distance_cut_tiles and galaxytilecut.js are gone; the map fetches sector-block nodes (/galaxy/nodes, keys m.shell.band.wedge) chosen by projected size (planetgen.galaxy.nodecut, static/galaxynodecut.js), and a star's fade is its node's weight (weightCalculator). Read "tile" below as the cube tile the map had before; the wire format, packing and cache carry over to nodes unchanged.

Status (2026-10-15): decided; partly built. Done: MAP.148 (#1160), MAP.149 (#1141), MAP.150 (#1382), MAP.153 (#998), MAP.154 (#1303), MAP.155 (#1253) and MAP.188 (#1289), and MAP.152 (client part built 2026-10-15, see its "As built" paragraph; merged 2026-10-16). Section 2, "What the map does today", describes the map before MAP.148 and MAP.149.

Research and design, 2026-10-09, from Boss's message of 22:56Z. No code changes. The rewritten MAP.146 text and the proposed split are in sections 7 and 8, for the TODO thread to file.

Boss's goal: one continuous 3D world from the whole galaxy to a star system. Scroll wheel zooms, double-click flies to a clickable thing, the observer can be inside a block or sector and sees its contents without looking around what is in the way, the region being looked at is rendered well and the surroundings are visible but quiet, and only stars an observer could see are drawn.

1. Answer in brief

  1. Stars: one smooth law, not tile steps. Draw a star at opacity a = smoothstep((m_lim - m) / 1.5), where m = M + 5 log10(d / 10 pc) is its apparent magnitude from the camera and m_lim is chosen each frame so about N stars (default 20,000) are drawn. The inverse-square "floor luminosity at distance d" is the same law written the other way: L_min(d) = L_min(d0) (d/d0)^2, a straight line of slope 2 on a log-log plot. Today's rule is already this law, but frozen into steps (section 2.2).
  2. Near field: three cheap, standard tools together. (a) A depth fade, so anything closer than a fraction of the focus distance dissolves. (b) A soft-edged see-through tube from the camera to the focus ("ghosting"), so what is in the way thins out as it gets in the way. (c) The container the camera is inside is drawn from the inside, as faint edges, with its contents at full strength. These replace the one-off blocker formula in MAP.121.
  3. What is visible is what is clickable. The picker ignores anything whose current opacity is under 0.35 and anything nearer than the fade's start distance, using the same function the shader uses.
  4. Camera: free, with the observer able to be inside. Wheel zooms toward the point under the cursor, speed proportional to the distance to the nearest object (so it can never overshoot into one). Double-click flies to the thing clicked on a van Wijk and Nuij path. The stage-by-stage presets and the arc, slab and segment picks stop being the way you navigate.
  5. Data under the camera: a distance-cut of the existing octree tiles plus MAP.146's aligned cells. Nearer tiles are finer. A 60 degree forward view needs about 140 to 280 tiles instead of today's 27 (section 4.5; empty tiles not counted).
  6. Focus and context are one number. Each region gets a prominence from 0 to 1 (the thing looked at, the container, the neighbours). Prominence scales opacity and lowers m_lim locally (context shows only stars 2.5 magnitudes brighter), so the sector in view is rich and its surroundings are present but cheap.
  7. MAP.146's earlier recommendation stays as its data layer: exact-centered frame on aligned cells. For continuous flight, prefer the 3-ary cell pyramid (section 6).

2. What the map does today (checked in the repo)

2.1 Camera and stages

  • galaxystageview.js: stage presets (galaxy, arc, slab, segment, block, sector, system). Each flies to a preset camera. Zoom runs from MIN_ZOOM 1/8 to MAX_ZOOM 2.5 times the stage's fit; the galaxy only from fit to twice as close (GALAXY_MIN_ZOOM 0.5). A system opened in place goes to SYSTEM_MIN_ZOOM 1e-9. A wheel move from one stage to the next takes one gesture with a 900 ms cool-down (ACROSS_COOLDOWN_MS, MAP.125).
  • galaxymap3d.js: an orbit camera: a target and a distance back along a quaternion. The camera is always outside the thing shown. The near plane follows min(BASE_NEAR, dist * 1e-3) and the depth buffer is logarithmic, so a close near plane costs no precision.
  • Wheel factor is 0.0025 per pixel of wheel travel (WHEEL_ZOOM_PER_PX), so a 100 px notch scales the distance by about 0.78.

2.2 Stars: the rule is a limiting magnitude in steps

queryDb.generated_star_floor_sol(level) lists in a tile only generated stars at least 0.004 L_sun * (tile_edge / 32 pc)^2. That is a limiting magnitude of 13.35 at a distance equal to the tile edge, at every level (computed: level 11, 32 pc, floor 0.004 L_sun, M 10.82; level 7, 512 pc, floor 1.02 L_sun, M 4.80; level 3, 8.2 kpc, floor 262 L_sun, M -1.22; each is 13.35 at its own edge). The floor quadruples (1.5 magnitudes) per level, and a view uses one level, so the limit steps by 1.5 magnitudes at each zoom level and is measured against the tile, not against the camera. Boss's gradient is this law made continuous and tied to the observer.

  • Brightness on screen: starBright comes from log luminosity only (STAR_LOG_LUMINOSITY -4 to 6), with the Sector Map's boost (starlight.js, MAP.87). It does not change with distance: a supergiant 10 kpc away and an identical one 10 pc away draw the same.
  • Stars are drawn with depthTest: false, at a fixed 6 to 30 pixels (STAR_MIN_PX, STAR_MAX_PX), so they are never hidden by blocks and never occluded by anything, including something in front of the camera.
  • Budgets: 27 tiles per view of one level (GALAXY_VIEW_MAX_TILES), plus up to 8 finest tiles around a target; 150 to 850 generated stars per tile (GALAXY_TILE_STAR_BUDGET), 4,000 in a finest tile; 70,000 stars per view overall (GALAXY_VIEW_MAX_STARS, MAP.109; 45,000 since MAP.249).

2.3 What is in front of the camera

  • Block fills use a fade uniform and depthWrite: !translucent; there is no near fade on blocks. Clouds do have one (CLOUD_NEAR_FADE [1.2, 2.0] in cloud radii, smoothstep), and CLOUD_PICK_MAX_PX 150 stops a cloud being picked once it fills the screen.
  • Picking (mappick.js): layers in priority order; a mesh layer raycasts and takes the nearest hit with a meaningful entry; points are picked on screen within a pixel reach. A layer marked occludes blocks what is behind it. Nothing checks how visible a hit is, and a ray starting inside a container hits that container's own faces. That is the "clickable what is right in front of the camera, and the object the camera is in" problem.
  • MAP.121 and MAP.141 already specify a blocker fade and faint neighbours (design doc map-ui-and-frontend-libraries.md section 5.4): neighbour alpha a0 exp(-d / lambda), floor 0.08, ceiling 0.3, and a fade by the distance of a neighbour's center from the camera-to-focus line. Good, but center-based, per region, and not a general near field.

4. Design

4.1 The visibility law for stars (A in Boss's message)

  • Per star: m = M + 5 log10(d / 10) with M = 4.83 - 2.5 log10(L / L_sun) and d the camera distance in parsecs. Opacity a = smoothstep(0, 1, (m_lim - m) / 1.5). 1.5 magnitudes is the factor 4 that today's floors step by, so the ramp replaces each step by a ramp of the same width.
  • Brightness: draw the point's strength from apparent flux, not luminosity. A star's glow and core alpha follow 10^(-0.4 (m - m_ref)) through the same tone curve the Sector Map uses (starlight.js), so the two maps agree and distance dims a star as it should. Close stars are tone-mapped (F / (F + F_half)) so one star next to the camera cannot flood the screen; their disk gets its true angular size once it is more than 1 pixel across.
  • Choosing m_lim: each frame, build a histogram (64 bins) of m over the stars in the loaded tiles (at most 70,000, so one pass) and take the magnitude at which the count reaches N_target. Defaults: 20,000 on desktop, 8,000 on a phone (unmeasured starting values). This needs no model of the star population because it uses the stars actually loaded, and it automatically shows fewer faint stars in a packed core and more in thin space.
  • Distance a star can be seen (d_vis = 10^((m_lim - M)/5 + 1) pc):
star M m_lim 6.5 (naked eye) 9 12 15
red dwarf 12 0.8 pc 2.5 pc 10 pc 40 pc
Sun 4.83 22 pc 68 pc 272 pc 1.1 kpc
A star 1 126 pc 398 pc 1.6 kpc 6.3 kpc
giant -1 316 pc 1.0 kpc 4.0 kpc 16 kpc
bright giant -5 2.0 kpc 6.3 kpc 25 kpc 100 kpc
supergiant -8 7.9 kpc 25 kpc 100 kpc 400 kpc

Supergiants stay visible across the galaxy (it is 15 kpc in radius), which is right; a Sun-like star disappears past 1 kpc even at 15.

  • Server side, nothing to change at first (done in MAP.152: the floors are gone, see 4.5). The tile floors stay as a superset (a tile never lists less than any observer at a sensible distance would want). Once tiles are chosen by distance (4.5) the floor for a tile follows its distance and the two rules coincide.

As built, first stage (MAP.148, static/starmagnitude.js). Client side on the tiles already fetched. m_lim is the larger of the histogram limit and a calibration limit: the 98th-percentile absolute magnitude of the stars MAP.153 is showing, taken at the focus distance D, plus the ramp. That keeps the factor at 1 for a star at the target distance at any camera radius (the dependency in the TODO), so the law only dims stars much farther than the focus and never thins the same stars twice with the rank birth radius. The histogram counts only stars in the view cone and weights each by its MAP.153 zoom opacity, 64 bins, with the middle of the ramp as the count point; it is redone at most every 150 ms when the camera or the star set changes, and the uniform eases toward it with a 0.3 s time constant. Targets: 20,000, or 8,000 when the screen is narrow (700 px) or the pointer is coarse. Brightness by flux acts on the core only (vBright), from 0.5 at the faint end of the ramp to 1 three magnitudes inside it; the halo keeps its luminosity-based glow, which Boss's rule for a fixed small point with a big glow asks for. Landmarks have magnitude -40. Not done yet: tiles chosen by distance (with MAP.152) and dropping the server floors.

4.2 Prominence: render what is looked at well (B, last paragraph)

Every region (cell, block, sector) has a prominence p in 0..1:

  • Looked at (p = 1): contains the focus target, or its center is within about 10 degrees of the view axis (w = 1 - smoothstep(10, 25 degrees, angle)).
  • Container the camera is inside (p = 0.8).
  • Neighbours and the rest (p = clamp(exp(-d / lambda), 0.08, 0.3), the formula already in design doc 5.4), with lambda about one region width.

p scales a region's fill opacity and shifts its stars' limit: m_lim_eff = m_lim - 2.5 (1 - p) (a factor of 10 in flux). A context sector shows only its bright stars, with no per-star labels or hit tests, so it is cheap. The sector in view shows everything down to m_lim.

4.3 The camera (C)

  • Wheel: dist <- dist * exp(-0.0025 * deltaY) as now, but toward the point under the cursor, which stays fixed on screen. That point is, in order: the picked star, remnant or cell under the cursor; else the first sample along the cursor ray where the model's integrated column density passes a threshold (the page already evaluates the density in galaxyprisms.js); else the point at the current focus depth. As built (MAP.225, 2026-10-17): the wheel zooms toward the star, remnant or cell under the pointer (else the point of the focus plane under it), which stays where it is on the screen: zoomTowardPointer in galaxystageview.js, limitApproach and targetAboutAnchor in mapcontrol.js. As built (MAP.245, 2026-10-17): in an opened sector the point under the pointer is the star (one with a system, not left off the map) within 14 px of it on the screen, found by starUnder; the stars are not picked (MAP.101), and the sector's own cell or wall behind a star is never taken for the point (it lay 2.3 pc behind a star 0.28 pc away and the camera slid sideways past the star; TEST.213). A step in is limited twice (limitedStep): to half the way to the point, minus a clearance of 2% of the view's distance (MC.limitApproach), and so that the eye never comes within that clearance of any star of the sector on the way (MC.limitPathClearance). The column-density ray sample, which MAP.245 left out, is MAP.247 (next paragraph).

  • Clearance: each step moves by min(requested, 0.5 * (d_nearest - d_clear)) where d_nearest is the distance to the nearest pickable thing, so speed is logarithmic in the distance to it (Mackinlay) and the camera never lands on a star or inside a wall by accident. d_clear is a small multiple of the thing's drawn radius.

  • Double-click: target is the thing under the pointer (a star, sector, cell or system). Fly on the van Wijk and Nuij path, with the end distance chosen so the target fills about 60% of the view height. This is MAP.140's go-to, extended: a double-click on a block or cell flies in; a double-click on empty space flies a fixed fraction (half) of the way toward the point under the cursor. Offer the same flights from a "Go to" button and Enter, as MAP.140's notes require. As built (MAP.226, 2026-10-16): a double click (a second click within 350 ms and 8 px, mapcontrol.js onDoubleClick, used instead of the second click) on an object of the sector scene flies the camera to it, keeping the angle: a star with a system to where its system's disk is 60 px across, a cloud or body to where it fills 60% of the view height, any other point at the distance the view has. The end distance goes through the view's zoom policy, so it never lands closer than the zoom allows. In an opened system a double click on a body is the body flight it always had. A double click on a block (or a second tap on a touch screen) is still the block's click. The same flight is the info panel's "Go to" button and Enter with an object selected and no choice lit (picking an object takes the light off a choice the arrow keys left lit). Double-click on empty space is built by MAP.245. As built (MAP.245, 2026-10-17): a double click on empty space (nothing picked) flies half way toward the point under the pointer (flyHalfWayTo: the step of a wheel tick of 0.5, limited as the wheel is, with the point staying where it is on the screen). On a block it is still two clicks, and on a star or any other object the flight to it. As built (MAP.247, 2026-10-17): where the cursor ray meets nothing pickable (no star within 14 px, no mesh the wheel may take), the point is the first place along the ray where the column of the galaxy's stars passes a threshold (MC.columnDensityPoint, columnAnchor in galaxystageview.js). The density is the page's own model, relativeDensity of galaxyprisms.js (1 is the local average), so the cell scene needs no density field of its own. The ray is sampled 192 times out to COLUMN_REACH (2) times the eye's distance to the focus plane point; the column is density times step length, and the threshold is COLUMN_SHARE (0.5) times the view's distance in average-density parsecs: in space of the local average density the point is half way to the focus, in a dense region (a bulge, an arm, the core) nearer, and in a sparse one the ray never reaches it and the focus plane's point is taken as before. The point is never nearer than COLUMN_NEAREST (0.25) of the way to the focus plane point, so a step in still moves the eye (the wheel's half-way limit would otherwise leave it where it is). It is not used in an opened system (its frame is the system's, not the galaxy's), before the galaxy has a shape, or with the pointer on the cross (within CROSS_PX, 12 px, of the middle of the map: MAP.199 has the wheel zoom about the cross there).

  • Observer inside: "I am in sector X" comes from the camera position (geometry.sector_address_at is closed form), shown in the header and the breadcrumb, which becomes a trail of containers (galaxy, ..., block, sector, system) derived from the position, not from state. Clicking a crumb flies out to it. As built (MAP.227, 2026-10-17): the breadcrumb is the trail of the cell holding the view's middle at the stage's own size (stageAtPoint in galaxystages.js), and the label over the map's top-left corner (galaxymap3d-where) names the sector it is in (sectorAtPoint). Levels finer or coarser than the stage's window are not derived from the distance; a crumb flies out to its stage as before.

  • URLs and bookmarks hold the camera (target, distance, orientation). Old block URLs and stage tokens stop working (no backward compatibility, Boss's rule). As built (MAP.228, 2026-10-17): the Galaxy Map's address is ?cam=x,y,z,d,qx,qy,qz,qw (the view's middle and distance in parsecs and its turn, ten significant digits; galaxystages.js cameraQuery, parseCamera), written in place once the view has been still for 350 ms and added to the history by a stage hop, with the sector and display parts of the query left as they are. Opening one works the stage out from the camera (stageForCamera: the finest window of 9 cells, centered on the cell holding the view's middle, whose own fit is at least the distance; the galaxy when even the coarsest is smaller) and puts the camera there; a history entry also keeps its stage so Back, Forward and a reload return to it exactly. ?win= and &g= are no longer read; bookmarks of a stage keep the address. The 3-ary ladder's levels, the window data and the stage hops by click and wheel are as before.

  • What goes: the arc, slab and segment picks as the way to move, their presets and the 900 ms stage-hop cool-down. The slab strip can stay as an optional section-plane tool (clip everything above a layer) for looking at one slice; that is a view tool, not a stage.

4.4 Scale and precision

Float32 positions at distance D from the origin resolve D / 2^24: 0.003 pc (600 AU) at 49 kpc, 12 AU at 1 kpc, 0.12 AU at 10 pc, 0.012 AU at 1 pc. Galaxy-scale positions are fine for stars as points; anything closer than about 100 pc needs positions relative to the camera, computed in double precision per tile (a tile origin as a double, vertex data as float32 offsets, and a per-tile uniform tileOrigin - camera). Status (MAP.152, 2026-10-15): the per-tile origin is not needed. The map already draws every star as an offset from a whole-parsec frame at the camera's target (MAP.102), computed in double precision on the CPU, so a star within a few hundred pc of the camera is exact to far under a pixel; the error only reaches pixel size for a star within about 0.003 pc of a camera 100 pc from its target, where the system scene draws instead. The dynamic range from a 4 pc sector to 1 AU is 825,000, which a log depth buffer and a near plane of 1e-3 * distance (both in place) cover. A system's disk becomes drawable when it subtends about 20 pixels: for a 50 AU system at 1000 px and 50 degrees of view that is about 3,000 AU (0.014 pc).

4.5 Which data the camera pulls (distance-cut)

Today one level covers the whole view (27 tiles). For a camera inside the scene, refine the octree by distance: split a node while its edge is more than k times its distance from the camera (and it meets the galaxy and the view cone). Counts below are upper bounds (empty tiles are not removed), for a 60 degree forward view:

camera k = 2 k = 1 k = 0.5
Sun radius, in the plane 128 140 452
2 kpc out, 100 pc above the plane 112 272 634
bulge, ring 100 128 204 560
galaxy edge, 14 kpc 128 280 664

All-round free look at k = 1: 684 to 936 tiles. At roughly 300 stars per tile, k = 1 forward is 40,000 to 85,000 stars, so per-tile budgets must shrink with the number of tiles to stay under the 70,000-star cap; the m_lim histogram then trims it on screen. A request holds at most 128 tiles, so this is 2 to 8 requests, cached by key. A node's floor luminosity (edge squared) is the magnitude law at constant angular size, so choosing levels by distance and the magnitude law agree. Phase this: first the client-side law on the tiles already fetched, then the distance-cut.

4.6 The near field (B, middle paragraphs)

  1. Depth fade (always on). Multiply a translucent fill's or an outline's alpha by smoothstep(z0, z1, viewDepth) with z0 = 0.15 D and z1 = 0.5 D, where D is the camera-to-focus distance (so the hidden zone scales with how close the user is looking). A camera inside a block loses the block's near faces by the same rule. The existing cloud fade [1.2, 2.0] radii is the precedent; for the 3-ary pyramid, apply it per cell at its own size.
  2. Focus tube (ghosting). With camera C, focus F and fragment P: t = ((P - C) . (F - C)) / |F - C|^2 and rho = |P - C - t (F - C)|. For 0 < t < 1 (in front of the focus) multiply alpha by 1 - (1 - a_min) * (1 - smoothstep(R_t, 3 R_t, rho)) with R_t = max(r_focus, 0.12 D) and a_min = 0.25 (MAP.187: a wider, shallower edge so the window leaves no visible rim or halo; it applies to the blocks' density and cell boxes only, never to a star). One dot product and one smoothstep per fragment; two uniforms. The tube can widen toward the camera into a cone for a cutaway feel. This reduces to the MAP.121 blocker rule for a whole-region fade and replaces it. Use ghosting, not removal: context stays faintly visible.
  3. Inside a container. Draw its faces as back faces at low alpha (or edges only) and everything in it at full strength; do not raycast its own walls.
  4. Order. Translucent prisms are sorted back to front with depthWrite: false (a few hundred; MAP.121 already says this). Dithered discard (a 4 x 4 or blue-noise threshold on the fade alpha) makes the near fade independent of order. If overlapping translucent fills still look wrong, add weighted blended OIT for the fills only, since stars are additive anyway.
  5. Picking agrees with drawing. Compute visibleAlpha(point) = fillAlpha * nearFade * tubeFade * prominence on the CPU with the same constants; a hit counts only if it is at least 0.35, and nothing within z0 is hit. Stars use the screen-space picker and are skipped if their opacity a is under 0.35.

As built (MAP.149, static/nearfield.js). Worked in view space (camera at the origin looking down -z, focus at (0, 0, -D)), where depth is -z and rho is the distance from the z axis, so no world positions or camera vectors reach the shader. Three differences from the sketch above: the tube's 0 < t < 1 test is a smooth ramp, smoothstep(0, R_t, D - depth), so flat slabs in the focus plane are untouched and nothing pops as the plane is crossed; the 0.35 pick rule applies to the near-field share alone (depthFade * tubeFade at the hit point), not to fillAlpha, because block fills are 0.1 to 0.5 and the rule would otherwise make every block unpickable; and the blocks are sorted at build time only (the dithered discard is not built). The container the camera is inside (found by blockContains on the stage's block bounds) is drawn by the same mesh with its back faces on and every other block's back faces discarded, at INSIDE_FACE_ALPHA 0.25; the picker skips back faces. Stars use the depth fade and tube in their vertex shader; the phenomenon picker skips a star under 0.35. The faint-context and prominence rules (contextOpacity, prominence, limitingMagnitude) are built and tested but nothing draws neighbouring regions yet; MAP.150 and MAP.148 use them.

As built (MAP.152, server part: tiles by distance). planetgen.galaxy.viewport.distance_cut_tiles (twin: static/galaxytilecut.js, held equal by tests/test_js_unit.py and tests/js/galaxytilecut.test.mjs) splits the root cube while a tile's edge is more than DISTANCE_CUT_EDGES (2 here; 3 since MAP.249, below) times the camera's distance to its box, down to level 11, keeps the tiles inside a 70 degree cone (50 since MAP.249) round the look direction and inside the galaxy's box (galaxymap3d.tile_bounds_pc: the galaxy's radius in the plane, its layers' reach above and below), and returns them nearest first, at most 512. Over the sample cameras of tests/test_api.py that is 100 to 320 tiles, a dozen or so of the coarsest level and up to about sixty of each level finer. The map fetches the cut at k * 1.15 and k / 1.15 together (fetch_cut_tiles, the fade band) plus, within 120 pc of the target, the finest tiles round it (DETAIL_RADIUS_PC 8). The page's first frame carries the 64 nearest of them (INITIAL_TILE_LIMIT); the rest are fetched as it opens. The server floors (generated_star_floor_sol) are gone: a tile lists the brightest of its level's budget, none of the generated stars above level 3.

Budgets were refit so a view stays under the 70,000-star cap (45,000 since MAP.249, 2026-10-18) by construction. Per tile, by level: bright stars GALAXY_TILE_BRIGHT_BUDGET (300 for the coarsest tiles, falling to 40 at level 8 and finer, 300 in the finest) and generated stars GALAXY_TILE_STAR_BUDGET (30 at level 3 rising to 160 at level 11, 1,200 in the finest); galaxy_view_star_cap adds the caps of any set of tiles, and test_a_views_star_payload_has_a_stated_cap takes the worst of 120 cameras looking every way (about 63,000, so the stated 70,000 held; MAP.249 below). The nearer, finer tiles list the most because the camera is among them. This settles the old flat 400 bright stars a tile, which a cut of hundreds of tiles cannot afford.

MAP.249: a coarser, narrower cut (factor 3, cone 50 degrees). Boss found the map unusable while zooming (6:49 PM EDT, 10-17): every zoom step asked for roughly 450 tiles, 1 to 3 MB of packed tiles, and the server took tens of seconds to build the cold ones. The fade under the cut. A star shows at its tile's share (static/starfade.js): a tile's "leaving" share rises from 0 to 1 as the camera's distance to its box goes from 0.87 to 1.15 edges over CUT_EDGES, and a tile's share is its leaving share times what its ancestors leave it, so a tile and its halves always add up to one. Stars listed in both a tile and its parent keep their full brightness through the hand-over; stars in only one fade in or out over the band. This replaces MAP.153's rank birth radius (stage 1 of zoom-star-visibility.md), which assumed one tile level per camera radius. Detail tiles fade by distance alone (whole within 60 pc, gone at 120). The shares are worked out on the page whenever the camera moves (updateStarShares) and handed to the star shader as an attribute.

4.7 Hand-off between scales

Cross-fade, with hysteresis so nothing flickers: a sector's own scene (all stars) fades in when the camera is within 1.5 edges of its center and is full by 1 edge; it unloads past 3 edges. A system's disk fades in at about 20 pixels (section 4.4) and unloads at 10. Blocks fade out through the near fade as the camera enters them. One scene graph throughout (MAP.121's one engine).

As built (MAP.152, client part, 2026-10-15). The wheel carries the zoom across every stage in both directions: zoomed right in on the whole galaxy or a window (MIN_ZOOM of its fit, GALAXY_MIN_ZOOM for the galaxy), the next wheel tick takes the block under the pointer as a click would (the window one size down, or the sector opened in place); zoomed right in on a sector it opens the star at the middle (MAP.125); zoomed right out (MAX_ZOOM of the fit) of a system, a sector or a window it goes up. The existing stage fade and camera flight are the cross-fade; the hysteresis is the width between the two ends (a new stage starts at its fit, a factor of 8 from one end and 2.5 from the other) and mapcontrol.createAcrossGate: one move across a gesture, none within 900 ms of the last, and the next only after the wheel has been quiet for 300 ms, so a long scroll carries across one stage and stops there. The per-tile camera-relative origins are not built (section 4.4: met by MAP.102). Not built then: a sector's scene fading in by the camera's distance from its center without a stage change; that is the next paragraph.

As built (MAP.152, the sector's scene by distance). static/sectorreach.js holds the rule and galaxystageview.js applies it every frame in a window of sectors (not on the sector page's pinned map, not once a sector or system is open): chooseSector picks the charted sector nearest the camera within 1.5 edges (FADE_IN_EDGES), keeps the one already up until the camera is 3 edges away (UNLOAD_EDGES) and lets another take over only when it is under 85% of the distance of the one up. A sector must stay in range for 250 ms (DWELL_MS) before its scene is asked for, so a camera passing through fetches nothing. The scene is galaxysector.js's, opened as ambient: sectorscene.setFade(share) scales every material (a point's alpha, a cloud's peak alpha, a glow's strength, a plain material's opacity), the share is 1 - smoothstep(1, 1.5, distance / edge), the galaxy's own tile stars in that cell stay until the scene is whole and then go (the same stars, so only a moment of the same star twice), the sector's block fades to ENTERED_BLOCK_FADE as its scene comes in, and its stars can be hovered and picked from a third of the way in (PICKABLE_SHARE). It is not "the sector opened": no history entry, no flight, entered stays empty, the panel and the address are the window's, and opened/closed are not called. Opening the sector (a click, or a wheel tick once the scene is whole) takes the scene over with no second fetch; from the wheel it opens where the camera is (enterSector(..., {fly: false}), the camera does not move and the fit is re-derived so the zoom carries on), and "Open system here" on one of its stars does the same and then opens the system. A window of sectors lets the camera come to SECTOR_NEAREST_EDGES (0.5) edges from the sector at its middle: its old floor, an eighth of the window's fit, stopped about 1.4 edges out, short of where the scene is whole.

As built (MAP.152, the system disk). The same shape one scale down, in an opened sector (static/systemreach.js, applied by galaxystageview.js every frame): the system of a star of the sector fades in as its disk comes to LOAD_PX (20) pixels across, is whole at FULL_PX (40) and is dropped below UNLOAD_PX (10), with a 250 ms dwell and a 15% margin to take over from the system up. The disk's size is radius * mapHeightPx / (distance * tan(fov / 2)), with the system's real radius once its scene is known and 50 AU (NOMINAL_RADIUS_PC) before (the real radius of each system seen is kept, so a system smaller than 50 AU, found too small to stay when first loaded, is not fetched again until its own disk is big enough). The scene is galaxysystem.js's, opened as ambient like a sector's (groupfade.js fades it, it cannot be picked, no flight, no history entry, enteredSystem stays empty); one more wheel tick at the nearest zoom opens the system from the scene that is up (no second fetch) and the camera flies to fit it as ever. To make the disk reachable the zoom floor of an opened sector follows the star nearest the middle of the view: the camera may come to where that star's disk is 60 pixels across, but no closer to the middle than keeps the star within NEAREST_STAR_REACH of it, so the middle has to be moved onto a star first (Shift-click or right-click on it); with no star near the middle the floor is the old eighth of the sector's fit. The wheel still zooms about the middle of the view, not the pointer; zooming toward the point under the cursor is MAP.146's free camera. While a body is highlighted in a system fading in, its orbit lines take their highlight opacities over the fade until it is whole.

As built (MAP.243, the halo and the satellite galaxies). The galaxy's outline (the densities that say which sectors can hold stars) no longer decides what the map can open. A window of the map and a sector's address are made of every cell the grid names, out to the designation's last shell with all its bands (REACH_OUTLINE in galaxyprisms.js); the whole-galaxy view still draws the real outline's level-243 blocks, and a block's total still comes from the stored outline. Before, a star in the halo or a satellite galaxy had no block or window under it: the wheel stopped at 6,912 pc and a click read "Block 0·0·0, 0 sectors charted". Now the whole-galaxy view has a halo pick layer: a star or a phenomenon beyond the outline is picked on the screen (HALO_PICK_PX, 10 px), its position and brightness shown; the wheel run in toward it hands off to the window round the cell that holds it (windowInto, not the disk's block behind it), and on level by level (81, 27, 9, 3 cells) to the sector, as for a block. The server's window and stage reads are outline-free. canvasEl.galaxyOutsideOutline and the halo_site fixture (a loose group of stars and a black hole beyond the outline) are for the browser tests.

5. Cost (what I can say without a browser)

  • Shader: near fade, tube and magnitude ramp are a few multiply-adds and two smoothsteps per fragment or vertex; the tube needs three uniforms. Expected negligible next to overdraw. Not measured.
  • Histogram for m_lim: one pass over up to 70,000 stars per frame. Not measured; if it is too slow, run it every few frames or on camera move only.
  • Tiles: section 4.5 (upper bounds, 140 to 280 forward at k = 1, instead of 27).
  • Wire: 52 bytes a star is the GPU buffer, not the download; the wire was 270 B of JSON and is packed binary since MAP.159 (galaxy-map-wire-format.md), and 100,000 stars at 52 bytes is 5.2 MB of buffer, so per-tile budgets must drop as tiles multiply.

6. Cells and regions (MAP.146's original content, kept as the data layer)

From research/drilldown-region-sizes/report.md: frame exactly centered on the click (the ADM.29/30 round region as layer, ring and wrap-aware slot ranges), data from the aligned cells covering it, read from the existing per-block cache, size as cells across. For continuous flight prefer the 3-ary pyramid (1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729): a level switch changes cell size by 3 instead of 9, which a cross-fade hides, and the view can hold 5 to 15 cells across (up to 3,375 prisms) without a gap. Cost: cells are less square (centreline arc 0.84 to 1.25 of an edge against 0.94 to 1.06 at 9-ary). Per-level aggregates (counts, age, luminosity) must exist for every level so colour and density need no scan; with a moving camera the stage query that scans sector rows cannot serve. Ratio 2 was not measured.

Grid opacity (MAP.188)

The blocks of a drill-down stage are translucent (10% to 50%, never more than MAX_FILL_OPACITY). A ray through n cells meets n faces, so unscaled they add up to 1 - (1 - a)^n, which at deep zoom, looking through many cells, is nearly opaque. static/gridopacity.js estimates n for the stage (the longest ray's path inside the stage's box times the cells per unit length, from the median cell edge) and multiplies every block face's and line's alpha by the share that keeps n of the most opaque cell add up to at most GRID_ACCUMULATION_CAP (0.5): min(1, (1 - 0.5^(1/n)) / a_max). The share is a shader uniform set with the camera; one face or a flat slab seen from above is untouched. The lines between slabs are drawn at 0.18 and scaled the same way; the outline of the hovered or selected choice stays at full strength. The map's galaxyGridOpacity() test hook reads the estimate.
