1. Verdict

  1. AD1 for methane can be filled from free data, and the data downloaded. Two independent routes, both READ and consistent with each other:
    • The K&T 2010 band model itself, as k-tables by Patrick Irwin (Oxford), 14 May 2010: 3689 wavelengths, 400 nm to 5.0 micron, κ at 100, 198 and 296 K plus the line-spacing ratio, and correlated-k values on a 10 pressure by 11 temperature grid (1e-8 to 10 atm, 50 to 300 K). The count matches the paper's own abstract ("for each of ~3700 wavelengths, we present a temperature dependence").
    • [R2258] and 1998 as NASA PDS tables (300 to 1050 nm, one effective cold methane spectrum, plus full-disk albedos of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Titan).
  2. The note's catalogue id is wrong. The Karkoschka data is PDS volume gbat_0001, not go_0016 (go_0016 to go_0022 are Galileo orbiter images). It holds both the 1993 data (1994 paper) and the 1995 data (1998 paper); the NMSU web pages cite only the 1994 paper.
  3. Not covered by any free source found: ammonia and water coefficients in the visible. K&T 2010 is methane only. [R2259] gives ammonia only as band depths in Jupiter's albedo (equivalent widths 0.05, 0.32, 0.62, 0.81 nm for the 552, 645, 790 and 930 nm bands, read from Fig. 5); no coefficient table. Water: the 1994 suggestion of a 930 nm water band was withdrawn in 1998 (the band is probably ammonia). AD1 stays partly open for NH3 and H2O.
  4. Gaps in the methane data: 5.0 to 5.5 micron (the paper says its data reach 5.5; the Oxford tables stop at 2000 cm-1 = 5.0 micron); below 450 nm every coefficient sits at a 1e-5 floor in the tables (65 wavenumbers), so the true weak absorption there is not resolved; the Oxford page says its K&T tables are "not yet fully verified"; [R2099] reports K&T 2010 models 1.4 to 1.8 micron less well than the WKMC-80K line list (outside the visible).
  5. Several of the note's details are wrong (section 4): Neptune paper citation, the eleventh data set, PMC5105178 is not a different paper, one pressure level in the filter table, the Uranus methane range.

Columns: vacuum wavelength (nm), air wavelength (nm), methane absorption coefficient (1/(km-amagat)), then full-disk albedos (Jupiter at 9.8 deg phase in 1993 and 6.8 deg in 1995; Saturn; Uranus and Neptune geometric; Titan). Methane k is zero below 405.6 nm (1993) and 401.2 nm (1995 low), and reaches 44 (1993), 47 (1995 low) and 51.6 (1995 high) 1/km-am at the strongest bands. It is one effective cold-temperature spectrum ("the corresponding temperature may be near 100 K", 1994 paper), so no pressure or temperature dependence.

3.2 Oxford K&T 2010 k-tables (READ)

Header: "k-table for [R2265] band model, Patrick Irwin, AOPP, University of Oxford 14th May 2010". Files ch4_karkoschka_vis.par (19300 to 25000 cm-1, 400 to 518 nm, 25 cm-1 step, 50 cm-1 FWHM, 229 wavenumbers) and ch4_karkoschka_IR.par (2000 to 19295 cm-1, 518 nm to 5.0 micron, 5 cm-1 step, 10 cm-1 FWHM, 3460 wavenumbers). Together 3689 wavelengths. Units "(km-amagat)^-1".

  • Grid: 10 g-ordinates; pressures 1e-8, 1e-7 ... 10 atm (10 values); temperatures 50 to 300 K in 25 K steps (11 values); each (wavenumber, temperature) block carries the band-model line and 10 pressure rows of 10 k values plus the transmission fitting error.
  • Band-model line: wavenumber, wavelength (nm), κ(100 K), κ(198 K), κ(296 K), δ/α_D0. This is exactly the input the K&T Voigt-Goody model needs (open-access equations 1 to 5, READ; NEMESIS tkark.f implements it, READ). Sample (copied data, extracted CSV; wavenumber cm-1, nm, κ100, κ198, κ296, δ/α_D0):
19300.00, 517.99, 0.90E-03, 0.19E-02, 0.41E-02, 8.0
16100.00, 620.95, 0.561, 0.532, 0.505, 8.0
 2000.00, 4998.64, 0.0476, 0.0471, 0.0383, 17741

(ch4_karkoschka_tomasko2010_band_parameters.csv has all 3689 rows.)

  • Strength across the range: median κ(100 K) 2.2e-2 (500 to 700 nm), 0.54 (700 to 1000 nm), 7.5 (1.0 to 1.5 micron), 34 (1.5 to 2.5), 7.4 (2.5 to 5.0); largest 2.25e5 1/km-am near 3.27 micron.
  • Cross-check against the PDS tables: κ(100 K) against the 1995 low-resolution PDS k at the same wavelengths, where both exceed 0.001: correlation of the logarithms 0.97 (500 to 700 nm), 0.995 (700 to 900 nm), 0.998 (900 to 1050 nm); median ratio within 0.04 dex (about 10 percent) in all three ranges. At the band centres: 619 nm 0.719 against 0.720, 727 nm 4.75 against 3.93, 790 nm 0.569 against 0.571, 890 nm 20.3 against 24.3. So the Oxford tables and [R2259] agree (the 2010 abstract says its coefficients below 1000 nm are "generally close to [R2259]"), which supports reading the Oxford files as the real K&T 2010 parameters; this is a consistency test, not proof of provenance.

Whether the Oxford tables are the paper's own supplementary values: the paper's abstract speaks of about 3700 wavelengths and [R0852] thank Karkoschka and Tomasko for "machine-readable tabulations ... in the Supplementary Materials" of K&T 2010 (READ, PMC6157968); the Oxford file has 3689 wavelengths with three-digit values. The supplement itself is on ScienceDirect and was not reached.

3.3 Other free items found

  • NEMESIS repository (cloned with git): raddata/karkoschka.dat = the 1993 PDS methane column to 1000 nm (identical except the last row), extended to 1060 nm; tkark.f = K&T transmission; no K&T 2010 k-table inside (README: tables "under embargo ... available on a common server at some point"). NEMESIS README also says the visible ammonia bands (552, 645 nm) were originally modelled from [R2777] and that this is "obsolete by newer ammonia line data" (those line data embargoed); Lutz and Owen was not looked up.
  • Oxford ammonia tables on the same page: [R2095] (JQSRT 62, 193), 4000 to 11000 cm-1 (0.91 to 2.5 micron), self-broadened; and two tables at 25025 to 34975 cm-1 (286 to 400 nm). Neither reaches the 552, 645, 790 nm bands. Not downloaded.
  • [R4109, R4110] ([R4110], READ abstract): 6195 methane features in Titan ESPRESSO spectra (R about 190000), detection limit about 0.02 km-am^-1, comparison with experimental data at similar temperature; and [R3752] [R3752]: an empirical high-resolution optical methane line list (RRS-2026) from the same Titan data. Not needed for band strengths; useful as an independent check at 380 to 790 nm.
  • [R4186] (Icarus 215, 292; [R4186], READ): independent Uranus methane retrieval, see section 4.

5. What this means for AD1 (for the TODO thread and the lanes)

  • Methane (CH4): the data exists in two free forms. For the atmosphere-colour work (reflected visible light of a giant, 0.4 to 1.0 micron), κ(ν, T) from the three-temperature band parameters with the Voigt-Goody formula ([R0855] eq. 1 to 5) gives absorption as a function of path, pressure, temperature and methane fraction, replacing the "neighbouring band 10 percent" stand-in; the PDS single-spectrum table (gbat_0001) is the simpler form and a test value. Source rules apply: where Oxford and PDS differ (890 nm band, up to 20 percent; weak bands below 700 nm, up to about 0.15 dex) both are verified, K&T 2010 is the newer, so it wins, and the PDS spread is the width for the draw.
  • Build-time download (Boss's decision of 2026-10-17): both hosts answered here (atmos.nmsu.edu, users.ox.ac.uk); no licence text on either page, the PDS volume is public. The Oxford IR table is 19 MB compressed (68 MB text); the band-parameter CSV is 0.24 MB and holds everything the Voigt-Goody route needs. The Oxford page says "not yet fully verified" for these tables, and its host is a personal academic page (a stability risk): a build-time fetch should fall back to the labelled stand-in. For offline tests a few rows of the CSV are enough.
  • Ammonia and water: AD1 remains open for the visible NH3 bands (552, 645, 790, 930, 1030 nm) and H2O; the only free numbers are the four equivalent widths above (read from a figure, labelled). The [R2095] NH3 tables start at 910 nm (partly overlapping the 930 and 1030 nm bands, a lead, not read). The stand-in for ammonia stays.
  • Not usable under NO FIXED CONTENT: fitting a given planet's measured albedo (the PDS albedo columns are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune and Titan data). The methane coefficients are physical data (laboratory plus Huygens), the albedo columns are not to be copied into the generator.
  • Citation fixes for the plan files: the AD1 source is [R2265], Icarus 205(2), 674 to 694; the Neptune paper is Icarus 211(1), 780 to 797 (2011), [R2266]; the Uranus paper is Icarus 202(1), 287 to 309 (2009); the dataset is PDS gbat_0001, not go_0016; the filter-table source is [R3900] (Nature Communications 7, 13262).
  • Free routes found for other gaps: the [R0855] text (CC BY-NC) prints the full Voigt-Goody temperature interpolation; [R4109, R4110] and [R3752] give an empirical Titan methane line list at 380 to 790 nm; eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov and the Europe PMC REST host both return full text of open-access papers.

6. Not done

  • K&T 2010 supplement itself (ScienceDirect refused) and [R4753] (MDPI refused) were not read; the Oxford file is the nearest copy found.
  • Raw STIS cubes at MAST, PSG data, and [R2264] were not looked up.
  • The Oxford tables were cross-checked only against the PDS tables, not against laboratory data or Huygens.
