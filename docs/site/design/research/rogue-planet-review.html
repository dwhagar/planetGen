Planets 1 lane, 10-30. Boss (4:54 PM EDT 10-30): "physics is the driver for rogue planets". This reviews every quantity the rogue-planet generator uses, says whether each is physics, a sourced stand-in or a flat rate, names the physical replacement, and checks how many bodies the planet architecture actually ejects against microlensing. It is the R1 item of interstellar-asteroid-fields.md section 13; R2 (GEN.1111) builds the ejection-made rogues and R3 (GEN.1112) replaces the defaults. Nothing in the generator changes in this report.

Source marks: [read] a paper whose text was opened for this report or for GEN.1089 and the oddity lanes; [R] recalled, to be confirmed by the replacement item before it is relied on.

Refresh of 2026-11-07 (main with train 13): sections 1 to 7 are the review as written on 10-30 and stay as the record of what the generator did then. Section 8 gives the state of every row now (all sixteen are built, deleted or turned into a named stand-in) and which of RP1 to RP9 are done. The flat rate, the hand-cut mass bins, the uniform age, the speed with no kick, the envelope, water and heat defaults and the cluster gap are gone; what is left is the top-up dial and its stand-ins.

Update (GEN.1286, 2026-11-05): rows 5, 6, 8, 9 and 12 below describe the old defaults. Those constants (ROGUE_PLANET_GAS_GIANT_MASS_THRESHOLD_JUPITER, ROGUE_HYDROGEN_ENVELOPE_CHANCE, ROGUE_WATER_RICH_CHANCE, ROGUE_FROZEN_ATMOSPHERE_MIN_MASS_EARTH, ROGUE_ACTIVE_HEAT_FLUX_W_M2) and the unread modules rogue_volatiles and rogue_radiogenic are deleted: a rogue is the planet chain against a starless host (starless_host), which gives the giant class from the radius, the water from the formation distance against the snow line, the radiogenic content from the donor's composition and the air from the body's own conditions. One effect is not modelled: the envelope a planet loses to its star's XUV in the time before the ejection (the host has no luminosity).

Result in one paragraph

The rogue-planet count is a flat rate (5.8 per star, ROGUE_PLANET_RATE_PER_STAR), not an output of any system. The architecture removes about 0.3 bodies per star that could count as ejected (about 0.22 thrown by a crowded giant plus about 0.08 swept by a hot Jupiter, together roughly 9 Earth masses per star, nearly all of it gas giants), against a flat rate that gives about 2.7 per star over the range microlensing probes (0.33 to 6,660 Earth masses) and a microlensing central value of 21 per star over that range. So the architecture alone is about nine times short of our own flat rate and about seventy times short of the survey central value over all stars. Section 6 (GEN.1119) refines this: the ejection step is on scale (about 2.5 planets thrown out per giant-hosting star, the figure [R0258] [read] give for stars with a Jupiter and a Saturn analogue), but only 5 to 20 percent of stars host a giant, and the architecture deletes its 125 sub-minimum bodies per star and merges its rocky pairs (losing 10 percent of mass each time) rather than scattering them. Most of Sumi's population is therefore not scattered planets, as [R4533] and Ma et al. 2016 also find. The physical build is a scatter step (GEN.1089) that classes each loss as ejection or collision by the Safronov number (done, GEN.1119), cluster unbinding (GEN.291) and embryo ejection while giants grow; the flat rate then becomes a test target.

1. What the architecture ejects today (measured)

Method: 1,500 stars drawn from a Kroupa-like mass function (0.1 to 1.5 solar masses), [Fe/H] normal(-0.1, 0.2), snow line 2.7 M^2 AU, disc edge 200 AU, planet_architecture.build, one fixed seed per star; the three loss steps wrapped to count what they remove (throwaway script, not committed; numbers will move with later architecture epochs).

Quantity per star Value
Oligarch chain bodies before thinning 134.7 (26.4 Earth masses of cores)
After sculpt merges 130.3 (24.1 Earth masses); loss 2.3 Earth masses, the 10 percent per merge of COLLISION_MASS_RETENTION
Dropped as below PLANET_MIN_MASS_EARTH (0.1 inner, 0.6016 outer) about 125 bodies; not tallied as mass or as ejected
Planets kept 5.0 (60.8 Earth masses including gas)
Thrown out by _scatter_crowded_giants (the lighter of a crowded giant pair) 0.22 bodies, 6.2 Earth masses
Swept by _clear_around_giants (inside a hot Jupiter or within the clearing spacing of a moved giant) about 0.08 bodies, about 3.3 Earth masses (400-star pass)
Giants per star 0.045 (0.1 to 0.3 solar masses), 0.11 (0.3 to 0.7), 0.16 (0.7 to 1.5)

Reading: the architecture does have the right mechanism (giants scatter and sweep) but it only fires on the 5 to 16 percent of stars with a giant, it never records speed or fate, and a lost body is deleted, not ejected, bound to a star, or collided. It has no Safronov-number test: Theta = (M_p / M_star)(a / R_p) is about 0.07 for an Earth at 1 AU and about 10 for Jupiter at 5.2 AU, so a scatterer ejects only above Theta of about 1; below that, scattering circulates the orbit or ends in collision. That test is the physics that splits "ejected" from "merged".

4. Small findings in code

  • ROGUE_PLANET_RATE_PER_STAR is 5.8 but the comment on PHENOMENON_DENSITY_PC3 (tuning.py near line 3568) and the block comment above it both say 6.5 and the terrestrial share 5.6. The bins sum to 5.8 and give 4.98 terrestrial. The R3 pass fixes the wording when it replaces the rate.
  • The terrestrial bin holds 86 percent of rogues by count, so the rate and the lower edge (rows 1 and 4) decide almost everything; a physical replacement of row 4 moves the total the most.

6. Result of GEN.1119 (RP1): the losses classed

planet_architecture.losses and ejection_yield (with scatter.loss_fates) now class every loss of the build as ejected, collided or bound by the Safronov number the perturber has at the body's distance (ejection to collision as Theta squared, registry rows EJ1 and EJ2, section 6.127 of the gap filling plan). Nothing is dropped: planets and losses add up to the whole chain (tested). The same 3,000 sun-like stars that gave section 1's counts, measured with the classifier:

Quantity Value
Ejected planet-mass bodies (0.1 Earth masses or more) per star, all stars (Kroupa-like mass mix) about 0.15, 6.4 Earth masses; all of it giants thrown out by a heavier crowding giant
The same per giant-hosting sun-like star ([Fe/H] +0.2, 639 of 3,000) about 2.5 planets, 165 Earth masses, of which about 1.8 are lighter than 20 Earth masses
Chain bodies below the planet minimum (about 125 per star) none ejected: no planet reaches them, they stay bound as one record per star
Merges and core cap collided, their lost mass kept as debris (about 2.3 Earth masses per star)
Hot-Jupiter sweeps about 0.002 bodies per star, and with Theta below 0.1 they collide, not leave

What this changes in the section 1 reading. [R0258]'s 2.5 terrestrial-mass bodies per star assumes every star has a Jupiter and a Saturn analogue. Our architecture ejects about 2.5 planets per star that has a giant, which is the same scale, so the mechanism is right. Over all stars the yield is the giant-hosting share (5 to 20 percent) of that: about 0.15 to 0.5 per star. So the per-star shortfall against Sumi's 21 per star is not a defect of the ejection step; it says the free-floating population is not mostly scattered planets, in line with [R4533] and Ma et al. 2016. RP9's test target is therefore two numbers: about 2.5 per giant-hosting star (Barclay) and a total that matches the microlensing range once the other sources (embryo ejection while giants grow, cluster unbinding RP7, formation by collapse) are added. The time_yr of a loss is the disc era (DISC_LIFETIME_YR, 3 Myr) for these giant-driven losses; later instability delays are rogue_age's (RG1, RG2). The ejection record carries the body's Hill radius at the encounter for the moon survival test (RP4).

What is still a dial or open. The Rogues-level ceilings (RT1) and the top-up's placement share (RT2) are Boss's dials and carry no physics. The microlensing yield (21 per star) is not reproduced by scattering alone and is not expected to be ([R4533], Ma et al. 2016); the remainder is left to the top-up, which labels its rogues "apparent". The top-up's age (uniform) and lowest mass (0.1 Earth masses) are stand-ins until a donor field supplies them.

Small findings of section 4. The 6.5 against 5.8 wording went with the flat rate (ROGUE_PLANET_RATE_PER_STAR no longer exists). PHENOMENON_DENSITY_PC3 stays for the brown-dwarf and other phenomena rates, not for planets.
