The heavy-element mass of giant planets (GEN.460 input, read this session)

docs/design/composition/giant-heavy-element-law.md

Research note, no behaviour change. GEN.460's text says "Chachan's coefficients are not yet in the item and must be read first". They are now read. Source states: READ is a number found in the paper itself.

1. [R0710] ([R0710], 30 Sep 2025), READ

Sample: 147 warm giants (equilibrium temperature under 1000 K) with measured masses and radii, from 20 Earth masses to 20 Jupiter masses, from the Exoplanet Archive; thermal evolution models with updated hydrogen-helium equations of state and an atmospheric boundary condition that varies with the envelope metallicity. More than three times the sample of Thorngren et al. 2016.

Quantity Value Notes
Law M_Z = M_core + f_Z (M_p - M_core) (their equation 6) M_Z is the heavy-element mass (everything but hydrogen and helium), bulk
M_core 14.7 (+1.8 -1.6) Earth masses set mainly by the lower-mass planets, where M_Z is about M_core; 15.9 +- 1.8 without 12 low-mass hot planets (Teq over 900 K, up to 0.3 Jupiter masses)
f_Z 0.09 +- 0.01 the heavy-element share of the gas accreted beyond M_core; about 7 times the solar 0.014
Astrophysical scatter sigma_mult = 0.66 +- 0.08, a fraction of the predicted M_Z (0.67 +0.08 -0.07 when normalised by the stellar metallicity) applied as a spread proportional to M_Z; the paper does not give the distribution's shape beyond "scatter as a fraction of the model value"
Below M_core the model assumes Z_p = 1 up to M_core, so M_Z = M_p there (the straight formula would exceed the mass) stated in the paper's text under equation 6
Bulk metallicity Z_p = M_Z / M_p; declines linearly with M_p for M_p << 150 Earth masses and flattens at f_Z
Stellar normalisation Z_p / Z_star = a / M_p + b, a = 2.6 +- 0.3 (M_p in Jupiter masses), b = 3.3 +- 0.5 enrichment of 29.3, 5.9 and 3.6 for 0.1, 1 and 10 Jupiter masses
Against a power law the model beats M_Z = alpha M_p^beta with a Bayes factor of 921 so [R4352]'s form is the older, poorer fit
Classical core accretion Z_p = 1 at 10 and 0.5 at 20 Earth masses is inconsistent with the population; Z_p reaches 0.5 at about 32 +- 3 Earth masses

Not read in the paper (and so not used): the fit to individual planets, the hot-planet sample (inflation changes the inferred M_Z), planets above 20 Jupiter masses.

2. Against [R4352] ([R4352], read in an earlier lane, part C of the body composition research)

M_Z = 57.9 (M / M_J)^0.61 Earth masses, scatter 0.26 dex (factor 1.82), 47 giants from 20 Earth masses to 20 Jupiter masses. At 1 Jupiter mass: Thorngren 57.9, Chachan 14.7 + 0.09 (317.8 - 14.7) = 42.0 Earth masses (COMPUTED); at 0.1 Jupiter mass (31.8 Earth masses): Thorngren 14.2, Chachan 16.2; at 10 Jupiter masses (3178): Thorngren 236, Chachan 299. The ratio of 1.4 at Jupiter's mass agrees with the open-gaps report's "1.4 to 1.6 times lower between 0.3 and 3 Jupiter masses". Under the source rule (two verified fits that disagree: the newest wins), [R0710] is the default and [R4352] stays selectable and the other end of the bracket.

3. How the heavy-element mass reaches the interior draw (GEN.642)

physics/giant_interior.py takes heavy_mass_earth as an input. GEN.460 draws it: M_Z = min(M, M_core + f_Z (M - M_core)) times a draw with the paper's fractional spread, kept inside 0 < M_Z < M and above the envelope's own metallicity floor (by rejection, a stand-in until the distribution's shape is read from the paper's likelihood). For the envelope the atmosphere's Z is f Z_p with f about 0.2 for Jupiter and Saturn ([R4350], read in the Atmosphere lane's notes); GiantSubject.envelope_z carries it.
