Convention: "PARAMETRISATION" means the shape or number is my proposal to make the generator run, not a measured law. It must be drawn from a stated distribution and replaced if a better published distribution is found. Solar System bodies appear only in section 4 (test targets) and in "calibration source" columns, never as presets.

1. Scope and interfaces

Covers

  • Asteroids: taxonomy versus meteorite analogue, grain density, bulk density, porosity, albedo, distribution of types with distance, chondrite chemistry, radiogenic heating (long-lived and Al-26).
  • Comets, Kuiper belt objects (KBOs), Centaurs, Oort cloud bodies, interstellar objects, dwarf planets: refractory-to-ice ratio, ices, porosity, density-size trend.
  • Moons of every origin (circumplanetary disk, impact, capture, ring, binary dwarf-planet), including how moon composition relates to the parent mass and to orbital distance (ice line).
  • Fill of the GEN.425 gap: low-pressure ice and silicate densities, and small-body porosity with a compression regime (hand-off point to the equation-of-state agents).
  • Generator routes 1 and 2 with formulas, distributions and the exact fields to store.

Takes from other parts

  • Star: luminosity L (for snow line), [Fe/H] (already stored, schema v95), age. Snow line snow_line_au already exists in physics/formation.py.
  • Agents B and C (rocky planet and volatile-rich planet bulk composition, equation of state): zero-pressure ice phase densities at pressure, EOS for large icy moons. My mixing rule stops where self-compression matters (section 3.2, failure modes).
  • Orbit: semi-major axis, parent mass and radius, eccentricity, parent equilibrium temperature.

Hands over

  • Per body: ice, rock and metal mass fractions, grain density, porosity, bulk density, rock-family analogue, radiogenic heat (long-lived and Al-26), mean albedo, origin type.
  • To the atmosphere lane (interface only): gravity, surface temperature, outgassed species list, volatile inventory (H2O, CO2, CO, CH4, NH3, N2, H2S, SO2 as mass fractions of the body, or percent relative to water for comets).
  • To the radiation lane (ground radiation later): ground elemental composition and radiogenic K, Th, U ppm.

Interpretation for a random system: the sequence is an ordering by temperature at formation (dry, high-albedo, igneous inside; hydrated C in the middle; anhydrous ice-rich P and D beyond). The Solar System positions are not portable. The portable variable is x = a / a_snow with the snow-line scaling below. Scaling is a parametrisation; nothing in the sources tests it for other stars.

Snow line scaling already in the code: snow_line_au = 2.7 AU * sqrt(L/Lsun) (SNOW_LINE_AU_AT_1_LSUN = 2.7, the 170 K blackbody value per the coordinator). Sibling formula: a_snow = (278.3 / T_ice)^2 sqrt(L/Lsun) AU (COMPUTED check: T_ice 170 K gives 2.68 AU). Real scatter is about 0.2 dex ([R3193, R3196], quoted by the coordinator; not re-verified here, RECALLED). In this part the scatter becomes a per-system multiplicative draw: a_snow,sys = a_snow x 10^N(0, 0.2). Because the scatter is larger than the zone widths, the type mix should be sampled from smooth weights in x, not from sharp edges.

Failure modes of the circumplanetary-disk law:

  • r_ice in the formula does not scale with the planet radius; it was fitted to Sasaki's Jupiter-like disk. Applying it to a planet far from 1 M_J is an extrapolation (section 5).
  • Moons migrate. Present distance is not formation distance. Sasaki et al. find inner moons are rocky and outer moons icy at the end of formation (READ), not that composition equals the local present distance.
  • Saturn's small inner moons are icy although they sit well inside any disk ice line. This is the ring-origin [R0920] case: abstract-level reading says spreading of a planetary ring beyond the Roche radius forms a retinue of satellites with masses increasing with distance, which fits Saturn, Uranus and Neptune; with fast spreading a single large satellite forms, as for Pluto and Earth. READ for the abstract; the PDF text is garbled, so the composition details are NOT REACHED.

3. How the generator should compute or draw it

3.1 Choice of route

Route 1 (keep the drawn density, fill a composition) is recommended for planets and any moon already drawn by the existing pipeline, because the density is a stored quantity and changing it would alter every seed. Route 2 (draw composition first, derive density) is recommended for asteroid belts, asteroid fields, comets, KBOs and for moons generated from now on, because those objects have a known formation location (x = a / a_snow, or r / r_ice) and the science gives composition as a function of location, not density. Both routes share one mixing and porosity module so the same fields come out.

3.2 Shared mixing rule

Inputs per body: mass fractions f_ice, f_rock, f_metal (sum 1), phase grain densities rho_ice, rho_rock, rho_metal (table 2.1 and 2.2).

  1. Grain density (mass-weighted harmonic mean, the "small-body mixing rule" of the GEN.425 report): rho_g = 1 / (f_ice/rho_ice + f_rock/rho_rock + f_metal/rho_metal).
  2. Total porosity phi from the compaction law (2.10): phi = 1 - (1 - phi_micro)(1 - phi_macro); phi_macro(R, rho_g, phi0, P0) from the shell integration, or from a pre-built table indexed by radius.
  3. Bulk density: rho_bulk = rho_g (1 - phi).
  4. Compression regime: valid while the central pressure is below about 1e8 Pa for rock and 1e7 Pa for ice-rich bodies (grains crush or ices flow above this; the numbers are my estimates, RECALLED). Above it, call the EOS of agents B and C. In practice that means bodies above about 1000 km radius (rock) or 500 km radius (ice-rich). The simple rule also fails for differentiated interiors: Ganymede's ice fraction is under-predicted by 10-15 points (2.11).
  5. Never use a bulk density below the grain density of ice at the porosity ceiling: phi at most 0.8 (comets, pristine) and 0.6 (non-comet).

3.3 Route 1: keep the drawn density, fill the composition

Given the drawn rho_bulk and radius R:

  1. Draw phi0 and P0 (distribution below), compute phi_macro(R). If the body is comet-like (nucleus under 20 km), use phi from the comet distribution (3.5).
  2. Grain density rho_g = rho_bulk / (1 - phi).
  3. Pick the rock analogue (family) from x = a/a_snow (or r/r_ice for moons) via the zone-weight table (3.4); its dry grain density rho_d ([R0544] grain) and metal content give the rock+metal block.
  4. Ice fraction (two-component solve): f_ice = (1/rho_d - 1/rho_g) / (1/rho_d - 1/rho_ice), clamp to [0, f_ice,max]. Here f_ice,max = 0.6 (PARAMETRISATION from section 2.7: Pluto-like rock fraction 0.7 with the tail up to 0.4 ice for ice-rich bodies).
  5. If rho_g exceeds rho_d the body is denser than its rock analogue: set f_ice = 0 and raise the metal fraction: f_metal = (1/rho_r - 1/rho_g) / (1/rho_r - 1/rho_metal) (rho_r = rock block excluding metal), clamp to the family range.
  6. If rho_g is below rho_ice: infeasible; raise phi until rho_g = rho_ice and warn (invariant test).
  7. For class D the existing 3.93-5.51 g/cm3 draw gives rock only. Replace it with draws from section 3.3.1.

3.3.1 Class D density replacement (PARAMETRISATION, based on 2.7): draw rock mass fraction f_rock ~ truncated normal(0.70, 0.08) on [0.45, 0.95], f_ice = 1 - f_rock - f_metal (f_metal small, 0 for D), compute rho_g from the mixing rule, and apply the compaction law. Check against the KBO and moon tables in section 4.

Because the composition then follows from density, Route 1 can only reproduce densities that lie between the analogue grain density and the ice grain density. That is its limit.

3.4 Route 2: draw composition first

3.4.1 Asteroid belts and fields

  1. Per system: draw snow-line scatter s ~ N(0, 0.2) in log10 and set a_snow,sys = a_snow 10^s.
  2. For each belt or field (distance a, width), compute x = a / a_snow,sys.
  3. Taxon weights: smooth functions of x calibrated to the DeMeo-Carry Table 6 kernel (2.4) (PARAMETRISATION): for x well below the Solar-System-calibrated inner boundary, mostly igneous and ordinary classes (S, V, E, K, L, A); for x around the middle, C and B; beyond, P and D. The kernel gives the sequence, not the edges.
  4. Draw the taxonomic mass fractions as a Dirichlet sample centered on the kernel weights (PARAMETRISATION, concentration parameter as an adjustable input).
  5. For each taxon, draw rho_bulk from the [R0666: Table 3] mean and scatter (normal, truncated), the analogue group, and Britt grain density.
  6. Albedo per taxon: DeMeo-Carry mean (Table 4) times a log-normal scatter (sigma in log10 to be fitted; none published in what I read).
  7. Aggregate: mean albedo, mean bulk density, mean porosity, ice fraction, metal fraction (mass-weighted).
  8. Existing families (carbonaceous, stony, metallic, icy, basaltic, mixed, dust, collisional) map to taxon bundles: carbonaceous = C+B(+P), stony = S+Q+L, metallic = M+X, icy = P+D, basaltic = V, mixed = Dirichlet over all, dust = no fixed taxon (sub-mm grains; use CI/CM chemistry at porosity 0), collisional = fragments of the dominant taxon. This is a re-labelling, not a new draw. Keep existing family frequencies as the prior.

3.4.2 Comets and KBOs

  1. Draw delta_RI (refractory-to-ice mass ratio) log-uniform on [0.5, 8] (PARAMETRISATION from 2.6: COSIMA 0.2-3 to Fulle 7.5).
  2. f_ice = 1 / (1 + delta_RI); the refractory share splits into silicate (about 55 %), organics (about 45 %, COSIMA via Choukroun) of the dust.
  3. Volatiles by species as percent of H2O from [R3835] / Harrington [R3535]: CO and CO2 log-normal with medians 3.3 % and 12 %, ranges 0.3-26 % and 2-30 %; the other species as lognormal around the 67P table with sigma 0.5 dex (PARAMETRISATION). CO/H2O formation-temperature dependence is not sourced here (section 5).
  4. Porosity: comet nuclei phi ~ U(0.5, 0.8) (Choukroun 65-79 %); KBOs under 100 km phi ~ U(0.3, 0.6); apply the compaction law for larger bodies.
  5. rho_bulk from the mixing rule.
  6. Interstellar objects: same draw, but the CO/H2O ratio is drawn from a wider log-uniform on [0.01, 1.6] (Borisov exceeded Solar-System comets by a factor of 15-40 in CO/H2O; one object only, so the width is a PARAMETRISATION).
  7. Dwarf planets: rock fraction ~ N(0.70, 0.08) (Bierson and Nimmo) plus a collisional tail toward 0.9 (Barr and Schwamb: Haumea, Eris), densities from the mixing rule plus EOS above about 500 km radius.

3.4.3 Moons from a circumplanetary disk

  1. Parent mass M_p and age tau_G (disk lifetime, draw log-uniform 1-10 Myr, PARAMETRISATION; Sasaki used 5 Myr as the reference).
  2. Total satellite mass: draw alpha/f log-uniform on [1e-6, 5e-4] and M_T/M_p = 2.5e-4 (alpha/f / 3e-5)^(1/3) (Canup and Ward Eq. 4.15). Range 0.8e-4 to 6.4e-4.
  3. Number of large moons N and partition of M_T: Sasaki et al. find four large moons for Jovian-type disks and one dominant icy moon for Saturn-type disks. The mass partition among bodies is not published as a distribution I could read; use Sasaki Table 1 as a calibration for the shape only. Draw N in 1-5 and a Dirichlet partition (PARAMETRISATION).
  4. Ice line: r_ice = 20 R_J (M_p/M_J)^(2/3) (tau_G/5 Myr)^(-1/3) in absolute kilometres (1.43e6 km at M_p = M_J, 5 Myr). Compare with the formation distance, not the present distance.
  5. Ice fraction as a log-linear ramp: f_ice = f_inf * clamp(ln(r/(0.4 r_ice)) / ln(2.5), 0, 1) with f_inf ~ truncated normal(0.35, 0.10) on [0.1, 0.6]. Parameters 0.4 and f_inf are a PARAMETRISATION. COMPUTED check against the Galilean distances at r_ice = 20 R_J: Io 0, Europa 0.062, Ganymede 0.24, Callisto 0.35 (ordering correct, Ganymede and Callisto under the interior-model values, consistent with the simple-mixing failure mode in 2.11).
  6. Rocky block of each moon: anhydrous inside the ice line; between 0.4 and 1.0 of r_ice partly hydrated (serpentine, antigorite density 2.585); outside, C-like or CI-like rock. PARAMETRISATION; Europa's 8 % water/ice (2ND-HAND) supports a partial transition.
  7. Metal fraction by family (H about 17 wt %, L about 8, LL about 3: RECALLED) or from Lodders Fe minus oxidized Fe.
  8. Density from the mixing rule + compression (EOS above the regime limit).
  9. Inner moons of a ring-origin system: ice fraction near the ring composition (ice-rich); selected when the parent has a massive ring and the moon forms near the Roche radius. Rules not sourced (section 5).

3.4.4 Impact-formed moons (terrestrial parents)

  • Drawn when the parent is a terrestrial planet and the moon is generated by a giant-impact event. Moon/planet mass ratio is an input of the impact generator, not of this part (Earth-Moon 0.0123, a calibration only, RECALLED).
  • Composition from [R2724]: iron-poor (metal share a few wt %, the rest bound as FeO), depleted in moderately volatile elements, near-zero water (1-1000 ppm). Metal fraction ~ U(0.01, 0.05) (PARAMETRISATION, from the "few wt %"). Ice fraction 0.
  • Parent mantle composition is the source composition (agent B hand-off).

3.4.5 Captured moons

  • Drawn when the moon is flagged captured (retrograde or high inclination). Composition from the KBO rule (3.4.2), then ice lost by a factor drawn U(0, 0.5) (PARAMETRISATION from Barr and Schwamb, qualitative).

3.7 Consistency checks (invariants a test can assert)

  • sum of mass fractions = 1 within 1e-9.
  • rho_bulk reproduced from the stored fractions, grain densities and porosity within 1e-6 relative.
  • rho_g between min and max component grain densities.
  • f_ice = 0 where r < 0.4 r_ice (disk moons); monotonic non-decreasing in r.
  • phi decreases as mass increases for a fixed family (toy-model table 2.10).
  • Class D bulk density below 3000 kg/m3 for f_rock below 0.9.
  • Al-26 heating exceeds long-lived heating by a factor above 1000 at formation.
  • bodies above the compression-regime limit must carry an EOS-sourced density flag.

3.8 Agreement with the GEN.425 report

I read /mnt/project-files/reports/bulk-composition-sources-check-2026-10-17.md.

  • Agree: the generator's uniform per-class density draw does not follow composition, and class D draws a rocky density; ice and silicate zero-pressure densities (the gap) are filled in 2.1; the small-body mixing rule rho = 1 / sum(w_i/rho0_i) is kept (3.2) with a porosity term and compression limit added; the snow-line constant of 2.7 AU is a blackbody value and has about 0.2 dex real scatter (3.4.1).
  • Differ or extend: the rule is a zero-pressure mixing rule; it must not be used for bodies of Ganymede size (2.11 shows a 10-15 point under-prediction); porosity (about 10 % for meteorites, 65-79 % for comets) changes bulk density by more than the compositional difference between neighbouring classes.
  • [R2865] (30-45 % ice) is taken from that report and not re-verified here; my own COMPUTED rock fractions (2.7) give 0.58-0.96 rock, i.e. 4-42 % ice.
