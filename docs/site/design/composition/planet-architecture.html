The model (planet_architecture.build)

  1. Disk scatter. The disk's solids scatter 0.7 dex [R0114].
  2. Inner system. Whether the star has one: a Bernoulli draw at the share anchors (0.85 at 0.15 Msun, 0.75 at 0.6, 0.30 at 1.0 and 1.4, log-linear in mass; [R4812, R4898]). It starts with 1 + Poisson oligarchs (the mean itself log-normal), a mass scale of its own (log-normal about the median, INNER_SYSTEM_SCALE_SIGMA_DEX, 0.4 dex, GEN.1335: with no spread every Sun-like system started at the same mass and the Earth-size planets the surveys count were missing), neighbours alike in mass about that scale (peas in a pod), the first at a period near 5 days [R3193, R3196], each next at a spacing in mutual Hill radii.
  3. Outer chain. From 1.3 times 400 days outward, at the isolation mass of the feeding zone with a steep taper beyond PLANET_OUTER_GROWTH_AU.
  4. Sculpting. Pu and Wu's law, x = 0.5 K - 0.6 - 0.35 log10 tau, survival 1 - exp(-x^2), zero below K < 1.2 + 0.7 log tau. The earliest unstable pair merges (angular momentum conserved, mass retention 0.9) until every pair outlasts 1 Gyr.
  5. Floors. Bodies lighter than 0.1 Earth masses (inside the snow line) or 0.60 (beyond) are debris. No chain starts, and no hot Jupiter settles, closer than the distance where a blackbody would be at the dust sublimation temperature (DUST_SUBLIMATION_K, 1500 K; sublimation_au), which keeps the luminous stars' planets out of the vapor of their own rock.
  6. Gas. planet_mass.gas_outcome per core with an extra metallicity exponent.
  7. Migration. Hot Jupiters at a share 0.0092 times 10^(2.2 [Fe/H]) of giants (period 1 to 10 d), clearing everything inside; other giants move in by e^-|N(0, 0.72)|, scattering planets within 8 mutual Hill radii. Giants move in on their own, so when two land within 8 mutual Hill radii the heavier throws the lighter out (_scatter_crowded_giants).

A whole-disk oligarch chain alone gave about three times too many planets (tested), which is why the inner system is its own draw.

Hot Jupiters are compared on a mixture of metallicities (N(-0.03, 0.2) dex, the survey's), not at solar [Fe/H], because the rate rises with 10^(2.2 [Fe/H]). Spacing: median 14.6 mutual Hill radii, 93 percent of pairs over 10 (observed mode near 20, 93 percent). Giants rise 7 times between [Fe/H] -0.4 and +0.4; the 10^(1.2 [Fe/H]) law of [R2172] gives 9 over that range (tweak L3). A Sun-like star holds 6.8 planets on average, 0.11 of them giants between 2 and 8 AU: GEN.950's lower solids beyond the snow line took the cold giants from 0.17 to 0.11 at the same constants, and the re-tune brought back most of it.

Known weaknesses (tracked, not hidden)

  • Giants inside 2.5 AU and the exact metallicity slope are still loose. Giants between 2 and 8 AU are 0.11 per Sun-like star against 0.141 observed, the best the search found with the 182 K line. Several tuned constants sit near the edge of their search range (PLANET_OUTER_GROWTH_AU 11.4); a disk-evolution model (GEN.509, GEN.510) replaces them.
  • Hot-Jupiter statistics need n of several thousand to test.
  • The inner masses pile up near the inner core cap (PLANET_INNER_CORE_MAX_EARTH 12); warm super-Earths of 1 Earth mass or more are about 0.06 per star against 0.20 observed.
  • An F-type host has a multiplicity (2.9) above the observed 1.8 of [R4812]; the multiplicity has no stellar-mass dependence yet.
  • The transit multiplicity has more doubles than [R4898] (0.22 against 0.17), because the harness uses its inclination law and the model's singles are intrinsically fewer.

Wiring (train 4)

StarSystem._plan_architecture builds the planet_architecture.Plan for the star (the architecture cut to the planets the system holds, then further oligarchs for a system asked for more); _generate_planets takes the planet of slot i from it: its mass and formation distance, at the slot's distance (never inside the previous planet's orbit limit). PLANETS False gives none, NUM_ORBITS exactly that many, MAX_PLANETS True the most the disk holds (an inner system and a starting count two widths above the mean), False the fewest. SLOTS, FORCE_CLASS and HABITABLE_WORLD requests keep the formation law (planet_mass.planet_mass). A hot Jupiter keeps its formation distance for the composition and its final distance for the orbit. Planets inside the orbit floor of the star (_orbit_floor_au) are left out. A belt no longer takes the place of a planet in its slot (the slot roll and its constant BELT_REPLACES_BELOW_EARTH, row PA13, are gone: GEN.1068): every planned body is a planet, and the belts are the gaps and the unfinished tail of the finished chain (belt_birth, belt_origin). The architecture's own spacing is kept (the old previous planet + 5 Hill radii rule pushed planets outward and a census of 300 Sun-like systems showed it halving the giants); validate_system still enforces the stable spacing of the whole. Census after the fixes: the planets of the plan and of the finished systems agree (7.0 and 6.7 per Sun-like star; giants 0.19 in both; inner planets 59 of 59). The removed constants are ARCH_*, PLANET_ASSEMBLY_SCATTER_DEX, GIANT_IMPACT_SURVIVAL_FRACTION, PLANET_SPACING_RATIO_RANGE, FIRST_PLANET_HZ_FRACTION_RANGE, INITIAL_PLANET_DISTANCE_FACTOR and PLANET_OCCURRENCE_REFERENCE_OBJECTS; registry rows PM1, PM3, PM4, PM6 and PM7 are retired into PA1 and PA2. The change alters the plan stage: --affects plan, a new generator epoch, and the life, air and class shares move with it.
