Status: built 2026-10-24 (lane "Source and tune atmosphere draws"). Module src/planetgen/physics/radius_valley.py, four targets in physics/data/tuning_targets.json (e1-valley-slope, e1-valley-radius; e1-valley-flux-slope, e1-valley-mass-slope from GEN.722), rows e1 and e2 of scripts/atmosphere_recipes_data.py, tests in src/tests/test_radius_valley.py. It uses the Kepler recovery model (GEN.504, kepler-recovery.md) and the [R2741] radius law of bulk_structure.py. Decision D2 of tunable-gaps.md (XUV energy-limited loss with variable efficiency, a core-mass distribution as a third knob, Lopez and Fortney as the radius law) is followed.

Superseded in part (GEN.822, envelope-escape-wiring.md): the energy-limited loss, its efficiency and the eta ridge of sections 1 and 2 are gone; rows e1 and e2 and the survey now use the hydro-based loss of [R2512], integrated from ONSET_GY = 1 Gyr, with the core-mass width at 0.25 in ln and the f_env0 bracket at 0.05 to 0.2. The targets and the survey method here still hold.

1. What is tuned and what to

Rows e1 (the planet keeps its envelope) and e2 (it lost it) weigh min(1, f_lost / f_env0) against its complement. Two constants matter: the starting envelope fraction f_env0 and the efficiency of the loss. Nothing measures them for one planet; the population does, through the valley in the period-radius plane ([R3500], READ in the worker report): slope d log R / d log P = -0.11 +- 0.02, and a valley radius of 1.84 +- 0.03 R_E (6371 km) at 10 days.

radius_valley.survey draws 4000 planets (host 0.8 to 1.3 solar masses, period log-uniform 2 to 100 d, core mass log-normal around 3.72 R_E-masses, the peak of [R3797] starting envelope log-normal around f_env0), applies the loss of the row, the Lopez and Fortney radius, and weights each planet by the chance Kepler would have found it. valley_of finds the minimum of the smoothed radius histogram in five period bins from 2 to 64 d and fits a line. One survey is one sample of the harness; 24 surveys are compared with each target (the harness's 2 sigma rule, sigma combining the published error and the spread of the surveys).

2. The result: one ridge, not a point

The two knobs are degenerate, as the tunable-gaps note predicted: a stronger loss and a larger starting envelope give the same valley. The surveys put both targets inside 2 sigma along eta = 10 f_env0 for f_env0 from 0.035 to 0.08 (slope -0.13 to -0.14, radius 1.82 to 1.85). Below 0.035 the slope steepens past -0.16; above 0.09 the envelopes of the starting draw reach giants. The row therefore draws f_env0 log-uniform in 0.035 to 0.08 and sets its efficiency to 10 f_env0 (times (15 km/s / v_esc)^2, Owen and Wu 2017), instead of two independent constants; an off-ridge pair does not reproduce the valley (a test pins that with a no-loss and a ten-fold loss).

3. What it does not do

  • Row e5 (envelope metal fraction) stays partly verified under GEN.509's other half; no JWST population mean exists.
  • The recipes are not read by the generator yet, so no generated value changes and no epoch is taken. Row e1's loss uses the planet's total mass and core radius as the row did (mass_earth, core_radius_earth); the survey uses the core mass. The two differ by the envelope fraction, a few percent.
  • The three points GEN.509 left open (the mechanism, the stellar-mass slope and the widths) are closed in sections 4 and 5 below (GEN.722).

4. The mechanism and the stellar-mass slope (GEN.722)

Read for this item (abstracts, 2026-10-25): [R0354] fit the valley as log R = c + alpha log F + beta log M* and measure alpha = 0.069 +0.019 -0.023 and beta = -0.046 +0.125 -0.117; they quote the predictions of core-powered mass loss (alpha 0.08, beta 0.00) and of photoevaporation (alpha 0.12, beta -0.17) and favour the first. [R3500] find the period slope -0.10 +- 0.03 consistent with both mechanisms and no evidence that it changes with stellar mass; the mean size of sub-Neptunes goes as M*^0.25 +- 0.03 (a core-mass trend, cores up to about 10 M_E M*/M_sun). [R1733]: core-powered mass loss predicts the period slope -0.11 down to 0.1 solar masses and a stellar-mass slope (in period space) of (3 zeta - 2)/36, about 0.37 at 1 solar mass and 0.13 at 0.1, for L ~ M^zeta. A 2025 re-analysis of 1923 planets [R2195] finds the period slope -0.142 +- 0.006 (it says toward core-powered), the flux slope 0.136 +- 0.014 for 0.8 to 1.2 solar masses (toward photoevaporation), and says its stellar-mass slopes agree with neither model well. [R3797] (GEN.509) needed about 5000 planets to separate the two.

Decision. No mechanism is chosen: the sources disagree by axis, and the standing source rule for two in question is the widest range. The generator keeps what it has, an energy-limited loss whose energy is proportional to the bolometric flux (XUV_ENERGY_J_M2 * F, no dependence on the star's mass of its own), labelled a stand-in. That loss gives, over 0.5 to 1.4 solar masses and the whole ridge f_env0 0.035 to 0.08, alpha = 0.07 and beta = 0.00 (+-0.02), which is the core-powered pair and inside 1.1 sigma of both of Berger's measurements (beta = -0.17 of photoevaporation is 1.0 sigma from the measurement, so the survey cannot tell them apart). The slope in period space comes out -0.115, the stellar-mass dependence of the valley at a fixed period 0.26 (the theory above says 0.33 to 0.37 for FGK stars; not a target). Two new targets (e1-valley-flux-slope, e1-valley-mass-slope) pin alpha and beta; their tests run 24 surveys of 12000 planets (radius_valley.mass_slopes_of, WIDE_HOST_MASS_RANGE_SOLAR). No knob was bent: the stellar-mass slope came out of the loss as written, so nothing in the generator changes. If a later item wires in a stellar-mass-dependent XUV energy (a real photoevaporation), beta is the number to re-check.

5. The widths (GEN.722)

The core-mass width (CORE_MASS_SIGMA, 0.4 dex) and the starting-envelope width (ENVELOPE_SIGMA, 0.6 dex) have no published value read; they stay labelled stand-ins. A sweep of 3 by 3 widths (core 0.3, 0.4, 0.5; envelope 0.4, 0.6, 0.8; f_env0 0.045, five surveys of 12000 planets each) gave alpha 0.059 to 0.076, beta -0.031 to 0.006 and period slope -0.095 to -0.126, all inside 1 sigma of the targets, and a valley radius at 10 days of 1.72 to 1.84 (the radius is where the widths act: along the ridge of section 2 it moves by about 0.1 for the whole box, which f_env0 absorbs). The tests run the corners (0.3, 0.4), (0.3, 0.8) and (0.5, 0.8) against the three slopes; the corner (0.5, 0.4), a wide core distribution with a narrow envelope one, puts the period slope 2.1 sigma off, so the bracket is the band around the centre, not the whole box. The centre (0.4, 0.6) stays. Rows RV1 to RV4 of section 6.10 of the gap-filling plan record the tiers.

7. The generator draws the envelope (GEN.1323)

Sections 1 to 6 tuned the recipes and the survey, and the generator did not read them: a rocky planet came out bare whatever its star, so no generated planet of 1 to 4 Earth radii inside 100 days was above 2.0 (the validation sweep of GEN.753; [R1478] find about half). physics/envelope_capture.py and planets.settle_envelope now wire them in. Report: reports/gen1323-sub-neptune-envelopes-2026-11-07.md.

  • The start. A rocky planet of 1 to 20 Earth masses draws the envelope it had when the disk went: log-normal, median 0.1 of its mass (EC1, the middle of the f_env0 bracket), width 0.6 (the survey's), held at 0.2 (EC5, the top of the range the radius law was fitted over; the survey holds 0.5). Below the 3.72 Earth masses the survey is centred on the median falls as (mass / 3.72)^3 (EC2, a stand-in with no source): a flat start would give every Earth-mass planet an envelope.
  • When. Twice. A planet is made with the envelope the star's age and its orbit give it then (planets.apply_envelope, in Planet, before the air is made), so the requests that judge a planet by its class (a forced class, a habitable world) see the envelope. When the age and the orbit are final the loss of GEN.822 is taken again (planets.settle_envelope, in the settle pass of the system, like planets.settle_giant); a planet whose envelope moved by under 2 percent (EC6) is left, any other has its radius, density, gravity, air, class and spin made again. The start is drawn from a stream of the planet's own, keyed on its mass, formation distance and star, so both passes draw the same start and the run's stream does not move.
  • Rogue planets. The same code builds them, and with no star there is no light and no loss: a rogue planet of 1 to 20 Earth masses keeps the whole envelope it started with.
  • What is stored. The envelope as envelope_mass_fraction (the solid fractions give way, the five still add up to 1); the radius of the law of Lopez and Fortney at the 20 mbar level of the planet's mass and the envelope left (EC4); the volume, density and gravity from it; the air, class and spin again; moons that no longer fit are dropped. The planet type (planet_type) follows the stored share: transitional from 0.5 percent. A planet whose envelope the star stripped is left exactly as drawn. A hydrogen air the air solver made is not counted twice (envelope_share.air_share is 0 for a planet with a captured envelope).
  • What it does. On 240 G2V systems, inside 100 days 45 percent of the planets of 1 to 4 Earth radii are above 2.0 and the gap holds the rest; most planets of 4 Earth masses and more beyond 100 days are sub-Neptunes of 2 to 4 Earth radii, including those in the ecosphere; planets of 1 to 2 Earth masses are mostly rocky. The survey of this note is unchanged and keeps tuning the loss.
  • Class. A rocky planet whose envelope is 0.5 percent of its mass or more is judged by the giant classes (planet_classes.quantities, as its planet type is transitional or gas): a sub-Neptune comes out a gas dwarf (T) or an ice giant (I), not a hot or small rocky world. In 600 G2V draws at 0.3 to 1.5 AU the gas dwarfs go from 5 to 65 and the cold dwarf and rocky classes thin by the same.
  • Dense rocky worlds. A class that needs a bare dense core (V, 2 g or more) is out of reach of the envelope draw at most places: the heavy planets that have the gravity are sub-Neptunes except close in, where the star stripped them. A request for a class (a slot, FORCE_CLASS) therefore draws, after its free attempts, every other planet as a core that finished growing after the gas disk went and captured nothing (Planet(late_core=True), StarSystem.build_planet); such cores are rare in the population and a request is the only way to one. Forced V stays reachable (24 of 40 seeds before, 12 of 40 after).
  • Shares. The envelope and a small hydrogen air's share (envelope_share.fill) each come out of the solids, which hold the whole less what the body was drawn with, so the five fractions add to 1 (fill summed to 1 plus 9e-9 for a body drawn with 0.1 percent envelope and a share of 7e-6).

8. A planet under an envelope has no ground (GEN.1334)

GEN.1323 gave rocky planets their envelope, and a planet of 0.5 percent of its mass or more in hydrogen and helium (planet_type.TERRESTRIAL_MAX_ENVELOPE, a sub-Neptune or a gas dwarf) still got a rocky planet's surface: an ocean and a land fraction, a ground of minerals, a surface dose, a liquid, a day and night on the ground, a score for life, and a carbon dioxide and nitrogen air at 5 to 40 MPa on top of the envelope. planet_type.has_ground(body) is now the one test of whether a body has a surface (a giant has none, nor has a planet under a deep envelope, nor has one whose envelope share is not stored and whose body type is g), and the modules that made a surface ask it where they asked for a giant:

  • The air. The body is solved as a giant is (surface_air.setting_of, mixture_subject.kind_of_body): at the 1 bar level of a hydrogen and helium envelope with its metals (mixture_giant, family envelope or giant), from the temperature of that layer, not at a pressure the rock below would give. The pressure stored is that level's (100 kPa).

  • The surface fields. hydrosphere, surface_composition, surface_liquid_world, radiation.generate_dose and its refresh, habitability_world (and the class scores of life_classes) and daylight leave their fields empty for such a body, as they do for a giant; generation/life.py gives it no life, no change of its air by life, and the orbital flavor text. A planet whose envelope changes when the star's age and its orbit are final is made again (planets.settle_envelope), with a ground if it fell under the line.

  • Moons and rings. A body whose envelope is 1 percent or more is a giant parent (moon_composition.parent_kind) for the masses of its moons and for their origin alike (before, it drew massive rocky impact moons and then disk moons of ice too large for it to hold), and has the small ring-feeding moonlets of a giant (rings.apply).

  • What a page calls the pressure (UX.297, GEN.1532). The pressure stored for such a body (100 kPa) is the 1 bar reference level, a convention like a giant's (surface_air.REFERENCE_PRESSURE_BAR), not a surface, so every sample of class T planets shows 100 kPa on 88 percent of them (the rest are thin hydrogen air over a ground, with their own drawn pressure). planet_type.pressure_label gives the name: "Surface pressure" for a ground and "Pressure at the 1 bar reference level" for none. The property sheet (and so the API), the description's facts, the system map's side panel and the summary sentence use it; the stored column and the generated values do not change.

No new number: the 0.5 percent line is PT1 and the 1 percent line is the giant parent's (GEN.466); nothing is tuned.
