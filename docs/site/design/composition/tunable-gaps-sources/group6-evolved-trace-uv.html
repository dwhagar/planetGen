1. Summary and verdicts

Most rows in this group are not probabilities. j1, j5, m2, l1, l3, m5, m6 and the abiotic part of m3 and m11 are outcomes of inventory (group 2), escape, photochemistry and speciation, so they should be computed, with the volatile inventory as the only random element. j2, j3, j4, m4 and the biotic parts of m3 and m11 have no observational ensemble at all and the only constraint is the Solar System, which breaks the NO FIXED CONTENT rule if used to tune a knob. The two rows that do have real data are p4 (tau_sat; N=55 stars in the early-M bin of [R2118], N=219 volume-complete mid-to-late M dwarfs in [R3017]) and, weakly, l3 and l1 (upper limits on transiting planets around 1148 K2 white dwarfs and 313 polluted white dwarfs, one microlensing and one transiting giant). The single LU[0.162, 2] Gyr for p4 mixes a mass dependence: 0.162 Gyr is the early-M X-ray value (READ), 2 Gyr is the fully convective value (READ, 2.3 +- 1.3 Gyr for 0.1 to 0.2 Msun). p6 can be replaced by a continuous percentile draw using the published [R2183] tracks (MIT-licensed code, integer percentiles 2 to 98, masses 0.1 to 1.25 Msun).

Verdict key: (a) published ensemble plus bracket suffices for a tunable seeded draw; (b) tunable only to a few stated constraints; (c) needs a source not in the public record. "Derive" means the quantity should be computed by physics, not drawn.

Places where a verified source overrides a partly verified one (usability rule):

  1. m2: part 09 says "O3 about 10 % of Earth column ([R1137], 2ND-HAND)". The paper's Table 3 (READ) gives abiotic O3 columns of 4.5e15 (Sun), 1.0e15 (K2V), 8.8e15 (M3.5V), 1.8e15 (M4V) and 1.3e18 (F2V) molecules per cm2 against 8.6e18 for present Earth: that is 0.05, 0.01, 0.10, 0.02 and 15 percent. Only the F-star case is near 10 percent. READ wins.
  2. p4: the project stand-in "2 Gyr" for tau_sat is overridden for early M by [R2118: Table 2] (READ, 162 Myr) and supported for 0.1 to 0.2 Msun by [R3017] (READ).
  3. m4: "TEST 20 ppb Venus claim ([R1672] READ)" is contradicted by two other READ papers ([R4549] upper limit < 1 ppbv at 3 sigma; [R2696] finds the line forms above 80 km and is fit by mesospheric SO2). Both sides are verified; the claim cannot be a calibration point.
  4. j1: "runaway limit 1.04 to 1.11" is replaced by READ values 1.0512 [R2446], 1.107 at 1 Earth mass, 0.99 at 0.1 and 1.188 at 5 Earth masses [R2447].
  5. j5: [R1813] (abstract READ from NTRS) conditions its "no false positives" result on the planet lying in the conventional HZ with no irrecoverable water loss, which does not contradict [R2769] (READ) but restricts it. The 2015 arXiv preprint of Harman (1509.07863, READ abstract) has the opposite headline (K and M stars may build O2); the 2018 version supersedes it.

2.1 j1 to j5: Venus-, Mars-, Titan-, Pluto-like and abiotic-O2 atmospheres

Are they probabilities? No. Each is the result of (inventory delivered, group 2) x (escape history, tables d and p) x (photochemistry) x (redox, table a). The part 09 weight c[lo,hi] is a mixing weight between overlapping gates, not a measured fraction. The honest model: draw the volatile inventory and redox; compute whether the planet is above the runaway limit, loses its water, keeps CO2-N2; the row label is then an output.

Verdict (point 3). j1 (b): the gate is derived from READ limits; there is no ensemble for the atmosphere fraction, only the two JWST planets as an upper-limit check and the Venus Zone occurrence of planets (not atmospheres). j5 (b): the physics (Luger and [R3946]) is published and outcome-dependent on the O2 sink assumption (surface absorption of about 100 bar is assumed by Luger and Barnes, READ). j2, j3, j4 (c): no extrasolar ensemble exists; the only constraint is the Solar System (Mars, Titan, Pluto and Triton).

Knobs (point 4). If the generator keeps a draw for the residual after the physics gate: knob w_retain in [0, 1] (log-uniform not appropriate because 0 is allowed; use uniform). For j1 the physical gate parameters are S_eff,runaway(M, Teff) with target 1.05 to 1.19 (READ, Kopparapu) and a 3-D alternative of about 1.10 (Leconte, abstract). The water-loss knob is the EUV exposure (group p) and initial water (group 2). The O2-sink knob (fraction of O2 absorbed by the surface) is unsourced except by assumption in Luger and Barnes; bracket [0, 1]. Over what range the targets hold: Kopparapu 2600 to 7200 K, 0.1 to 5 Me.

Consistency test (point 5). Simulated: fraction of zone-h planets with thick CO2 or O2 atmospheres at T-1 b and c insolation (Teq 391 to 400 K for b), compared with "no thick atmosphere" for two planets; tolerance is a binomial limit on N=2, so the test is "population must not forbid 0 of 2 with high probability". A selection model is needed: transit geometry and the JWST target list (nearby M dwarfs, short periods).

No-fixed-content flags (point 6). j2 to j4 are Solar System-only constraints (Mars, Titan, Pluto). The 97:3 CO2:N2 split ([R4154], READ in part 08) is a magma-ocean calculation, not a Venus copy, but Venus is the test point. The "[R3945] crust composition" speciation (section 2.4) is Earth-only.

Group for a builder (point 7). Builder A: j1, j5, m2 (and the abiotic part of m3) share one model: runaway gate, water loss, O2 build-up, and a photochemistry look-up of O3 columns. j2 to j4 form a second small builder (retention and vapour pressure) with no tuning data.

Physical bracket (point 2). l1: P_eq(Ts) of the silicate vapour at the surface temperature (i6 formula from the part 09 table, [R2380] READ in part 09, not re-read here). The exosphere criterion (collision-free above a column) is a physics statement, not a probability. l3: survival requires an initial orbit outside the engulfment radius, with tides and mass loss ([R4532], READ). Pulsar planets: atmosphere survival from the energy-limited escape and wind stripping calculation of Patruno and Kama (READ).

Verdict (point 3).

  • Is the polluted-WD fraction the right tuning constraint for l1? No. [R2415] measures the fraction of white dwarfs that accrete rocky debris (27 to about 50 percent for 20 to 200 Myr cooling ages, Teff below 23000 K, A-type progenitors). It tunes the upstream gate "this WD hosts rocky bodies that reach it" (and, with debris-disk lifetimes, the number of exposed rocky bodies). It says nothing on whether an exposed body keeps a silicate-vapour exosphere. l1 is a deterministic function of Ts through P_eq, so the draw should be removed or kept only as a width on the threshold (0.5 dex DESIGN), with no data to tune it. Verdict for l1 and l2: (c) as probabilities, derivable from physics.
  • l3: (b). Constraints: one hot giant (WD 1856 b), one cold giant (Blackman), transit upper limits (hot Jupiters < 1.5 % of 1148 K2 WDs; 0.8 percent transit fraction of 313 polluted WDs, both short-period only), and a prediction of more than half of WDs hosting Jovian companions (2ND-HAND). The c[0, 0.1] prior is not supported as a number: the right quantity is P(survive) = P(initial a above the engulfment radius of the progenitor, after tides) x occurrence of giants around 1 to 8 Msun progenitors. This should be derived in the generator from its own giant occurrence (group 1 table f) and Veras' engulfment rule. Bracket-only draw: the widest defensible upper value is the published prediction (> 0.5, 2ND-HAND); the transit limits constrain only P < 27 d.
  • Pulsar planets: (c). No observation of any pulsar planet atmosphere exists; the record has 3 to 5 planetary systems around about 3000 neutron stars (Patruno: roughly 3000 known; 2200 young pulsars, 400 millisecond pulsars, READ). The only physics is Patruno and Kama's survival calculation. A pulsar-planet atmosphere should be derived (escape against X-ray and wind), not drawn.

Knobs (point 4). l1: threshold log10 P_eq,0 = -6 (DESIGN), width 0.5 dex (DESIGN); no target statistic. l3: knob = occurrence of giants beyond the engulfment radius; targets: (i) transit fraction < 1.5 % (K2) and 0.8 (+0.6, -0.4) % for hot companions, (ii) one microlensing detection (cannot constrain a fraction without a survey completeness model). Engulfment knob: a_engulf = 1.2 x R_max(M_prog) for Jovian planets (the "20 percent" figure is an example for a 2 Msun AGB star, READ; use Veras' stellar-model-dependent value).

Consistency test (point 5). Simulated hot-giant fraction at WDs vs the K2 and TESS upper limits (inject-and-recover with transit probability a_R/R_WD and detection limits is needed). A population test for l1 does not exist.

No-fixed-content flags (point 6). Pulsar-planet atmospheres and white-dwarf exospheres have no Solar System anchor either; the only anchors are one pulsar system and one WD 1856 b / Blackman planet each. The existing recipe (dIW ~ N(-1, 0.5) for WD debris) is from six polluted WDs ([R1154], READ in part 05), an extrasolar constraint, so no flag there.

Group for a builder (point 7). One builder: l1 to l3 with the WD pollution gate, engulfment rule and pulsar escape. It shares nothing with the other groups except the rocky-body list from table i.

Is any of this an observational probability? No. There is no measurement of any of these gases on any extrasolar planet, so no ensemble can be built today. m2 is an output of a photochemistry grid (columns vary by four orders of magnitude between stellar types, from 1e15 to 1.3e18 cm-2 at the same CO2 and volcanic flux); m3, m4, m11 (CH3Cl) are mostly biosignature design flags whose probability equals "life exists and produces it". A bracket-only seeded draw is the honest answer for m3, m4, m11 with the widest defensible bracket [0, 1] (m4 narrowed to [0, 0.05] if kept as a design choice about biology). The part 09 priors c[0.05, 0.5], c[0, 0.05], c[0, 0.3] have no data behind them: they are choices. m2 should not be a presence probability: the generator can look up an O3 column (Domagal-Goldman Table 3 grid and Gao) as a function of star type, CO2, H2 flux, desiccation and a lightning flag.

No-fixed-content flags. Every constraint on m3 (400 ppbv), m4 (Venus 20 ppb) and m11 (lightning NO flux based on modern Earth; SNIPPET) is Solar System-only. The rule is broken if any knob is tuned to those.

Group for a builder. m2 with Builder A (section 2.1). m3, m4, m11 (CH3Cl) form a "design flags" group that belongs with the life-chemistry design, not with a fit.

2.4 Halogens: HCl and HF (m5, m6) and the derived-budget question

Can a derived halogen budget replace c[0.1, 0.5]? Yes for the stellar input, only partly for the planet. HCl and HF are equilibrium species of the Cl and F in the crust or mantle, not a coin flip. [R3945] ([R3945], READ) show that HCl and HF are "the dominant Cl- and F-bearing gases at lower temperatures", appear at percent level only for the continental-crust composition (Table 2: F 0.053 wt %, Cl 0.047 wt %) and are below the 0.1 % plot threshold for BSE composition (F 0.002 wt %, Cl 0.004 wt %); the part 09 values (HCl 1.4 % at 1000 K 100 bar; HF 4.5 % at 1000 K 1e-6 bar) are crust-composition results. COMPUTED from Table 2: crust over BSE enrichment is 26.5 for F and 11.8 for Cl; molar Cl/F is 0.48 in the crust and 1.07 in the BSE; the solar-photosphere Cl/F number ratio is 10^(5.31 - 4.40) = 8.1 (meteoritic 10^(5.23 - 4.42) = 6.5). So the planet's halogen mixing ratio is set mainly by differentiation (crust enrichment) and volatile depletion, not by the star.

No F or Cl abundance exists for main-sequence FGK stars from direct lines; both come from molecular lines (HF, HCl) in cool giants or sunspots, so the generator's host F and Cl must be scaled from [Fe/H] and [alpha/Fe] by a chemical-evolution relation. The published scatter (0.15 dex for Cl) is smaller than the planetary processing uncertainty.

Planet side. Halogen depletion in terrestrial bodies: [R0818] (Nature 551, 614; SNIPPET only: Cl in chondrites about six times lower than earlier estimates, Br/Cl and I/Cl matching BSE) says most Earth halogens sit in surface reservoirs. Volcanic degassing on Earth: HCl 4.3 +- 1 Tg/a, HF 0.5 +- 0.2 Tg/a, S-Cl and S-F partitioning contrasts of about 9 and 36 (SNIPPET from a search result). Every constraint on the star-to-planet transfer (condensation, depletion, crust enrichment) comes from Earth, chondrites and Venus: a Solar System-only calibration.

Verdict. (b) for a derived HCl/HF budget (stellar scaling is data-supported, speciation is published), but the planet-side depletion and crust-enrichment factors have no extrasolar data. As a probability it is (c). Recommend: compute HCl and HF mixing ratios from (a) host [Cl/Fe]-like scaling, (b) a volatility depletion curve vs formation temperature (condensation temperatures are not verified here: RECALLED), (c) a crust-enrichment factor tied to the differentiation draw (table a, groups 2 and 3), (d) Schaefer speciation, (e) removal by an ocean or by condensation as salts (derived from the j and c tables). The only random elements are inventory and differentiation. If a seeded presence draw is kept, the widest defensible bracket is [0, 1] for "above 1 ppm"; c[0.1, 0.5] has no support. Knobs for the derived approach: depletion exponent (unsourced), crust enrichment 11.8 (Cl) and 26.5 (F) (Earth-only, COMPUTED from READ Table 2). Test: the Venus HCl (about 0.5 ppm) and HF (about 5 ppb) values are RECALLED and Solar System-only; no exoplanet test exists.

Group for a builder. m5 and m6 alone, plus the speciation call (Schaefer fit exists in part 05).

Answer for p4. For 0.5 to 0.6 Msun (B-V 1.3 to 1.4, K7 to M1) the X-ray tau_sat is measured: 162 Myr, with a 0.31 dex systematic range, from N=55 stars with cluster ages. For 0.2 to 0.3 Msun the activity age of fast rotators is 0.6 +- 0.3 Gyr and for 0.1 to 0.2 Msun it is 2.3 +- 1.3 Gyr, from N=219 stars with kinematic ages that assume constant star formation (a lower limit, Medina says). The overall M-dwarf mean is 2.4 +- 0.3 Gyr. The Halpha activity lifetimes of [R4668] are longer (M4 4.5, M5 to M6 7, M7 8 Gyr) and use different physics (Halpha, not X-ray; heating simulation ages). So a single LU[0.162, 2] Gyr mixes mass: the lower edge is the measured early-M X-ray value, the upper edge is the fully convective value from Medina, and late M can exceed it [R4668]. Mid-M (0.3 to 0.5 Msun) has no direct X-ray tau_sat in the record.

Recommended replacement. tau_sat(M) piecewise in log mass: 0.16 Gyr (sys 0.08 to 0.33) at 0.5 to 0.6 Msun, 0.6 +- 0.3 Gyr at 0.2 to 0.3 Msun, 2.3 +- 1.3 Gyr at 0.1 to 0.2 Msun, interpolated; spread drawn as log-normal with the published uncertainty. Or compute it: R_X = R_X,sat for Ro <= Ro_sat on the drawn spin-down track (Mors code, MIT licence; README states a function that returns the age a star stays saturated; masses 0.1 to 1.25 Msun). Caveat: Johnstone says the tracks do not describe fully convective M dwarfs well after about 2 Gyr and that fully convective stars may not spin down much. The Ro_sat constants are convention dependent (0.0605 with times against 0.13 to 0.16 with [R4780] times): never mix them.

Consistency test for p4 and p6. Tolerance: the published uncertainties above. Selection models are needed: volume-complete (Medina and Pass use 15 pc), detection bias in X-ray catalogues (Magaudda includes only X-ray-detected stars) and in rotation catalogues (Johnstone: Kepler misses the slowest rotators). p6: weights. Tu 2015 ([R4443], READ) uses the 10th, 50th and 90th percentile tracks of the initial rotation distribution for 0.9 to 1.1 Msun only, with t_sat of 5.7, 23 and 226 Myr as in part 09; the constraints are clusters at 150, 550 and 1000 Myr (230, 134, 36 stars) plus NGC 6530 (28 stars) and h Per (117 stars); the abstract gives saturation lasting "from about 10 Myr to about 300 Myr". [R1500] ([R1500], READ) use the 25th, 50th and 90th percentiles as slow, median, fast with initial periods of 10, 7 and 1.4 d from 13 cluster distributions. [R2183] (READ) uses the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles at 150 Myr for four masses, 12 clusters in 7 age bins, and provides tracks at every integer percentile 2 to 98 for masses 0.1 to 1.25 Msun (code MIT-licensed, raw.githubusercontent.com/ColinPhilipJohnstone/Mors/master, README READ; the track data were not downloaded). No paper assigns weights to three tracks. The 0.25/0.5/0.25 weights can be justified (COMPUTED, reasoning only): tracks at the 10th/50th/90th percentile are close to the medians (12.5, 50, 87.5) of quartile bins [0, 0.25], [0.25, 0.75], [0.75, 1], which have probability 0.25, 0.5, 0.25. The best replacement is a uniform percentile u in [0.02, 0.98] mapped onto Johnstone's integer-percentile tracks, which also makes the mass dependence automatic (tracks for 0.1 to 1.25 Msun) and removes the solar-mass restriction of Tu 2015. The 200-fold spread in L_X at 100 Myr (Tu, part 04) is real and must be drawn. Knob: percentile u (no tuning needed: it is the observed 150 Myr distribution); target: the cluster rotation percentiles (READ). Test: reproduce Johnstone's modelled X-ray distributions against cluster data within the published scatter.

No-fixed-content flags. Johnstone's tracks are fitted to open clusters and field stars of all masses, not the Sun alone; the Sun appears only as a minor weight in fits. No Solar-System-only knob in p4 or p6.
