1. Summary and verdict table

The public record is richest for stellar C/O, the bulk heavy-element mass of giants and the brown-dwarf Teff-to-type map; it is thin for clouds, hazes and Kzz. Two rows are not probabilities: g1/g4 (type is a function of Teff with scatter, so draw mass and age, evolve, then map) and h2/h3 (regimes are outputs of equilibrium chemistry, computable with MIT-licensed code and NASA-format polynomials). A public data set found in this pass changes f5 to f7: LExACoM (Lothringer, Lowson and Fu 2025, [R2751]; CSV public; 69 planets with retrieved C/O and metallicity, updated to 2026-08-28) has no Solar System member, so it can tune m and C/O within the no-fixed-content rule.

    Verdict: (a) if f5 is redefined as bulk Z (center C25, width 0.22 to 0.29 dex); (b) for atmospheric m, with f (0.2, Jupiter and Saturn only) replaced by a distribution tuned to LExACoM.

    Design note corrected: part 09 f5 says "[R0710] gives lower Z for sub-Saturns". The paper says sub-Saturns (< 0.3 MJ) have high bulk Zp, 0.2 or more, not a selection effect. Consequences for the rows. g2 (L, 1300 to 2200 K), g3 (T, 700 to 1300 K) and g5 (Y, below 500 K) are the same map and need no weights. g4, the "T/Y transition 500 to 700 K", is not a type: in the empirical map 500 to 700 K is T8 to T9 (686 K at T8, 567 K at T9), and the measured Y0 dwarfs start at 510 K and below. 2.4 h2 and h3: hot-Jupiter regimes at 1500 and 2200 K

Question 4.

  1. Ensemble. The edges are not observed; the observational anchors are WASP-18 b (design cites [R0134], not re-read here), the high-resolution detections of Fe, Ca, Mg and ions in WASP-76 b, WASP-121 b, WASP-189 b ([R1505], READ) and the JWST emission spectra (WASP-121 b, SNIPPET).

  2. Physical bracket and what was computed. The regimes are outputs of Gibbs-energy minimisation. I wrote a minimiser (element potentials, scipy) and fed it the NASA-9 polynomials in easychem/thermo_easy_chem_simp_own.inp from the easyCHEM 2.1.0 sdist (DATA; NASA CEA thermo.inp format, 115 species parsed, including TiO, VO, SiO, Na, K, Fe, Mg, FeH, H-, e-, Na+, K+, Fe+; no Ti+, Mg+, AlO, CaO or condensates). Inputs: H=1, O/H 4.90e-4 times Z, C/O 0.55, He/H 0.0851 ([R0169] values from memory, RECALLED), ideal gas, ten neutral species, no ions, no condensation. Sanity checks: H2O formation free energy at 1500 K reproduces the standard value (-13.2 RT, COMPUTED); CH4 dominates at 800 K, 1 bar. Results (COMPUTED, Z = 1; Z = 10 changes T by less than 70 K except where stated):

P (bar) T where CH4 = CO T where H2O is 90 percent of the CO-limited value T where H2O is 50 percent T where H2 is half dissociated
0.001 not run 2331 K 2567 K 2574 K
0.01 not run 2542 K 2843 K 2884 K
0.1 966 K (Z=10: 892 K) 2792 K 3182 K 3278 K
1 1156 K (1052 K) 3095 K 3609 K 3794 K
10 1438 K (1280 K) not run 4164 K 4499 K
100 1907 K (1635 K) not run not run not run

At 1500 K, CH4/CO is 7e-9 at 1 mbar and 7e-3 at 1 bar, so below about 1 bar the gas is CO-dominated at 1500 K. The 1500 K edge of h1/h2 is therefore the equilibrium CH4-to-CO line at about 5 to 10 bar; at photospheric pressures (0.01 to 0.1 bar) the line sits near 900 to 1000 K, and quenching (part 05 s7.3) decides what is seen. The 2200 K edge of h2/h3 corresponds to only 1 to 4 percent H2O depletion at 1 mbar to 0.01 bar; real thermal dissociation (half the water) lies at 2570 to 3600 K for 1 mbar to 1 bar, and observed depletion in ultra-hot Jupiters also involves H- opacity and ionization, which this subset cannot do (Ti+ and Mg+ absent). The dayside is hotter than Teq. The edge is a pressure-dependent output; a fixed 2200 K is not supported by the computed table.

  1. Verdict. Derive, do not draw. The "weights" Phi((T-1500)/100) and Phi((T-2200)/100) have no probabilistic content. What can be tuned are the physical inputs (P-T profile, Z, C/O).

A cold trap needs a nightside or deep layer cooler than the Ti condensation temperature (Tcond near 1550 K or above for the Ti-bearing perovskite in Pelletier's and Gandhi's text). Retention requires vertical mixing faster than grain settling, which is Spiegel's Kmin(a), a requirement not a measured Kzz.

  2. Verdict. (c) for the Kzz prior LU[1e7, 1e11]: no population measures Kzz, and the two WASP-107 b analyses differ by 2.6 to 3.2 dex for the same planet (verified vs verified: both stand). The design's WASP-107 b 10^11.6 is right for [R4106] but not unique. (b) for a single empirical constraint that can replace the Kzz-grain machinery: the Teq above which Ti-bearing gas survives. In the verified data, WASP-76 b (Teq 2210 K) and WASP-121 b (about 2350 K) have no TiO/Ti, MASCARA-2 b has no TiO, and WASP-189 b (2641 K) has TiO and Ti: the threshold lies between about 2350 and 2640 K, from N = 3 to 6 planets. The design states "TiO above 2000 to 2300 K, VO above 1700 to 1900 K"; the verified record puts the Ti threshold 100 to 300 K or more higher, and shows VO retained at 2210 K (WASP-76 b). Here the verified record overrides the design's partly verified edges.

    Observed rate: TiO detected in 1 of 4 clearly tested ultra-hot Jupiters (WASP-189 b yes; WASP-76 b, WASP-121 b, MASCARA-2 b no; WASP-33 b disputed), no larger compilation found. Wilson 95 percent interval for 1 of 4 is 0.05 to 0.70 (COMPUTED), not a tuning constraint.

  3. Knobs: T_trap (threshold Teq for Ti retention): target 2350 to 2640 K bracket from N=3; the Kzz-grain pair stays unconstrained and degenerate: for fixed Teq, retention probability depends on Kzz and grain radius only through Kmin(a) = 10^(7.48 + 1.76 (log10 a + 1)) (design interpolation; reproduces Spiegel's 3e7 at 0.1 micron and 1e11 at 10 micron), so a detection rate cannot separate them.

  4. Consistency test: simulated fraction of ultra-hot Jupiters with gaseous TiO against Teq, compared with the list above (yes at 2641 K; no at 2210, 2250, 2260, 2350 K). A step at T_trap reproduces it; the Kzz-grain form (0.44 at 1 micron in the design example) cannot be told from 0.25 or 0.70 at N=4.

  5. Flags: none.

  6. Group. Builder D.

2.6 n6: silicate vs haze aerosol crossover near 950 K

  1. Ensemble. [R1513, R1515], [R1513]: an aerosol microphysics model on a grid Teq 700 to 2800 K, gravities 4, 10, 25 m/s2, metallicity 1 and 10 times solar, compared with the 1.4 micron water-band amplitude A_H (difference of maximum and minimum radius between 1.3 and 1.65 micron) of the WFC3 sample of Fu et al. 2017 ([R1469], N=34 hot Jupiters to hot Neptunes, with planets of radius below 0.5 RJ removed by Gao). Result (READ): silicates dominate aerosol opacity above about 950 K, hydrocarbon haze below; water-band amplitude is non-monotonic with Teq (increasing cloudiness with Teq above 2300 K and between 1100 and 1600 K, decreasing between 1600 and 2300 K and below 1100 K); robust to gravity and metallicity. Fu 2017 (READ): Spearman 0.43 between A_H and Teq (binned 0.85), slope 0.0008 +-0.00016 per K, degenerate with mass, hot planets under-sampled. [R1300] (READ, abstract): 62 planets, uniformly processed WFC3; two populations, a cloud-free one over a wide Teq range and a "partial cloud" one that goes from mostly cloudy near 500 K to mostly clear near 1500 K; cloud/haze minimum at 1460.86 (+316, -405) K. [R3438] (READ, abstract): Kepler offsets suggest a silicate to MnS transition near 1600 K. [R4105] ([R4105], READ abstract): a clear-to-cloudy continuum in 10 hot Jupiters.

  2. Physical bracket. Silicate clouds form where Teq is high enough for Mg2SiO4 and MgSiO3 to condense at observable pressures, and photochemical hydrocarbon haze needs methane in the upper atmosphere, which needs T below roughly 1000 K (the equilibrium CH4=CO line computed above is 892 to 1156 K at 0.1 to 1 bar). The 950 K of Gao is therefore the CH4 line plus the silicate-cloud sink depth, not an independent constant. So compute the crossover from the chemistry module (builder C) instead of drawing it.

  3. Verdict. (b). A published ensemble (N=34 and N=62) exists, but it constrains cloud amplitude, not which aerosol dominates: the 950 K is a model inference, and no width is published. The 100 K width is a design choice. Estrela's 1461 K minimum and Gao's 950 K are different quantities and do not contradict. Gao predicts mid-infrared silicate features; JWST/MIRI quartz-cloud detections ([R1665], WASP-17 b, RECALLED) would test it and were not reached.

  4. Knobs: crossover Teq (target 950 K, model; observed amplitude minimum 1461 +316/-405 K) and width (no published target). Test amplitude: A_H vs Teq slope 0.0008 +-0.00016 per K (Fu 2017) over 700 to 2400 K.

  5. Consistency test: simulate A_H for the population with cloud and haze opacities and compare slope and binned Spearman (0.43 raw, 0.85 binned) with Fu 2017; with the Estrela cloud-free fraction as a second test. A transmission-signal selection model is required (only transiting planets with H*>1 ppm at WFC3 precision were observed).

  6. Flags: none.

  7. Group. Builder D.

2.7 n7: haze yield LU[0.01, 1]

  1. Ensemble. [R2009] (READ abstract): lab haze for super-Earth and mini-Neptune gases at 300 to 600 K and 100 to 10000 times solar, rates 0.04 to about 10 mg/hr (250x), Titan simulation 7.4 mg/hr. [R0932] (READ abstract): 6 warm Neptunes, feature amplitude correlates with Teq or with H/He mass fraction at 95 percent, with more haze at low Teq or higher metallicity (not separable). [R2674] (READ abstract): two Kepler-51 super-puffs flat to 0.6 scale heights, aerosols above 3 mbar; supports the Teq hypothesis, disfavours the H/He one. [R0516] ("Clouds and clarity", READ abstract): 15 exo-Neptunes, 200 to 1000 K, best described by low sedimentation efficiency f_sed about 0.1 and 100 times solar metallicity, intrinsic scatter about 0.5 scale height, attenuation up to 6 scale heights at 500 to 800 K.

  2. Physical bracket. Haze mass production is limited by the photolysis rate of methane ([R1513] parameterise it by comparing methane upwelling with photodissociation, READ), so a yield above one is impossible and zero for carbon-free gas.

  3. Verdict. (c). The lab rates were measured on sub-Neptune gases, not on hot giants, in mg/hr, not a fraction of photolysed carbon, so "y = LU[0.01, 1]" is not the lab quantity. The population statistics constrain a combination of haze, cloud, metallicity and mean molecular weight, so the yield cannot be separated: Crossfield and Kreidberg say explicitly that temperature and H/He fraction cannot be separated at N=6, Brande's intrinsic scatter of about 0.5 scale height at N=15 absorbs any single knob, and Fu 2017 finds the temperature trend degenerate with mass. Boss would have to supply a haze production rate per unit methane photolysis for hot gases (none reached) or agree to treat y as a nuisance with the A_H scatter as its prior.

  4. Knobs: y (no constraint), with the target amplitude statistic being the Brande 0.5 scale-height intrinsic scatter for 200 to 1000 K planets (N=15, READ abstract).

  5. Consistency test: A_H vs Teq for 200 to 1000 K simulated Neptunes against the Brande attenuation (up to 6 scale heights at 500 to 800 K); selection model: transit signal-to-noise cut.

  6. Flags: the only lab calibration is a Titan simulation (7.4 mg/hr); Titan is a Solar System body, so any scaling of y to the Titan rate is Solar System-only.

  7. Group. Builder D.

2.8 T12: giant interior, diluted vs compact core

  1. Ensemble. The core-plus-envelope population fit (C25: Mcore 14.7 +1.8/-1.6 Earth masses, fZ 0.09 +-0.01) and T16's 57.9 M^0.61 Earth masses fit. T16 tests homogeneous vs central-core extremes on four planets and finds the model effect "much smaller than observational uncertainties" (READ); C25 says the metal distribution (core vs uniform) "leads to differences that are much smaller than the astrophysical scatter" (READ) and adopts 10 Earth masses in the core and the rest in the envelope. WASP-107 b core 11.5 (+3.0, -3.6) Earth masses ([R4106], READ). The core-mass placeholder log-uniform 5 to 20 is consistent with C25's 14.7.

  2. Verdict. (c). The population does not distinguish diluted from compact cores; C25's "Mcore" is a population-level parameter of the mass-metal relation, not a measured core. Structure is known only for Jupiter and Saturn (gravity fields) and weakly for WASP-107 b: Solar System-only for the structural question. The generator should keep the continuous Mz and draw the core fraction as a nuisance parameter between "all in envelope" and "10 Earth masses in the core".

  3. Knobs: Mcore 14.7 (+1.8/-1.6) for the mass-metal relation; no knob for dilution.
