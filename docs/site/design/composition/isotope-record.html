All real materials exist (Boss, 2026-11-08): deuterium is a share of hydrogen, not a fictional substance, and every stable or long-lived isotope of an element a body holds can be drawn. This note says what a body stores for it, which laws move the shares away from the natural mix, and what is left for the second part. The code is physics/isotope_record.py; the stand-ins are section 6.236 of the gap-filling plan (ISO1 to ISO9).

1. What is stored

A planet, moon or rogue planet carries a list of rows, isotopes, kept in planet_isotopes, moon_isotopes and rogue_planet_isotopes (schema v235): element, isotope, fraction (the atom fraction of the element; the rows of one element add up to 1) and origin (natural, physical, decay, radiogenic). It is built after the air, the interior and the ground, on the body's own streams (no draw of the run's moves), and again when an edit moves the air or the interior. A body saved before v235 has no rows.

2. Which elements a body holds

The elements of what it is made of, read from the stored composition: the rock (O, Mg, Si, Al, Ca, Fe and each minor oxide with a share above zero; C and Si for a carbon rock; K, Th and U where the body stores a radiogenic content), the core (Fe and each light element with a share), the water (H, O), each ice (the elements of its formula), an envelope (H and He, and C, N, O where it holds heavy elements) and every gas of the air.

4. What a generated sample shows

A solar-metal planet 4.5 Gyr old at 1 AU draws D/H near 2e-4 (the ocean's 1.56e-4 scattered), 13C/12C 1/89, 15N/14N 1/274, a 99.96 percent 40Ar air and 235U 0.76 percent of its uranium. An icy body at 30 K draws D/H near 5e-4. A hydrogen giant keeps the birth gas's 2.1e-5. A metal-rich star's bodies are richer in 13C and 15N, a metal-poor star's in deuterium.

5. The short-lived and cosmogenic isotopes (GEN.1377, GEN.1421, GEN.1489)

Extinct tracers. A body with rock carries 26Al, 60Fe, 53Mn, 129I and 244Pu as rows of origin extinct: initial_ratio is the ratio to 27Al, 56Fe, 55Mn, 127I and (for plutonium, which has no stable isotope) 238U in the gas the star was born from, and fraction is what is left per atom of that reference element at the star's age, a number that is nothing for any star older than a few hundred million years. 26Al is the ratio radiogenic already draws. 60Fe is a sum of decayed additions about the gamma-ray mean of the galaxy, built the way 26Al is (birth_radioisotopes). 53Mn, 129I and 244Pu use the same sampling about a tenth of the early Solar System's ratios (ISO10, ISO11; mean lives from the review's Table 2). Every body of a star shares the star's ratios.

Cosmogenic tracers. A body with air carries 3H and 14C as rows of origin cosmogenic: fraction is the steady-state atoms per atom of the element in the pool the isotope mixes into (the air, and for hydrogen the surface ocean's water, for carbon the air and ocean together at the Earth's ratio). The production is the Earth's global rate times the share of the cascade the air column catches (1 - exp(-X/150 g/cm2)), the targets per gram of air (tritium from N and O, radiocarbon from N alone) and the field's shielding (a body with no dipole gets 2.09 times the Earth's rate). The Earth's own air gives 1.6 and 0.345 atoms per cm2 per second and 14C/C of 1.3e-12, 3H/H of 1e-20. The sheet lists 3H/1H and 14C/12C beside the others.

Small bodies (derived, not stored; GEN.1487): a belt, a comet, an interstellar comet and an interstellar field have no record table. isotope_record.small_body_record works one out from the values the row stores (its ice share, the host star's [Fe/H] and age, the temperature the ice formed at) through record_of, the planet laws of section 3 with no second law, so the sheet and the API list the same ratio lines (D/H, 13C/12C, 15N/14N, the count) and a census can call the function on any row it draws. The ice is split into water, NH3, CO2, CH4, N2 and CO by the disk budget at the formation temperature (ISO18, ISO19), so a warm belt holds water only and shows D/H alone, and nitrogen lines appear where nitrogen ice froze. A belt's temperature comes from its distance and its snow line, a comet's and an interstellar comet's is the 30 K of the cold anchor (ISO17, ISO20), a field's the water line's; a body with no host star takes the gas [Fe/H] of its galactic radius and the Earth's reference age (ISO20). The scatter is one draw named by the body (system and distance, or its id), so a body always shows the same line. A member of a belt or field is given its own rock, metal, water and ice fractions.

Star surfaces (derived, not stored; GEN.1488): isotope_record.star_surface_record gives a star's surface the same kind of record from the element mass fractions of its surface (stellar_composition.star_makeup(...).surface, the one the chemistry rows use) and its [Fe/H] and age. Every element with a share gets the natural mix, with the laws of section 3 where the star moves it: 13C/12C and 15N/14N of the star's birth gas, a red giant's carbon at the 12C/13C of the first dredge-up (ISO22), and the hydrogen of a star past its protostar stage with the deuterium it has left (ISO21; a protostar keeps the gas's). The sheet lists the same ratio lines as a planet's. Left out: the lithium and beryllium a star burns, the 3He of the dredge-up and the s-process and r-process isotope mixes of the heavy metals.

The mantle's lines (derived, not stored; GEN.1490). The table holds one row per isotope of a body, so the record is the body's own mix and has no reservoirs. isotope_record.mantle_sheet_rows gives a rocky body's sheet five more lines from that record: the D/H of the mantle's water (the body's D/H times 1 + MANTLE_DELTA_D, -80 per mil, ISO23), the 15N/14N (-5 per mil, ISO24) and 13C/12C (+3 per mil, ISO25) of its mantle, and the 129Xe/130Xe of its mantle gas: the solar 6.286 (ISO27) plus the Earth's excess (7.9 against it, ISO26) scaled by the body's own birth 129I/127I over the early Solar System's (ISO28), the iodine over xenon and the closure time being the Earth's. A star poorer in 129I gives its worlds' mantles a smaller excess. The 136Xe/130Xe of the mantle gas (GEN.1507) is the solar wind's 1.797 (ISO35) plus the Earth's fission excess of 0.68 (the ridge sources' 2.478, ISO36) of which 28 percent (ISO37) is 244Pu's and scales with the body's birth 244Pu/238U over the early Solar System's, the 238U part being the Earth's for every body (ISO38); the numbers are [R5219], and the fission yields and branching ratios are not used because the Earth's measured excess is scaled instead. Not built: a stored mantle reservoir (which needs a reservoir column on the three isotope tables and so a migration; the derived lines are the least step that needs none) and the split of the air's own xenon from the mantle's.

6. Limits, and what is next

10Be and 26Al made in the air (no global rate in hand; beryllium has no pool the record holds), cosmic-ray production in an airless or ocean-covered surface, the stellar wind and the star's own flares (the Earth's galactic flux is used for every star), the daughters of the long-lived chains (lead, strontium, neodymium), the mass-independent oxygen anomalies, the stored split between a body's reservoirs, the sheets of the individual members of a belt or field are not in this record yet. The page lists the ratios the record moves away from the natural mix.

Cosmogenic isotopes of a rock surface (GEN.1489). A body with a rock surface (rock in its make-up, no carbon rock, no envelope that is most of its mass) holds beryllium as an element (9Be, natural) and carries 10Be and 26Al over the beryllium and aluminium of its surface layer, fraction being the atoms of the isotope over the atoms of the element in that layer. Two parts add: the part made in the rock itself and the part that fell out of the air. The in-situ part starts from the lunar anchor, the 10Be of a surface with no air and no field at saturation (1.05e10 atoms/g, from the 10 dpm/kg measured in the lunar basalt 74275, ISO32), with 26Al the 6.75 production ratio over the half-lives (ISO33); it is cut by the air column over the surface (exp(-X/150 g/cm2), the law of the air's own production, ISO14), by the dipole (NO_FIELD_FACTOR, ISO15) and by the ocean over ocean_fraction of the surface (a few meters of water stop the cascade, so the covered part makes none), and grown towards saturation by 1 - exp(-lambda t) over the star's age. The fallout is the air's own production of 10Be (nitrogen and oxygen as targets, 0.029 atoms/cm2/s on the Earth, ISO29) and of 26Al (argon, 1280 atoms/cm2/a, ISO30) over the decay constant, spread over the layer (ISO31, 160 g/cm2). The layer's beryllium (1.5 ppm) and aluminium (from the rock's Al2O3, 15 percent where none is stored) are stand-ins (ISO34). On the Earth the fallout is nearly all of the 10Be and the in-situ part a thousandth of it; on a body with no air the whole cascade reaches the rock and the Moon's 10Be/9Be of 1e-7 results. 26Al is one row with the short-lived tracer (the tables hold one row per isotope): its fraction is their sum and its origin the larger part's.

6. Limits, and what is next
