1. What exists in the code (read on 2026-10-17)

  • Stored per star. metallicity_fe_h on stars and bright_stars (migration 0095) and on galaxy_components (clusters and satellites, which also carry a seed), age_gy, lifespan_gy, mass_kg, yerkes_class, temperature, luminosity, radius and uid (80 bits in the first layout, 96 bits by default since Boss, 2026-10-15; nullable). bright_stars also holds population, seed, initial_mass_sol and phase_end_age_gy. The stars table has no population and no initial-mass column; the population is a column only for the bright stars and otherwise lives in the sector's POPULATION setting at generation. Neutron stars and black holes have their own tables (mass, radius or horizon, spin) and no [Fe/H].
  • How [Fe/H] is drawn (physics/metallicity.py, tuning.py 756-768): normal about a population mean, then clamped to (-3.5, +0.6). Means and sigmas: young 0.00 and 0.10, intermediate 0.00 and 0.15, old -0.20 and 0.25, bulge -0.05 and 0.40, nuclear cluster +0.20 and 0.40, nuclear disk +0.20 and 0.30, halo -1.6 inside 25 kpc and -2.2 outside with 0.5. The disk gradient is 0.06 dex/kpc ([R1856] gives 0.053, part A 2.6; GEN.405 owns it). The stream is metallicity:{mass}:{age}:{distance} or metallicity:bright:{seed}, separate from every other draw. A binary's secondary copies its primary's value (generation/binary.py 136); cluster and satellite stars take their component's peak with sigma 0.15 and 0.30 (COMPONENT_FE_H_SIGMA).
  • What reads it: planet occurrence, the disk's solids (the star's metal mass fraction over the Sun-like reference's, Z/Z0, GEN.464; it was 10^[Fe/H], which is 1.7 to 3 times too low for a metal-poor high-alpha star), giant factor 10^(2 [Fe/H]), the close-binary odds and the horizontal-branch colour shares. There is no X, Y or Z, no element ratio, no alpha enhancement and no age-metallicity link. Age and [Fe/H] are drawn independently.
  • Remnant figures: main-sequence lifetime 10 Gy times M^-2.5; white dwarfs use the Kalirai IFMR (0.109 M + 0.394, clamped 0.5 to 1.35 Msun) and a radius of 5800 km times M^(-1/3); neutron stars draw mass uniformly in 1.1 to 2.2 Msun and radius uniformly in 10 to 13 km; black holes 5 to 20 Msun with spin 0 to 0.998.

3. The composition of a star from the stored values

Inputs, all stored or fixed constants: [Fe/H] (call it F), age, kind, mass, the optional population, and a key. Model constants are in the appendix and trace to part A 2.2, 2.4 and 2.7.

  1. Key. abundance:{uid} (the star's uid; where it is null, abundance:{mass}:{age}:{distance}, the quantities that already seed the [Fe/H] stream; a bright star uses abundance:bright:{seed}). A stream of its own, so no earlier draw moves. Draws in fixed order: u (branch), four normals (C, O, Mg, Si), u (CEMP), u (CEMP carbon). All are always consumed.
  2. Branch. thin if age < THICK_START_GY (8.0 Gy, D10); otherwise high-alpha if u < P(F) = 1/(1+exp((F+0.350)/0.132)) ([R0349] fit, part A 2.6), else thin. Because u is fixed per star, the branch flips at most once as F falls, so an edited [Fe/H] never flickers. P(F) is 0.50 at -0.35, 0.757 at -0.5, 0.968 at -0.8 and 0.9999 at -1.6, so the halo is high-alpha without any population label. The fit comes from part A; [R0349] is not among Boss's uploads, so it could not be checked against the paper (partly verified).
  3. Means [X/Fe] for C, O, Mg, Si, Ca from the hinge fits of part A 2.7 (N14 NLTE for C and O), with F clamped to each branch's sampled range (thin -0.9 to +0.5, high-alpha -1.6 to +0.1).
  4. Residuals: the four normals through the Cholesky factor of the BF16 correlation matrix (C-O 0.56, C-Mg 0.69, C-Si 0.71, O-Mg 0.36, O-Si 0.37, Mg-Si 0.77), times the branch sigma (thin 0.044, 0.052, 0.066, 0.042; high 0.050, 0.065, 0.073, 0.073 for C, O, Mg, Si).
  5. Others: S, Ar follow Ca, Ne follows O, everything else [X/Fe] = 0 (N not fitted). GEN.1360 added nine elements to the scale (log eps from A21 Table 2; Li is the CI-chondrite 3.25 and takes no diffusion offset): Ti, V, Cr and Mn have straight-line means by branch fitted to the 1,615 dwarfs of BF16 with the repository's own branch (scripts/fit_stellar_xfe_iron_peak.py; Ti is +0.14 dex higher on the high-alpha branch at [Fe/H] -0.7, as the alpha elements are) and a residual correlated with C, O, Mg, Si and Ca (the nine-by-nine table, whose first five rows are those of step 4); P and Zn have the trend of the printed bins of [R0349, R2785] for both branches over the bins' [Fe/H] range; Co, Cu and Li are solar-scaled with a scatter (the papers opened print no value); P, Zn, Co, Cu and Li residuals are independent. The 14 normals are drawn in the order of StarDraws.normals, the nine new ones last of all. Cl and F stay as mixture_rocky has them. Registry rows XE1 to XE7 (gap-filling-plan 6.215).
  6. CEMP: for F < -2, with probability 0.25, [C/Fe] is replaced by 0.7 + 1.3 u (Frebel and Norris: 20 to 30 percent, [C/Fe] > 0.7). Part A's rise to probability 1 below -4 is unreachable because the stored [Fe/H] stops at -3.5.
  7. X, Y, Z and elements. r = Z/X = (sum over metals of PROTO_i 10^(F + [X_i/Fe]) + remainder 10^F) / X0; X = (1 - Y_P)/(1 + (1+g) r); Z = r X; Y = 1 - X - Z; each element w_i = X (PROTO_i/X0) 10^(F + [X_i/Fe]). Y_P = 0.24672 ([R3544], READ). g = 1.674, derived as (1 - Y_P - X0)/Z0 - 1, not the rounded 1.68 of part A 2.4: with 1.68 the Sun reproduces X to 1e-4 only (0.71201 against 0.7121), with the derived g exactly. The observed dY/dZ is 2.1 +- 0.9 (READ), so 1.674 is 0.5 sigma low and inside it. The 14 listed elements cover 99.7 percent of Z0; the remaining 4.9e-5 scales with Fe (partly verified, flagged).
  8. Ratios: log eps_i = A21 + F + [X_i/Fe]; Fe/Si, Mg/Si, C/O, O/Si as number and mass ratios (mass factors 1.9884, 0.8654, 0.7507 and 0.5697).
  9. Phase shifts (additive on the birth values, applied by class): subgiant IV none; red giant III and II first dredge-up (section 10, example F); supergiants IB to 0 open (the grid stops at 2.5 Msun); Wolf-Rayet and carbon stars from the flags of GEN.243 and GEN.249; Pop III only if a kind is added (X = 0.75328, Y = 0.24672, Z = 0, no draw).
  10. Radioactive birth abundances for GEN.459: Eu from the Eu fit, Th = Eu x 0.405 x 10^[Th/Eu] with [Th/Eu] = 0.014 +- 0.045 ([R0479], READ), U/Th birth 0.572; K not fitted. Limit: Th itself is measured near solar [Fe/H] only (slope -0.338 +- 0.049 inside +-0.1 dex, master 15.1), so Th elsewhere is inferred through Eu (partly verified). Cross-check on U/Th with [R3416] (first edition, read in Boss's papers: Th 83.4 ppb, U 21.8 ppb): decayed back 4.568 Gy (appendix birth_u_th) the mass ratio is 0.554; the Sun's atomic 0.572 is 0.587 by mass, so the two sit 6 percent apart. Our files' "[R3417]" values (Th 0.0849, U 0.0229 ppm; 1.8 and 5.0 percent above) cannot be confirmed and give 0.572, so the label here is "2003 edition" and the 6 percent is carried as the uncertainty on U/Th. Rule applied: verified for the label; most permissive for the spread.

Z/Z0 against 10^[Fe/H] (recomputed here; matches part A 2.5 to three digits in Z/Z0): thin +0.2 gives 0.862 (neon on O) or 0.884 (neon on Mg); thin 0.0 gives 0.981 or 0.988; high-alpha 0.0 gives 1.298 or 1.274; thin -0.5 gives 1.380 or 1.386; high-alpha -0.5 gives 2.043 or 1.962; high-alpha -1.0 gives 2.982 or 2.760; -2.0 gives 3.027 or 2.798; -3.0 gives 3.031 or 2.802. GEN.464's test reads this table.

5. The open questions of the item, answered

Q1. Are the branch and the four offsets stored or derived from the seed? Derive. Part A 3.3 and part B list five new columns; GEN.440 and the item rule them out, and nothing needs them stored: every value is a function of the stored [Fe/H], age and key. The branch draw u is fixed per star so edits are stable; changing a star's [Fe/H], age or mass in the admin (edit-everything.md) re-derives the composition with no stored state to repair. Cost: one 4 by 4 Cholesky factor (a constant) and seven draws per call.

Q2. Neon and oxygen. Take oxygen from the [R3294] NLTE triplet fit (plateau +0.606 on the high-alpha branch; the [R0349] own-O plateau is +0.582, the two differ by up to 0.1 dex elsewhere, part A 2.7 note b) and let neon follow oxygen, with a NE_FOLLOWS switch to magnesium. Neon is not measured in photospheres (set values range 7.93 to 8.15, part A 2.1), so it is the least certain entry of Z. The two options differ in Z/Z0 by at most 8 percent (2.982 against 2.760 at -1.0); the generic single-[alpha/Fe] model gives about 2.0, so both stand 1.4 to 1.5 times above it. The physical reason for neon with oxygen is recalled, not read: open. Part A 3.2 step 4 says neon follows magnesium while the filing of GEN.464 says the lane preferred oxygen; this draft follows GEN.464 and keeps both numbers as the filing requires.

Q3. Age-metallicity link (A-D3). [Fe/H] stays unlinked to age (D12). The age gate carries the one link, and Boss's papers move it. [R1863] (read): the thick disk formed from about 13 to about 8 Gy ago, with the age scale uncertain by 1 to 1.5 Gy; the inner thin disk starts about 8 Gy ago and the outer thin disk 9 to 10 Gy ago; thick/thin mass ratio at least 0.2, possibly up to about 1. The first draft gated at 3.0 Gy, the project's intermediate/old boundary, which would make 3 to 8 Gy stars thick-disk. The paper contradicts that, so the paper wins and the gate is 8.0 Gy (range 6.5 to 9.5). Rule applied: verified.

The cost, computed here (appendix P(F), 3e5 draws, ages uniform): for the project's old disk (3 to 10 Gy, [Fe/H] N(-0.20, 0.25)) the high-alpha share is 0.329 with the gate at 3.0, 0.165 at 6.5, 0.095 at 8.0 and 0.023 at 9.5. For the solar-neighbourhood mix N(-0.10, 0.22) with ages uniform 0 to 10 Gy it is 0.216 with no gate, 0.151 at 3.0 and 0.043 at 8.0. P(F) is Bensby's fraction over all ages, so applying it only above 8 Gy under-produces thick-disk stars: 0.095 of old-disk stars, against a thick disk of at least 0.167 of disk mass (ratio 0.2; a number share is not a mass share, and the whole-disk ratio belongs to the density lane, GEN.134). A stand-in correction multiplies P by (10 - 3)/(10 - gate) = 3.5 and caps it at 1; it gives 0.189 for the old disk, but it jumps at 10 Gy and gives 0.23 at [Fe/H] = 0, so it is not adopted. The honest fix needs the age-resolved thick fraction from [R0349] (not among Boss's uploads, so every Bensby-based value here is part A's, unchecked against the paper) or [R3532] (not uploaded; [R3531] covers one galaxy, FCC 170, with about 3 Gy age resolution and no thick/thin ratio, so it cannot test the gate). Open. At [Fe/H] = -0.5, where the branches differ most, Z/Z0 is 1.380 thin against 2.043 high-alpha (section 3), so a thin-branch star of 3 to 8 Gy there has 32 percent less Z than a thick-disk star would.

Also open, for GEN.134: the project's old window ends at 10 Gy and the halo starts at 10 Gy (tuning.py 718), while Haywood has the thick disk forming back to 13 Gy and Aumer and Binney fit a disk age of 12.6 Gyr (tau_max, Table 6 fit), so the old disk window stops 2.6 to 3 Gy short of the disk's start. Not changed here.

Independence of [Fe/H] and age otherwise loses little. [R0349] gives -0.027 dex/Gy with scatter 0.265 dex (second-hand through part A, partly verified), which for ages uniform over 0 to 10 Gy is an explained sd of 0.078 dex and a correlation of 0.28. A young star (sigma 0.10) is below -0.5 with probability 3e-7 and an intermediate one (sigma 0.15) 4e-4. Linking would move every stored [Fe/H]. Bensby's kinematically thick fraction of 0.08 at 0 to 2 Gy (shape only) is not reproduced; Haywood's age floor says it is a kinematic misclassification, so a young alpha-rich star is not produced on purpose.

6. How the abundances change the star, and whether the tables of GEN.407 change

GEN.407 is built (PR #1985, epoch 4); GEN.502 holds what it left. What composition does, with what the repo research can support:

  • Lifetime. Fuel is the hydrogen fraction X. Against the proto-solar 0.7121: example A 1.014, B 1.036, C 1.053, Pop III 1.058 (section 10). Burning rate depends on luminosity, which depends on opacity and mean molecular weight; no read source in the repo gives dL/dZ or dT/dZ. GEN.467 should take (mass, X, Z); the project's 10 Gy M^-2.5 is replaced there. Built (GEN.467, 2026-10-17): physics/ms_lifetime.py is the fit of [R2055] (MNRAS 315, 543, eqs. 4 to 7 and the Appendix coefficients, read from the paper), a function of mass and [Fe/H] (Z = 0.0154 x 10^[Fe/H], the proto-solar Z above); a solar mass lasts 10.2 Gy, 20 solar masses 9.0 Myr (5.6 before), 150 solar masses 3.2 Myr (0.04 before). Every caller passes the default [Fe/H] of 0 until the composition is drawn first (9.0). For a check, the Sun's central hydrogen from three models is 0.708 at zero age, 0.340 at 4.57 Gy, 0.145 at 6.5 and 0.008 at 8 (READ); the fit X_c = X0 (1 - (t/8)^1.15) returns 0.336, 0.150 and 0 (error under 0.01).
  • Opacity. Z sets the Rosseland opacity. The only opacity law in the repo research is the hydrogen-burning edge M_edge = 0.0865 (kappa/1e-2)^(-1/9) Msun ([R0606], READ): a 10 percent opacity change moves the edge 1 percent. Nothing else in the generator reads it.
  • Luminosity, radius, temperature, colour on the main sequence. Boss's cluster text says metal-poor stars are hotter and brighter at the same mass (globular-clusters.md 34, an unchecked note) and puts the 12 Gy turn-off at 0.85 Msun against 0.93 in the code; no tracks were read (MIST and BaSTI were unreachable, stellar tables check). Open; the tables stay Z-independent as a stand-in. Colour is a blackbody of the stored temperature (GEN.416); composition does not change it in this design.

8. How a star's iron, magnesium and silicon set the rocky planets' core fraction (GEN.428)

The planet draw reads the host's number ratios from section 3. With mu the standard atomic weights (Fe 55.845, Mg 24.305, Si 28.0855, Ca 40.078, Al 26.9815, O 15.999), the core mass fraction with all iron in the core is, from [R4467: Eq. 8] as read in part B 2.4:

CMF = (Fe/Mg) mu_Fe / [(Fe/Mg) mu_Fe + mu_bar], mu_bar = (mu_Mg + mu_O) + (Ca/Mg)(mu_Ca + mu_O) + (Al/Mg)(mu_Al + 1.5 mu_O) + (Si/Mg)(mu_Si + 2 mu_O).

Check: Fe/Mg 0.71, Si/Mg 0.79, Ca/Mg 0.07, Al/Mg 0.09 give 0.2917 (part B table: 0.292). Ca and Al come from [Ca/Fe] (section 3) and the Bensby [Al/Fe] fit (thin 0.032 - 0.029 min(F - 0.06, 0); high 0.225 - 0.298 max(F + 0.48, 0)). Results:

9. Clusters and multi-star systems (GEN.269)

  • Multi-star systems. All members form from one cloud: they share the group's [Fe/H] (already true for a binary, binary.py 136; for the member tree this is the storage half of GEN.269, Core 2's finding of 2026-10-16, 4:58 AM EDT), and the branch and four offsets come from the key of the group's primary (abundance:{primary uid}), not the member's. Birth X, Y, Z and ratios are identical across members. Each member then takes its own phase shift (a giant sibling differs from a dwarf one) and its own remnant layers; the pollution and carbon-star flags are per member. A merger product keeps the real system age for the age gate. Mass-transfer pollution of an accretor is open. Within-group scatter: 0, open; no source was read ([R1943]'s 0.11 dex is between catalogues, not between siblings).
  • Clusters. The cluster record holds its [Fe/H] (peaks -1.55 and -0.55, CLUSTER_FE_H_GROUPS); its stars draw [Fe/H] about it with sigma 0.15 (recalled in the code, open). Branch and residuals are keyed on the cluster seed (abundance:cluster:{seed}), so the residuals are shared and only the F-dependent means move with each star. A cluster of 10 to 13 Gy ages passes the age gate and the logistic: at -1.55 it is high-alpha with probability 0.9999, at -0.55 with 0.82. Satellite galaxies follow the same rule as a stand-in; their real alpha pattern is not in the research (open).

14.2 The star's page (UX.254, as built)

The star's sheet (describe/sheet.py, the page and GET /api/objects/<ref>/sheet) shows its chemistry in the Composition group, worked out when the sheet is made from the stored class, state, mass, [Fe/H] and age by star_makeup: the disk population (thin disk, or thick disk or halo, alpha-rich), [alpha/Fe] (stellar_abundances.alpha_enhancement, the mean [X/Fe] of O, Mg, Si and Ca, in dex), the metal mass fraction Z the star formed with, and, for a red giant, the helium added by the first dredge-up. No column and no stored value: the same star reads the same on every request. The draws are keyed as the planets' host ratios are (bulk_draw.star_key: the stored mass in kg and the distance of its sector from the galactic center, light-years, which sectors.galactic_radius_pc gives; the galaxy's default for a star in no placed sector), so the page and the planets agree. A star with no [Fe/H], a black hole or neutron star, or a kind whose downloaded data are missing shows none of the rows. A remnant whose mass changed after its planets were drawn is keyed on the mass it has now, and the planets on the mass they saw (an open item; the main-sequence and giant stars, which keep their mass, agree).

THICK_START_GY = 8.0                                                           # [R1863], range 6.5 to 9.5 (D10)
G = (1 - Y_P - X0) / Z0 - 1                                                    # 1.674026
REST = Z0 - sum(PROTO.values())                                                # 4.95e-5, scaled with Fe
SD = dict(thin=dict(C=.044, O=.052, Mg=.066, Si=.042), high=dict(C=.050, O=.065, Mg=.073, Si=.073))
CORR = [[1,.56,.69,.71],[.56,1,.36,.37],[.69,.36,1,.77],[.71,.37,.77,1]]      # C, O, Mg, Si (BF16)

def xfe(el, F, br):                                                            # part A 2.7
    F = min(max(F, -.9), .5) if br == "thin" else min(max(F, -1.6), .1)
    t = br == "thin"
    return {"Mg": (.006 - .343*min(F-.03, 0)) if t else (.357 - .373*max(F+.73, 0)),
            "Si": (.017 - .166*min(F+.07, 0)) if t else (.253 - .199*max(F+.99, 0)),
            "Ca": (-.041 - .163*min(F-.42, 0)) if t else (.282 - .251*max(F+.99, 0)),
            "O":  (-.011 - .373*F) if t else (.606 - .495*max(F+.91, 0)),
            "C":  (-.030 - .163*F) if t else (.191 - .521*max(F+.10, 0))}[el]

def chol(a):
    n = len(a); L = [[0.0]*n for _ in range(n)]
    for i in range(n):
        for j in range(i+1):
            s = sum(L[i][k]*L[j][k] for k in range(j))
            L[i][j] = math.sqrt(a[i][i]-s) if i == j else (a[i][j]-s)/L[j][j]
    return L

def derive(F, age, key, ne="O"):
    rng = random.Random(key); u = rng.random(); z = [rng.gauss(0, 1) for _ in range(4)]
    u_cemp, u_c = rng.random(), rng.random()
    P = 0.0 if age < THICK_START_GY else 1/(1+math.exp((F+0.350)/0.132))
    br = "high" if u < P else "thin"
    L = chol(CORR); cz = [sum(L[i][k]*z[k] for k in range(4)) for i in range(4)]
    d = {e: xfe(e, F, br) + cz[i]*SD[br][e] for i, e in enumerate(("C", "O", "Mg", "Si"))}
    if F < -2 and u_cemp < (0.25 if F > -4 else 1.0): d["C"] = 0.7 + 1.3*u_c    # CEMP
    d["Ca"] = xfe("Ca", F, br); d["S"] = d["Ar"] = d["Ca"]
    d["Ne"] = d["O"] if ne == "O" else d["Mg"]
    for e in ("N", "Na", "Al", "K", "Fe", "Ni"): d[e] = 0.0
    r = (sum(PROTO[e]*10**(F+d[e]) for e in PROTO) + REST*10**F) / X0          # Z/X
    X = (1-Y_P)/(1+(1+G)*r); Z = r*X
    return dict(branch=br, d=d, X=X, Y=1-X-Z, Z=Z)

def ratio(d, a, b):                                                             # mass ratio; F cancels, d[Fe] = 0
    return 10**(EPS[a]-EPS[b]+d[a]-d[b]) * AW[a]/AW[b]

def cmf(F, d, al):                                                              # al = [Al/Fe]; section 8
    n = lambda a, x: 10**(EPS[a]+F+x)
    mg = n("Mg", d["Mg"]); fe, si, ca, alu = n("Fe", 0)/mg, n("Si", d["Si"])/mg, n("Ca", d["Ca"])/mg, n("Al", al)/mg
    mubar = (AW["Mg"]+AW["O"]) + ca*(AW["Ca"]+AW["O"]) + alu*(AW["Al"]+1.5*AW["O"]) + si*(AW["Si"]+2*AW["O"])
    return fe*AW["Fe"] / (fe*AW["Fe"] + mubar)

# Remnants ([R4361]; Msun 1.98841e30 kg, Rsun 695.7e6 m as in `constants.py`)
G_, c_, hbar, h, me, mu = 6.6743e-11, 299792458.0, 1.054571817e-34, 2*math.pi*1.054571817e-34, 9.1093837015e-31, 1.66053906660e-27
def white_dwarf(xc):                      # zero-temperature Chandrasekhar star, mu_e = 2; integrate dx/dr and dm/dr to x = 1e-4
    from scipy.integrate import solve_ivp
    A = math.pi*me**4*c_**5/(3*h**3); B = 8*math.pi*mu*2*(me*c_)**3/(3*h**3)
    f = lambda r, y: [-G_*y[1]*B*y[0]**3/r**2 / (8*A*y[0]**4/math.sqrt(1+y[0]**2)), 4*math.pi*r*r*B*y[0]**3]
    ev = lambda r, y: y[0]-1e-4; ev.terminal = True
    s = solve_ivp(f, [1, 5e7], [xc, 4/3*math.pi*B*xc**3], events=ev, rtol=1e-10, atol=[1e-12, 1e10], method="LSODA")
    return s.y[1][-1]/1.98841e30, s.t[-1]/695.7e6             # Msun, Rsun; a grid of 80 xc in 0.3..12 is interpolated
def black_hole(m, a):                     # m in Msun; returns r_s km, T_H K, r_+ km, ISCO in GM/c^2, efficiency
    M = m*1.98841e30; rg = G_*M/c_**2
    z1 = 1+(1-a*a)**(1/3)*((1+a)**(1/3)+(1-a)**(1/3)); z2 = math.sqrt(3*a*a+z1*z1)
    isco = 3+z2-math.sqrt((3-z1)*(3+z1+2*z2))
    return 2*rg/1e3, hbar*c_**3/(8*math.pi*G_*M*1.380649e-23), rg*(1+math.sqrt(1-a*a))/1e3, isco, 1-math.sqrt(1-2/(3*isco))

def birth_u_th(th_ppm, u_ppm, t=4.568):                                         # mass ratio U/Th t Gy back; 235U atom fraction 0.7204 percent
    l = lambda h: math.log(2)/h
    a8, a5, m8, m5 = 0.992742, 0.007204, 238.0508, 235.0439
    fu = (a8*math.exp(l(4.468)*t)*m8 + a5*math.exp(l(0.7038)*t)*m5) / (a8*m8 + a5*m5)
    return u_ppm*fu / (th_ppm*math.exp(l(14.05)*t))                             # 2003 edition 0.0834, 0.0218: 0.554; "2014" labels: 0.572
