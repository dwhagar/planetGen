What it replaces

GEN.703 gave an air one grey optical depth from band centres whose strengths were stand-ins. It could not say what a 15 micron band does when the water vapour thins aloft, put Titan twice too warm, and warmed Earth by a third to a half of what a doubling of carbon dioxide is measured to. Boss's decision of 4:01 PM EDT on 2026-10-24 was to use the public line-list data, downloaded at setup and reduced to small tables. This is that.

Setup downloads each file, checks its sha256, reduces it and deletes it (transient pieces). The reduction keeps the band mean of the k-distribution: all the (wavenumber, g) values of a band are sorted by k, cut at the new g edges, and each interval's mean is the new k. The result is 129 bands (20 cm-1 wide near 10 to 5 micron, 50, 100 and 250 cm-1 wide further out) by 10 g points (six under g = 0.9, four over it so the strongest lines are resolved), on the 3400 K tables cut at 2000 K. The tables are in the cache folder, never in the repository. Generation requires them (GEN.590): a missing file makes fetch.DataMissing name the dataset and the setup command, and a run told --disregard-missing-data takes the labelled stand-ins instead.

The optical depth of a layer

line_opacity.layer_depth(prepared, temperature, pressure, water_fraction, molecules_cm2) returns the depth of every layer on 130 bands (the first is 0 to 200 cm-1, below the tables) by 10 g points:

  1. Line absorption of H2O, CO2 and CH4: the table read at the layer's temperature (linear in 1/T) and pressure (linear in ln P), times the molecules of that gas.
  2. Random overlap of gases in a band: all 10 x 10 sums with their joint weights, sorted again and cut at the g edges (overlap). Where one gas is thin (largest depth under 0.05) it adds only its mean.
  3. The water vapour continuum (MT_CKD 4.3): self and foreign coefficients at eight wavenumbers between 200 and 3600 cm-1, temperature exponent on the self part.
  4. Collision-induced absorption: k(T) x1 x2 n N for each pair of the air, k the band mean at the layer's temperature.
  5. Gases without a table (stand-ins IR1, IR2): a plateau at each band of the gas table with exponential wings, sorted into a distribution.
  6. The far wings (rows FW1 to FW7): the tables cut every line at 25 cm-1, and a Venus-like air needs what lies beyond. physics/co2_far_wing.py (GEN.1019, rows FW1 to FW5; it replaced the flat stand-in IR3): each carbon dioxide band's Lorentz tail beyond 25 cm-1 times the [R3490] sub-Lorentzian factor, out to 500 cm-1, with the band strengths taken from the carbon dioxide table at its lowest pressure. GEN.1020, wired into the column through solve(..., cloud=(tau_550, radius_um)): the acid cloud's infrared opacity (cloud_infrared, Mie droplets from the HITRAN sulfuric acid indices, rows CF1 to CF5) and the far wings of water, sulfur dioxide and carbon monoxide in a carbon dioxide air (rows FW6, FW7). With both, the clear-sky Venus-like test air reaches 757 K (measured 735 K); the wing alone gave 539 K.

The column

radiative_convective.solve(partial_pressures, gravity, absorbed_flux, aloft_share=0, aloft_pressure=None) cuts the air into 40 layers equally spaced in ln P from a ten-thousandth of the surface pressure, and finds the surface temperature at which

  • the profile follows the adiabat of the mixture (heat capacity from species_thermo at each temperature; the water pseudo-adiabat once the vapour saturates, or in an air with no water the methane pseudo-adiabat, IR14; cold hydrogen at its ortho and para equilibrium, IR15) up to the tropopause, and is in radiative equilibrium above it, and
  • the infrared flux to space equals the absorbed flux.

The flux is the two-stream form with a diffusivity factor of 1.66 and a source linear in optical depth between level temperatures, solved band by band and g point by g point. The water vapour has Manabe and Wetherald's (1967) relative humidity, RH = RH_s (p/p_s - 0.02) / 0.98, with the surface humidity the mixture's own vapour over the saturation pressure, and none above the coldest level below (the cold trap). aloft_share puts that share of the absorbed flux at aloft_pressure_pa as a Gaussian in ln P instead of at the ground: the sunlight a haze takes (the anti-greenhouse).

GEN.1030: with the dry adiabat alone the ice giants came out 20 percent too warm at 1 bar. Methane condenses near 80 K and 1.3 bar there and the condensation shallows the lapse rate, and the heat capacity of hydrogen below 200 K is the equilibrium one (4.4 R at 60 K against the 3.5 R the fit of species_thermo holds from 200 K up), which shallows it again. At the measured effective temperatures the column gives Uranus 79 K (76 measured), Neptune 78 K (72) and Jupiter 167 K (166); Saturn comes out 149 K against 134 K measured, a miss this change does not close. The check is test_the_giants_sit_near_their_measured_temperature_at_one_bar (Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune).

A step of the adiabat that began unsaturated and crossed the saturation corner used to take the saturated slope for its whole length; the branch is now fixed for a step and the step is cut at the crossing (test_the_adiabat_is_continuous_and_rises_with_the_surface_temperature_across_the_saturation_corner).

The runaway greenhouse (GEN.1194)

The question was whether the model's outgoing flux has a plateau, the Simpson-Nakajima limit, as water vapor thickens: a first look at the generator's own air (Manabe and Wetherald's humidity, IR7, 77 percent at the surface) found the clear-sky flux still rising by 2.3 to 2.8 W/m2 per K at 334 K, 414 W/m2 against 282 to 310 published, and blamed the water window, the continuum's temperature dependence and the line wings. A band-by-band look at the 321 K column showed the opacity is not at fault: the MT_CKD continuum gives the 1,000 cm-1 window an optical depth of 2.2 (176 kg of vapor per m2, WATER_CONTINUUM, IR4), the same as a hand count with the coefficient and its temperature exponent, and the window leaves 77 percent of the surface's blackbody flux only because the vapor falls to nothing aloft in the Earth's profile.

With the relative humidity held at the surface's value up the whole troposphere (saturated=True, the air the published limits use) and the cloud left out (clear_sky=True), on 1 bar of nitrogen with 40 Pa of carbon dioxide and vapor at saturation at the surface:

absorbed flux, W/m2 285 300 315 330
saturated profile, surface K 307.6 322.4 342.5 374.6
Manabe and Wetherald profile (IR7), surface K 293.2 298.8 304.5 309.6
slope of the saturated surface, K per W/m2 1.0 1.3 2.1 to 2.4

The saturated air has the elbow of the runaway (the flux stops being enough near 300 to 315 W/m2, inside 282 to 310 [R1607] plus a few percent for the nitrogen and not pure steam); the Earth's profile has none. So the generator, which keeps the Earth's profile for a world with an ocean (IR7), leaves the runaway to the water-loss history (runaway_history, GEN.639) and to the steam start of GEN.1238, as before; saturated and clear_sky are the way to ask the model for the limit (stand-in IR17, T1, L0), and test_radiative_convective_real.py holds the elbow and the finding. The 3D models that put the inner edge at 1.1 times the present flux or more (a dry subtropics) are the reason not to make the generator's own air saturated. Pure steam airs are not part of this check: the model takes 2 to 3 minutes and does not converge for them at fixed vapor, a limit already stated below.

Limits (registry rows IR1 to IR17, gap-filling-plan.md 6.6b)

  • The cloud is a share of the outgoing flux (IR12), not a layer, and there is no condensation but water. The humidity profile is Earth's. Sunlight is deposited by the caller, never absorbed by the gases here.
  • The stratosphere is clear-sky radiative equilibrium without ozone heating. A gas without a table has stand-in bands.
  • The tables hold 100 K as the lowest temperature and 100 bar as the highest pressure (edge value beyond); H2 and He broadening is under-represented for H2O and CO2.
  • Venus passes only with the acid cloud's infrared opacity (the sourced far wing alone leaves it near 540 K; the droplet radius within the published modes spans about 620 to 820 K). Very dense or pure-steam airs take 20 to 30 seconds.
  • Speed: one to several seconds a call; the generator reads the fitted table (GEN.850).
  • A drier Earth (1000 Pa of vapour against 400 Pa) gives a noisier doubling (1.5 to 2.0 K held) because the tropopause moves one layer at a time (a follow-up).

Wiring

radiative_convective.warming takes one to several seconds a call (up to half a minute for a dense steam or carbon dioxide air), too slow for the several calls a planet's fixed point makes. GEN.850 puts a fitted table in front of it (greenhouse_table.py, greenhouse-table.md): the generator reads the table, the model is what the table is fitted to, and infrared_opacity.py and the grey closure of greenhouse.py are deleted. This module's band stand-ins for gases without a table (IR1, IR2) stay in line_opacity.py alone. The cloud of the Earth's longwave effect is IR12 and the haze's sunlight is IR8 and IR13.
