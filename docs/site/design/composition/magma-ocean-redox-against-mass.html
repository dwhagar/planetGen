Status: research note, written on the night of 2026-10-24 (as the lane reported) by the Citations lane. Rows a1, a2 and a3 of the atmosphere possibility table (scripts/atmosphere_recipes_data.py, part 09 of docs/design/composition/atmosphere-composition-parts/) are checked against first-hand sources. GEN.482 (the check) changed nothing the generator draws; GEN.482b (section 7, done the same night) wired the findings; no epoch, because nothing in the generator draws the mixture yet (the golden battery is unchanged).

1. The answer in short

  • a1, the magma-ocean surface redox against mass. The present mean, clamp(-2 + 1.25 (log10 M + 2), -2, 0.5), is inside the published ranges for all three Solar System anchors (table 3.1: the Moon, Mars and Earth), within 0.2 log units of the middle of each. The row is therefore a checked fit to three sourced points, not a free guess. What the sources do not support: the width (0.5 flat is too narrow for Earth, where the published span is 3.0 log units wide, so a standard deviation of about 0.9), and the flat clamp at +0.5 above 1 Earth mass (the physics keeps rising up to the pressure where the calculations stop, about 140 GPa at the magma-ocean base). Both are now wired (section 7).
  • a2, the later upper mantle. 3.5 + 2 log10 M with width 1.0 passes through the three sourced anchors within one standard deviation (Earth IW+3.5, Mars about IW to IW+1, Moon IW to IW-2). It is a three-point fit and nothing more: no source gives the law above 1 Earth mass, and at 10 Earth masses the code gives +5.5, which nothing supports.
  • a1 and a2 are drawn independently, but the sources say they are linked: the later mantle is never reduced below its own magma-ocean surface and the gap grows with mass (section 5). GEN.482b takes the larger of the two.
  • a3, small-body building blocks. The check bracket in the row (-1.4 to 0) only covers the reduced half of Schaefer and Fegley's Table 2. The same table puts carbonaceous material (CI, CV) at IW+1.6 to IW+2.6 at 1 bar and 1500 K. Row a3's draw (normal, center -1.25, width 0.22, from white-dwarf debris) is for differentiated rocky debris and does not describe C-complex bodies (section 6).
  • Na and K condensation temperatures (the primary source the gap note asked for): [R2726: Table 8], 50 percent condensation at 10^-4 bar: Na 958 K, K 1006 K, both in feldspar (section 8).
  • Not found: the degassed 40Ar fraction against tectonic regime (no source with a number other than Earth's about 43 percent in part 07), [R1953] (paywalled, Boss cannot fetch it), [R1491] coefficients (the earlier paper note says equations without coefficients).
  • On the row states: a1 and a2 move from DESIGN to DERIVED in the table (state "partly verified", with the limits written in the row). a3 stays as it is (section 6). This does not change a number the generator draws.

3. The surface redox of the magma ocean against mass (row a1)

3.1 The anchors

delta IW is log fO2 minus the iron-wuestite buffer (the repo uses the [R1951] buffer, the scale of [R1951]).

Body Base of the ocean (P, delta IW) Surface of the ocean (this note's reading) Source Row a1 now
Moon (0.0123 Earth masses) 3.5 to 4.8 GPa; core-forming delta IW -2.2 to -1.5 ([R1951: Table 1] and text); Deng base 5 GPa, -2 about -2.2 to -1.2: little Fe3+ is stabilised at these pressures, so the surface stays near the core-forming value (Deng: lower end of the lunar basalts, IW-2; Hirschmann Table 1 and text) [R1075, R1951] -1.9
Mars (0.107) 14 GPa; core-forming -1.5 to -1.1 Eq. 21: -1.4 to -0.7; corrected Sossi relation Eq. 34: -0.4 to +0.2 (Fe3+/FeT 0.026 to 0.038, section 6.3 and Fig. 9 inset); Deng: about IW [R1075, R1951] -0.7
Earth (1) 28 to 53 GPa, 2250 to 4100 K; core-forming -2.5 to -1.4 (hotter is more reduced) Eq. 21: -1.2 to +1.4; Eq. 34: -0.2 to +1.8 (Fe3+/FeT 0.034 to 0.103); [R4154]: about +0.5 on its own scale, 0 on the corrected scale ([R1951] section 6.3); Deng: at the lower bound of the present mantle (IW+3.5 plus or minus 1.0), so about +2.5 [R1075, R1951, R4154] +0.5

The widest honest span for Earth is the union of the first-hand model results, -1.2 to +1.8, middle +0.3. Deng's +2.5 comes from a cold temperature profile and from the older equation of state treatment; [R1951] uses Deng's equation of state with a temperature function and the chromium correction, and is the newer work. By the project's rule (two verified sources in doubt: the newer one) the Hirschmann range is the center and Deng's value is the upper tail, kept in the width. Hirschmann himself states that the uncertainties "remain too great to establish with certainty the redox state" of the atmospheres above Earth's and Mars's magma oceans.

3.2 The published views of Earth

[R1488] lists three, and the table above sits between the first two. (i) One model: oxidized surface close to the FMQ buffer (Fe3+/Fe-total 0.03 to 0.1). (ii) [R4154]: IW+0.5 plus or minus 0.7. (iii) [R2329]: graphite flotation holding the shallow ocean down to IW-5 at low pressure. View (iii) is a different mechanism, not a spread of the same one: it is the only way the sources get far below -1.2. It stays out of the draw and is flagged as an alternative (limit of the row).

3.3 The check of the row

Body Published middle Published half-range Row a1 mean Difference
Moon -1.7 0.5 -1.9 -0.2
Mars -0.6 0.8 -0.7 -0.1
Earth +0.3 1.5 +0.5 +0.2

Every difference is inside half the published half-range, so the mean is consistent with all three. A least-squares line through the three middles has a slope of 1.0 per dex of mass against the row's 1.25; the data do not tell them apart (the Earth middle depends on an assumed core-forming temperature, 2300 to 4100 K, which Hirschmann names as the main control).

3.4 What the sources do not give

  • The width. The row uses 0.5 (Deng's one-sigma). The published half-ranges are 0.5 (Moon), 0.8 (Mars) and 1.5 (Earth), which as uniform distributions are standard deviations of 0.29, 0.46 and 0.87. A width that grows with mass from 0.5 at 0.1 Earth masses to 0.9 at 1 Earth mass fits the three: 0.5 + 0.4 clamp(log10 M + 1, 0, 1) (an L2 tweak: tuned to the published ranges, not to a statistic). The floor 0.22 the row carries from the Fe3+/FeT measurement stays.
  • Above 1 Earth mass. The row clamps at +0.5. The physics (Deng: the volume difference of FeO1.5 and FeO stays positive to 140 GPa, so the surface of a deeper ocean stays more oxidized) says the surface keeps rising with the pressure at the ocean base; none of the sources gives a super-Earth number, and both Hirschmann and Deng stop at Earth. The only first-hand slope is the Mars-to-Earth one, 0.9 to 1.25 per dex. A pressure-only slope from the three Deng points is about 2 per dex. The two bracket the extension, 0 (the clamp) to 2 per dex, center about 1. Where the base pressure passes 140 GPa the calculation does not apply; that is at about 5 Earth masses if the base pressure scales as M to the 0.55 (an assumption of this note, not a number read from a source). This is the part that stays a stand-in.
  • The graphite tail (section 3.2) and the Cr and Ti effects on the crystallised mantle ([R1951] section 6.4 and 6.5) are not mass laws and stay out.

4. The row states

  • a1: state "partly verified"; the part 09 status moves from DESIGN to DERIVED. Limit: three Solar System anchors, the width and the extension above 1 Earth mass are not sourced, the graphite-flotation alternative is not drawn.
  • a2: same, see section 5.

5. The later mantle (row a2)

Anchors (all first-hand):

Body Later upper mantle Source Row a2 now
Earth IW+3.5, constant within about 1 log unit since the early Archean; above IW+3 in spinel peridotite ([R1951] section 4) [R1075] (refs 37, 6, 7); [R1951] +3.5
Mars the most reduced basalts record IW [R3287, R3966], many up to IW+5; the primitive upper mantle IW to IW+1 [R1951] introduction and section 4; [R1075] +1.6
Moon lunar basalts IW to IW-2 [R1075] (refs 8 to 10) -0.3

With the width 1.0, Mars is +0.6 above the middle of its range and the Moon +0.7 above its middle; both are inside one standard deviation. The law is a straight-line fit to three points and says so.

Two structural facts from the sources that the row does not use:

  1. Deng and Hirschmann find that for Mars and the Moon the later mantle is close to the magma-ocean surface (no large later oxidation), and for Earth later processes add at most 1 to 3 log units (bridgmanite disproportionation, late accretion, Hirschmann section 6.4). The gap (a2 minus a1) in the sources is about 0 to 2 at the Moon, 0 to 1 at Mars and 1 to 3 at Earth. In the code it is 1.6, 2.3 and 3.0 as means, and the two rows are drawn independently, so a draw can put the later mantle below its own magma-ocean surface.
  2. Above 1 Earth mass the row is an unsupported extrapolation (5.5 at 10 Earth masses, clipped at 6).

6. Small-body building blocks (row a3)

The row, as it ships, is a normal distribution centered at -1.25 with width 0.22, from 22 polluted white dwarf values ([R1152] and 2023, already in the repo), with the Schaefer and Fegley bracket -1.4 to 0 as a check. [R3944: Table 2] gives the fits of log10 fO2 against temperature for outgassing at 1 bar. Against the [R1464] iron-wuestite line (constants from PetroBuffer, -27489/T + 6.702; the [R1951] scale the repo stores was not recomputed here and differs by a few tenths of a log unit), the polynomials give delta IW:

T (K) CI CV H EH eucrite
1000 +2.84 +2.12 -0.90 -1.43 -1.38
1200 +2.48 +1.85 -0.67 -1.19 -1.11
1500 +1.98 +1.65 -0.53 -1.03 -0.91
2000 +1.22 +1.57 -0.51 -0.98 -0.80

The authors' text agrees: the most oxidized mixture is CI at "about QFM-1" (about IW+2.6 at 1500 K by the [R1464] buffers; the polynomials above give +2.0, so the text is read off a figure) and the most reduced is EH at "about IW-1", with all mixtures between the two. The unsupported part of row a3 is therefore not its reduced end (which the white-dwarf data and Schaefer and Fegley both give, -1.0 to -0.5 at 1500 K) but its use for every small body: carbonaceous (CI, CV, C-complex) material outgasses at IW+1.6 to +2.6. The two scales also differ (white-dwarf values are core-formation redox of differentiated parents; Schaefer and Fegley are outgassing of chondritic material), so no single number covers both. The row is not changed: nothing reads a3 for a body with an air today (section 7, point 5).

7. GEN.482b: what was wired (no epoch: the generator does not draw the mixture yet)

  1. a1 width: 0.5 + 0.4 clamp(log10 M + 1, 0, 1) (0.5 at 0.1 Earth masses and below, 0.9 at 1 and above).
  2. a1 mean above 1 Earth mass: plus clamp(log10 M, 0, 0.7324), that is 1.0 log unit per dex of mass up to 5.4 Earth masses (+1.23) and held there, where the base pressure passes the 140 GPa of Deng's calculations (assuming the base pressure scales as M to the 0.55). The bracket 0 to 2 per dex stays the stated uncertainty; this is the part that stays a stand-in.
  3. a2 mean: 3.5 + 2 clamp(log10 M, -3, 0): the Earth value is held above 1 Earth mass (no source for a super-Earth's later mantle), and the 6 clip of the row is no longer reached.
  4. draw_redox takes the larger of a2 and a1 for the later mantle, so a body never moves to a later mantle more reduced than its own magma-ocean surface ([R1075, R1951]: the later processes only oxidise).
  5. a3: not changed. No code reads a3 for a body with an air today (an asteroid is airless and takes no mixture; the rocky path takes a3 only when a1 is absent), so a split by building-block type has no consumer. It waits for one (the first body that outgasses from a carbonaceous parent), with the carbonaceous range IW+1.6 to +2.6 of section 6 as its source.

8. Na and K condensation temperatures

[R2726: Table 8] (equilibrium condensation temperatures for a solar-system composition gas at 10^-4 bar total pressure; 50 percent condensation): Na 958 K (host: feldspar, the initial phase albite NaAlSi3O8 dissolving), K 1006 K (feldspar, the initial phase KAlSi3O8 dissolving). For reference in the same table: Fe 1334 K (alloy), S 664 K (troilite), Zn 726 K, Mn 1158 K, Mg 1336 K, Si 1310 K. This answers the gap note's request for a primary source and ties "K depletion follows the nebular temperature at the formation distance" to numbers; it does not by itself give the planet's K abundance (that needs the temperature at the formation distance, the disk model's, not read here).

9. Limits

  • [R1951] states that the model is an extrapolation in temperature, pressure and melt composition (section 6.2) and that the two Fe3+ models differ by 0.7 to 1.3 log units in delta IW at the same Fe3+/FeT; the ranges above carry both.
  • The pressure-scaling exponent 0.55 and the "about 5 Earth masses" are this note's assumptions.
  • [R1491, R1953] were not used: the first was read earlier (equations without coefficients), the second is paywalled and Boss cannot reach it.
  • The 40Ar degassed fraction against tectonic regime was not found in any open source read; part 07's Earth value of about 43 percent stays the only anchor.
