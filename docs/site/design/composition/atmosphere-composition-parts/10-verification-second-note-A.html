1.4 Claim 6: Hertz-Knudsen-Langmuir and sticking

# Claim Verdict Evidence Label
6a dQ/dt = alpha_s P_vap sqrt(M/(2 pi R T)) PARTLY The papers write the sublimation flux without any coefficient (Grundy eq. 2, Blakley eq. in section 2: dQ/dt = -p_vap sqrt(M/(2 pi R T_QCM))) and put a sticking coefficient Sc only on the condensation term, dQ/dt = Sc p_QCM sqrt(M/(2 pi R T_room)) (Grundy eq. 3). The general HKL form with an evaporation coefficient alpha_e multiplying the flux is textbook, but it is not the form in these papers READ
6b "Unity sticking defines a maximum limit for equilibrium vapor pressure; alpha below 1 implies lower equilibrium vapor pressures" The paper says it (CONFIRMED as a statement); the physics is CONTRADICTED Blakley section 4.3 and Fig. 9: "With a lower sticking coefficient, the values we calculate for equilibrium vapor pressure would be even lower"; the Sc = 0 curve is about 3.5 times lower for O2 at 29 K and about 1.3 times for NH3 at 88 K (read off Fig. 9). Mechanism: Sc = 0 deletes the redeposition term Phi p_gauge in eq. 6, so the inferred p_vap falls to the net-loss term; that bounds the inference, not the equilibrium. The equilibrium pressure of a solid is fixed thermodynamically and independent of kinetic coefficients. A coefficient alpha < 1 scales both fluxes (detailed balance), so a measured net flux then implies a larger p_vap (flux/(alpha sqrt(M/2 pi R T))). Phi is calibrated with Sc = 1 assumed, so it already absorbs Sc in the redeposition term. Do not scale p_vap by a sticking coefficient in the generator READ, COMPUTED (logic)
