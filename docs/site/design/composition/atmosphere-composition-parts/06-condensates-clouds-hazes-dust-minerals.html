1. Scope and how the numbers feed the generator

Input to this part (from parts 01 to 05 and the orbit/star model): a pressure-temperature profile T(P), gravity g, metallicity [Fe/H] (or [M/H]), partial pressures or mixing ratios of the condensable gases, stellar UV flux, surface type, wind and surface-grain properties. Output from this part, per body: (1) which condensates exist, their base pressure (T-P profile crossing the condensation curve) and the gas removed by condensation (feeds back into part 05 mixing ratios); (2) cloud and haze optical depth, particle radius, density, complex refractive index at 450, 550, 650 nm, single scattering albedo and Angstrom exponent (feeds atmosphere colour GEN.414 and radiation parts); (3) dust lifting threshold, airborne dust radius and column optical depth; (4) the surface or crust mineral list and vapor species for lava worlds. Method order for a generator: (a) compute T_cond(P, [Fe/H]) for each species from 2.1 to 2.5 using the rules in 3.1 to 3.4; (b) sort by T_cond at the profile, find base pressures, deplete the limiting element in order (relative masses at the end of 2.1); (c) set particle size from the Ackerman and Marley sedimentation rule (3.5, 3.6) with a drawn f_sed; (d) take optical constants from 2.8 and compute optical depth from column mass and radius; (e) add photochemical haze using the production rule in 3.7; (f) add dust from 3.8 and 3.9; (g) convert optical depth to cloud albedo with 3.10.

2.3 Effective saturation expressions and microphysics inputs used by [R1513, R1515] and virga

[R1513, R1515] ([R1513], supplementary tables 2, 3, 4) and virga-exo 2.0.2 (virga/pvaps.py, gas_properties.py) give log10 P_sat in bar versus T. These expressions for the sulfides, Cr, Mg2SiO4, Fe, TiO2 and Al2O3 are effective equilibrium expressions for the net reaction with the metal gas as the controlling species (the metallicity term carries the H2S or oxygen scaling), not pure vapor pressures of the compound. KCl illustrates this: at 1400 K the Morley/Gao expression gives 0.30 bar, while the NIST Antoine sets for KCl vapor give 0.09 bar (COMPUTED from the two NIST sets in 2.5); extrapolating the NIST sets to the dilute gas pressure 2.55e-7 bar (outside their fit range) gives about 793 K against 801 K from the Gao/Morley expression (COMPUTED, one data point, extrapolated).
