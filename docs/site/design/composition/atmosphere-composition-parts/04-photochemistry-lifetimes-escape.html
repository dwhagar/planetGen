1. Scope and how the numbers feed a generator

Inputs from earlier generator stages: host spectral class and age (UV/XUV history), orbital distance, planet mass and radius, surface pressure and temperature, bulk composition (N2, CO2, H2, O2, H2O ...), volcanic and biological source fluxes, magnetic field flag.

Outputs of this part:

  1. Photochemical lifetime of every trace species against UV, scaled by host star (sections 2.3 and 2.4). The generator multiplies the table value by the orbital flux scaling S and a geometry factor, then compares it with sink and source terms to decide which species can accumulate.
  2. A rule set for the CO2/CO/O2 triple, methane and haze, sulfur and nitrogen families (section 2.6).
  3. An ozone column, its host-star and O2 dependence, and the false-positive channels for O2/O3 (section 3).
  4. Escape rates and which gases are lost over a given age, using XUV evolution laws and a choice of escape formula (section 4).
  5. Exosphere density and source/sink scalings for airless bodies (section 5).
  6. Flare, particle and lightning perturbations (section 6).

2. Photochemistry

2.1 Starter network (rate formats and values)

Format used by the Photochem mechanism file (DATA, checked by COMPUTED reproduction of one value): k = A T^b exp(-Ea/T) with T in K and Ea in K, in cm3 molecule-1 s-1 for bimolecular reactions and cm6 molecule-2 s-1 for low-pressure limits. Falloff reactions give only a low-pressure pair (k0) and a high-pressure pair (kinf) and no Troe keys, so the form is the Lindemann expression k = k0 [M] / (1 + k0 [M] / kinf). That reading of the file is an assumption (RECALLED convention, section 9). 2.2 What the reachable mechanism cannot give

The JPL 19-5 evaluation was unreachable, so Troe falloff parameters (F_c, N) and the ClOx reactions listed above have no verified source. The generator should treat the Lindemann form as an upper bound on the transition-region rate and flag ClOx rates NOT REACHED in the "Boss must research" list.

Critical-pressure rule (Hu, [R2026], Appendix D, eq. D5, READ): a gas of mixing ratio f is photolysed efficiently only above the level where P < P* = mu0 g M_atm / (sigma_UV F), where sigma_UV is a mean UV cross section and F the stellar photon flux factor defined in the paper. Table 6 of the paper gives mean cross sections and 1-ppm critical pressures P* in Pa (READ): H2 1.10e-17 cm2, 207 Pa; O2 7.18e-21, 3.17e5; H2O 1.72e-18, 1330; NH3 1.31e-18, 1730; N2O 1.38e-20, 1.65e5; CH4 1.81e-17, 126; CO2 7.44e-22, 3.06e6; H2S 1.11e-18, 2050; OCS 2.74e-20, 8.33e4; SO2 3.74e-18, 609. A gas with P* larger than the surface pressure is effectively shielded and is not photolysed in the lower atmosphere.

2.6 Cycles and family rules

CO2, CO and O2. The hydrogen-oxide cycle (OH, HO2, H2O2) recombines CO and O into CO2 catalytically. [R1511] ([R1511], READ) show this cycle breaks down when H is below about 1 ppm and the direct CO + O -> CO2 path takes over at below 0.1 ppm. In their desiccated M-dwarf CO2 atmosphere (GJ 436 spectrum, Mars-like 1 bar, 240 K surface, diffusion-limited H escape) they found abiotic O2 up to about 20%, O3 above 1 ppm, CO of 10 to 40%, and a cap of about 50% on CO2 destruction. Hu et al. 2012 (READ) found abiotic O2 in a 1 bar CO2 atmosphere of 6.4e-7, 3.8e-6 or 1.3e-3 for H2 emission of 3e10, 3e9 or 0 molecules cm-2 s-1, with O3 columns up to a third of the Earth column, and CO lifetimes of 0.1 to 700 years across the benchmark atmospheres. [R3986, R3987] ([R3987], READ) find that biospheres can maintain about 100 ppmv CO even at low H2 flux, and around M dwarfs CO of hundreds of ppm to several percent, with a CO runaway for Proxima at the highest fluxes; the model fluxes were CH4 1.5e11 and CO 3.0e11 molecules cm-2 s-1 with a maximum CO deposition velocity of 1.2e-4 cm s-1. Earth-like modern CO of about 0.1 ppmv is a TEST value from the same paper. [R1814] (the original paper, not on arXiv) is represented only through [R3014, R3016] and [R1511] (2ND-HAND).

Methane. [R3999] (astro-ph/0510224, READ): methane lifetime of 10 to 12 years on an Earth-like planet versus about 200 years for an AD Leo planet; OH is about 1e6 cm-3 on the Earth case versus about 1 cm-3 for active M stars and about 1e-2 cm-3 for quiescent M stars. 3.4 Host-star dependence

From the J-value table (2.4): the O3 photolysis lifetime (unattenuated) rises from 124 s (Sun) to 0.8 to 2.0 h for M dwarfs, and the O2 photolysis lifetime is 3.9 d (Sun), 20 h (K2V), 2 to 4 d (mid M dwarfs). The ratio of O3 destruction to O2 photolysis therefore falls by a factor of about 10 to 100 around M dwarfs, which is the origin of the large M-dwarf ozone columns in [R2466, R3999]. The generator rule: ozone column = Kozakis fit at the host T_eff, then multiplied by a factor from a seeded spread; the table here gives the physical direction and the sizes of the J ratios.

6.3 Volcanic gases: photochemical lifetimes

Use the lifetimes in section 2.4 divided by an attenuation factor and multiplied by the diurnal factor (2 to 4). Sun-column unattenuated lifetimes: SO2 1.4 h, H2S 1.0 h, OCS 4.0 h, CS2 9 min, HCl 2.3 d, H2SO4 gas 11 h, NH3 1.9 h, HCN 20 h. Observed tropospheric lifetimes are much longer because of shielding, OH reaction rates and deposition: Earth tropospheric H2S and SO2 about 2 days (Hu 2013, READ; TEST). SO2 + OH + M gives k = 6.6e-13 cm3 s-1 at 220 K and 1.2e-12 at 298 K (2.1), so an OH abundance of 1e6 cm-3 gives an OH-limited lifetime of 10 to 18 days (COMPUTED); around active M dwarfs OH is about 1 cm-3 or less ([R3999], READ) so this channel is shut and photolysis and deposition dominate. For a haze-forming sulfur flux see Hu 2013 thresholds in 2.6.

7. Generator recipe (formulas and validity)

  1. Photochemical lifetime of species i against the host star: tau_i = 1 / (J_i,table x S x f_geom x f_shield). J_i,table from section 2.4 (S = 1); S in units of the stellar constant; f_geom 0.25 to 0.5 (diurnal or global mean); f_shield from the column (tau = 1 mixing ratios in 2.5, critical pressure in 2.5). Valid for the six host spectra tabulated; interpolate between hosts by T_eff. Not valid for F stars below 200 nm.
  2. Ozone: Kozakis fit from the project doc, with the draw between the 1-D fit and the 3-D Cooke value (3.2); host-dependence direction from 3.4.
  3. Escape: Jeans parameter with the project convention; energy-limited form with eta 0.05 to 0.2 or the Owen and Wu variable law (4.2); the hydro fit only inside its validity box (4.2); the diffusion limit as a cap for hydrogen (4.3); XUV history from Ribas for G stars, Jackson for other types, Tu for rotation spread (4.5).
  4. Exospheres: release by thermal, PSD, MIV and sputtering (5.1), loss by photoionization scaled as 1/d^2 (5.2).
  5. Perturbations: flare protons conditional on the magnetosphere (6.1), lightning NOx scaling by flash density (6.2).
