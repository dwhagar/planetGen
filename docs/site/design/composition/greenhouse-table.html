What it does

surface_air.solve finds an air and its surface temperature together: it asks for the greenhouse of the air it has five to ten times a body. The model that knows the answer (radiative_convective.warming: 40 layers, 130 bands by 10 g points of the ExoMolOP correlated-k tables of water, carbon dioxide and methane, the HITRAN collision-induced pairs, the MT_CKD water continuum) takes one to twelve seconds a call and up to a few minutes for a dense air, so the generator reads a table fitted to it:

ln(T_s / T_e) = net(features)          T_e = (F / sigma)^(1/4), F the absorbed flux, G = (T_s / T_e)^4 - 1

net is a network of two tanh layers; its weights are the file. The grey closure of GEN.703 (infrared_opacity.py, Robinson and Catling's closure in greenhouse.py) is deleted. The downloaded line data (1.4 GB) are still downloaded at setup, because the table is fitted to them and checked against them: the file carries a digest of the dataset pieces it was fitted to (data_digest), and greenhouse_table.table() refuses it when they differ ("fit it again").

The features

Twenty-one numbers describe an air (greenhouse_table.FEATURE_NAMES): the logarithms of the effective temperature, the gravity and the surface pressure; of the mole fractions of H2O, CO2, CH4, NH3, SO2, H2S, CO, HCN (GEN.852), N2, O2, H2 and He (an absent gas sits at FRACTION_FLOOR); of the share of the other infrared-active gases (those the gas table flags ir) and of the weak ones (weak), which the model gives stand-in bands (IR1, IR2); of the mean molar mass; the heat-capacity ratio at T_e; and the share of the absorbed sunlight a haze takes aloft with the logarithm of the pressure it takes it at over the surface pressure (GEN.851 below). A feature outside the fitted range is held at its edge, as the k-tables hold their edge beyond 100 K and 100 bar; the table is fitted up to a surface pressure of 1e8 Pa (1000 bar), above which the model does not converge, and the output is capped at 4000 K.

The fit

scripts/fit_greenhouse_table.py (steps sample, designed, solve, fit; the work folder is outside the repository):

  • The airs. The greenhouse calls of the generator over seeds 1000 to 1800 (27,000 calls, 14,900 distinct airs, haze included since GEN.851), one perturbed copy of each (the pressure scaled by up to a factor of e, every gas by up to about 1.5, the gravity by 1.35, the flux by 2.2, and in one in five a haze share log-uniform from 0.003 to 0.9 at a pressure between 3e-6 and a tenth of the surface's), and 8,000 designed airs of four families the generator seldom makes but the table must be right for (terrestrial, carbon-dioxide, cold methane-nitrogen, hydrogen-helium; since GEN.852 also cold ammonia, volcanic sulphur and reducing carbon-monoxide airs, and the hydrogen family with hydrogen sulphide and carbon monoxide).
  • The solves. The model on each air, in parallel and resumable; airs the model does not converge on are dropped.
  • The loss. The error of ln(T_s / T_e) weighted by 1 / (0.004 + 0.03 y)^2 (a few percent of the warming, with a floor), so a thin air is fitted as closely in absolute terms and a thick one in relative; L-BFGS-B on numpy; the validation is on fresh seeds the fit never saw.
  • The file holds the weights, the feature ranges, the digest and the measured error (summary). tests/test_greenhouse_table.py::test_the_file_states_an_accuracy_the_fit_reached holds the file to the accuracy it states.

Fitting again is needed when the model or its data change (the digest says so): python scripts/fit_greenhouse_table.py sample|designed|solve|fit /path/to/work.

Accuracy

The network has three hidden layers (128, 128, 64 tanh units) and was fitted in two rounds: the first on the generator's airs and the designed families, the second adding airs the first table's own fixed point visits (DAgger-style, so the table is right where it will be asked). Airs where the model "converged" to a surface colder than its effective temperature (a numerical artefact of dense hot airs) are dropped. The validation is 1,306 airs from seeds the fit never saw (validation2, taken under the fitted table); the error is the relative error of T_s and the error in kelvin:

The table fitted before it read 0.54 K, 16.8 K and 170 K on 1,306 airs of the model before the line lists changed; the two sets are not the same airs, and the old table on the 570 first airs of this set read 5.7 K median and 52 K at the 90th percentile, because the model moved under it.) The third fit round added 1,805 airs around the airs the checks use (the Earth grid of 225 to 255 W/m2 and 600 to 4,000 Pa of vapour, a carbon-dioxide sweep from 100 to 2,400 ppm, Mars, Venus, a 5 bar carbon-dioxide air, Titan clear and under haze, a cold nitrogen-methane air, hydrogen-helium, and Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune from 0.03 to 10 bar, each three times over), because the first six fits, which saw only the generator's airs and the designed families, each missed one of those checks by 5 to 40 percent. Against the model on the checks the file stands within 2 K on the Earth-like grid, 0.2 K on Titan, 14 K (2 percent) warm on Venus and within 5 K on the three giants. A carbon-dioxide doubling gives +0.70 to +0.99 K in the table and +0.86 K in the model.

The table was fitted for GEN.1030 and GEN.1032, on the model with the methane pseudo-adiabat, the ortho-para heat capacity of cold hydrogen (IR14, IR15) and the sourced far wings of carbon dioxide (GEN.1019): about 22,000 training airs. Against the model it stands, in a clear-sky carbon dioxide and nitrogen air at Venus's gravity, 2 to 8 percent warm from 5 to 92 bar (336 K against 329 K at 5 bar and 160 W/m2; 20 bar and 300 W/m2 is the worst, 509 K against 475 K), on the Earth-like airs between 225 and 255 W/m2 within about 3 K, and 1 to 2 percent warm on the three giants at their measured effective temperatures. It is blind to the methane share of a hydrogen air: the model cools by 7 percent as methane goes from 0.5 to 4 percent of a Uranus-like air (condensation shallows the lapse rate), and the table by under 1, so a drawn ice giant with a few percent of methane is read about 5 percent warm. A further round with 3,000 designed one-bar hydrogen and methane airs did not teach it, and it moved the Earth-like airs 5 to 8 K cold (a fit of this size is sensitive to its seed there), so it was not shipped. The 500 to 1000 K row is dominated by a few dense steam and carbon dioxide airs at 10 to 200 bar; the table is weakest there.

The loss weights (an error floor of 0.003 on ln(T_s/T_e) and a relative term of 0.025) make the fit follow the cool airs the habitability and the life read; the hot dense airs (over 500 K: runaway greenhouses and Venus-like airs) are the model's own weakest and the table's: a hot dense air is right to the first tens of kelvin, not to the degree. The weights are stated in scripts/fit_greenhouse_table.py (NOISE_FLOOR, RELATIVE_ERROR).

The refit for the temperate wet branch (GEN.1304)

GEN.852 part 3 left the table 12 K cold on the branch an Earth-like world stands on: the Earth twin of the fixed point (1 bar nitrogen, 40 Pa of carbon dioxide, vapor at 0.77 of saturation, 239 W/m2 absorbed) sat at 276.0 K where the model puts it at 288.0 K, and the runaway sweep at 255 and 285 W/m2 read 282.8 K and 295.7 K against the model's 294.9 K and 307.7 K, which is why test_atmosphere_state.py was red on main. The table sat under the model by 4.8 K at 400 Pa of vapor, 8.2 K at 1,200 Pa and 10.3 K at 2,000 Pa at 239 W/m2: it underweighted the feedback of the vapor, and test_greenhouse_table.py did not see it because its Earth air (1,000 Pa of vapor) was one anchor in 22,000.

The work folder of the GEN.852 fit was not kept (hours of model time), so the table was not fitted from the beginning. scripts/fit_greenhouse_table.py gained the steps of a refit from the file:

  • wet draws temperate nitrogen-oxygen airs with their vapor free (100 Pa to 3e4 Pa), 20 kPa to 400 kPa, gravity 4 to 16, 150 to 400 W/m2 absorbed, carbon dioxide from 10 ppm to 2 percent, a third of them near one bar and the Earth's gravity with 100 to 3,000 ppm (the airs the checks use), and a third with no oxygen or no argon. 10,000 airs, all solved by the model.
  • solve --cool-below 340 runs the model only on the airs the file puts under 340 K (about half a second to ten seconds an air), in a random order, so any prefix is a fair sample. 12,388 airs of the generator (seeds 1000 to 1300 and perturbed copies, 7,388 of them random and 5,000 drawn from the hazy and hydrogen-chloride-bearing ones, because the first 7,000 left those the weakest) were solved.
  • fit --start --distill --hold anchors begins from the weights and the standardisation of the file, takes the model's answer for every solved air, takes the file's own answer for the hot airs the model was not asked about (over 340 K, minutes an air), and holds 2,500 copies of the airs the tests hold the table to (Mars, Venus from 5 to 92 bar, a 5 bar carbon dioxide air, Titan clear and hazy, a cold nitrogen-methane air, hydrogen-helium, the three giants; not solved, held at the old answers) so the correction does not move them. A temperate wet air that the generator made is not held at the old answer (it is in the fit only where the model solved it).

Result against the model: the Earth twin 287.7 K (model 288.0), 255 W/m2 at 294.5 K (294.9), 285 W/m2 at 307.0 K (307.7); at 239 W/m2 the air with 400, 1,200, 2,000 and 5,000 Pa of vapor reads 279.4, 287.3, 290.4 and 293.5 K (model 279.3, 287.3, 291.9, 294.0). On the 10,000 wet airs of the fit the median error is 0.5 K and the 99th percentile 3.4 K (the GEN.852 file: 3.8 K and 20 K, and 2.8 K cold on average).

The refit for hazy and hot dense airs (GEN.1310)

GEN.1304 left two open: the airs the file put at 300 to 500 K (median 3.9 %, 90th percentile 42 %) were held at the GEN.852 answers where the model had not been asked, and the hazy airs read 9.0 % at the 90th percentile. GEN.1310 solved them with the radiative-convective model (solve, resumable, about 3 s an air per 4 cores): 6,012 hot dense airs the GEN.1304 file puts at 300 to 520 K, and 8,644 hazy ones, both drawn in a random order so any prefix is a fair sample. They were added to the GEN.1304 training set and the file was fitted again from the GEN.1304 weights (fit --start --distill --hold anchors, 12,000 iterations, 128-128-64). Hot and hazy airs the model did not finish in the time allowed were dropped from the fit; a first fit that kept them pinned to the old answer did less well on the 90th percentile of 300 to 500 K (33 % against 31 %) and of the cool hazy airs (7.4 % against 5.5 %), so the second fit leaves them out.

The guards (stand-in IR11)

The network was fitted on airs that always hold an absorber and are at least 10 Pa thick, and left alone it gave a noble-gas air a greenhouse of 0.4. Two guards multiply ln(T_s / T_e): a surface pressure under THIN_AIR_PA (10 Pa) scales it by P / 10 Pa (an optically thin air warms with its column), and a share of gases that absorb or collide in the infrared (everything the gas table does not flag none) under ABSORBER_SHARE_MIN (1 percent) scales it by the share over that. Both are registry row IR11 (tier T5, tweak L0).

The cloud (stand-in IR12)

The model is a clear-sky one and a body's albedo is its all-sky one, so a world that keeps the Earth's clouds would lose their longwave warming (26.5 W/m2 of the 266 W/m2 the clear-sky Earth sends out, CERES EBAF, 0.10). The sky sends out (1 - s) * clear, s = CLOUD_LONGWAVE_SHARE * h / 0.77 for a surface relative humidity h (at most 1.3 times), nil without water vapour. A first version put the cloud into the column as a grey layer; it hid the whole of the vapour's feedback (the doubling of carbon dioxide went from +1.72 to +0.99 K), so the cloud is a share of the flux, which masks forcing and feedback alike by a tenth. The CERES share leaves the Earth twin of the fixed point at 284.6 K (the clear-sky model's own bias of 3 K); 0.13 puts it at the measured 288 K (tier T9, tweak L3 in the registry). The cloud falls with the surface humidity, so a world whose vapour is held while it warms loses cloud: the fixed point's vapour follows the temperature and does not.

The haze (GEN.851, stand-in IR13)

The aerosols were made after the air, from its final temperature. They are part of it now. The three haze draws (K_zz, yield, grain radius) come from a stream of the body's own (aerosols.haze_stream_of, seeded by the mass, radius and distance, which are fixed before the air), carried in the Setting; at each step surface_air.solve reads aerosols.haze_of for the air it has: the depth at 550 nm from the CH4/CO2 gate, the diffusion-limited supply and the fall integral, the share of the absorbed sunlight the haze takes (the tholin's absorption at each wavelength over the star's blackbody, on a slant path of 2) and the pressure where half of it is taken from the top, in the haze's mass profile. Those two go to the table. generate_aerosols makes the same haze of the final air (the life stage may have changed the air since, and the stored depth then follows the air, not the temperature). A hazy air is cooler at the ground than the same air clear (the anti-greenhouse of Titan): test_haze_in_the_air.py.

What moves

Every planet and moon with an air: the surface temperature, and with it the class, the habitability and the life draw. 28 percent of the airs the generator makes shift by more than 5 K against the grey closure, the dense ones by 60 to 190 K (the closure was too cold); the worlds with a haze are cooler. The Earth twin of the fixed point stays at 288 K (287.7 K since GEN.1304; it read 276.0 K between GEN.852 part 3 and GEN.1304). Airs above 1000 bar (the pressure the table and the model stop at) rise past the table's top value by the adiabat, ln(T_s / T_e) gaining (1 - 1/gamma) ln(P / P_top) with the air's own heat-capacity ratio (GEN.1240, stand-in IR16): a convective air is as warm as its adiabat takes it from a radiating level the opacity sets and the column does not move, so a steam air of 1e5 bar reads 1,800 K where the held table read 700 K. It is not fitted to the model, which does not converge there, and the solver's own ceiling (2,000 K) still bounds it.

Limits

  • The table is as good as its training airs: an air of a kind the generator and the designed families never made is the network's extrapolation, held at the feature ranges' edge.
  • The derivative of the temperature with the flux (the climate sensitivity of GEN.972 to GEN.974) is smooth but not exact: the table's slope error is a few hundredths of the slope.
  • The line data are the ones of GEN.715; the stand-ins of the model (IR1 to IR13) are in the table with it.
