Research note, no behaviour change. It lists each source with the numbers read in it and what is still missing, for the belt member draw (GEN.566), the comet size cut of GEN.559 (GEN.560's interstellar size law), the hosting gate can_host(body) (GEN.579) and the sub-satellite gate row (GEN.580). Raw papers stay out of the repo.

Source states: READ is a number found in the paper itself this session; 2ND-HAND is a number quoted by a paper that was read (the original was not); RECALLED is from memory and not usable; STAND-IN is a placeholder named in the gap plan registry. Rules (standing): verified wins; two verified in doubt, the newest wins; two in question, the widest range.

1. Size-frequency laws

Cumulative law used here: N(>D) proportional to D^-q. A magnitude law dN/dH proportional to 10^(alpha H) is the same law with q = 5 alpha ([R1439, R4107] both state the relation; a crater differential slope is q_diff = -(5 alpha + 1)). Diameter from absolute magnitude: D[km] = 1329 / sqrt(p) * 10^(-H/5) (p the geometric albedo).

1.2 Correction found while reading

docs/design/architecture/unified-star-like-objects.md line 140 states "the real density is about 1e12 per pc^3 (range 1e11 to 1e14)" for interstellar objects, marked [S]. The measured 100 m scale density, 0.1 au^-3 [R2159] to 0.2 au^-3 (Do et al. 2018), is 7e14 to 1.8e15 per pc3, three orders of magnitude higher. The conclusion of that paragraph (the objects cannot be rows and the count below a lowest size is computed) holds with the higher number. TODO files this.

1.4 Correction: Ivezic's indices are differential (GEN.1061)

The first version of this table gave Ivezic et al.'s 2.3 and 4 as cumulative exponents q. The paper (section 6.6 and its Fig. 25) states them as the index alpha of dN/dD proportional to D^-alpha, which is a cumulative q = alpha - 1: 1.3 below 5 km and 3.0 from 5 to 40 km. (Taken as cumulative, 530,000 bodies above 1 km would leave only 3 above 40 km; as the paper has them it is about 130.) Found by the Aux 7 lane in the tide work. The belt ledger (physics/belt_ledger.py) uses the corrected exponents; q = 3 holds the same mass in every doubling of size, so the law has to steepen above 40 km, which the measurement does not reach (stand-in BR20).

1.3 What is missing

  • Comets, long period and Oort cloud. No size law was read for long-period comets ([R1437] and Fernandez and Sosa 2012 are recalled and not read; the network did not return them from arXiv). GEN.559 needs this and waits on it.
  • Main belt below 400 m and the near-Earth population as a whole ([R0484, R1822] are recalled, not read; neither is on arXiv).
  • Kuiper belt between the breaks. [R1439] gives alpha on each side; the diameter of the transition depends on the albedo, which is not one number (hot objects have a bimodal albedo, the paper says), and DEEP's tapered law is not given here as parameters. Read DEEP V's fit table before the draw uses it.
  • Dwarf and giant end. The largest member of each population (Pluto-size) follows from the same laws: the hot law predicts a largest body about Pluto's mass ([R0362], astro-ph/0308467, abstract READ).
  • Interstellar object size law has no measurement, only detection constraints; the table above says what the surveys rule out. A draw cannot take one number as a result; it should take q from a range, widest first, 2.5 to 4.5, until a detection pins it. (This is a recommendation under the widest-range rule, not a read value.)

2.1 Sub-satellites of moons (the tidal gate row)

[R2437] (MNRAS, [R2437], READ):

  • A submoon's orbit is stable out to a fraction f of its moon's Hill sphere; f = 0.4895 for prograde low-eccentricity orbits ([R1138], 2ND-HAND), about 0.4 when the planet's perturbation is included ([R3457], 2ND-HAND).
  • Tidal survival over T = 4.6 Gyr (their equation 1, adapted, read from the typeset page): R_moon >= [ 39 M_sub k2 T sqrt(G) / (2 (4 pi rho_moon)^(8/3) Q) * (3 M_p / (f a_moon)^3)^(13/6) ]^(1/3), with the moon's Love number k2 (0.25), tidal quality factor Q (100), density rho_moon (2500 kg/m3), orbital radius a_moon, the planet mass M_p, the submoon mass M_sub (10 km radius, 2000 kg/m3 by default) and f the Hill fraction above. The dimensions work out (metres). Computed here with these defaults (not read): Moon 125 km, Callisto 331 km, Titan 354 km, Iapetus 35 km (all pass), Ganymede 1126 km (passes; the paper's text lists only the other four, so its figure uses something this note does not capture), Europa 3096 km, Io 8470 km, Rhea 2187 km and Triton 1489 km (all fail).
  • Their result: 10 km submoons survive only around large (1000 km scale) moons on wide orbits. Among the Solar System's moons only Titan, Iapetus, Callisto and the Moon pass; none has a submoon, so formation or other effects (mass concentrations) also remove them. A mass ratio of about 1e-5 is a rough guide to long-lived submoons.

Missing: the tidal-stripping timescale by the planet for ordinary moons is only what that paper's equation gives; no measured submoon exists to calibrate against.
