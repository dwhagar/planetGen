Status: built (src/planetgen/galaxy/density.py). The model dates from the "revision 2" design pass of 2026-09 (Track C). That revision's main proposal, a stored plan of every qualifying sector, was never built; it is kept in archive/galaxy-disk-density-rev2.md. This file describes the model as the code uses it, on the cylindrical grid until 2026-10-15 (the grid is shell, band and wedge since then, galaxy-coordinate-system.md).

1. The model

A thin disk carrying the spiral arms, a thick disk and a boxy bar bulge, each fitted to the Milky Way's published structure (GEN.118, GEN.119):

R     = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)
theta = atan2(y, x)
v(z, h) = exp(-|z| / h)              # the form surveys fit (GEN.544); column 2 h

thin(R, z)  = exp(-R / L) * v(z, h)                       # L, h: the shape's
                                                          # disk scale length/height
thick(R, z) = a_T * exp(-R / L_T) * v(z, h_T)             # L_T, h_T: the drawn length and height
                                                          # ratios times L and h (GEN.532; height
                                                          # ratio 2.5 to 4.2); 0.77 and 3 are the
                                                          # Milky Way reference check
bulge(x, y, z) = bulge_amplitude * exp(-r_s^2 / 2)        # in the bar's frame:
r_s^4 = ((x'/x0)^2 + (y'/y0)^2)^2 + (z/z0)^4              # x0 = bulge_scale_radius_pc,
                                                          # y0/x0, z0/x0 drawn per galaxy (GEN.533);
                                                          # 0.39 and 0.27 are the Dwek reference
theta_arm(R)      = spiral_reference_angle_rad + ln(R / spiral_reference_radius_pc) / tan(pitch_angle_rad)
arm_factor(R, th) = 1 + arm_amplitude * cos(arm_count * (th - theta_arm))

relative_density = k_norm * (bulge + thin * arm_factor + thick)
  • Thin disk. disk_scale_height_pc is the exponential scale height surveys quote, and the profile is that exponential, exp(-|z|/h) (GEN.544): its midplane density is the one the fits give, and its column is 2 h times it. It replaced the isothermal sheet sech^2(z/2h), whose tail is the same but whose column is 4 h times its midplane, so for one mass its midplane was half the fits'. Only the thin disk carries the arms. The nuclear stellar disk keeps the isothermal sheet (_sech_vertical).
  • Thick disk (shape.structure, galaxy/structure_draw.py, GEN.532). Its mass, scale length and scale height are ratios to the thin disk's drawn from the galaxy seed: the mass (0.14 to 0.99 at the circular speeds of 3e9 to 2e11 solar masses of stars) between the fits of [R0865, R4824], the length by Comeron 2018 and the height from 2.5 to 4.2. The amplitude is derived from the mass ratio: a_T = ratio * arm_level / (length_ratio^2 * height_ratio). The Milky Way's published values (900 pc, 2.0 kpc, 4% of the thin disk's density at the Sun, 18% of its mass; BHG16) are structure.REFERENCE_STRUCTURE, a check target.
  • Bulge (tuning.BULGE_*). The bar's boxy/peanut core as COBE/DIRBE sees it edge on: [R1202]'s G2 model. bulge_amplitude is its central density over the thin disk's. Its long axis angle is drawn per galaxy, uniform on [0, 180) degrees, with the axis ratios (GEN.533, galaxy/structure_draw.py draw_bar); the Milky Way's 27 degrees leading the Sun, and its 0.39 and 0.27, are the Dwek reference, a check only.
  • The Sun is not placed: no Sun position is read and no point of the galaxy is the calibration point (GEN.404; Boss, 2026-10-16, 9:43 PM EDT: the galaxy stores no home or Sun position). R0 = 3.15 L (8.2 / 2.6 kpc) is a test reference only. model_terms(shape) returns the derived values; the Galaxy Map's prisms read them with the shape.

The nuclear disk's sech^2 is computed as 4e / (1 + e)^2 with e = exp(-2|x|) (_sech_squared), because 1 / cosh(x)^2 overflows once |x| passes about 710. The disks' exp(-|z|/h) is clamped at 700 scale heights.

Normalization (GEN.404). build_galaxy_shape computes k_norm so the density, in units of the real local stellar density (LOCAL_STELLAR_DENSITY_LY3), integrates to the systems the galaxy's stellar mass makes (structure.stellar_mass_solar over density.mean_system_mass_solar, GEN.545): density.total_mass is the integral of the unnormalized model (the thin and thick disks, the bulge, the nuclear cluster and disk and the stellar halo, each in closed form). No point of the galaxy is the calibration point and no Sun position is read. A galaxy of the reference structure (5e10 solar masses) held 1.25e11 systems at the recalled 0.4 solar masses a system (1.03e11 at the derived mean, below), and the density at the reference radius was 0.38 of the local density the earlier calibration pinned there (2.6 times sparser): the model's disk was a sech^2 sheet whose midplane density is half the exponential fits quote for the same column. GEN.544 made the vertical profile the exponential the scale heights are fitted with, with the column (the mass) unchanged: the density at the reference radius is now 0.75 of the old pinned value (twice the GEN.404 value, 0.376 to 0.750), the thin disk's column is the same, and the reference galaxy's bulge amplitude is 3.11/2 because BHG16's 3.11 was measured against the sech^2 disk and the bulge keeps its 31% of the mass (mathcheck.REFERENCE_BULGE_AMPLITUDE). The model's fixed parts are ratios to the shape's own lengths, so the same formulas work for a galaxy of any size.

The mean system mass (GEN.545). The galaxy's number of systems is its stellar mass over the mean mass of a system, and that mean is no longer a recalled constant (tuning.MEAN_SYSTEM_MASS_SOLAR was 0.4, "within a factor of 1.5"); it is worked out from the generator's own draws (generation/system_mass.py) for stars formed at one age:

  • the placed star: an initial mass from the initial mass function (Kroupa, 0.08 to 150 solar masses), at its birth mass until its last giant phase ends (stellar_evolution.youngest_progenitor_sol), then its remnant: a white dwarf by the initial-final relation, a neutron star or a black hole at the mean of the kind's mass range. The remnants are systems too;
  • the other stars of its system: the star-count table's mean number by the placed star's class with the 8 to 12 tail (multistar.expected_star_count: 1.31 stars for a 0.1 solar mass primary, 1.62 for 1, 2.37 for 5, 3.08 for 50), each with a birth mass of the primary's times a uniform 0.1 to 1, taken to its own state at the same age.

The mean over a population's ages weights each by the star formation there (the disk's three populations by their history, galaxy.age_law; the others uniformly over their windows), and the mean over a galaxy or a cell weights each population by its systems (density.population_units: the thin disk shared among its populations, the thick disk in old, the bulge, the nuclear cluster and disk, the halo). By systems and not by mass because the density counts systems. For the reference galaxy: young 0.72, intermediate 0.57, old 0.51, bulge 0.50, halo 0.49, so 0.508 solar masses a system overall, a primary drawn from the primary mass function (GEN.928: the Chabrier function less the companions of heavier primaries; the Kroupa function gave 0.483 with these words: a placed star of 0.35 times 1.42 stars a system) times 1.43 stars a system. The reference galaxy holds 0.98e11 systems (1.03e11 on the Kroupa function, 1.25e11 at 0.4) and its density at the reference radius is 0.59 of the pinned local density (0.62 on the Kroupa function, 0.750 at 0.4): the gap that remains is real if the pinned density (Mamajek's 0.0984 stars per pc^3) counts the stars of a system singly, as it probably does, since a system here holds 1.42 of them. The compact companions of massive stars' trees add under 0.2% (not counted), and so do the companions of the remnants the scatter places (a few percent of those systems). A test draws the systems with the generator's own samplers and compares (test_gen_system_mass.py). A cluster's or satellite's mean (once the recalled CLUSTER_MEAN_SYSTEM_MASS_SOL, also 0.4) is derived the same way from the component's own age (GEN.598, Component.mean_system_mass_solar): 0.47 for an 11 Gy globular cluster, 0.49 for the Large Cloud, 0.9 for a cluster a few thousand years old.

Stellar halo (GEN.234). A spheroid with a broken power law, A * (max(m, core) / break) ** -n, where m = sqrt(R^2 + (z / q)^2): inner slope 2.5, outer slope 4.5, break at 25 kpc (9.6 disk scale lengths), axis ratio q = 0.7, flat inside 500 pc (tuning.HALO_*; sources and the numbers it gives are in intergalactic-and-halo-contents.md sections 2.1 and 4). The radii are ratios to the disk scale length, and the amplitude is solved like k_norm: the halo holds the galaxy's own share (structure.stellar_halo_system_fraction, GEN.532: its drawn halo mass over its stellar mass, scaled to the model's counts; 1.17% for the Milky Way's 1.4e9 solar masses of 5e10 at 0.4 solar masses a system against 3.0e11 systems) of the visible components' stars (density.halo_terms). It replaces the flat floor GEN.78 put under the density (0.1% of the local density everywhere, which was half the real halo at the Sun and 200 to 340,000 times too much at 50 to 262 kpc). The halo is 0.19% of the local density at the Sun and 3e-9 at 262 kpc, and no cell beyond 8.7 kpc from the center expects more than about 0.01 systems from it. The alternative normalisation to the local density ([R2211], 0.5%, about 2.7 times higher) is tuning.HALO_LOCAL_DENSITY_FRACTION; density.halo_local_density_scale gives the factor. The outline (galaxy/skeleton.py) reads the disk, the bulge and the components (the clusters and satellites of galaxy_components, added to the bound by ComponentField.bound, GEN.160 and GEN.237) but not the halo, so the halo adds no cell to it. The halo still fills every cell of the reach: a cell outside the outline is generated from its own density, halo term included, and its sector profile says halo (galaxy/profile.py, GEN.235; see intergalactic-and-halo-contents.md section 5.3). The halo is the halo entry of the profile's mix, with the age window of the halo population (tuning.STELLAR_POPULATION_AGE_RANGES_GY["halo"]) and its [Fe/H] (metallicity.halo_mean_fe_h, tuning.METALLICITY_HALO).

2. The shape (planetgen plan)

GEN.401: no shape parameter has a default value any more. planetgen plan draws each from the galaxy seed (galaxy/shape_draw.py, the laws in intergalactic-and-halo-contents.md 5.13, rows 4 to 6) unless the option is given; an option given replaces its draw, and the bulge's amplitude is solved for the disk actually used.

Parameter Where it comes from
--disk-scale-length-pc Size-mass relation of late-type galaxies [R4048], R_d = R_50 / 1.678, log-normal scatter
--disk-scale-height-pc Length over q = 7.3 with a 0.13 dex scatter [R2484]
--bulge-scale-radius-pc A third of the bar's length, which is drawn in disk scale lengths by stage [R1289]
--bulge-amplitude Solved so the bulge's mass is the drawn bulge-to-total of the bulge and disks ([R1656], by stage)
--arm-count Shares of [R1836] (two arms in 64.5 percent)
--pitch-angle-deg Falls with stellar mass between Yu and Ho 2019's two binned means (earlier notes say 2018; Crossref gives ApJ 871, 194, 2019 for the pitch-angle paper), 5.5 degrees of scatter
--arm-density, --interarm-density 1.4 and 0.6, the arm contrast (not drawn; no source)

The Milky Way's measured shape (BHG16: 2,600 pc, 300 pc, 1,580 pc, 3.11, two arms at 15 degrees) is kept only as the reference the math check measures the model's machinery against (physics/mathcheck.py).

The shape is stored in galaxy_shape with k_norm.

Sources: [R0422]:529 ("BHG16"); Dwek et al. 1995, ApJ 445:716; [R4649]:1874. tests/test_galaxy_milky_way.py and the math check (galaxy_bulge_to_total, galaxy_thick_to_thin_disk) compare the model with them on the reference shape.

3. Which sectors exist

E = expected_system_count_at_density_1   # 6.31 systems for a 4 pc sector
predicted_star_count(center) = E * relative_density(center)
a sector qualifies  <=>  predicted_star_count >= 1

The threshold is deterministic: a cell's fate is a fact of its position, not a coin flip. At a given ring and layer the bulge is largest along the bar and arm_factor is at most 1 + arm_amplitude, so each term at its own maximum bounds the density over angle (exactly, away from the bar), and that bound falls as R and |z| grow. So galaxySkeleton.build_layer_extents finds each layer's outer ring in one pass, and that outline is the galaxy's edge (galaxy-coordinate-system.md, section 4). A sector inside the outline is checked exactly when it is visited.

When a qualifying sector is generated, its own relative_density is the --density multiplier, so its system count follows the local richness.

4. Stellar populations (7.38.0)

population_densities splits the density at a point into young, intermediate, old disk, bulge, halo and cluster populations. The bulge term is the bulge population, the halo term the halo population (10 to 13 Gyr, [Fe/H] about -1.6 inside 25 kpc and -2.2 outside) and the thick disk is old. The cluster population (10 to 13 Gyr) is 0 until the cluster components (GEN.237) add their density. The thin disk is shared by each population's share of star formation, its own scale height (structure.population_height_ratios, GEN.532: the squared vertical dispersion of the population over the old disk's, thinner for younger stars) and its own arm contrast (STELLAR_POPULATION_ARM_AMPLITUDE, strongest for young stars: 0.75, 0.42 and 0.10 for the young, intermediate and old, GEN.931; the old disk is the observed contrast of about 10% [R1158, R1231], the young one the gas ratio of 4 to the Kennicutt-Schmidt index 1.4, and the intermediate one falls with the log of the age between them; registry 6.65). The parts always sum to relative_density. Each system in a galaxy sector draws its star's population from this mix, so O and B stars and supergiants gather in the arms near the plane (CHANGELOG 7.38.0).

The bright-star scatter and backfill draw from these populations. How to draw them without visiting every cell (an exact block-first draw from the ring-and-layer density bound), why dropping sectors and boosting the rest is rejected, and what the backfill and a fill of the sparse outer rim cost, are in sampling-backfill-and-resume.md.

5. Why it works this way

  • Exponential disk, bulge and log spiral. The brief was "assume a spiral galaxy similar to the Milky Way", and each parameter has a real Milky Way basis (section 2). Why this form was picked over other profiles is not recorded.
  • Calibrated between the arms. The Sun sits between two arms (the Local Arm or Orion Spur), and calibrating there means typical solar-neighborhood space reads as 1.0. Calibrating on an arm would make everything off-arm look thinner than the real neighborhood.
  • k_norm computed, never typed in. It must change whenever any shape parameter changes.
  • A Milky Way bulge and thick disk (GEN.118, GEN.119). Revision 2 shrank the bulge to an amplitude of 1 and a 200 pc sphere so the old shell build stayed tractable. That left it 0.6% of the stars against the Milky Way's ~31%, and edge on the galaxy was a flat disk. The bulge is now the COBE bar and the disk heights are the published ones, so a side view looks like the real galaxy and the model can be tested against published numbers. The cost: at the default shape the outline reaches layer 1,020 (4.1 kpc) instead of 317, and a full galaxy holds about 3.4 times as many systems (see the changelog for the numbers), close to the Milky Way's real 100-400 billion stars.
  • A hard threshold instead of a random occupancy roll. Revision 1 used a hash-based Bernoulli roll; revision 2 replaced it with "a sector exists where it expects at least one star", which needs no randomness and defines the edge from the model.
  • Nothing stored per sector. Revision 2 measured a full plan table at about 10.5 billion rows and about 1 TB. Since position and density are pure functions, only the outline is stored: one galaxy_layer row per layer (635) and one galaxy_column row per ring (about 3,900).
  • Populations by position. Before 7.38.0 every sector drew stars from the same mix; tying ages to place puts young massive stars in the arms, as in a real disk. The reason in the release note is that "star ages follow where a sector sits".

Alternatives considered and rejected

  • The galaxy_sector_plan table and galaxyPlan.py resumable build (revision 2, sections 4-8 of the archive copy).
  • A probabilistic occupancy roll per sector (revision 1, described only in revision 2; revision 1 itself is not in the repository).
  • Metallicity gradients: listed as an open question in revision 2 and never built. (Its other open question, thin and thick disks as separate components, is built: GEN.119.)
  • A spherical or oblate bulge ([R3009]'s axisymmetric model was the other candidate for GEN.118): the observed bulge is a bar, boxy edge on, so the COBE fit was used. Not modelled: the long thin bar beyond the bulge and the stellar halo's inner structure. The nuclear star cluster and nuclear stellar disk are modelled (GEN.280, schema v99, realistic-galactic-core.md section 3); see fill-order-curves-and-core.md, section 4.

Corrections made to the previous version

  • It described the shell grid (shell_index, shell_slot_index, Fibonacci slots), which 6.0.0 replaced with rings, layers and slots.
  • It used E = 4.32 for an 11.5 ly sector; the 4 pc sector gives 6.31.
  • It calibrated at R_sun from the Sun's distance; the code calibrated at a fixed ratio of the scale length (3.15 since GEN.118) until GEN.404 normalized the whole galaxy to its stellar mass.
  • It pointed to galaxyGen.py, galaxyPlan.py, sectorGen.py and SQLite, none of which are used now.

ADM.49: user-set arm, inter-arm, core and bulge densities

The thin disk's arm factor is arm_level * (1 + arm_amplitude * cos(m (theta - theta_arm))). The user sets the density on an arm crest a and between arms i (the disk's own units, usual 1.4 and 0.6), and density.arm_terms(a, i) gives arm_amplitude = (a - i) / (a + i) and arm_level = (a + i) / 2. The Gaussian core this item added was replaced by the nuclear star cluster and nuclear stellar disk (GEN.280, schema v99, realistic-galactic-core.md section 3); core_amplitude became nuclear_amplitude. The bulge density is bulge_amplitude. Superseded (GEN.404; Boss, 2026-10-16, 9:43 PM EDT): no point is the calibration point and no Sun position is read; the total is set by the galaxy's stellar mass, so a denser arm or core raises those places against the rest of the galaxy (8.2 kpc is a test reference only). The skeleton and object-first bounds, the stellar populations, the component masses and the Galaxy Map's prisms all read the same terms.

GEN.625: the all-Auto window of the size and the flatness

A galaxy with the mass, size (or compactness), flatness, bulge share and bulge size all on Auto draws its disk size and flatness through shape_draw._auto_size: the seed's draws of the size-mass relation ([R4048], median half-light radius over 1.678 for the scale length, scatter 0.47 falling to 0.34 in ln) and of the flatness ([R2484], median 7.3, 0.13 dex), and then again from the same two streams, one more draw each, until the shape raises none of dials.shape_warnings (the thin disk's crest at least 20 times the one-star density, a scale height of at least 8 pc, a bulge of at least 4 pc) and the flatness is inside the Structure dial's 3 to 20. The crest tested is the plan's own (plan_figures.crest, GEN.626) of the shape built at every dial's Auto value and the default sector edge, which costs one density normalization a draw (the disk history is cached, so the accepted draw's build is free). At most 32 draws are made; the shape with the best crest is kept if none passes (it does not happen at the Auto masses). The first draw is the unchanged one, so a seed that did not warn keeps its galaxy; one seed in five drew a shape that warned and gets a later draw. Pin any of the five and the draws are the seed's own first ones, as before, and the grid's warnings fire for what the pin makes.

Why a window and not a shifted law: the crest goes as the mass over the scale length cubed times the flatness, and the size-mass scatter is a log-normal with no upper bound, so a bounded sourced law cannot keep every draw clear of the warning; the Auto mass is already a window (3e9 to 6e9, Boss, 10-21) and this is the same kind of statement, the galaxies the 4 pc plan represents well. The sourced medians and scatters are untouched; the window removes the large, thick tail the plan cannot hold.

Measured over 1,000 seeds before and after, against the plan's own crest (with the nuclear disk of GEN.628): the share of Auto draws that warn 17.5 percent to 0, the mean disk scale length 1,470 pc to 1,224 pc (47 percent of the 2.6 kpc a Milky Way disk has, [R0422], used only as a test yardstick), the mean stellar mass 4.3e9 solar masses (8 percent of a Milky Way's 5.4e10), the mean flatness 7.6 to 7.8, the 5th percentile of the crest 6.1 to 23.6.
