Summary

What is out there. Almost nothing, by the generator's own yardstick. A sector that "exists" today expects at least one system. The stellar halo is about 1.5e9 solar masses [S], 3% of the disk and bulge, and spread so thinly that a cell at the Sun's distance from the centre expects about 0.01 systems [C]; at 20 kpc, 4e-4; at 50 kpc a cell expects 3e-5 [C]; at the 262 kpc edge of the Milky Way's neighbourhood, 2e-8 [C]. The intergalactic average is 1e-7 systems per cell or less [C, R]. Only discrete things reach one system per cell: the Magellanic Clouds, the Sagittarius remnant, dwarf spheroidals and globular clusters. Everything else is a sparse Poisson haze of old, metal-poor stars, ejected stars and ejected neutron stars, with a gas background of fractions of an Earth mass per cell.

What the project does today [S, C]:

  1. The grid is already a tiling of all space, but its addresses stop short. The 80-bit object ID cannot name a cell whose band number passes 2,047, which means every shell from 1,304 (5.2 kpc) outward is only partly addressable: above latitude 31 degrees at the disk's edge (shell 3,764), no cell has an ID. Shells end at 4,095 (16.4 kpc). The real halo, the Magellanic Clouds (50 kpc) and the Milky Way's virial radius (about 200 kpc) are outside.
  2. The "halo" is a flat floor, and it is wrong in both directions. MIN_RELATIVE_DENSITY is 0.1% of the local density everywhere (0.0063 systems per cell). It is half the real halo at the Sun, 200 times too high at 50 kpc, and 340,000 times too high at 262 kpc [C]. If the grid were simply filled out to 262 kpc under the floor, it would hold 7.4e12 phantom systems, 25 times the real galaxy's 3.0e11 [C].
  3. Nothing beyond the disk outline can be generated, the hypervelocity stars are squeezed inside it (they are redrawn when they land outside), and the bright-star floor (9,000 L_sun) finds no halo star at all [C].

Recommendation (section 5, decisions in section 8):

  • One rule set, a profile for every cell. Every cell in the reach has a profile computed from its address alone: expected systems, a mix of stellar populations (age, metallicity, motion), gas, dark matter, and a factor for each rare kind. The same counting, population, scatter and fill rules run everywhere; only the profile differs. Nothing is generated unless asked; an asked sector is generated and saved wherever it is, even if empty (as GEN.76 already does). The qualifying outline stays as an optimisation (map shading, random starts), not the definition of a sector.
  • Reach 65,535 shells = 262 kpc (1.18e15 cells), with 96-bit object IDs (approved). It contains the Magellanic Clouds, the Sagittarius remnant, the globular clusters, the satellites out to Leo I and the Milky Way's virial radius [S]. Andromeda and the rest of the Local Group stay sky objects (GEN.9 stage 1). Decided (Boss, contradiction sweep card, 7:30 AM EDT 2026-10-21): the neighbour galaxies are drawn from the seed by default; the real Local Group table (multiple-galaxies.md) is a test reference only.
  • Replace the flat floor with a measured stellar halo: a broken power law, 1.5e9 solar masses, 3% of the visible mass, old and metal-poor. It lowers the cell density out there by up to five orders of magnitude and, by itself, makes no sector qualify.
  • Four kinds of content beyond the disk, each cheap: the halo itself (density term), discrete components (globular clusters and satellites in one table, built in the same train), wanderers (hypervelocity stars, kicked neutron stars) with analytic radial laws, and computed gas and dark-matter figures on the sector page instead of rows.
  • Uncharted sectors stay implicit. Storing every cell of the 16 kpc sphere would take 365 years of single-worker time at the measured fixed cost per sector and 29 TB for bare rows [C]; the 262 kpc sphere is out of the question. A cell gets rows only when someone asks for it.
  • The scatters fill the sphere too, with the same algorithm: the halo, old clusters and old satellites place no bright or massive star at the default floor and cut (a 10 to 13 Gyr population has none) [C]; the Magellanic Clouds place a few thousand; about 2.4 million extra black-hole rows appear outside the disk, 4% on top of the disk's scatter [C]. The one part that cannot reach 262 kpc as built is the phenomena pass, a band-by-band walk; it moves to the object-first sampler the star passes already use.

  • The 80-bit layout was sized for the outline, not the sphere. Its band field is 12 biased bits (bands -2048 to 2047). A shell's top band is about 1.57 S, so from shell 1,304 on the polar bands (and the caps) have no address. At shell 2,500 (10 kpc) every cell above latitude 47 degrees is unaddressable; at the outline's edge, above 31 degrees [C]. This is the bug GEN.230 already files, and the layout decision it asks for is answered here: a full sphere needs the 96-bit fit, not a 41st bit. The default galaxy never meets it because its outline reaches only band 1,456; a halo, which is not confined to the plane, would meet it at once. provisional_sector_designation and parse_sector_designation also hard-code 12/12/16 bits while fit_layout already widens the object ID per galaxy [S, spherical.py, object_uid.py], so the designation functions need the layout.
  • 96 bits cover every sphere up to 524 kpc and cost 2 more bytes per object ID than today [C]. Beyond that, 128 bits. The layout is chosen once per galaxy by the plan step (outline_object_id_layout), so a galaxy that wants no halo keeps 80 bits.
  • The default galaxy's outline (skeleton.build_shell_extents) stops at the first shell whose equatorial band cannot reach one expected system. It is built from the disk and bulge terms only; no halo term changes where it ends (the halo at 15 kpc is 0.26% of the 0.159 relative density the edge needs) [C].

2.3 Stars and remnants that leave the galaxy

  • Hypervelocity stars (ejected by the central black hole). Ejection rate: at most 1e-5 per year at 2 sigma, from a 2024 Gaia search that found none new and predicts 5 to 45 unbound ones in the whole catalogue; 0.7e-4 per year for a top-heavy centre; about a dozen candidates known [S]. A steady stream at speed v puts rate x r/v stars inside radius r, so the density falls as r^-2 (the law tuning already uses): at 500 to 1,000 km/s that is 160 to 2,200 inside 16.4 kpc and 2,600 to 36,000 inside 262 kpc [C]. The repository draws 1e3 to 1e4 for the whole galaxy, consistent with the total out to about 100 kpc [S, interstellar-object-rates.md].
  • Neutron stars kicked out. About 40% of neutron stars and 2% of black holes reach escape speed (one population synthesis, in compact-remnant-regions.md) [S]. From the usual kick distributions against the repository's 557 km/s escape speed I get 22% for either of two published kick laws [C on R inputs], so 20% to 40%. On 1e9 isolated neutron stars that is 2e8 to 7e8 unbound. Flying at 300 to 500 km/s over 10 Gyr they spread over 3 to 5 Mpc; about 1e7 are inside 262 kpc [C, assuming steady production]. Per cell that is 1e-8.
  • Hostless supernovae and runaway stars are real but add nothing a cell-level rule needs [S, interstellar-object-rates.md].

2.5 Intergalactic stars

  • Cluster-scale intracluster light. Virgo: 15.8 +- 3 +- 5% of the starlight from planetary-nebula counts, about 7% in a later reanalysis of the core; simulations give 10% to 50% rising with cluster mass; rich clusters of 20% or more are reported [S]. This is a galaxy-cluster effect, far from the Milky Way's group.
  • The Local Group has no intragroup-light measurement that I found. What sits between the Milky Way and Andromeda (about 770 kpc [S, multiple-galaxies.md]) is tidal debris, ejected stars and neutron stars, and gas [I].
  • The cosmic average. About 5.6e8 solar masses of stars per Mpc3 [R] is 9e-8 systems per 64 pc3 cell, and a tenth of that in a void [C, R]. The disk outline needs 1.1e7 times more.

2.6 Dark matter

It matters here as gravity only. The repository's NFW halo (galactic-potential.md) gives 0.0091 solar masses per pc3 at the Sun (0.345 GeV per cm3), 2.0e-4 at 50 kpc, 3.2e-5 at 100 kpc and 2.1e-6 at 262 kpc, which is 1.4e-4 solar masses per cell; 1.0e12 solar masses lie inside 262 kpc [C on S]. No object is generated from it, and the orbit code already feels it.

2.7 What I could not find

Nothing in these searches gave a rate of free-floating planets or brown dwarfs in the halo, a count of discrete high-velocity clouds, an intragroup-light fraction for the Local Group, or the hot gas density at a given radius from a direct measurement. The proposal does not need them; section 7 says where they would matter.

3. What the generator does with far space today [S]

  • Density. relative_density is the disk, bulge and thick disk plus the flat floor max(model, 1e-3) (galaxy/density.py). The floor is counted as "old" population.
  • Outline. build_shell_extents walks outward from the core and stops at the first shell whose equatorial band cannot qualify (expected systems at least one); galaxy_shell stores one row per shell with its highest band. GalaxyBounds.contains refuses any address outside, and random_address draws uniformly over the outline's cells.
  • Scatter. The bright-star and mass passes are object-first (object_first.py): one Poisson count per shell from a certified bound at about 1.2 microseconds per band [S]. The phenomena pass (phenomenon_scatter._band_rows) still walks every band with 32 longitude bins. Every band to 262 kpc is 6.75e9 bands (the sum over shells of pi times the shell number) [C]: 2.25 hours a star pass, and too many for the phenomena walk.
  • Asking. ensure_sector_generated already generates and saves a requested address even when its draw is empty (GEN.76), and an address outside the outline only with outside_ok, at the floor's density (GEN.81).
  • Hypervelocity stars were placed along a random ray to the outline's reach and redrawn when they landed in a band the outline excludes, so they stayed near the plane and inside 15 kpc (hypervelocity_rows; GEN.238 removed the redraw and the outline).
  • Rates are per star (phenomenon_rate_per_star), so they follow whatever star count a cell has; nothing changes for a cell with few stars.
  • Cost of a sparse sector: about 40 ms fixed plus the systems (70 to 90 ms per system for a sector of one system); a per-cell Poisson draw without the database is 0.06 ms [S, sampling-backfill-and-resume.md].

4. The halo density model, with numbers

Form [I, parameters from section 2.1]: a stellar halo term added to _raw_density, a spheroid of axis ratio q,

m          = sqrt(R^2 + (z / q)^2)               # R cylindrical, z height
halo(m)    = A * (max(m, r_core) / r_break)^-n   # n = n_in for m < r_break, n_out beyond
Parameter Baseline Range in the sources Tag
Mass (A is solved from it) 1.5e9 solar masses = 3.75e9 systems at 0.4 solar masses a system 1.1 to 1.9e9 S
Inner slope 2.5 2.3 to 3.3 S
Outer slope 4.5 4.6 to 4.8 S
Break radius 25 kpc 17 to 27 kpc S
Axis ratio q 0.7 0.6 to 0.8 S, I
Core flat inside 0.5 kpc (the bulge dominates there) I

As with the other terms, the radii are ratios to the shape's disk scale length (the break is 9.6 scale lengths) and the mass is a fraction of the visible mass (3%), so a galaxy of any size gets a halo that scales with it. The amplitude is solved like k_norm, never typed in.

  • No cell qualifies. The largest halo expectation anywhere beyond 8.7 kpc from the core is 0.01 systems per cell; the outline is unchanged [C]. The three extreme parameter sets of section 2.1 move the 50 kpc value between 3.4e-6 and 4.5e-5 and the 100 kpc value between 1.2e-7 and 1.9e-6 [C]; the conclusion that a halo cell is almost always empty holds in all of them.
  • Where halo cells are as dense as a threshold: expectation of 0.01 out to 8.7 kpc (3.0e10 cells), 1e-3 out to 22 kpc (4.8e11 cells), 1e-4 out to 39 kpc (2.7e12), 1e-5 out to 65 kpc (1.2e13), 1e-6 out to 108 kpc (5.7e13) [C]. A lower qualifying threshold therefore buys a few more shells at an enormous cell count each; it is not a way to "fill" the halo.
  • Halo systems outside radius r: 2.7e9 beyond 5 kpc, 1.6e9 beyond 16.4 kpc, 1.0e9 beyond 25 kpc, 3.6e8 beyond 50 kpc, 1.3e8 beyond 100 kpc, 3.0e7 beyond 262 kpc [C]. Inside 262 kpc the sphere has 1.18e15 cells for 3.7e9 halo systems: one cell in 317,000 is occupied [C].
  • The star spacing matters for navigation. Beyond about 30 kpc the typical gap between halo stars passes 50 pc, the hop limit Boss set for the route planner (NAV.43 to NAV.45), so a halo star can be reached only from a satellite or cluster, or by a longer hop [I].

5. The proposal: one rule set, a profile for every cell

Boss's rule (2026-10-15): the program has the same rules everywhere in the sphere, and each uncharted sector carries parameters that tune those rules to where the sector is and what is expected there. Nothing is generated anywhere unless asked, as now; a sector that is asked for is generated, by the same rules, wherever it is. The first neighbourhood pull stays in the galactic region.

5.1 Decided and carried over

  • Reach 262 kpc, 96-bit object IDs: approved (Boss, 2026-10-15). 65,535 shells, 1.18e15 cells. The reach is a plan-time setting stored with the galaxy; a cell beyond it is refused ("outside the grid"), as cells are today. At 500 km/s a wanderer needs 510 Myr to cross it [C], so none leaves within any plausible game clock. A galaxy with the halo is re-planned, or renumbered by a migration like the shell move's (no backward compatibility); the 80-bit layout stays for a galaxy planned without a reach.
  • Asked means generated and stored. Today ensure_sector_generated already saves a requested address even when its draw comes out empty (GEN.76) and, with outside_ok, an address outside the outline at the halo floor's density (GEN.81). That behaviour becomes the rule for the whole reach: the outline check and outside_ok go, the reach is the only refusal, and every asked sector is saved and marked generated, empty or not. An uncharted cell is never stored; its profile is computed from the address when someone looks.
  • The neighbourhood pull stays in the galactic region. The random start and the default neighbourhood still draw from the qualifying outline (random_address); an explicit address, span or block anywhere in the reach is accepted. A neighbourhood request out in the halo reports its expected system count (the profile's lambda summed over its cells) and its cost before it runs, since a cubic kiloparsec of empty halo is 1.6e7 cells at 40 ms each.

5.2 The rules that never change by place

  1. Address to profile. A pure function of the galaxy shape, the component table and the address: profile(address). No database read, closed form, the same code for an uncharted preview, a scatter and a fill.
  2. Count. A sector's systems are a Poisson draw with mean lambda x cell volume, where lambda is the profile's total density (today's args.density = relative_density(position), generalised).
  3. Population. Each system picks one population in proportion to the profile's mix (pick_population), and its star is drawn from that population's age window and metallicity (sample_living_star(population=...)).
  4. Pre-placed objects. The bright-star pass, the mass pass and the phenomena pass draw from the same profile, aggregated per shell by a certified bound. A sector's fill builds what was placed in it and draws only the rest (FillContext, placed_star_fraction), so its expected total does not change.
  5. Rare kinds. Each kind's global rate per star times a factor the profile supplies (gas, young stars, metallicity; section 5.3).
  6. Components and wanderers add to the profile; they do not have rules of their own (sections 5.5 and 5.6).
  7. Determinism. A cell's draws come from its address and the galaxy seed, a shell's scatter from the seed and the shell, a component's from the seed and its id; none depends on what else was generated.

5.4 The scatters over the whole sphere

  • Bright-star and mass passes. The algorithm does not change: one Poisson count per shell from a certified bound, bands in proportion, accept by true density over bound (object_first.py). It already costs about 1.2 microseconds per band [S]; to 262 kpc that is 6.75e9 bands, 2.25 hours per pass on one worker, or about 2 minutes with the bound taken over groups of 64 bands [C]. A group's bound is the bound of its nearest band, which the halo term gives exactly because it falls with the spheroidal radius; the efficiency loss is under 10% beyond 8 kpc [I]. Changes: the halo term in band_majorants (replacing the floor's share), the new populations in fractions, band groups beyond the outline. At the defaults the passes place nothing in the halo, clusters or old satellites; they place a few thousand stars in the Clouds (below).
  • Built (GEN.322): the bright-star and mass passes cover the reach. generation/object_first.py ReachGroups cuts the part of a shell beyond the outline into groups of 64 bands (for_shell: from the outline's last band edge toward the pole, the last group taking the polar cap; a shell the outline does not reach is cut from the equator), each bounded by extent_majorants (the old band_majorants formula on any extent, a group's being its nearest point), and bright_stars._reach_spots runs the same algorithm over them: one Poisson count from the groups' bounds, a group in proportion to E * volume * bound, a point uniform in the group, kept by true density over bound, the band, wedge, tier and sector capacity then read from the point's own address. A group takes the larger fractions of the tiers its two edges are judged to be in (reach_bounds); the candidate's tier is exact (ScatterLimits.tier_at). The reach draws from a stream of its own ({seed}:objects:reach:{shell}, after the shell's outline part), so the rows inside the outline are the rows they were, and a pre-placed star's sector is skipped as before. Shells past the outline's last are drawn one at a time while a coarse bound says one can expect OBJECT_FIRST_FAR_MEAN (4) stars or more; the faint shells beyond that last such one (from shell 12,600 at the defaults, 50 kpc) are drawn together (ReachGroups.far: runs of shells 1/128 of their distance thick, 16 steps of colatitude a hemisphere, about 7,000 regions for 53,000 shells; scatter_far, one task), so a plan does not visit 62,000 shells. Measured at the defaults (floors 9,000 and 500): the bound is 86 to 89% efficient between 8 and 40 kpc and 99% beyond 80 kpc (the loss is the disk's arm and population-share bound, as inside the outline), the far pass 79%; the whole reach takes about 72 s on one process, about 122,000 rows beyond the outline. The costs are OBJECT_FIRST_GROUP_BANDS, OBJECT_FIRST_FAR_* in tuning.py; tuning.SCATTER_COVERS_REACH is a developer switch the tests turn off (a toy galaxy's fixed-size halo would fill the reach). Companions ride with their primary row as in the outline (insert_bright_stars), and a row is one object of its class, so the 1 + p_placed bookkeeping is the phenomenon pass's (GEN.323). A galaxy scattered before this has no stars beyond its outline: planetgen plan --redo-scatters mass luminosity draws them.
  • Built (GEN.323): the phenomena pass covers the reach. generation/phenomenon_reach.py runs the same algorithm as GEN.322 over the same ReachGroups, once per class (black holes, neutron stars, planetary nebulae, supernova remnants, magnetars, in SCATTER_CLASSES order) from a stream of its own per reach ({seed}:phenomena:reach:{shell}, far:{first shell}), so the rows inside the outline are the rows they were: one Poisson count from E * rate * volume * bound over the groups, a group in proportion, a point uniform in the group, kept by weight over bound, the shell, band and wedge read from the point's address. A component's systems are its mass over the mean mass of its systems at its age (Component.mean_system_mass_solar, GEN.598: 0.47 at 11 Gy); one Poisson count of systems * the largest tier share of its population is placed by inverse transform of the profile table (isotropic direction), and each candidate is kept with its landing tier's share over that largest, so the tier, the sector and the cell follow from where the star lands.   • Built (GEN.526, 2026-10-17): the satellites' young and intermediate stars. galaxy/satellite_history.py works a satellite's history out from what its row holds (stellar mass, half-mass radius, mean stellar age), with no share typed in. (1) The stars formed from SATELLITE_HISTORY_SPAN_GY (13 Gy) ago to now at a rate exp(-lambda t), the one-parameter family whose mean age can be set to the row's: a rising rate for the Large Cloud (mean age 5 Gy, lambda about -0.11 per Gy), a falling one for the dwarf spheroidals. (2) The present rate is the share of the mass formed in the history's last instant; the Schmidt-Kennicutt law inverts it to the gas the satellite needs ([R2321], eq. 4, read in [R2321]): since GEN.1192 the gas is an exponential disc 1.8 times as long as the stars (SATELLITE_GAS_TO_STELLAR_SCALE_LENGTH_RATIO; the HI of a late-type dwarf reaches 1.8 +/- 0.8 times the optical size, [R4277], abstract read), the law is A Sigma^n (Sigma / (Sigma + Sigma_s))^(m - n) (the steepening to 2.1 where the gas thins that the main disc's law has, so a thin disc lasts longer than a dense one), and the central density is the one whose disc integrates to the present rate. (3) Where the gas at the half-mass radius is below 3 solar masses per pc2 (the low end of [R3951]'s 3 to 10, ApJ 609, 667, read in [R3951]) the satellite forms no stars now: its young share is 0, and the stars its history put in the last 3 Gy stay its intermediate share. Not built here: the Clouds' gas in the nebula and remnant kinds (built in GEN.528, below), a satellite's young stars in the system-type tables, and the satellites' own metallicity by age.
  • Built (GEN.1193): the gas of a satellite by its distance from the host. generation/satellites.py link 7: dwarfs of the Local Group within about 270 kpc of the Milky Way or Andromeda mostly hold no HI and those beyond mostly do ([R1671], abstract and section 3.4 read). 5.6 Wanderers

  • Hypervelocity stars keep their pre-placement. Direction uniform on the sphere, distance uniform in [0, reach] (density r^-2), speed 500 to 1,000 km/s; the outline test goes (they are wanderers, not disk members). With the code's 1e3 to 1e4 total read as "out to 100 kpc" (section 2.3), 84% land beyond the disk [C].
  • Kicked neutron stars and black holes are an ejecta population: unbound fraction 20% to 40% of neutron stars and 2% of black holes [S, C], density N / (4 pi r^2 v T) out to v T (3 to 5 Mpc), with v the speed above escape (300 to 500 km/s) and T = 10 Gyr [I]. Against the bound halo neutron stars they take over near 50 kpc: ejecta per cell 1.3e-7 at 50 kpc, 3.3e-8 at 100 kpc, 4.8e-9 at 262 kpc (2e8 unbound, 300 km/s), 3.5 to 21 times the halo's at 100 kpc and 39 to 230 times at 262 kpc, depending on the assumptions [C]. They are not pre-placed (1e7 rows inside 262 kpc, 1e-8 per cell); they join the cell's lambda for neutron stars and black holes and are drawn at fill.

5.11 Wolf-Rayet stars and nebulae

Superseded in part (GEN.939 and GEN.940, 2026-10-30). A Wolf-Rayet star's present mass is its track's and its luminosity follows that mass by the helium-star relation of MIST's Wolf-Rayet rows, cut at 1e5 to 3e6 Lsun; the log-uniform 1e5 to 1e6 range and the 0.4 to 0.6 of the initial mass below describe the model before that change. See post-main-sequence-stars.md.

Why they do not exist. They were never built, and nothing in the repository or its history decides against them. They appear first in the anomalies research of 2026-10-09 (anomalies.md, GEN.113's output) as Tier 2 item 3, "Wolf-Rayet class and ring bubble", with the note "needs a W spectral class in the star model"; GEN.113's Done clause was the list, no build item was filed from it, and MAP.132 only mentions a Wolf-Rayet marker. The star model is the blocker: evolve_star has main-sequence, supergiant, subgiant, giant and remnant states and no W state, SPECTRAL_PROBABILITIES_LARGE_STAR stops at O, and NEBULA_CLASSES has no wind-blown class [S].

The star: a state of massive stars, not a new scatter. A Wolf-Rayet star is a very massive star in the last stretch of its life, stripped to a hot helium core with a dense wind (temperature 30,000 to 200,000 K, luminosity 1e5 to 1e6 solar [R]). The model:

  • A star of initial mass at least WR_MIN_INITIAL_MASS_SOL = 25 (solar metallicity) is Wolf-Rayet during the last 0.3 Myr of its life (WR_PHASE_MYR, at most half the life for the heaviest, which live 0.12 Myr at 100 solar masses), instead of a supergiant. Its present mass is 40% to 60% of the initial mass. Subtype by phase: WN for the first 60%, WC for the next 30%, WO for the last 10% (the heaviest only); spectral text WN6, WC8, Yerkes class W in the type string.
  • The threshold rises as metallicity falls (about 35 at half solar, 56 at a fifth [I], from the winds' metallicity dependence [R]), so it is a population parameter in the profile's per-population table, not a constant.
  • The bright and mass passes already place the massive stars; they then draw the state through evolve_star, so Wolf-Rayet stars come out of the passes without a new rate. A check that the rate is right without tuning [C]: a star formation rate of 1.65 solar masses per year [R] with the repository's IMF gives 4.0e-3 stars per year born at 25 solar masses or more, times 0.3 Myr is 1,180 Wolf-Rayet stars in the Milky Way, against the 1,200 +- 100 expected [S]. The same arithmetic for the Large Cloud (0.2 solar masses per year, threshold 35) gives 87 against about 150 known, and for the Small Cloud (0.04, threshold 56) 8 against about 12 [R]; the catalogue figures are recalled, so only the Milky Way match is firm.
  • Where they sit follows from the age window: none in any population older than about 10 Myr (so none in the halo, clusters, old satellites or the bulge), and in the thin disk along the arms with the other massive stars.

The nebula: class X, a Wolf-Rayet ring. A star blows a thin shell of swept-up gas or ejected material; about 40% of Galactic Wolf-Rayet stars show one [R, I]. New class X in NEBULA_CLASSES, family emission (ionized by the star), radius 2 to 35 pc (anomalies.md; NGC 6888 is 7.6 x 5.0 pc [S]), a thin shell with a hollow interior drawn with the existing nebula_shape mesh, with a host rule ("W", 0, 9, ("X",), 0.4) in NEBULA_HOST_RULES and an age limit near the star's age (NEBULA_CLASS_MAX_AGE_MYR["X"] = 5.0). Its one required star is the W star at the centre (a real system, section 5.10). A nebula that spans sectors follows the same cube-by-cube rule as the clusters. After the phase ends the star collapses and the shell becomes the core-collapse remnants the generator already makes.

In the sphere profile (section 5.3): kind_factors["wolf-rayet"] is not a number to tune but the massive-star density the profile already carries; the metallicity-dependent threshold is the only new per-population field. Disk and Magellanic-type cells get them; halo, old clusters, old satellites, the bulge and implicit uncharted far cells do not. Counts in the sphere at the default floors [C]: about 1,200 in the Milky Way disk, none beyond it except the Cloud estimates above if the Cloud population is built.

5.13.1 The chain

One root draw, then each link reads only the links before it. Every link that has a scatter draws it from the galaxy seed (unit_seed(galaxy_seed, "<link name>")), as a log-normal about the median law, so two seeds give two different galaxies and one seed gives the same galaxy.

  2. Halo mass Mh from M* by the stellar-to-halo relation (row 2; see the design caution there: the conditional is not the inverse of the median).
  3. Virial radius and concentration from Mh (row 3); the dark matter profile, the enclosed mass at any radius and the circular speed follow by calculation (row 12).
  4. Size and shape of the stars from M*: disk scale length, scale height, bulge share and size, bar, arms (rows 4 to 6).
  5. Central black hole and nuclear cluster from the bulge and the galaxy mass (rows 7 and 8).

5.14 Young open clusters and OB associations (GEN.244)

What is built. A generated galaxy draws its bound young clusters from its own disk (generation/open_clusters.py, physics/open_cluster_evolution.py), with no cluster typed in and none taken from a catalogue. They are galaxy_components rows of kind cluster and profile plummer (a globular cluster is a king), named Open cluster 00001 and on, stored with the globular clusters by the plan and read by the same component field.

  1. Formed. The disk's star formation history (galaxy/disk_history.py, GEN.469) gives the mass formed in each ring and step of the last Gy. The share that goes into bound clusters is the cluster formation efficiency of the ring's star formation rate density, 1 / (1.15 + 0.6 S^-0.4 + 0.05 S^-1) with S in solar masses per year per kpc2 ([R2502], eq. 45) [R, read]. Initial masses fall as M^-2 between 50 and 1e6 solar masses ([R2542]: slope -2, a physical upper limit as high as 1e6; [R2530]: 90% of embedded stars are in groups of 100 or more) [R, read]; 1e6 is the widest of the limits found (source rule: most permission). The mean initial mass of that function turns the bound mass into a Poisson count.
  2. Placed. At the radius of its ring, at the azimuth and exponential height (GEN.544) stars of its age have there (the young population under 0.1 Gy, so the arms hold the youngest, and intermediate over it), on the circular orbit with that population's dispersion. Its [Fe/H] is the disk's mean at its radius for that population, drawn with the population's scatter. Ages run from 1 Myr (younger clusters are still embedded in the cores and Bok globules GEN.241 and GEN.320 make) to 1 Gyr.
  3. Lost. Stellar evolution returns 10^((log t - a)^b + c) of the initial mass above 12.5 Myr ([R2542: Table 1], interpolated in log Z), and tides disrupt the cluster in t_dis = t0 (M/Msun)^0.62 with t0 = Cenv 1e-4 gamma (rho_amb / (Msun/pc3))^-0.5 and Cenv 300 to 800 Myr ([R2542]; Lamers, Gieles and Portegies [R4923]) [R, read]. Cenv is drawn once per galaxy from the seed; rho_amb is the model's mean stellar density where the cluster lies. A cluster below 50 solar masses is gone.
  4. Sized. A half-mass radius from a half-mass density of 10^(3 +- 1) solar masses per pc3 at birth, grown by a factor of 5 to 10 over 100 Myr (Portegies [R3580]) [R, read], held to a sixth of the cluster's Jacobi radius.

Stars. Every star of a cluster shares its age, so the cluster's stars belong to the population of the quarter-decade of age it falls in: twelve populations, open_cluster_00 (1 to 1.78 Myr) to open_cluster_11 (562 Myr to 1 Gyr), each with the thin disc's rotation and the dispersion its age heating gives the bin (galaxy.structure.disc_dispersion_kms). bright_stars.population takes the twelve names (migration 0110). The scatters leave the clusters to the component pass (as they do the globular clusters and satellites); the pass draws a cluster's bright and massive stars with one stream per cluster and the bright-star and mass walks read the age window of its population. A cluster adds its density to its cell's mix in its age's population, so the profile and the sector page name it ("Open cluster stars, 1 Myr to 1.78 Myr" for the youngest bin).

No phenomena of its own. A young open cluster places none of the component pass's remnants and nebulae: the disk's supernova remnants and nebulae already come from the disk's young population, and the cluster's stars are a fraction of a percent of it. An OB association is the unbound form of the same stars and is the disk's young population (arms included); it is not a separate component.

Stand-ins (labelled, tunable, replaced when verified). The [Fe/H] scatter of a cluster, COMPONENT_FE_H_SIGMA["open_cluster"] = 0.05 [stand-in, to verify]; the cap of a sixth of the Jacobi radius (Henon's tidally filling cluster, recalled) [stand-in]; the gas left out of rho_amb (Lamers et al. use stars and gas, so disruption here is slightly slower) [stand-in]; the twelve bins [model choice]. The Milky Way's catalogue (about 3,000 clusters, tens of thousands in all) is a recalled test figure only [S].

The central ring's upper mass and cloud disruption (GEN.1195). The galactic core ring (galaxy/core_gas.py) forms its clusters under the most massive cluster its gas can form, not the disc's 1e6: M_cl,max = eps Gamma M_T min(1, t_fb / t_ff,2D)^4 ([R3720], Eqs 2 to 10; physics/cluster_maximum_mass.py) with eps 0.1, Gamma the ring's bound share, Q from the gas's dispersion (5 km/s for the ring), the Toomre mass 4 pi^5 G^2 Sigma_g^3 / kappa^4, the ring's gas surface density Sigma_g the inflow times a residence time over its area (a stand-in, CORE_RING_GAS_RESIDENCE_MYR) and kappa from the nucleus's circular speed at the ring. It is 1.6e4 for the Milky Way shape (3.2e4 at 1e3 solar masses per pc2; the Arches and the Quintuplet 2e4), held under 1e5. The count is of the mass function held to it, so the same bound mass makes more, lighter clusters. The ring's clusters also lose mass to passing clouds ([R1555] Eq 24 as [R2504, R2505] put it in the ring, 5.7 Myr for 1e4 solar masses at 0.5 pc; open_cluster_evolution.cloud_shock_mass_lost_sol), integrated along their growing half-mass radius. Over 40 seeds the ring held 4.2 young clusters over 5e3 solar masses and 11 older ones over 5e4 (396 alive) before, and 0.6 and none (58 alive) after; the observed two and none are the plausibility target. The stand-ins are rows CMX1 to CMX7 (gap-filling-plan 6.145). The disc's rings are held to the same relation (GEN.1196): each cell of the formation table caps its initial masses at eps Gamma M_GMC,max of its ring's gas (the last Gy of the one-zone history), the flat rotation curve's kappa and the disc's gas dispersion (10 km/s), and the count of clusters follows the capped mass function. The Milky Way shape's rings change from shear-limited to feedback-limited at about 4 kpc (the paper: 4.3, 3.5 to 6.3 kpc) with a cloud maximum of a few 1e5; the heaviest disc cluster falls from 6.8e5 to 4.1e3 (clusters above 1e4: 484 to none) against the observed 1e4 to 4e4. It is under the observed level because the history's gas at 8 kpc is 4.3 solar masses per pc2 against the measured 13 (the cluster maximum goes steeply with the gas): row CMX8, and the gas is the next thing to read.

What the numbers do. For five drawn galaxies (stellar mass 3e9 to 7e10 solar masses) the draw gives 4,800 to 92,000 clusters alive, a median age of 60 to 150 Myr and a heaviest cluster of 6e5 to 9e5 solar masses; a compact disk makes many more than an extended one because its star formation rate density, and with it the cluster formation efficiency, is higher. Two readings to keep in mind: the efficiency fit gives about half of Kruijssen's own value for the Milky Way and about six times less than the bound rate Lamers et al. observe, so the counts lean low in the sense of the fit and high in the sense of the compact disks; and the upper mass limit of 1e6 makes the heaviest cluster of a rich galaxy heavier than any the Galaxy shows.

7. Honest uncertainty

  • The halo's normalisation is the weakest number: the stellar mass (1.5 +- 0.4e9, from a search summary of [R1026]) and the local density (about 0.5% recalled) disagree by 2.7 times. Either way every halo cell more than 9 kpc from the core is below 0.012 systems, and beyond 20 kpc below 2e-3 [C].
  • Slopes and breaks are still disputed: breaks from 10 to 30 kpc across studies (a 2026 DESI paper says "doubly broken", twisted) [S]. A single broken power law is a first model, not a map.
  • The kick numbers are recalled, so the 20% to 40% unbound fraction is a range from one population synthesis [S] and a recalled kick law [R, C]. Uniform-in-time production overcounts if star formation peaked early.
  • The Magellanic populations (GEN.526, built): a satellite's young and intermediate shares come from a one-parameter history set by its mean stellar age and a gas density that the Schmidt-Kennicutt law gives (section 5.5). The history family (an exponential in time) is a model choice [I]; the satellite table's masses, sizes and ages are still the recalled, unchecked ones of GEN.237 (GEN.369 re-derives them).   • Metallicity values and dispersions in section 5.3 are recalled and are inputs the generator does not have yet; until slice 1 lands, halo and cluster stars would still receive full planet systems.
  • Cluster remnants: the pre-placed cluster black holes (3.6e4) use the field's rate, which overcounts; clusters retain few.
  • I did not model high-velocity cloud counts, the hot gas density profile, intragroup light, stellar streams or Andromeda's halo; each is either too small to alter a cell (streams) or outside the reach (Andromeda).
  • Not checked: that nebula_field returns no gas outside the disk; that no page needs a pre-existing sector row to show an uncharted cell.
