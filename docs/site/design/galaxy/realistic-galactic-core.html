Summary

  • The real center is two stellar systems and a gas ring on top of the bulge [R, with the numbers of fill-order-curves section 4.2]: the nuclear star cluster (about 2.5e7 solar masses inside a few parsecs, Sgr A* at its center), the nuclear stellar disk (about 1e9 solar masses inside 200 pc) and the central molecular zone (3 to 5e7 solar masses of gas). The generator has none of them: its center is a bar bulge with a flat top, 880 to 1,060 systems per cell, against 1.1e4 to 3.4e4 in the real disk and about 1.5e7 in the cell holding Sgr A* [C].
  • Keep the rules, add terms. The three structures enter the density model as three more terms of relative_density, three populations in the profile's mix (nuclear cluster, nuclear disk, and the existing young for the star-forming ring), and a gas term in gas. The count rule (Poisson of lambda times volume), the population rule and the scatter rules do not change. No special object, and no special case in ensure_sector_generated (section 3).
  • The one new rule is a ceiling on what a cell resolves. A cell that expects more systems than the cell star ceiling (default 5,000, about 100 seconds at the measured 20 ms a system) fills only the stars at or above the fill luminosity cap (default 100 solar luminosities, raised for a cell that those stars would still fill past the ceiling, as the nuclear cluster's cell). The rest is unresolved: its count and mass are computed from the address, shown on the sector page and never stored. The per-sector state that records how deep a cell was taken (bright_level_sol) already exists and already supports topping a cell up to a deeper floor (section 4). Ordinary space never reaches the ceiling, so it is untouched.
  • The cost that decides it [C]: the cell holding Sgr A* has about 1.5e7 systems; at the measured 16.4 ms a system that is 68 hours and, at the default 48 KiB a system, 720 GB for one cell. At the default cap it is 4.8e4 systems, 13 minutes. The nuclear disk inside 200 pc is 2.5e9 systems in full; a run that sets its ceiling low enough makes it 8e6, about what the current 200 pc core fill already is (7.5e6).
  • Planets nearly vanish in the nuclear cluster [C]: with the survival rule of the cluster note, a planet outlives 12 Gyr only inside 0.03 AU at 0.5 pc from Sgr A*, 0.4 AU at 4 pc and 2 AU at 10 pc; in the nuclear disk the limit is 3 to 10 AU (section 7).
  • Density first, real-world, no fudging (section 3.0): each term is fitted to a published number and checked in mathcheck. Then tiered scatter limits (section 5.1, five settings with sliders in 5.2): the luminosity floor (9,000) and the mass limit (14) the scatter draws its line at get a core pair (9,900 and 15.4 by default, 10% higher), the baseline in the disk and bulge, and a halo luminosity floor of 500. They touch no density. They move the pre-placed young stars by 30% to 50% for the mass limit and 11% to 14% for the floor [C], change nothing for old stars (so nothing in the halo at the defaults), and are a separate lever from the resolution cap.
  • For Boss (defaults written in, section 10): the cap value; the tier boundaries; replacing the Gaussian core of ADM.49 by the new terms; and the two mass figures the model is tuned to.

2. What the model gives today

  • Density is flat through the middle: relative density 154 at the center, 14 to 17 systems per pc^3, no cusp [S, fill-order-curves section 4.2]. ADM.49 added an optional Gaussian core_amplitude (default 0, scale CORE_RADIUS_FRACTION = 0.1 of the bulge scale radius, 158 pc at the default shape) [S, density.py, tuning.py]. It has no cluster, no disk and no cusp, and at 158 pc it is the wrong size for the cluster and a rough guess for the disk.
  • The black hole is in: the potential has a softened Sgr A* (4.3e6, eps 1 pc) [S, galactic-potential.md], and the one core cell gets a quiescent black hole or, rarely, a quasar [S, add_galactic_nucleus].
  • There is no per-cell star cap (MAX_SYSTEMS_PER_SECTOR only limits the estimate text) [S]. A cell is Poisson around its expected count and placed by Poisson-disk growth. That is the part that cannot meet the real center.

3. The density terms

Built (GEN.280, schema v99): the nuclear cluster and disk are _nuclear_cluster and _nuclear_disk in galaxy/density.py, the nuclear_cluster and nuclear_disk populations of the profile, and galaxy_shape.nuclear_amplitude (planetgen plan --nuclear-amplitude) scales both; ADM.49's Gaussian core is removed. Departures from the text below, each so the numbers match the published ones in the default model: the cluster's mass is 6.4e-4 of the bulge's (the model's bulge holds 3.9e10 solar masses at 0.4 a system, not 1.5e10, so 1.7e-3 would put 6.5e7 in the cluster); the disk's total in the Milky Way structure is 1.5e9 (1.0e9 inside 200 pc; in a drawn galaxy it follows the galaxy's stellar mass, GEN.628 below); the cluster's cusp is held flat inside the radius where its density equals the core ball's mean (about 1.2 pc), so the ball's one cell holds exactly the mass inside 2 pc; and the age windows are single ranges with the observed mixes' means (cluster 3 to 12 Gyr, disk 4 to 12). mathcheck compares the mass, half-light radius, density at 1 pc, disk mass inside 200 pc and disk height with the published values. The gas term (3.4) and the young share of the disk are not part of this item.

All three are closed form, are ratios to the galaxy shape's own lengths and masses (so a toy galaxy gets a proportionate center), and are stored as shape fields next to the bulge's. population_densities returns them as populations, so the skeleton bound, the Galaxy Map prisms and the profile read one source.

3.1 The nuclear star cluster

A model with inner slope 1 (a Hernquist profile): rho(r) = M a / (2 pi r (r + a)^3), M = 2.5e7, half-mass radius 5 pc so a = 2.07 pc (a projected half-light radius of 3.8 to 4.2 pc, against the observed 4.2 [R]). It has a cusp (rho is proportional to 1/r inside a), as the observed cluster does (slope 1.0 to 1.4 [R]). [C]:

The inner cusp below 0.1 pc is not resolved by any cell (a cell is 2 to 4 pc); the model does not need it for generation, only for the orbit of anything placed there.

3.2 The nuclear stellar disk

An exponential disk with a sech^2 vertical profile, the isothermal sheet the thin disk had before GEN.544 (it kept this form; the thin disk is now exponential in height): rho(R, z) = rho0 exp(-R / R_d) sech^2(z / 2 h), R_d = 90 pc, h = 35 pc, rho0 = 215 solar masses per pc^3, which puts 1e9 inside 200 pc (65% of the total) [C]. It gives 215 at the center, 71 at 100 pc and 23 at 200 pc, inside the 50 to 150 mean the research notes quote [R]. Scaling: R_d and h in proportion to the bulge scale radius, mass as the nuclear-cluster mass times 40 (1e9 / 2.5e7 [R]).

3.4 The gas

The central molecular zone is a gas term in gas: about 4e7 solar masses in a ring of radius 100 pc and width 50 pc, thickness 30 pc [R]. It feeds the same kind_factors as the disk's gas (supernova remnants, molecular clouds, emission and reflection nebulae) and the young share of the nuclear disk (about 0.07 solar masses a year of star formation, so a young share of order 1e-3 of the disk's stars [R, I]). nebula_field must read this term, or the center keeps its nebula rate of the plain disk; I did not check how it reads gas today (the halo note flagged the same question).

Built (GEN.628, galaxy/structure_draw.py): the disk is no longer a fixed 61 times the cluster (that ratio was fixed Milky Way content and made the disk 21 percent of a 4e9 galaxy's stars against about 3 percent in the Milky Way). Its mass is drawn from the galaxy's stellar mass: the center log-uniform between the two fits of [R1487] ([R1487], Eqs. 1 and 2: log M_ND = 1.32 log(M*/1e9) + 7.62 photometric, 0.86 log(M*/1e9) + 7.99 spectroscopic; 16 TIMER barred galaxies; two verified fits that disagree, so the widest range), scattered by a placeholder 0.3 dex (the paper prints the fit errors, not the scatter of its points). Only a barred galaxy has a disk (the sample is all barred, and the ring below needs a bar). A 4e9 galaxy's disk is about 6 to 8 percent of its stars, a Milky Way mass galaxy's 2.8e9 to 7e9 against the 1e9 to 1.5e9 measured (the Milky Way is outside both fits). The disk's size ratios stay Milky Way stand-ins.

Built (GEN.282, galaxy/core_gas.py): derived, not typed in. The disk's gas inside the bar's gap (disk_history, GEN.469) leaves on 1 Gy (tuning.CORE_GAS_INFLOW_TIME_GY, a stand-in: the bar's inflow time is not sourced): 0.71 solar masses a year for the Milky Way-like shape against the 0.8 measured. The ring forms stars at 0.07/0.8 of the inflow (the review's continuity equation, [R1910]), 0.062 a year. The ring is the bulge's scale radius times the ratios of the published ring (radius 100 pc, width 50 pc, height 30 pc), and the cluster formation efficiency of its star formation rate density [R2502] gives a bound share of 0.61; the rest, times 0.1 Gy, are the field's young stars. They are taken out of nuclear_disk and put in young (about 1.4% of the disk's stars at the ring), so the total density, the skeleton's bound and the map prisms do not change; the profile's young share, and through it the supernova remnant, molecular cloud and nebula rates (nebula_field.gas_factor), follow the ring. The bound share forms ring clusters in the GEN.244 draw (about 400 within 300 pc), with the ring's own tidal field and circular speed (the nuclear cluster and disk; the bulge is left out, so the speed is a floor). A galaxy without a bar has no ring.

Known tension (stand-ins, labelled in tuning): the ring over-produces massive clusters. A drawn galaxy has 5 to 12 ring clusters younger than 10 Myr above 5e3 solar masses where two are known (Arches, Quintuplet), and 7 to 21 older than that above 5e4 where none are. The causes are the M^-2 mass function to 1e6 solar masses (the widest limit kept by the source rules), the ambient density leaving out the gas, and no cloud-shock disruption of the central molecular zone's clusters. A mass limit for the zone [R4436] and the shock disruption are research items.

4.2 The rule

Built (GEN.281, schema v98): steps 2 to 4 below are in planetgen/galaxy/resolution.py and the fill; the two settings are galaxy_shape.cell_star_ceiling and fill_luminosity_cap_sol (planetgen plan --cell-star-ceiling/--fill-luminosity-cap). A capped cell is taken to the cap by the existing level machinery; the dim systems are not drawn and the below-cut remnants are counted as unresolved. Step 5 is as described.

  1. The profile gives lambda (expected systems per volume) and so the cell's expected count N.
  2. Ceiling. The cell star ceiling is a setting of the request or run, default 5,000 systems a cell (about 100 seconds, 240 MB; Boss 1:46 PM EDT, cut from 50,000 after the nuclear cluster's cell took 14 minutes), the same for every cell in it. If N is at or below it the cell is filled whole, exactly as now.
  3. Fill luminosity cap. Above the ceiling the cell fills only the stars at or above the fill luminosity cap, a second setting (default 100 solar luminosities, the lowest the code accepts today). Those are the pre-placed ones from the passes (at or above the scatter's floor for the band) plus, between the cap and the scatter's floor, a Poisson draw per population of N times its bright_band_fraction [S, star_population.py]. The draw is keyed by address and by the cap, so a cell taken down in two steps ends with the stars one step gives (the property mass-backfill.md already relies on). At the defaults the Sgr A* ball resolves 4.8e4 systems, a 4 pc cell of the cluster 4.7e3 and a 10 pc cell 240 [C]. This is a step and not a slope: a cell expecting 49,000 systems fills all of them and one expecting 51,000 fills about 160. If the step bothers him, the same two settings can feed a ladder that picks the lowest floor fitting the ceiling instead (an earlier draft of this note); the cell's recorded level is the same either way.
  4. Unresolved. The stars below the floor are not made. Their expected count and mass follow from the same fractions (N times one minus the resolved share; the mass from the population's mean). The sector page shows "unresolved: about 1.5e7 systems, 6e6 solar masses, below 100 solar luminosities", computed from the address, never stored.
  5. State. The floor reached is recorded in sector_stats.bright_level_sol (the level GEN.44 and GEN.187 already keep) [S]. Nothing new is stored: the existing "top up from the old floor to a new one" path is how a cell goes deeper.

Resolved counts for the two populations that matter [C, bright_star_fraction]: bulge-age stars at or above 100 / 300 / 1,000 solar luminosities are 3.2e-3, 1.7e-3 and 8e-5 of all systems; a young population at 100 / 1,000 / 9,000 is 1.5e-2, 3.2e-3 and 3.1e-4. So the ball resolves 4.8e4 systems at 100, 2.6e4 at 300 and 1.2e3 at 1,000; a 4 pc cell resolves 4.7e3 at 100. The code refuses a floor under the brightest white dwarf (50 solar luminosities since GEN.333, 100 before [S, check_threshold]), so that is the fill cap's and the halo floor's lowest setting; the default cap stays 100. A cap under 50 would need white dwarfs counted in the fractions.

4.3 Why this and not the alternatives

  • Not a hard cap on the count. Dropping random systems down to the cap changes the density the cell reports and breaks "same rules everywhere".
  • Not smaller cells. A 1 pc edge in the core is a different grid; the edge is decided (4 pc, Boss) and the identifiers are 80 to 96 bits for it.
  • Not a statistical object. A "nuclear cluster object" with a population but no cells is what was dropped in 2026-10; Boss now wants the rules kept. The unresolved part is a statistical population, but of the cell, derived from the profile like the dark matter on the sector page.
  • Reuses the level. The floor is the existing per-sector depth; the cap is a rule choosing the first one.

4.4 Asking for more

A user, an administrator or a run can ask a cell for a lower floor; the work queue answers with the ETA and the storage (the existing progress and ETA path, halo note 5.5) and runs it as a job, the way the cluster core cells are queued. The cap is then a default and not a wall. A request for a floor below the cap's is the one place a human decision enters, and the page shows the cost before it runs.

4.5 Reading it back

  • Region queries (stage C, DB.34 and GEN.276) return resolved objects like any others, with one addition: a result includes the unresolved count and mass of each cell it touched, so a count "within 50 pc of Sgr A*" is never silently short. Page and query limits stay as they are; a 50 pc sphere around the center would be about 12,000 cells, each with at most 5e4 rows, so the query takes LIMIT and pagination as for any dense region [I].
  • The Galaxy Map reads density from the model, not the rows (prisms), so the center shows at once with no generation.
  • Runs. generate --core (GEN.24) takes the cap as an option; its estimate (PERF.3) already samples cells and now reports the resolved and the unresolved counts separately, so the user sees what a cap buys before the run starts.

5. Populations and the profile at the core

The profile's fields (lambda, mix, per-population age and [Fe/H] and dispersion, gas, kind_factors) are filled for the new terms exactly as for the halo and clusters: add a row to the population table, no rule. Two details:

  • Pre-placed shares. An old population holds no star at or above 9,000 solar luminosities or 14 solar masses (halo note 5.3); the nuclear cluster's old 80% adds none and its young 20% adds a few. The bright and mass passes therefore place almost nothing in the core ball by themselves; the fill and the cap floor produce its stars.
  • Compact remnants follow kind_factors = 1 for every population (5.2e-3 neutron stars per living star [C, halo note]): the ball holds about 7.8e4 neutron stars and, at roughly 1e-3 per star, 1.5e4 black holes; the literature figure for the inner parsec is of order 1e4 black holes [R]. They are dark and cheap (no planets, no spectrum), so I recommend counting a remnant as one tenth of a system against the cap; the factor is a guess that the build measures, not a fact.

5.1 Tiered scatter limits

Boss (8:50 PM EDT): the density plan matches real-world observations as closely as we can, with no fudging; then, for the scatter sweeps, the minimum mass and minimum luminosity are tweaked by region. So the density (section 3) and the fill (section 4) are never touched by the tiers. They change only where the scatter draws its line.

The limits that exist today [S]. All three are global to the galaxy, and the same in every cell:

Limit Stored Default and choices Governs
Luminosity floor L galaxy_shape.bright_star_min_luminosity_sol 9,000 solar luminosities; presets from 2,500 to 4 million (luminosity_floor.PRESETS) every star at least this bright is placed by the scatter
Stellar mass limit M galaxy_shape.bright_star_mass_limit_sol (GEN.185), set by --phenomenon-min-mass (tuning.PHENOMENON_MIN_MASS_SOLAR) 14 solar masses; presets 8 to 20 in steps of 2 (PHENOMENON_MIN_MASS_PRESETS) every star born at or above it is placed by the scatter; also the cut of the neutron-star and black-hole below-cut draws (GEN.168)
Compact-object limit C --compact-min-mass the word "star" (use M), or 1, 2, 4 or 6 solar masses the lowest neutron star or black hole the scatter places

The backfill rings (1, 2, 5, 8 solar masses) lie below M, and the fill draws what is left (FillContext.star_mass_limit_sol).

Built (GEN.333). Each band takes one of three tiers, and a tier is a floor and a mass limit (galaxy/scatter_limits.py, ScatterLimits). The baseline is whatever the galaxy was planned with (L and M, 9,000 and 14 at the defaults). The core tier takes its own floor and mass limit, which follow the baseline times CORE_TIER_FACTOR (1.10: 9,900 and 15.4) until they are set. The halo tier takes its own floor (halo_min_luminosity_sol, default 500, from 50 to 4,000,000) and the baseline's mass limit, since a halo star is old and the mass pass places none of them. C follows M when it is "star" and is left alone when the user set a number. The tier's limits need not be a preset or lie inside the 2,500 to 4 million range: validation stays on the user's own values. The three settings are stored with the galaxy (DB.54, below).

Where the tier is chosen. From the population mix of the band (a shell and a band, the unit the scatter walks), at the band's center line and averaged over eight longitude bins, by the dominant term of lambda (tuning.TIER_DOMINANCE_SHARE, one half):

Tier Limits When
core its own floor and mass limit (9,900 and 15.4) the nuclear terms (cluster and disk, 3.1 and 3.2) are at least half of the band's density
baseline L and M everything else: thin and thick disk, bulge, arms (a cluster's or satellite's own mix is a component, not a place, and the scatter places none of their stars)
halo its own floor (500) and M the halo term is at least half of the band's density

Discrete, not a blend. The scatter's object-first sampler takes one certified bound per band and then accepts by true density, and the backfill and the fill must use the same limit for a cell. One value per band, a pure function of the address, keeps all three in step with no new state; a blend would give every cell its own limit inside a band and break the bound. Boundaries are where the dominant term changes, so they follow the density and not a radius; for the Milky Way the core tier is the inner 150 to 250 pc and the halo tier begins where the halo term passes the disk and bulge together, a few kiloparsecs above the plane [I]. A cell's tier is on its sector page.

How the tiers sit with the ceiling and the cap. The fill luminosity cap (section 4.2) works below the band's luminosity floor and sets how deep one dense cell is filled; the tier sets where the scatter leaves off and the fill starts. Between the cap and the band's floor a dense cell draws a Poisson number of stars per population. The two never overlap and never leave a gap.

5.3 Horizontal-branch and AGB stars

Superseded in part (GEN.935, 2026-10-29). The luminosity, temperature and radius of a star after the main sequence now come from the MIST v1.2 tracks, and a star of 2.2 solar masses and more has core helium burning, so the hand-set luminosity and temperature ranges, the 0.1 subgiant and giant shares and the luminosity formulas below describe the model before that change; the horizontal branch (100 Myr, 35 to 50 Lsun, the colours by [Fe/H] and age) is unchanged. Boss (9:12 PM EDT): why not build horizontal-branch stars into the mix? There is no good reason beyond the stellar evolution model not having the phases.

What the model has [S, physics/stellar_evolution.py evolve_star]: main sequence, then a subgiant that brightens to twice its main-sequence light, then one giant phase of 0.1 of the main-sequence lifetime (log-uniform luminosity 50 to 1,000 solar luminosities, 2% of it a tip of 1,000 to 2,500, 3,500 to 5,000 K), then a white dwarf at once. That giant phase is the red giant branch. The core helium-burning phase (the horizontal branch and the red clump) and the asymptotic giant branch are missing, so a star goes from the branch tip to a white dwarf. It was a simple chain built before the model had a metallicity or a halo, and the horizontal branch's colour depends on both [R].

  • The potential needs the cluster and the disk. The galactic potential has the softened black hole (eps 1 pc) and the bulge. The density model's bulge holds about 2e5 solar masses inside 20 pc [C, 154 times 0.0986 systems per pc^3 times 0.4], against the cluster's 2.1e7, so a star placed by this model would orbit a potential about a hundred times lighter than the matter there, apart from the black hole (the potential's own bulge term was not checked) [I]. The nuclear terms go into galactic-potential.md's mass components so GEN.223's nightly update moves them correctly; at 10 pc the circular speed is 96 km/s and the period 0.6 Myr, at 1 pc about 35 kyr, at 0.1 pc about 1.4 kyr [C].
  • Rate of update. GEN.223 moves every object and updates rows only when next_update_due elapses (decided). In the cap-limited core only the resolved objects exist, so the update cost is the resolved count, not the 1.5e7.
  • Unresolved stars have no orbit. If a cell is later taken deeper, its added stars take their positions and velocities from the keyed draw at that epoch, as any late-added star would; the nightly update has not been applying to them before that.

11. The gravity of the stars a cell leaves out (NAV.62, study of 2026-11-05)

Boss (2026-10-16): "I would like to figure out how we can account for the gravity of items left out." The cap of section 4 leaves 99.3% to 99.7% of the systems of a capped cell unmade (the share at or above 100 solar luminosities is 3.2e-3 for bulge-age stars and 1.5e-2 for young ones); galaxy/resolution.py (GEN.281) derives their count and mass from the address. This section says what that mass does to a course and to the orbital update. No build; the work is the items at the end. All numbers are [C] from reports/nav62-unresolved-gravity-calc-2026-11-05.py (project files), which uses the section 3 models (Hernquist cluster, 2.5e7 solar masses, a = 2.07 pc; exponential nuclear disk, R_d = 90 pc, h = 35 pc; a central hole of 4e6), 0.4 solar masses a system, and a crude spherical M(<r) for the disk, good to about a factor of 2 in the covering fractions.

11.1 The pull is already in the model, as a smooth field

  • The galactic potential (galaxy/potential.py, GEN.338/GEN.285) is built from the density model (density.density_components: bulge, halo, thin and thick disk, nuclear cluster, nuclear disk), not from the stars a cell made. Every star the cap leaves out is in it as part of the smooth mass, so the orbital update of a resolved star already feels the mean pull of the unresolved ones. Nothing is added or double counted for them [S].
  • The galaxy's enclosed mass for a Hill sphere (galaxy/enclosed_mass.py, GEN.370) integrates the same density model, so a resolved star's keep-out radius already uses the whole mass inside it, unresolved included [S].
  • The influence set (galaxy/influence.py) adds the pull of the made stars as point masses on top of the smooth field. Their mean is in the smooth field too, but a made star's neighbours pull from every side, so the net of that part cancels at leading order and the double count is the difference, not the sum [I]. It is the existing design of orbital-updates.md section 10.1 and needs no change.

11.2 What the smooth field leaves out: the fluctuation

The unresolved stars also pull as point masses, each a little differently from the average. Their effect on a resolved star is the relaxation of the cluster [C]:

r (pc) systems per pc^3 speed (km/s) relaxation time (Gyr) speed change per Myr (km/s)
0.1 2.0e7 418 42 2.05
1 7.1e5 169 78 0.61
4 2.4e4 127 990 0.13
10 1.7e3 97 6,400 0.04
100 177 120 1.1e5 0.01

Per Myr of update the change is at most half a percent of the star's speed even at 0.1 pc, and it is a random walk, not a drift. The orbital update does not need a term for the unresolved mass at any zoom; the smooth field is enough. A star taken deeper later (section 8) is drawn at that epoch from its keyed draw, so nothing that was skipped has to be replayed.

11.3 A course does not bend to a smooth field, but it does cross unresolved keep-out spheres

A course bends round keep-out spheres (NAV.26); gravity does not steer the ship (course-avoidance.md section 6, last paragraph), so a smooth density has nothing to bend round. What the cap changes is the count of spheres: a star's keep-out is its galactic Hill radius (keepout.py), which in the core is small because the enclosed mass is large [C]:

r (pc) Hill radius (pc) share of space inside Hill spheres mean free path of a straight line (pc)
0.1 0.0003 0.003 0.15
1 0.0027 0.06 0.06
4 0.0083 0.06 0.20
10 0.018 0.04 0.58
100 (nuclear disk) 0.074 0.3 0.33

A straight line through the nuclear region would cross about 1,100 unresolved spheres across 400 pc of the midplane (on or off the center, the disk dominates), about 480 going straight through the disk at 100 pc from the center, and about 60 in the width of one 4 pc cell near the center. The made stars are 0.3% to 1.5% of that, so a planner given only the made stars bends round 3 to 15 spheres in 1,100 and the line is still full of unresolved ones.

The finding: a course cannot steer clear of the unresolved stars, and does not have to be made to. Making them all is the cost the cap exists to avoid, and a route that bends round every one of 1,100 spheres is a random walk, not a short detour. The rule the nebula already follows (course-avoidance.md section 2: a cloud is passed through with a note) fits: the course goes round the made stars and the central hole's sphere of influence (1.56 pc, NAV.54), and says how many unresolved keep-out spheres it passes through. The hole's sphere covers the cluster's innermost stars by itself (the stars inside its radius weigh about twice the hole, which is how that radius is defined), and a course with an end inside a sphere is already exempt from it.

11.4 Where it applies and what it costs

  • Only inside the capped region. A cell is capped only where it expects more than the ceiling (5,000 systems): the nuclear cluster and the nuclear disk, about 150 to 230 pc from the center in the midplane. A course that does not touch a sphere of that radius around the center needs no query at all: the test is one distance from a point to a segment.
  • Per query, for a course that does touch it: the cells along the segment (a line across the nuclear disk crosses about 75 cells of 4 pc), and for each a profile and cell_resolution, which is a pure function of the address with up to about 30 bright-fraction evaluations for a capped cell. The expected crossings of a cell are unresolved_systems / volume * pi * R_H^2 * chord (the chord of the segment in that cell), summed. The cost is about the cells touched times one profile; it is not measured here [I], and the first item should time it and cache by address.
  • Consistent with deeper fills. The unresolved count of a cell falls as it is taken deeper (section 4.4), and the stars made are real keep-out spheres from then on, so the same course bends round more and counts fewer; no stored number goes stale (nothing is stored).
  • No fixed table. The count comes from the density model and the address through cell_resolution, never from a stored list (the item's rule).

11.5 Belts, fields and nebulae (Core 1's note)

  • Belts and asteroid fields keep a mass of zero. A course keeps out of a field by its stored radius (NAV.51) and never by its mass. The orbital update would need a mass only if it were a visible fraction of the pull of the stars round it: the Sun's main belt is 5e-4 Earth masses, and a field a million times heavier is 1.5 Jupiter masses, 0.1% of one solar mass, below any star's own pull at any distance where the field's members are not themselves inside a Hill sphere. A real model is not needed for the course or the update.
  • Nebulae have a mass that matters (a giant cloud is 1e3 to 1e6 solar masses). Its pull on the update is GEN.295's to refine; the course does not need it (course-avoidance.md section 2).
