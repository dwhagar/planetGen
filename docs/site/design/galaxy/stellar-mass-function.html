Status (2026-10-30): built on bug1/star-epoch for the long-term star epoch (GEN.932, parent GEN.922). Boss approved the full, no-patch fix at 5:27 PM EDT 10-29 ("let's build a robust fix so we match the curve segments for all mass levels and star-like-object-types across the mass range").

Every star the generator makes, and every remnant, comes from one family of curves. This note says what the curves are, what is set and what is derived, and what each consumer reads.

The curves

Curve Name in code What it counts Where it comes from
Single stars imf.SINGLE (xi) Every star born, by mass Set: [R0705] (PASP 115:763, Table 2, disk, individual stars)
Primaries imf.PRIMARY (P) The star a system is built around, by its birth mass Derived: xi less the companions of heavier primaries (generation/primary_mass_function.py)

Single stars. A log-normal in log mass below 1 solar mass (centre 0.079, width 0.69 dex) and the power law dN/dM ~ M^-2.3 above, joined continuously at 1 solar mass. The function is kept as a continuous piecewise power law (tuning.IMF_BREAKS_SOL, IMF_SLOPES): the log-normal is cut at every 0.05 dex and each pair of nodes is joined by the chord of ln dN/dM against ln M, within 0.07% of the log-normal. The sampler, the integrals and the number counts of physics/imf.py stay exact on it. The birth slope is 2.3; the steeper 2.7 of the field [R2499] counts stars still alive, and the disk history reproduces it from this slope and the ages. The old two-segment function (1.3 below 0.5 solar masses, 2.3 above) did not have the log-normal's turnover, which is what the published counts of low-mass stars show.

Primaries. A system's companions are stars too, and each is lighter than its primary. So at each mass the stars born are the primaries of that mass plus the companions of the heavier primaries that fall there:

xi(m) = P(m) + sum over primaries M >= m of P(M) n_c(M) (share of M's companions that land at m)

n_c(M) is the mean count less one of the star-count table for the class of the star (multistar.companion_class), and a companion's mass is its primary's times a ratio from the law of [R3108: Table 13] between max(0.1, 0.08 / M) and 1 (multistar.companion_ratio_range, mass_ratio.py, GEN.951, below). Companions are never heavier than the primary, so the equation is solved from the top mass down in one pass, with no iteration. The result peaks higher than xi, as the published systems' functions do (Chabrier's own for systems has its centre at 0.22). Systems per star born is 0.695 and systems per solar mass of stars born 1.124 (systems_per_star, systems_per_solar_mass; they were 0.697 and 1.128 with a flat mass ratio).

What the derivation leaves out, and the reason: the companions past the seventh star of a system (a chance of about 1e-5 of a system); the brown dwarf companion (below the hydrogen-burning limit, outside xi); the mergers and stripped stars of multistar, which move a star between bins after its birth.

The mass ratio of a companion (GEN.951). At one orbital period the density of the ratio q = M2/M1 is a power law, q ** gamma_smallq over 0.1 to 0.3 and q ** gamma_largeq over 0.3 to 1, continuous at 0.3, with a fraction F_twin of the companions with q > 0.3 spread evenly over 0.95 to 1 on top of it ([R3108]:15, Eqn. 2 and Table 1). Their Table 13 gives the three numbers at log P = 1, 3, 5 and 7 for solar-type, A / late-B, mid-B, early-B and O-type primaries (tuning.MASS_RATIO_*, rows in the order of COMPANIONS_PER_DECADE_BY_PRIMARY_MASS, straight in log mass between them): a solar-type primary stays near flat (slopes -0.5 to +0.3) with a twin fraction 0.3, 0.2 and 0.1 at log P 1, 3 and 5; a massive one is flat at log P = 1 (twins 0.08 to 0.17) and by log P = 3 to 5 leans to light companions (slopes -1.7 to -2.0), nearly random pairings from the mass function, with no twins. The draw puts the masses before the periods, so the law used is the mix of the four periods' laws in the shares of the primary's companions at them (the companions per decade of the same table); the link between one companion's ratio and its period is lost, the average over companions is the table's (MR3 in the stand-in registry). A companion under the hydrogen-burning limit is not a star of the star-count table, so the law is cut at the lowest ratio that keeps the star above it and renormalised. The primary function's equation takes the share of a primary's companions that land in a cell from the same law (mass_ratio.Ratios.fraction_below), so the stars of the systems together still follow the single-star function. The mean ratio of a solar-type primary's companions is 0.53 (0.55 flat), a massive one's (12 solar masses) 0.38; the share with q > 0.95 is 12% against 6% flat for a solar-type primary and 4% against 6% for an early-B one.

What moves, and why it is not a defect

The massive share (8 to 150 solar masses) of the stars born goes from 0.637% to 0.713%. The bound neutron stars are fixed by the census (0.004 per star), so the share of the massive stars left to fail and make black holes is larger: the middle draw of the solar failed share goes from 13.6% to 17.5% (black_hole_formation.share_cap). A galaxy with the Milky Way's 5e10 solar masses of stars holds 1.0e8 black holes, in line with the published estimate, and a metal-rich busy shell holds 1.6 neutron stars to a black hole (it was 2.1).

Source tiers

The four numbers of the log-normal are Confident Unverified, level L0 (recalled, not read in the paper, and used as published); the Cummings pieces and the Ozel and Freire Gaussian are Verified. The primary function has no set number. Registry section 6.50 of research/gap-filling-plan.md.
