Status: built. The sector grid is spherical: shells, bands and wedges. Boss decided the move on 2026-10-15 06:39Z; the plan is spherical-sector-coordinates.md and the study behind it is spherical-shell-sectors.md. The grid went in with schema v91 (migration 0091_spherical_sectors.py). Code: src/planetgen/galaxy/spherical.py (the grid), skeleton.py (the outline), density.py (the density model, see galaxy-disk-density.md) and planetgen plan / planetgen galaxy. The Galaxy Map's block ladder, stages and windows sit on the same grid (galaxy/drill.py, galaxy/window.py, spherical-block-ladder.md; MAP.205, MAP.206).

The cylindrical ring, layer and slot grid this replaced is kept in archive/galaxy-coordinate-system-cylinder.md, and the older spherical-shell design before it in archive/galaxy-coordinate-system-shells.md. Words matter here: shell, band and wedge are the only names for the three coordinates. "Ring", "layer", "slot" and "slab" name nothing in the current grid and are not reused.

1. The galaxy frame

  • Origin at the galactic center, (0, 0, 0). The quasar, when a galaxy has one, sits in the core, shell 0.
  • Right-handed axes: X and Y in the galactic plane, +Z toward galactic north. +X is the zero meridian (wedges are counted counterclockwise from it). This is a generation-space convention and does not match any real sky frame.
  • Galaxy-scale distances are stored in parsecs (_pc columns); positions inside a sector in milliparsecs (_mpc); bodies inside a system in km (shown in AU). planetgen/physics/units.py has mpc_to_pc, pc_to_mpc, pc_to_ly and ly_to_pc.

2. The spherical sector grid

Every galaxy-placed sector is one cell of a grid of nested spherical shells around the galactic origin. With edge e (4 pc, section 3), a cell is named by three integers, (shell, band, wedge), stored as sectors.shell_index, sectors.band_index and sectors.wedge_index:

  • Shell k (radius): r in [(k - 1/2) * e, (k + 1/2) * e), so its centerline radius is k * e. A shell is always e deep; that is the one length the grid never adjusts. Shell 0 is the core, the ball of radius e / 2 (2 pc), a single cell.
  • Band (latitude): a ring of cells around a shell between two colatitudes. Each shell's two polar caps are cut off first, and the rest is cut into an odd number of bands of equal colatitude step, each about e of arc, so the galactic plane runs through the middle of the equatorial band. Bands are signed: band 0 is the equatorial band, positive bands lie north (z > 0), negative bands south.
  • Wedge (longitude): a band is cut by meridians, pole to pole, into wedge_count equal wedges. Wedge w spans longitude [2*pi*w/W, 2*pi*(w+1)/W) from +X counterclockwise.
  • Cap: the circular segment of a shell within colatitude alpha of a pole, one cell at each pole. Six wedges meet its rim with a gap of exactly one edge, so the rim is 6 * e around and the cap's chord radius is 6 * e / (2 * pi) = 3.82 pc (4 pc would be 6.28 wedges, and 6 is the nearest whole number); alpha = asin(rho / R). A cap is band +/-(h + 1) with wedge 0, where h is the shell's highest ordinary band (the shell holds 2 * h + 1 ordinary bands). A cap holds about 2.9 edge cubes.

One wedge rule everywhere (Boss, 2026-10-15 07:17Z). Every band of every shell has W = 6 * m wedges, where m is the number whose prime factors are only 2, 3, 5 and 7 that is nearest (in ratio) to 2 * pi * R * sin(phi) / (6 * e), phi being the band's center colatitude, and m is at least 1 (spherical.wedge_count). The wedge's arc at the band's center therefore stays near one edge, and the six meridians that cut every cap's rim are wedge boundaries in every band. The rule never looks at the outline, the window or the neighbouring shell, so every lookup stays closed form. Measured on shell 3,000 (plan section 2): arcs 0.981 to 1.019 of 4 pc, and 99.8% of all cells within 0.5% of an edge cube's volume; near the poles the small counts let arcs stray further (about 0.76 to 1.06 at shell 10). A shell's band count is the odd integer nearest R * (pi - 2 * alpha) / e (spherical.shell_layout).

Shell 1 is cut by hand (HAND_CUT_SHELLS). The rule would leave it caps of 4.8 edge cubes against 0.67 for the cells between, so its caps end at 37.5 degrees and it holds one band of 12 wedges (14 cells, volumes about 0.9 to 1.4 edge cubes). Shells 2 and 3 follow the rule like every other shell.

No undefined space (Boss, 2026-10-15 06:59Z). The core ball, every shell, every cap, band and wedge together tile all of space: every point has exactly one address, with no gap and no overlap, whether or not that sector was ever generated. The outline (section 4) only says which cells may be generated; it never decides which cells exist. sector_address_at answers for any point, inside or outside the outline, as far as the 40-bit birth-sector field reaches (shell below 4,096, about 53,000 ly; the default galaxy's outline ends at shell 3,764).

Lookups are closed form. sector_address_at maps a point to its cell (shell from |p|, band from acos(z / |p|), wedge from atan2(y, x)); sector_position_pc gives a cell's center (a cap's is on the axis at +/-shell * e, the core's is the origin); cell_volume, sector_cell_vertices_pc (8 corners) and sector_cell_test (a predicate for one cell) are computed. Only the address and center are stored (uq_sectors_address makes the address unique). cell_count, cells_in_band_range and nth_cell_in_band_range count and index the cells of a shell without listing them.

Neighbors. neighbor_addresses lists the cells sharing a face of nonzero area: the two wedges either side in the same band; the cells above and below in the shell (one or several, because neighbouring bands hold different wedge counts and are offset like brick courses); and, in the shell inside and the shell outside, every cell whose band overlaps this one's colatitudes and whose wedge overlaps its longitudes. A cap touches every wedge of the nearest band, and the core touches the whole of shell 1. A cell has about 7 face neighbours, against 6 for a cube, up to roughly 10 to 15 near a cap or a change in wedge count. Flat height slabs and aligned vertical columns do not exist any more: a region is an arc per (shell, band), not per ring.

Local frame. A sector's local axes (sector_orientation) are +X radially outward from the galactic axis, +Y toward increasing longitude and +Z galactic north, unchanged in meaning from before. System and phenomenon offsets (star_systems.position_x/y/z_mpc and the phenomenon equivalents) use this frame, and generation samples them inside the real cell (SectorCell: cube root for the radius, uniform in the cosine of the colatitude, uniform in longitude; a cap samples a disk), not a cube. The octant label (star_systems.quadrant, and on every placed phenomenon) comes from this frame.

Designations. provisional_sector_designation packs the address into the 40-bit birth-sector field of the object ID: shell << 28 | (band + 2048) << 16 | wedge, printed as uppercase hex; parse_sector_designation reverses it. It allows shells 0 to 4,095, bands -2048 to 2047 and 65,536 wedges a band (the default galaxy's widest band holds about 23,650). The core is 8000000, and for example (12, 3, 5) is C8030005. A sector's ID is its designation (see object-ids.md), the first group of every object ID born in the sector. Object IDs were renumbered from the new sectors when the grid changed (section 6).

Seeds. A unit of work is seeded from the galaxy seed and its address (galaxy/seed.py): a sector's seed string is sector:<shell>/<band>/<wedge>, for example sector:12/3/0, hashed with the galaxy seed. A sector's numbers therefore depend only on the seed and its address, never on the order sectors run in.

Zones. The Galaxy pages group sectors in Zones of ZONE_SHELL_DEPTH = round(100 ly / 13.05 ly) = 8 consecutive shells (planetgen/web/maps/galaxymap.py, spherical.sector_zone).

3. Sector size: 4 pc

tuning.DEFAULT_SECTOR_EDGE_PC = 4 (13.05 ly) is the shell depth and the arc every band and wedge aims at, used by the generator, the skeleton, the Galaxy pages and the Galaxy Map. planetgen plan has no edge option. It replaced 11.5 ly (3.53 pc) in 7.0.0.

The edge sets where the galaxy ends, because a sector exists only where it expects at least one star (predicted_star_count >= 1), and a bigger cell holds more stars at the same density. At the default Milky Way shape a 4 pc sector holds about 6.3 stars at local density, the galaxy reaches about 50,300 ly (shell 3,764) and the grid holds about 23.9 billion candidate cells (plan section 2, measured on the real density model). 2 pc was rejected because a local-density sector would average under one star, so ordinary solar-neighborhood space would not qualify, and 11.5 ly was the older, arbitrary default.

4. The outline: one band range per shell

Shell k can hold content in bands -high .. high around the plane (the two caps too when high is the shell's top band). planetgen plan walks the shells outward from the core and stops at the first shell whose equatorial band can no longer reach the qualification threshold (a sector expected to hold at least one star; the exponential density model never reaches zero, so the threshold is the edge). Each shell's run is found by bisection on an upper bound of the density over longitude (skeleton.build_shell_extents, the bound is bound_relative_density_at: the bar bulge at its long axis plus the disks times the largest arm factor), with the cap checked first, because the bound falls moving north from the plane until the last step toward the pole, where it can rise again.

planetgen plan stores:

  • galaxy_shape: one row with the shape parameters, edge_pc, expected_system_count_at_density_1, outer_shell_index, the bright-star threshold and seed, and the phenomenon scatter seed.
  • galaxy_shell: (shell_index, high_band_index, cell_count), one row per shell that can hold content. A shell with no row has none.

The build takes a few milliseconds. The outline is a safe superset: a sector inside its shell's range may still fall short (an inter-arm trough), and that exact check is one density call when the sector is visited. A band is never left partly in; the outline decides whole bands and the exact check decides sectors.

Validation before generation. Every planetgen galaxy mode (an address, a span of shells, bands and wedges, a neighborhood, a random start, a course) and regenerate_sector_cell ask skeleton.GalaxyBounds.contains(shell, band) first, and that asks only whether the cell is inside the grid's reach (GEN.235): the one refusal, with the reason (describe_beyond_reach). A cell outside the outline but inside the reach is not refused: it is generated from its own density with a random draw around the expected count, however sparse (GEN.76), even with --density or --num-systems. The outline only shapes the choices nobody named: a random start draws a uniformly random sector from inside the outline, every sector equally likely (GalaxyBounds.random_address), and a neighborhood, the map's planned slots and a span that names no range stay inside it (GalaxyBounds.qualifies). planetgen galaxy refuses to run before planetgen plan.

The reach, the outline and the profile (GEN.233, GEN.235). Three things bound a galaxy and they are not the same. The reach is every cell the grid can name: shells 0 to skeleton.DEFAULT_REACH_SHELL (65,535, 262 kpc at the 4 pc edge, which holds the Magellanic Clouds, the Sagittarius remnant, the globular clusters and the satellites out to Leo I), checked against what the object ID can address (spherical.check_reach, MAX_ADDRESSABLE_SHELL 83,037, 332 kpc). The outline is the part of the reach where the disk, the bulge or a component is expected to put a system in a sector (GalaxyBounds.qualifies); it reads the disk, the bulge and the clusters and satellites (their bound is added in skeleton.bound_relative_density_at), and not the stellar halo, so a cell with only halo stars is inside the reach and outside the outline. The profile is what a cell holds, wherever it is: galaxy/profile.py's sector_profile(address, skeleton) gives the region that dominates (nuclear, bulge, disk, halo, cluster or satellite), the expected systems, the mix of stellar populations with each one's age window, [Fe/H] peaks, velocity dispersion and rotation, the gas (including the superbubble the place lies in), the dark matter, the factor per rare kind and the neutron stars and black holes ejected from the galaxy that stream through it. It is a pure function of the shape, the sector edge, the address and the galaxy's seed, reads no database and draws no random number, so a preview, a scatter and a fill agree on it. The outline decides which cells the planner and the random start name; the reach decides which cells may be generated; the profile decides what a generated cell holds. See intergalactic-and-halo-contents.md section 5.

Bright stars (schema v43). planetgen plan also places every star at or above BRIGHT_STAR_MIN_LUMINOSITY_SOL at a fixed point in its sector, in bright_stars, before any sector is filled (the default is 1,000 L_sun, with the dimmer stars filled in near generated sectors by the mass backfill of GEN.187, sector_stats.bright_mass_sol). Filling the sector later builds a full system around each of them. The Galaxy Map draws them, so the arms show before any sector is filled. How much of a planet's naked-eye sky these stars and the backfill cover is in sky-view.md section 3.

Adding a system to a stored sector. POST /api/systems with a sector_id places a new system in an existing sector (store.add_system_to_sector): at a given position inside it, or else clear of the other systems' Hill spheres, with its location, containment and nearest systems filled in like a generated one.

5. Things that move

  • Hill spheres follow the real radius. Star.calculate_system_perimeter takes galactic_center_dist_ly, threaded from the owning sector's galactic_radius_pc. A system generated outside the galaxy is taken at the radius that holds half of the disk's stars (galaxy.scales.default_galactocentric_pc, GEN.404).
  • Galactic orbits. planetgen.cli.orbits turns every system, phenomenon and stand-alone facility along its galactic orbit, moves anything that drifts into another generated sector over to it, then recomputes containment and the stored nearest systems (nearest_systems). Which sector holds a moved point is spherical.sector_address_at; the object keeps its ID wherever it drifts (the ID names the birth sector).
  • The object ID is 96 bits. galaxy/object_uid.py: a birth sector address of 56 bits (shell 19, band 18, wedge 19), a serial of 28 bits and a body number of 12 bits; 24 hex digits, BINARY(12), the same for every object in every galaxy (GEN.230). The band is stored biased so a negative band packs as a plain integer. The top two bits of the serial say how it was given out (generated, runtime, field or region), so no allocator needs another's state. The 80-bit ID it replaced could not name a polar cell beyond shell 1,303; the 96-bit one names every cell out to shell 83,037. The kind of object is not in the ID (an ejected planet that becomes a rogue planet keeps it).
  • The cell key follows the position. Every table that holds a point in the galaxy frame (star_systems, the phenomenon tables, facilities, nebula_field, bright_stars, phenomenon_scatter) has cell_key, a stored generated column the database works out from the position columns: the object's 0.25 pc cube over a box of plus or minus 262,144 pc (the grid's reach), the three 21-bit cube numbers woven into one 63-bit Morton number, NULL for a point outside the box. A covering index (cell_key, x, y, z) lets a sphere be asked as a short list of key runs (galaxy/cellkey.py: cell_key, sphere_runs), which is how "what is nearby" reads (NAV.60). It is not the object ID: the ID is the permanent birth address, the key is where the object is now and changes when it moves into another cube (schema v93, DB.34, DB.35; see spherical-region-queries.md and the schema).

6. Migrations that cleared or converted sectors

Earlier grid changes deleted what no longer had a cell. _migrate_v31_to_v32 (cylinder) deleted every galaxy-placed sector with its systems and phenomena; the v33 edge change did the same and rebuilt the outline at 4 pc; the v35 slot rule deleted the sectors in every ring whose slot count changed. Sectors never placed in the galaxy were kept each time.

The move to shells (v91) deleted nothing. Migration 0091 renames the address columns and converts the data: every star system, phenomenon and facility keeps its place in the galaxy, its velocity and its epoch; the sector rows are rebuilt (one for every cell that holds an object and one for every cell whose center lay inside a generated sector, each new sector taking the name of the old sector holding its center); object IDs are renumbered for the new birth sector; the bright stars and the phenomenon scatter get the address of the cell holding their position; sector_stats is counted again; and galaxy_shell is built again from the stored shape. The details are in ../database-schema.md under v91.

7. Why it works this way

  • Spherical shells again, with a round core (Boss, 2026-10-15). The cylinder gave cells that were close to cubes, but it put the galactic center on an axis rather than at a point, made the core a column, and tied every region and block to flat height slabs. Boss chose "two meridian lines from pole to pole make a wedge (vert), shell (radius), and band (horizontal), together they identify a single 3D segment of the sphere", with the core a ball 4 pc across, the shell depth always 4 pc, and bands and wedges kept as close to 4 pc arcs as possible, especially at the poles. The study recommended keeping the cylinder; Boss decided otherwise, and the plan records the costs it accepted: more face neighbours, no flat slabs or aligned columns, and heavier cells at the poles near the core (a cap there holds about 2,800 systems against 1,114 for the cylinder's densest cell).
  • Caps with six rim wedges. Six is the whole number nearest the 6.28 wedges a 4 pc gap would give. Five (3.18 pc) and seven (4.46 pc) were measured and left cap volumes of 2.0 and 3.9 edge cubes against 2.9.
  • Wedges in multiples of 6 with primes up to 7 (Boss, 07:17Z). Any wedge count per band gave cell volumes of 0.94 to 1.09 of a cube but boundaries that shift between bands; doubling 6, 12, 24 left only 23% of cells within 10% of a cube. Multiples of 6 whose cofactor uses only 2, 3, 5 and 7 keep the six pole meridians as boundaries in every band and neighbouring bands mostly nested (95% on shell 3,000), with the same rule everywhere.
  • Equal-volume bands were not adopted (Boss, 07:22Z).
  • The plane through the middle of a band. An odd number of bands puts the galactic plane through the equatorial band's center, so band 0 is symmetric and the outline is symmetric about the plane.
  • Object IDs renumbered (Boss, 07:32Z). Every object got a new ID from its new sector in the conversion; positions are unchanged.
  • Validate before generating. Earlier random starts drew from a fixed region that could land outside the galaxy; checking afterwards wasted generation. Storing the reach lets every path refuse an address the grid cannot name up front; the outline only says which cells are planned.
  • Parsecs for galaxy scale. Milliparsecs give 8-digit numbers across the galaxy, and light-years would need an AU round trip to compare with the _mpc sector columns. Parsecs stay in the same unit family as milliparsecs.
  • Store the address and center only. Position, density and outline are pure functions of the address, so storing them would only copy a formula's output. galactic_radius_pc is stored anyway so "sectors within R of the core" can use an index.
  • Lazy generation. At about 23.9 billion candidate cells, generating everything up front is not possible. Sectors are generated when visited or asked for.

Alternatives considered and rejected

  • The cylindrical ring, layer and slot grid (built 6.0.0 to 8.0, replaced by v91; see the archive copy).
  • The first spherical-shell design with Fibonacci slots and Voronoi prisms (built in 5.3.6, replaced in 6.0.0; see the older archive copy).
  • A per-sector plan table of every qualifying sector, about 10.5 billion rows and about 1 TB (see archive/galaxy-disk-density-rev2.md).
  • A k-d tree or octree over sector addresses: not needed, since every lookup is closed form.
  • 2 pc, 3 pc and 5 pc edges (section 3).

Open questions still standing

  • One galaxy per database. There is no galaxy_id. Several galaxies in one database would need one on every galaxy table. The plan for other galaxies (GEN.9) recommends keeping it that way: a second galaxy is a second database, and the neighbours are rows of one new table. See multiple-galaxies.md.
