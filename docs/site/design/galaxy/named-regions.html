A region is a 9-block of the Galaxy Map's block ladder (spherical-block-ladder.md) that holds at least one generated sector: about 780 sectors, 36 pc across at the default 4 pc sector edge. A region exists, and shows on the galactic map, as soon as any sector in its cell is generated; ungenerated space has no region, and no sector line may join two unrelated places. This page is the naming, geometry and storage rules as built; the map, the pages and the search that use them are listed at the end as not built.

Names

galaxy/named_regions.py (pure: it reads no database) names a 9-block "Word Word Region", for example "Kanbee Get Region". The name is the gated phoneme codec (names/gated_phoneme_codec.py, kind region in names/naming_key.KINDS, under the galaxy's naming key) spelling seven hex digits as two words: digits 0 to 3 are the first word, digits 4 to 6 the second. No name comes from a list, so none comes from the Milky Way or the Solar System.

  • Digits 0 to 3 are the family hash: the hash of the cell's 81-block (family_of) under the key. All the regions of one 81-block share the first word, and the 81-block alone is "Word Reach" (family_name, "Kanbee Reach"). An 81-block holds about 729 9-blocks (681 to 1,430 where the grid squeezes), not 27: the ladder steps 81, 27, 9.
  • Digits 4 to 6 are the cell's ordinal among the 9-blocks of its family (ordinal_in_family, three hex digits, up to 4,095), so two regions of one family never share a name.
  • Neighbour rule. A family hash that equals the unsalted hash of a neighbouring 81-block with a smaller key is salted (":1", ":2", ...) until it differs, so the 81-blocks around one never share a first word and of two neighbours the one with the larger key changes. Neighbours are the 81-blocks holding the points one block's width from the anchor along the 26 directions of the cube (neighbour_families). A tie with a neighbour that is itself salted is possible in principle (about 1 in 65,000 per pair); the audit check below finds it.
  • The name depends on the cell, the key and the sector edge only, never on what has been generated, so a name never moves when sectors are added or deleted. A new naming key renames every region (the search table of API.36 is refilled then).

Two blocks 324 pc apart sharing a first word is harmless; neighbours doing so is what the salt prevents.

Anchor, framing sphere, extent

  • Anchor (anchor_pc): the centre of the cell's analytic bounds (drill_block_bounds).
  • Framing sphere (framing_radius_pc): radius min(12 pc, inscribed radius). 12 pc is REGION_RADIUS_PC, the sphere a standard fill of one anchor fills (tuning.DEFAULT_GENERATE_RADIUS_PC, about 109 sectors). The inscribed radius is the distance from the anchor to the nearest face. Measured at the default edge over the first 1,500 block shells: 14.6 billion 9-blocks, 2,190 with an inscribed radius under 12 pc (next to the polar axis) and 336 under 8 pc, none under 7.8 pc (scripts/measure_region_cells.py). So every cell has a framing sphere of at least 7.8 pc, no cell is dropped, and fewer than 1 cell in 6 million is framed by less than 12 pc.
  • Extent (region_extent, GEN.827): the bounding sphere of the largest face-connected clump of the cell's generated sectors (neighbours by spherical.neighbor_addresses; ties by the smallest address): the clump's centres plus a sector's circumradius (sqrt(3)/2 edges), the radius within 5 percent of the smallest sphere that holds them, never under the framing radius (a lone sector gets the 12 pc sphere about itself). The other clumps count toward the share charted but are not inside the sphere. Why the largest clump and not one sphere over all: in a model of a cell of 9 by 9 by 9 sectors, two ad hoc fills of 12 pc radius leave 2 or more clumps in 41 percent of cells, and one sphere over them would cover a gap of empty space (over 1.5 times the largest clump's sphere) in 41 percent; three fills, 45 percent and 21 percent (scripts/measure_region_clumps.py). A standard anchor fill makes one clump per cell.
  • Share charted is sector_count / capacity: the generated sectors over the sectors the cell's outline can hold. A standard fill is about 14 percent. Regions rank by it.

Storage (DB.107)

Table region_stats (schema 161, migration 0161; database-schema.md): one row per 9-block holding a generated sector, key (shell_index, band_index, wedge_index) (level 9 implied), with sector_count, capacity, clump_count, and the extent sphere extent_x_pc, extent_y_pc, extent_z_pc, extent_radius_pc (galaxy frame, parsecs). db/region_stats.py keeps it, called by db/block_stats.py whenever it puts right the blocks above a sector, so a sector save, a deletion and planetgen fill-block-totals leave the same rows whatever the order. A cell with no generated sector has no row. Readers: region_of(conn, position_pc), region_row, charted_percent, regions, labels (names and families of the stored regions under the active key).

On the wire (API.35)

A charted region's node carries name and parent (its family, "Word Reach"); a 9-block stage names it and lists its children the same way (child_m 9); only charted regions, only with a naming key. TILE_FORMAT is 5. See ../interface/galaxy-map-wire-format.md section 0.1.

The audit check: region names stay unique among neighbours (DOC.82)

tests/audit/region_names.py (findings(cells, key)), run by tests/test_audit_region_names.py and by the release audits (reports/audit-playbook-2026-10-22.md, part 1.3): for each region and each of its 26 neighbouring 9-blocks, no two share a full name, no two in different 81-blocks share a first word, and no two in one 81-block share an ordinal. A finding is a line naming both cells. An audit run builds the cells from region_stats.regions (or cells_around(point)) and the key in force and expects an empty list; any finding is High (a map label that reads the same for two places beside each other).
