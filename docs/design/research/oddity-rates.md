# Rates of the weird-worlds oddities against papers (GEN.1037)

Sources 1 lane, 10-30. This checks the rates and thresholds marked [R] (recalled) in [weird-worlds.md](../systems/weird-worlds.md) for W3, W4, W5, W10, W14, W15 and W20, the seven the design named for confirmation first. Every number below was read in a paper whose arXiv text I opened (secondary sources accepted by the rule of 8:16 PM EDT 10-29), or counted from the NASA Exoplanet Archive; "read" says which. Stand-in rows WR1 to WR7 in section 6.82 of [gap-filling-plan.md](gap-filling-plan.md) carry the numbers into GEN.725. Nothing in the generator changes.

## Result in one paragraph

Four of the seven design rates stand, three need changing. **Stand:** W3 (about 10 percent of giant planets above e 0.5, now with the paper's own distribution), W15 (super-puffs about 1 percent of small Neptunes; counted 0.8 to 1.2 percent), W20 (the bistable window is 10 to 15 percent of the flux, but it is zero for several common cases), and W4's hot-star gate (6,250 K). **Change:** W4 (the misaligned share is not "a third" but 8, 63 or 80 percent by host temperature), W5 (near-resonance is common, 15 percent of neighbouring pairs, not "1 to 2 percent of multis"; the heating-capable subset is smaller and is a different number), W14 (the design's "1 in 1,000 ultra-short-period planets" is 1 in 50 of the detected ones, and the paper turns that into a progenitor rate of 0.02 to 0.3 percent of stars). **Contested:** W10 (super-Mercuries fell from 5 of 22 to 1 of 21 when the sample was made homogeneous; the rate is a stand-in with a wide bracket).

## What each row means for the build

1. **W3** is an outcome of the eccentricity draw, not a separate oddity draw. Draw giant eccentricity from Beta(0.867, 3.03) for the RV-like population and name the tail. Rocky planets and members of compact multis have lower e (Winn and Fabrycky); no number for them was read here.
2. **W4** is three rates by host temperature on a gated population (close-in giant, a/R under 10). The polar peak (18 of 19 misaligned systems between 80 and 125 degrees) says the misaligned obliquity should be drawn near 100 degrees, not isotropically. Storing the angle is flavour; the Hill stability of moons is the only physics it touches.
3. **W5** needs two things the paper separates: the **near-resonant** share (15 percent of pairs, by age) which is only a period-ratio statistic, and the **heating-capable** share, pairs close enough to resonance (within about 0.6 percent) to librate and hold a forced eccentricity. The paper gives no number for the second; this is an open gap (Goldberg and Batygin 2023 is the paper to read), not a stand-in value, and the build should take the share from the distribution of the period-ratio offset until then. A young system (under 100 Myr) has a 70 percent chance per pair, which is where the Io-like heating oddity belongs.
4. **W10** has no settled rate. Keep it behind a low default and say so in the page text ("some planets are iron-rich"); do not name a real planet as an example.
5. **W14**: replace the rate by a progenitor population, let GEN.1041's lifetime decide the active ones. The age of the star enters twice (the planet must still be alive and the star must be old enough to be one of the observed few); with the module's 21 Myr lifetime and a 1 Gyr star the active share is 2 percent of progenitors.
6. **W15** is a small share of the planets that are 4 to 10 Earth radii and young or hazy; the density cut (under 0.1 g/cc) is rarer than the mass cut (under 10 Earth masses) by a factor 5.
7. **W20**: the design's "history draw" can be a rule. Star luminosity rises with age, so the flux a planet gets was lower before; a planet that began warm drops into the snowball only if its flux was ever below the lower edge of its window, and leaves it only above the upper edge or by CO2 build-up. That makes the branch deterministic from the luminosity history and the CO2 outgassing, with a coin flip only for planets whose history is unknown. This is my inference from the window and the monotonic luminosity, not a number from a paper, so it has no registry row.
