# Surface and crust composition (GEN.441)

Code: `physics/surface_composition.py`. Tests: `tests/test_surface_composition.py`. Research base: part E of the body composition research (`body-composition-parts/E-ground-radiogenic-shielding.md`, sections 2.2, 2.3, 2.6, 3.2 and 3.5) and the gated possibility-table rule (`composition-model.md` section 4b).

## What it reads and gives

Reads (a `SurfaceInputs`): whether the body is a giant (from its structure, `body_type`, never the class letter: GEN.917, GEN.915), surface temperature and pressure, the air's oxygen and other partial pressures, the bulk fractions of GEN.428 (water, other ices, rock, metal, rock FeO, carbon rock), the hydrosphere state, ocean fraction and class of GEN.461, the internal and tidal heat flow, the age and the plate-tectonics chance.

Gives (a `SurfaceComposition`): the areal `mix` (ocean, ice and the ground types, summing to 1), the mass `components` (rows of the shared materials table, summing to 1) and the grain state. A giant has no ground (`None`).

## The order of the draw

1. Covered share from the hydrosphere: liquid ocean (with `RANGES["ocean_salt"]` of salts) or ice. Only ices that stand at the surface temperature and air are kept (`ice_stable`: water by the repo's 273.15 K under air and 220 K in vacuum rule, other ices by their saturation curve against their partial pressure or the vacuum floor, which is water's vapour pressure at 220 K). A frozen body whose water and ices reach half the rock's mass is ice everywhere.
2. Dominant ground of the land from `surface-dominant-ground`: metal (metal share 0.7 or more), carbon (carbon rock), basaltic (heat flow over the drawn floor), else ultramafic or anorthositic by a draw whose chance rises with the rock's FeO.
3. Modifiers, in the fixed order felsic, clay, oxidised, evaporite, sulfur, each a uniform areal share of the land when its inputs allow it, scaled together to at most 0.9 of the land.
4. Grain: slab if molten (1400 K) or slab or crushed if under 0.1 Gyr, else powder.

The recipes (`surface-ground-types`) are Hu et al. 2012 Table 2 on materials rows. Stand-ins are named on the rows: kaolinite for Mg-smectite, `basalt_powder` for glass, `orthoclase` for K-feldspar. Hu's pyrite is the `pyrite` row (GEN.1384): the `metal` ground is that sample alone, as Table 2 gives it, where it stood as troilite and kamacite before the row existed.

## Parametrisations (all in `RANGES`, none a measurement)

The heat-flow floor 1e-4 to 1e-3 W/m2, the overturn scale 0.2 of FeO, the modifier share ranges, the 0.1 Gyr young-surface limit, the ice-rich ratio 0.5, the stripped-mantle share 0.7, the free-oxygen floor 20 Pa and the ocean salt share 0.035 (Earth's). Each stands for a fit against data and is on the part E gap list.

## Reads back

`band_albedo` gives the lab reflectance by band area-weighted over the ground types that have a measured spectrum (not a Bond albedo; the final albedo is GEN.462). `land_rock_ppm` gives K, Th and U for the land of a body from the reservoir models of part E table 2.8b (`RESERVOIRS`), for the ground dose.

## Stored (second slice, GEN.441)

`generate_surface(body)` runs right after the hydrosphere and sets `ground_type`, `ground_grain` and `ground_<type>_fraction` (twelve areal shares that add up to 1; schema 159). A giant's fields are `None`. The non-water ices' proportions are a stand-in (the median comet volatiles, `comet_population.median_volatiles`) until the species are stored with the bulk composition; the tidal heat flow is not yet an input, so the sulfur modifier waits on it.

The ground dose reads the stored ground: `land_rock_ppm(body)` is the area-weighted K, Th and U of the land (`RESERVOIR_OF`: clay and iron-oxidised ground follow basalt, being altered basalt; land with no reservoir takes basalt's), and `radiation._ground_dose` raises the bulk silicate's content to it. `radiogenic.SURFACE_RESERVOIRS` and `tuning.RADIOGENIC_FELSIC_FRACTION_MAX` are gone.

## Rock types from the oxides (GEN.474, lock-free step)

`GROUND_TYPES` is a library: each ground type is a fixed mineral list taken from the Earth's and the Moon's crusts (Hu, [R2024: Table 2]). `physics/rock_types.py` (registry RT1 to RT3, section 6.45) derives the ground type from the oxides of the body's own rock instead:

- **The norm.** The CIPW norm allocates the oxides to ideal minerals by mass balance in the method's fixed order (apatite, ilmenite, orthoclase, albite, anorthite, magnetite, diopside, hypersthene; quartz from the silica left, olivine, nepheline, leucite and periclase when it falls short). The norm adds up to the oxides given (tested); a peralkaline rock or one without the silica for any norm is refused.
- **What melts.** A mantle becomes a crust by partial melting. The minerals of the norm melt in three stages: the low-melting feldspars, quartz and diopside together in the proportions the rock holds them (an invariant eutectic, so the liquid keeps one composition while the stage lasts: a basalt for the bulk silicate Earth up to a fifth melted), then hypersthene, then olivine (a magnesian liquid, komatiite-like, over a third melted). K, Th and U are incompatible, so a batch melt holds them `1 / (D + F (1 - D))` times the source (D taken as zero); N-MORB over depleted mantle (22.8, 31.9 and 25.9 times for K, Th, U) is one melt of 3 to 4.5 percent for all three, which the test pins as a consistency check and never uses as an input.
- **The ground type** is anorthositic at 90 percent plagioclase in the norm (IUGS), ultramafic under 45 percent silica or over 18 percent magnesia (komatiite), then basaltic (45 to 52), intermediate (52 to 63) or felsic (63 and over) by the silica bands of the total alkali-silica diagram. The upper continental crust ([R3842: Table 11]) comes out felsic, the lower crust intermediate, a lunar highland anorthosite anorthositic and the bulk silicate Earth ultramafic, all as test points.

Limits: the staged melt is not a phase-equilibrium liquid (MELTS or pMELTS would give one: the stage mix is too aluminous and calcic for a MORB, 23 percent Al2O3 against about 15, and its high-degree liquids rise in silica as the orthopyroxene joins, so 25 to 30 percent melts are called intermediate); the norm's iron split is proportional; there are no hydrous, carbonate or sulfur rocks. 

**Wired (GEN.474, train 7).** The volcanic crust (the `basaltic` row of the dominant-ground table, where the heat flow is over the volcanism floor) is no longer always basalt: it is the ground type of the melt of the body's stored mantle. `rock_types.rock_oxides` builds the rock's oxides from the stored FeO and Al2O3 shares and Mg/Si and the star's Ca/Mg (the alkalis, titanium and phosphorus are not stored and are zero); `melt_fraction` takes the share that melts from the heat flow (3 to 30 percent, log-linear over five decades above the middle of the floor's range, 9 percent at Earth's heat flow: registry MF1, T9, L3); and `ground_type_of` of the staged melt names the ground. An Earth-like mantle makes basalt, a mantle richer in silica (Mg/Si near 0.7) andesite and a poorer or hotter one a magnesian ultramafic melt; about nine in ten of 800 generated volcanic bodies stay basaltic, a tenth turn ultramafic and a few turn intermediate. A new `intermediate` ground type (an average andesite's assemblage, registry RT4) and its areal-share column `ground_intermediate_fraction` (planets and moons, migration 0187) are added; its K, Th and U are basalt's until a reservoir of its own is read. The melt does not decide the primary crust (the overturn and anorthosite rules of a body that no longer melts stay), the felsic share of a mobile lid (still a uniform draw), or the crust's K, Th and U from the mantle's (the batch enrichment of `rock_types` is not used yet). A melt the staged allocation gives as pure plagioclase reads basaltic: the stored rock has no alkalis, so a calcium-poor mantle's first melt is feldspar alone, and an anorthosite is a flotation cumulate, not a mantle melt. The run's draw order is unchanged (no new draw). The library of Hu's assemblages stays as the types' recipes.

**The assemblage of a ground (GEN.474, part 2, unwired).** `physics/rock_assemblage.py` gives the mineral assemblage of a rock as the shares of its CIPW norm, each normative mineral on the row of the materials table of the same name (albite, anorthite, orthoclase, quartz, diopside, olivine and the rest; the orthopyroxene is split into `enstatite` and `ferrosilite` by the iron of the divalent pool; the metasilicates and eskolaite have no row and are returned as an unmapped share). It is the replacement for the fixed recipes of `GROUND_TYPES` that are still flagged stand-in (`ultramafic`, `anorthositic`, `basaltic`, `felsic`): the assemblage of a body's volcanic crust becomes the norm of the melt of its own mantle, not the Earth's list. On the test points the norm of the bulk silicate Earth is 65 percent olivine, 16 percent orthopyroxene, 6 percent diopside and 14 percent feldspar (Hu's `ultramafic` recipe is olivine 0.60 and enstatite 0.40), the upper continental crust is 22 percent quartz and 62 percent feldspar (Hu's `felsic`: 0.35 quartz, 0.60 feldspar, 0.05 biotite), a lunar highland anorthosite is 98 percent plagioclase (Hu's: 0.97), and the staged melt of a tenth of a mantle is 70 percent plagioclase and 29 percent diopside with no orthopyroxene (Hu's `basaltic`: 0.76 plagioclase, 0.08 diopside, 0.06 enstatite, 0.05 glass). The limits are the norm's (no mica or amphibole, so no biotite) and the staged melt's (RT1). Wiring it changes the stored ground shares and the albedo, so it is a lock item; `oxidized` stays a recipe because it is a weathering product set by the air, not by the melt.

## Mantle assemblages against polluted white dwarfs (GEN.1841)

**The test.** Polluted white dwarfs hold the disrupted bulk of rocky bodies round stars of 1 to 3 solar masses, so they are the one extrasolar check of the mantle assemblage of the section above. `scripts/white_dwarf_assemblage.py` draws 2,000 bodies the way the generator does (a host of 1 to 3 solar masses, 0.4 to 8 Gyr old, [Fe/H] normal about -0.05 of width 0.2, through the star model of `bulk_draw`; the dry partition; the oxides of the rock; the CIPW norm of `rock_assemblage`) and counts the bodies whose norm holds quartz or periclase at 5, 10 or 20 percent (the exotic mantles). Seed 4242.

**What the sources give (second hand).** Putirka and Xu 2021 put 23 polluted white dwarfs on mineral ternaries from Mg, Si, Ca and Fe: 11 fall in or next to the ternary of olivine, orthopyroxene and clinopyroxene (the peridotites and pyroxenites of the Earth), 12 are exotic (quartz pyroxenites, periclase dunites and kin), a 52 percent exotic share with a 95 percent interval of 33 to 71 percent; only one matches the bulk silicate Earth; no crust types are found; the sample is selected on Ca and the paper says Mg may be underestimated for cool white dwarfs (`reports/secondhand-citations-2026-10-24.md`). [R4425] finds 15 of 31 white dwarfs chondritic on the raw abundances (16 not) and 21 of 31 with a steady-state correction (10 not), none better fit by basalt or continental crust, against 75 percent of the Hypatia stars. The two verified sources are in doubt about "exotic" against "chondritic", so by the source rules the newer ([R4425], 2023) governs and neither is a tuning target. [R1832] has 8 of 202 helium-atmosphere white dwarfs core-rich at 3 sigma (4.0 percent) and 1 crust-rich (0.5 percent). **The per-star tables of the first two are not reachable**: the Putirka and Xu supplement (Table A1) is "on request" and its file is refused to automated readers, and Table 1 of [R4425] holds only the white dwarfs' temperatures, gravities and masses. So the test is on the stated counts, not on star-by-star ratios.

**Result (COMPUTED).**

| Reading of the oxides | Quartz or periclase at 5 / 10 / 20 percent of the norm | Minerals over 2 percent of the norm (share of bodies) |
|---|---|---|
| four oxides (MgO, SiO2, CaO, FeO of the rock) | 0.1 / 0.1 / 0 percent | diopside 100, enstatite 99, olivine 98, ferrosilite 97 |
| the whole stored set | 0.2 / 0.1 / 0 percent | anorthite and diopside and olivine 100, albite 97, enstatite 97 |
| all of the star's iron as FeO | 63.6 / 42.6 / 8.0 percent | diopside, olivine 100, periclase 75, enstatite 13 |

The star's Mg/Si is 0.89, 1.10 and 1.36 at the 5, 50 and 95 percent points; Ca/Mg 0.052 to 0.080; Fe/Mg 0.67 to 1.08.

1. **Every simulated mantle is inside the white dwarf range, in its Earth-like part.** The generator's mantles are olivine, orthopyroxene and clinopyroxene (with a plagioclase where the aluminium is stored): the region where 11 of the 23 polluted white dwarfs sit. None lies outside the range the white dwarfs reach.
2. **The generator does not reach the exotic tail**: 1 of 2,000 bodies has 10 percent quartz or periclase, against 12 of 23 white dwarfs on Putirka and Xu's reading. That is not a defect to tune out. A body of a star's own Mg, Si and Ca and a mantle FeO of 8 percent has Mg/Si between 0.89 and 1.36, which is a peridotite, and the quartz and periclase classes need the Mg/Si and iron a star does not have. The third row shows how much the iron convention alone moves it: a white dwarf's four-oxide mantle cannot tell core iron from mantle iron, and counting all of a star's iron as FeO makes 43 percent of the same bodies periclase-bearing. Whether the exotic white dwarfs come from such an accounting, from Mg lost to the settling of cool white dwarfs, or from real differentiated or processed parent bodies is not decided by the counts. [R4425] says chondritic for about half to two thirds of the white dwarfs and so does not ask the generator for the tail.
3. **What the white dwarfs do show beyond the stars is spread**: 10 to 16 of 31 are not chondritic where a quarter of the stars are not. The generator draws a body's iron and mantle from its star with a scatter of 0.05 dex (GEN.820), which is a planet's spread; the extra diversity of the debris is of parent bodies that were stripped of a core, a crust or their volatiles, and small-body parent bodies of that kind are for the belt and small-body rows (Lane 06), not for the planets of this test.
4. **The two other statistics do not bear on this test.** The core-rich share (4.0 percent of 202) is of the debris of differentiated planetesimals, not of planets (the planets' iron-rich share is 1 percent by weight, GEN.512 and GEN.820, tuned to the planets' own sample); the crust-bearing share (0.5 percent) needs a crust fragment of a body, which the generator does not accrete into a mantle.

**Next, if wanted.** A star-by-star test needs the abundances of the white dwarfs: the source papers' tables of the individual stars (the work behind Table 1 of [R4425], which gives their names) or the Putirka and Xu table from its lead author. The test would then put each star's Mg/Si, Ca/Mg and Fe/Mg through the same norm and ask whether the generator's draw of those ratios covers them. No value in the generator changes with this note.

## The salts a brine leaves (GEN.1366)

Code: `physics/evaporite_sequence.py`. Tests: `tests/test_evaporite_sequence.py`. Stand-ins: registry section 6.226 (rows EV1 to EV11).

The evaporite ground used to be four tenths halite, three tenths gypsum and three tenths calcite, and the ocean's salt was halite alone. Both are now the salts of the body's own brine, allocated to rows of the materials table in the order of the chemical divides (Hardie and Eugster):

1. The ions. The crust that weathers is the melt of the body's mantle at its heat flow, with the alkalis put back (the stored rock carries none: sodium at `waterworld_carbon.CRUST_SODIUM_WT`, potassium at the basalt reservoir's K). Each cation dissolves in proportion to its oxide moles times its mobility in the weathering series (`MOBILITY`). The anions are the ocean class's (`ANION_PARAMS`: carbonate per calcium, then chloride against sulfate for the rest of the charge), and the anion charge is the cation charge.
2. The steps. Acid water: jarosite then alunite. All waters: calcite from calcium and carbonate in equal moles. Carbonate left over is the alkaline (soda) path: magnesium carbonate (hydromagnesite, or magnesite when hot), then sodium carbonate (nahcolite at high carbon dioxide, else trona, thermonatrite or natrite by temperature). Calcium left over is the calcium path: calcium sulfate, then magnesium sulfate, then sodium sulfate, then (calcium still left) calcium chloride. Last, the chlorides: perchlorate (thin oxidizing air only), halite, carnallite, sylvite, bischofite, antarcticite.
3. The forms. Gypsum or anhydrite is read from the measured equilibrium by temperature and the activity of water [R5164]: a brine saturated with halite (activity 0.75) makes anhydrite above 18 degrees C. Mirabilite or thenardite at 32.4 degrees C, epsomite, hexahydrite or kieserite at 48 and 68 degrees C, and the sodium carbonate hydrates at 32.5 and 109 degrees C are stand-ins (EV4 to EV6).

`surface_composition.dried_salts` gives the mass shares for the evaporite ground and `ocean_salts` the ocean's (the same, less the calcite, which is the seafloor's). A body with no silicate rock has volcanic sodium chloride only, halite. The areal mix, the draws and the albedo rows (`ALBEDO_ROWS`, halite, gypsum and calcite) are unchanged; only the stored mass composition moves.

Limits: equilibrium on complete desiccation, not a basin's kinetics; no double salts (glauberite, polyhalite, langbeinite, kainite) and no bassanite; one activity of water for the calcium sulfate (the halite one where halite forms), not a curve along the evaporation; the class anion split and the mobilities are stand-ins, not a speciation of the brine.
