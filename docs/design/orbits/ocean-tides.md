# Ocean tides at a world's surface (GEN.1034)

Boss asked (4:00 PM EDT 2026-10-30) for the tide an ocean world's moons and star raise. `physics/ocean_tides.py` is the lock-free first part; the stored columns (`tide_range_spring_m`, `tide_range_neap_m`, `ocean_liquid`, registered in `db/column_units.py`) and the fill pass ride a generation train after the tides chain (train 7). The research draft is `reports/design-drafts/ocean-tides-and-liquids-2026-10-30.md`.

**What it gives.** For each raiser (the star and each moon) the equilibrium high-to-low range `(3/2) (m / M) (R / d)^3 R`: 0.535 m for the Moon and 0.246 m for the Sun on the Earth (published 0.54 and 0.25). The sea measures `1 + k2 - h2` of it against the floor that rises with the tide (0.69 for the Earth), times the thin-layer loading factor `1 / (1 - 3 rho_liquid / (5 rho_mean))` of the sea's weight and attraction (1.12 for water on the Earth). Spring range: every raiser that oscillates in line (their sum). Neap range: the largest less the rest, never below zero (the Moon and the Sun: 0.54 less 0.25). The eccentric orbit gives each raiser a range of `1 / (1 - e)^3` of the mean at its closest approach and `1 / (1 + e)^3` at its farthest.

**What it does not.** The range on a coast depends on the basin's shape and depth and on its resonance with the tide's period (the Bay of Fundy exceeds the equilibrium value many times over); the equilibrium value is the upper bound of an ocean that does not resonate, and no basin is modelled. The dissipation of the tide (the Earth's 3.5 TW, nearly all in the oceans) is not here; it belongs to the tide chain's orbit and spin (GEN.957, GEN.959). An ice-covered ocean has no free surface and gets zero with the reason. A raiser the world is locked to (the world turns at the raiser's orbital rate, either way round, within one part in a thousand) raises no oscillating tide; the radial tide an eccentric orbit still raises on a locked world is not counted.

**The liquid.** The equilibrium height does not depend on the liquid's density; the density sets only the loading factor. A liquid with `3 rho_liquid / (5 rho_mean)` above 0.9 is refused (a global layer of mercury on a rocky planet: the thin-layer formula goes singular and the fluid Love numbers are needed). Water is the only liquid until GEN.1035 (the surface liquid inventory) says what a world holds; mercury and other liquid metals are carried by the weird-conditions research.

**Stand-ins.** OT1, OT2, OT6, OT7 and OT8 of `docs/design/research/gap-filling-plan.md` section 6.68. OT3 to OT5 of the draft (the dominant liquid, the basin depth, the exotic liquids) are GEN.1035's. The loading factor and the Love-number relation are from memory (Farrell 1972; Pugh and Woodworth 2014 chapter 3; Love 1909), not opened, so their rows stay below Verified.
