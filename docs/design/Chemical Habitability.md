Chemical Habitability, Geochemical Toxicity, and Environmental Margins of Terrestrial Exoplanets
================================================================================================

Geochemical Determinants of Terrestrial and Aquatic Toxicity
------------------------------------------------------------

The habitability of a terrestrial exoplanet is conventionally evaluated through macroscopic astrophysical parameters: stellar irradiance, planetary mass, orbital eccentricity, and the presence of liquid water within the circumstellar habitable zone (Cockell et al., 2024; Schwieterman et al., 2019). However, the geochemical inventory and surface thermodynamic state determine whether a planetary surface is biologically viable, biochemically inert, or actively cytotoxic (Cockell et al., 2024). Toxicity in planetary environments is defined by the disruption of macromolecular integrity, the inhibition of energetic metabolism, or the destruction of cellular membranes in carbon-based life (Cray et al., 2020; Hallsworth et al., 2007). These toxic states emerge through specific chemical species, extreme ionic conditions, and thermodynamic constraints on water availability (Belilla et al., 2019; Cray et al., 2020).

Heavy metals and metalloids—most prominently arsenic, cadmium, lead, mercury, and hexavalent chromium—act as potent cellular poisons across phylogenetic domains (Cockell et al., 2024). Their toxicity stems from their behavior as soft or intermediate Lewis acids, showing high affinities for sulfhydryl ($-SH$) groups in cysteine residues (Cockell et al., 2024). This affinity drives the non-functional displacement of essential transition metal cofactors, such as ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$), zinc ($\text{Zn}^{2+}$), and manganese ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$), in catalytic metalloenzymes, which permanently arrests core metabolic pathways (Daswani, 2020).

Oxychlorine species, including perchlorates ($\text{ClO}_4^-$), chlorates ($\text{ClO}_3^-$), and hypochlorites ($\text{ClO}^-$), introduce powerful oxidative and metabolic hazards (Catling et al., 2010; NASA, 2024). While kinetically metastable in dilute aqueous solutions at ambient temperatures, perchlorates become reactive oxidants when exposed to ionizing radiation or thermal activation (Catling et al., 2010; Puiu, 2017). Physiologically, oxychlorines compete with iodide ($\text{I}^-$) at the sodium-iodide symporter in vertebrate systems, disrupting thyroid hormonogenesis, basal metabolic regulation, and systemic organ performance (Catling et al., 2010; Davila et al., 2013). In bacterial and archaeal systems lacking specialized dissimilatory perchlorate reduction pathways, oxychlorine compounds promote severe intracellular oxidative stress, mutating nucleic acids and degrading cell membranes (Curr Trends Biol Sci, 2025; Wadsworth & Cockell, 2017).

Reactive oxygen species, such as hydrogen peroxide ($\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$), the superoxide radical ($\text{O}_2^{\bullet-}$), and hydroxyl radicals ($\text{OH}^\bullet$), represent unavoidable intermediates of photolytic and radiolytic processes in volatilized water and oxidized regoliths (Catling et al., 2010). Hydroxyl radicals possess oxidation potentials capable of non-selectively abstracting hydrogen from organic backbones, inducing fatal cross-linking in proteins and double-strand breaks in nucleic polymers (Catling et al., 2010). Volatile chalcogens, including hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) and sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$), act as double-edged environmental drivers; while capable of supporting specialized chemolithotrophic respiration, elevated partial pressures in anoxic atmospheres irreversibly arrest aerobic respiration by outcompeting oxygen at cytochrome $c$ oxidase (Canfield & Thamdrup, 1994; Gaillard et al., 2022).

Beyond isolated chemical poisons, physical chemistry defines global habitability through water activity ($a_w$) and solution chaotropicity (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Cray et al., 2013). Water activity, defined as the ratio of the vapor pressure of water in a solution to the vapor pressure of pure water ($a_w = \frac{p}{p_0}$) at constant temperature, governs thermodynamic water availability (Cray et al., 2020; Stevenson et al., 2015). Terrestrial microbial life is bounded by an absolute lower threshold for cell division at $a_w \approx 0.605 - 0.611$, observed in extremely xerophilic fungi and halophilic archaea (Stevenson et al., 2015; Toner et al., 2017). Below this limit, intracellular hydration shells collapse, halting enzyme activity and osmotic turgor maintenance (Hallsworth et al., 2007; Stevenson et al., 2015).

Chaotropic solutes, characterized by large, low-charge-density ions such as magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$), calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$), perchlorate ($\text{ClO}_4^-$), and iodide ($\text{I}^-$), weaken electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding networks in aqueous matrices (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Cray et al., 2020). By disrupting water ordering, chaotropes lower enthalpy barriers for denaturation, unfolding functional protein structures and fluidizing lipid bilayers to the point of lytic collapse (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Hallsworth et al., 2007). In contrast, kosmotropic solutes, such as sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) and phosphate ($\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$), stabilize water structures and preserve macromolecular configurations, though excessive concentrations restrict the conformational flexibility required for enzymatic turnover (Cray et al., 2020; Hallsworth et al., 2007). The balance between water activity, ionic strength, and chaotropicity frequently creates uninhabitable environments within bodies of liquid water, demonstrating that liquid stability alone is insufficient for biological viability (Belilla et al., 2019; Cockell et al., 2024).

Endogenic Geochemical and Abiotic Drivers of Toxic Solutes
----------------------------------------------------------

The generation, transport, and concentration of toxic chemical suites on a terrestrial exoplanet are governed by fundamental endogenic processes: mantle thermodynamics, magmatic outgassing, tectonic regimes, hydrothermal circulation, and chemical weathering (Cockell et al., 2024; Daswani, 2020; Gaillard et al., 2022).

The composition of a planet's atmospheric and oceanic volatile inventory is coupled to the mantle oxidation state, quantified as oxygen fugacity ($f_{\text{O}_2}$) relative to standard mineral buffers such as Fayalite-Magnetite-Quartz (FMQ) or Iron-Wüstite (IW) (Gaillard et al., 2022). On planets with oxidized upper mantles ($f_{\text{O}_2} \ge \text{FMQ}$), volcanic degassing releases sulfur primarily as sulfur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$) and carbon as carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$) (Gaillard et al., 2022). Dissolution of subaerial and submarine $\text{SO}_2$ plumes into surface waters triggers disproportionation and hydrolysis:

$4\text{SO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 3\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{S}$

This reaction delivers massive sulfuric acid fluxes to surface reservoirs, overwhelming local buffering systems and generating hyperacidic marine environments (Daswani, 2020; Gaillard et al., 2022). Conversely, reduced mantles ($f_{\text{O}_2} \le \text{IW}$) outgas sulfur predominantly as hydrogen sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) and carbon as carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) or methane ($\text{CH}_4$) (Cockell et al., 2024; Gaillard et al., 2022). Under high submarine lithostatic pressures, volatile solubility models show that $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ is preferentially partitioned into hydrothermal fluids (Gaillard et al., 2022). This sustains widespread euxinia in the absence of biological oxidation sinks, enriching water columns with toxic dissolved sulfides (Gaillard et al., 2022).

Hydrothermal circulation across ultramafic lithospheres drives serpentinization, which dictates both solution pH and redox conditions (Cockell et al., 2024). When oceanic fluids interact with olivine-rich peridotites at temperatures between $150^\circ\text{C}$ and $350^\circ\text{C}$, the oxidation of ferrous iron in the fayalite component by water produces magnetite, molecular hydrogen, and serpentine-group minerals (Daswani, 2020):

$3\text{Fe}_2\text{SiO}_4\text{ (fayalite)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4\text{ (magnetite)} + 3\text{SiO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2$

$6\text{Mg}_2\text{SiO}_4\text{ (forsterite)} + \text{SiO}_2 + 8\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{Mg}_3\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5(\text{OH})_4\text{ (serpentine)}$

This process consumes hydronium ions, releasing hydroxide and generating hyperalkaline effluent fluids ($\text{pH } 9-12$) enriched in $\text{H}_2$, dissolved methane via abiotic Sabatier and Fischer-Tropsch-type syntheses, and calcium cations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) (Cockell et al., 2024; Russell et al., 2015). If serpentinization occurs on a global scale—such as in an ocean basin lacking subaerial granitic continents—marine chemistry transitions toward an alkaline state (Kempe & Degens, 1985). The extreme pH of these waters denatures standard nucleic acid structures and saponifies biological lipids, presenting severe challenges to cellular integrity despite providing abundant chemical energy (Cockell et al., 2024; Russell et al., 2015).

Chemical weathering cycles act as secondary geochemical filters on planetary toxicity (Krissansen-Totton et al., 2018). On worlds where atmospheric $p\text{CO}_2$ is maintained at high levels to counteract faint stellar conditions, carbonic acid leaching accelerates the denudation of basaltic crusts (Catling & Kasting, 2017):

$\text{CaSiO}_3 + 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{HCO}_3^- + \text{SiO}_2$

While this process provides climate-stabilizing negative feedbacks via carbonate precipitation, an imbalance in cation extraction can saturate surface reservoirs with heavy divalent metals (Krissansen-Totton et al., 2018).

In arid conditions with thin atmospheres, aqueous weathering is replaced by photochemical oxidation and ionizing radiolysis (Catling et al., 2010). Solar ultraviolet flux ($\lambda < 200\text{ nm}$) and galactic cosmic rays penetrate directly to the regolith, breaking surface water vapor down into $\text{OH}^\bullet$ and $\text{HO}_2^\bullet$ radicals (Catling et al., 2010). These oxidants react with atmospheric and detrital chloride salts to form chlorate ($\text{ClO}_3^-$) and perchlorate ($\text{ClO}_4^-$) through pathways mediated by ozone (Catling et al., 2010):

$\text{Cl}^- + \text{O}_3 \to \text{ClO}^- + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{h\nu\text{, ROS}} \text{ClO}_4^-$

Over geological timescales, this abiotic photochemistry transforms the upper meters of surface regolith into a caustic, oxidizing layer that destroys organic compounds and poses a chronic toxicity hazard across planetary terrains (Catling et al., 2010).
Plausible Chemistries and Architectures of Terrestrial Exo-Oceans

-----------------------------------------------------------------

Exo-ocean chemistry reflects the interaction between initial volatile inventories, mantle-crustal petrology, atmospheric composition, and ocean depth (Daswani, 2020; Kite & Ford, 2018; Krissansen-Totton et al., 2018). Deviations from Earth-like baseline conditions give rise to several distinct oceanic archetypes within the ecosphere.

### Hyperacidic Sulfate Oceans

On volatile-rich worlds where mantle outgassing is dominated by sulfur species ($\text{SO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$) rather than carbon, or where mafic continental weathering is outpaced by volcanic acid flux, oceans become massive sulfuric acid reservoirs (Gaillard et al., 2022; Wadsworth & Cockell, 2017). Characterized by pH values between $1.0$ and $4.0$, these waters are sustained by high concentrations of dissolved bisulfate ($\text{HSO}_4^-$) and sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) (Daswani, 2020). The low pH accelerates the dissolution of crustal minerals, mobilizing toxic transition metals such as $\text{Fe}^{3+}$, $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Cu}^{2+}$, and $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ into solution at concentrations orders of magnitude higher than Earth baselines (Cockell et al., 2024; Léger et al., 2004). In such waters, proton motive forces are disrupted; terrestrial acidophilic archaea rely on inverted membrane potentials to withstand comparable environments, but high heavy-metal concentrations establish a steep evolutionary and physiological hurdle (Clarke et al., 2013; Dalmasso et al., 2016).

### Alkaline Soda Oceans

When a carbon-rich planetary atmosphere contacts an ultramafic or basaltic lithosphere undergoing widespread serpentinization without subaerial granite exposure, oceans evolve into alkaline soda reservoirs (Cockell et al., 2024; Kempe & Degens, 1985). These waters are characterized by high pH ($9.0 - 11.5$) and elevated concentrations of sodium bicarbonate ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) and sodium carbonate ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$) (Glein, 2023; Kempe & Degens, 1985; Russell et al., 2015). This chemistry fundamentally alters nutrient availability, particularly for phosphorus (Glein, 2023). In neutral to mildly acidic waters containing free $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, orthophosphate precipitates as insoluble apatite ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3(\text{OH,F,Cl})$), leading to the prebiotic "phosphate problem" where phosphorus becomes scarce for biological synthesis (Toner & Catling, 2020).

In a soda ocean, carbonate ions outcompete phosphate for calcium, precipitating calcite or aragonite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and stripping the water column of free $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ (Glein, 2023; Toner & Catling, 2020):

$\text{Ca}^{2+} + \text{CO}_3^{2-} \to \text{CaCO}_3\downarrow$

This leaves dissolved orthophosphate unconstrained in solution, allowing phosphorus concentrations to rise into the millimolar and molar ranges (Glein, 2023; Toner & Catling, 2020). While this chemical state resolves a major nutrient constraint, its high alkalinity destabilizes nucleotide ester bonds and RNA backbones, requiring specialized cellular envelopes and stable internal pH regulators to avoid alkaline cell lysis (Russell et al., 2015).

### Hypersaline Chaotropic Brines

On worlds with low hydrospheric water mass fractions or endorheic continental hydrological basins subject to prolonged arid desiccation, oceans and epicontinental seas concentrate into hypersaline brines (Belilla et al., 2019; Toner et al., 2017). Unlike marine systems dominated by kosmotropic sodium chloride ($\text{NaCl}$), advanced geochemical evaporation pathways precipitate halite early, leaving residual brines dominated by calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$) and magnesium chloride ($\text{MgCl}_2$)—analogous to terrestrial features like Don Juan Pond and the Dallol geothermal complex (Belilla et al., 2019; Cray et al., 2020; Toner et al., 2017). These systems combine low water activities ($a_w \ll 0.60$) with high chaotropicity ($>70\text{ kJ/kg}$) (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Cray et al., 2013). The divalent $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ cations disrupt hydrogen bonding in cellular hydration shells, destabilizing proteins and membrane structures and establishing a physicochemical barrier that arrests native biological function (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Cray et al., 2013).

### High-Pressure Ice-Sealed Oceanworlds

Planets possessing deep hydrospheres—where water comprises $>1-10\text{ wt}\%$ of planetary mass—develop oceans hundreds of kilometers deep (Kite & Ford, 2018). At abyssal depths, immense hydrostatic pressures ($>1\text{ GPa}$) force liquid water to freeze into high-pressure ice polymorphs (Ice VI, VII, and X) regardless of temperature (Choukroun & Grasset, 2007; Kite & Ford, 2018). This high-pressure ice mantle forms a physical barrier that isolates the liquid ocean from the underlying silicate mantle, shutting down direct water-rock interactions and hydrothermal circulation (Kite & Ford, 2018; Léger et al., 2004).

Without seafloor silicate weathering or hydrothermal inputs, the ocean is cut off from primary geochemical buffering and mantle phosphorus fluxes (Kite & Ford, 2018; Léger et al., 2004). Atmospheric carbon dioxide equilibrates with this isolated water column without being sequestered into mineral carbonates, causing sustained acidification down to $\text{pH } 3-5$ (Kite & Ford, 2018; Léger et al., 2004). These oceans become dilute, nutrient-poor reservoirs characterized by thermodynamic disequilibrium, where native abiogenesis is limited by severe macronutrient starvation (Kite & Ford, 2018).

Atmospheric Pressure and Photochemical Regimes in Marginal Habitability
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Atmospheric column depth and mass dictate the surface radiation environment, gas retention, and chemical equilibrium of surface waters, creating distinctive toxicity regimes in marginal atmospheres (Catling et al., 2010; Schwieterman et al., 2019).

On planets with thin atmospheres ($P_{\text{surf}} \le 0.1\text{ bar}$), low column depths allow stellar ultraviolet (FUV and NUV) radiation and galactic cosmic rays to penetrate unhindered to the surface (Catling et al., 2010; Wadsworth & Cockell, 2017). This high radiation flux drives the photolysis of volatile water and carbon dioxide, producing reactive oxidants without supporting an ozone protective layer (Catling et al., 2010). The resulting surface environment features dry deposition of superoxides, hydrogen peroxide, and oxychlorines, producing a reactive chemical blanket across topsoils (Catling et al., 2010). Any standing water remains vulnerable to rapid atmospheric escape via thermal jeans and non-thermal hydrodynamic escape, raising residual salinities and concentrating toxic mineral components (Lichtenberg et al., 2019; Wordsworth et al., 2018). While unshielded microbial life is rapidly sterilized by combined UV and chemical oxidation, technological survival remains viable through pressurized shielding, utilizing ambient oxychlorines as consumable oxidizers and oxygen reservoirs (Davila et al., 2013; Wadsworth & Cockell, 2017).

Conversely, thick atmospheres ($P_{\text{surf}} \ge 2 - 10\text{ bar}$) generate distinct toxicity profiles driven by gas partial pressures and photochemical stagnation (Schwieterman et al., 2019). To maintain surface temperatures near the outer boundary of the habitable zone, climate models require high carbon dioxide partial pressures ($p\text{CO}_2 \ge 1 - 5\text{ bar}$) (Krissansen-Totton et al., 2021; Schwieterman et al., 2019). While simple unicellular organisms can adapt to high dissolved inorganic carbon across diverse pH ranges, these atmospheres severely limit complex aerobic life (Schwieterman et al., 2019). For mammalian physiology, carbon dioxide levels exceeding $p\text{CO}_2 \approx 0.005 - 0.01\text{ bar}$ trigger hypercapnia, systemic acidosis, and neurological dysfunction, with lethal thresholds around $0.05\text{ bar}$ (Schwieterman et al., 2019). This physiological constraint restricts the Habitable Zone for Complex Life (HZCL) to roughly $20-30\%$ of the traditional microbial habitable zone (Schwieterman et al., 2019).

Stellar spectral irradiance strongly influences atmospheric chemical balance (Schwieterman et al., 2019). Terrestrial planets orbiting low-mass M-dwarf stars receive a stellar flux with an elevated ratio of far-ultraviolet to near-ultraviolet radiation (FUV/NUV) relative to Sun-like G-dwarfs (Schwieterman et al., 2019). Near-ultraviolet photons ($\lambda \approx 290 - 330\text{ nm}$) are required to photolyze tropospheric ozone ($\text{O}_3$), producing excited oxygen atoms ($\text{O}(^1\text{D})$) that react with water vapor to generate hydroxyl radicals ($\text{OH}^\bullet$):

$\text{O}_3 + h\nu \to \text{O}_2 + \text{O}(^1\text{D})$

$\text{O}(^1\text{D}) + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{OH}^\bullet$

Because M-dwarfs emit low NUV fluxes relative to their FUV output, tropospheric $\text{OH}^\bullet$ concentrations are reduced (Schwieterman et al., 2019). The reaction with hydroxyl radicals represents the primary atmospheric sink for carbon monoxide:

$\text{CO} + \text{OH}^\bullet \to \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}^\bullet$

Without adequate hydroxyl production, this sink fails (Schwieterman et al., 2019). As FUV photolysis continues to split atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ into $\text{CO}$ and atomic oxygen, carbon monoxide accumulates abiotically, reaching concentrations between several hundred parts per million and a few percent of total atmospheric volume (Schwieterman et al., 2019). While primitive chemotrophs can exploit $\text{CO}$ as an energetic substrate, these atmospheres are toxic to complex aerobic organisms (Schwieterman et al., 2019). In mammalian physiology, carbon monoxide binds to hemoglobin with an affinity approximately 200 times higher than that of oxygen, blocking cellular respiration and leading to asphyxiation even in oxygenated environments (Schwieterman et al., 2019).

## Thermodynamic Extremes: Eutectic Cryo-Brines and Moist-Greenhouse Leaching

Planetary habitability is bounded by thermal extremes that transform surface water from a balanced solvent into a hostile, toxic medium (Hallsworth et al., 2007; Toner et al., 2017).

At the sub-freezing cryo-margin, mean planetary surface temperatures fall well below $273\text{ K}$ (Cockell et al., 2024; Toner et al., 2017). Under these conditions, pure liquid water cannot persist, and aqueous environments are restricted to concentrated eutectic brines (Rettberg et al., 2022; Toner et al., 2017). Different salt mixtures depress the freezing point of water to varying extents (Toner et al., 2017). For example, the eutectic temperature ($T_{\text{eut}}$) drops to $-21.1^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{NaCl}-\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $-36^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{FeCl}_2-\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $-50.4^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{Mg(ClO}_4)_2-\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and $-70^\circ\text{C}$ for $\text{Ca(ClO}_4)_2-\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Marion et al., 2003; Toner et al., 2017).

While eutectic brines allow liquid solutions to remain stable in cold environments, their physical chemistry introduces severe biological stresses (Cray et al., 2020; Toner et al., 2017). Freezing depression requires high solute concentrations, which sharply lowers water activity (Hallsworth et al., 2007; Stevenson et al., 2015). A perchlorate or chloride brine near its eutectic point exhibits water activity levels well below the terrestrial biological minimum ($a_w \approx 0.20 - 0.55$) (Stevenson et al., 2015). Concurrently, the chaotropic effects of concentrated divalent cations ($\text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}$) destabilize intracellular structures (Ball & Hallsworth, 2015; Cray et al., 2013). Unshielded cells experience rapid dehydration and structural degradation, rendering cold eutectic brines chemically uninhabitable despite their liquid state (Belilla et al., 2019; Stevenson et al., 2015).

At the high-temperature margin, increasing stellar irradiance drives a planet toward the moist-greenhouse and runaway-greenhouse thresholds (Ingersoll, 1969; Komabayashi, 1967; Nakajima et al., 1992). The runaway greenhouse state is governed by the Komabayashi-Ingersoll and Simpson-Nakajima limits (Goldblatt & Watson, 2012; Ingersoll, 1969; Komabayashi, 1967). These establish an upper limit on the outgoing longwave radiation (OLR) that a water-saturated troposphere can emit to space:

$\text{OLR}_{\text{limit}} \approx 280 - 310\text{ W m}^{-2}$

When absorbed stellar flux exceeds this ceiling, the planet cannot radiate away incoming energy, and surface temperatures rise continuously (Goldblatt & Watson, 2012; Ingersoll, 1969; Nakajima et al., 1992). Before entering a full runaway state, a planet passes through a moist greenhouse phase (Cockell et al., 2024; Kasting, 1988). In this regime, surface temperatures range between $50^\circ\text{C}$ and $90^\circ\text{C}$, causing water vapor to saturate the troposphere and bypass the cold-trap into the stratosphere (Cockell et al., 2024; Kasting, 1988). There, stellar UV radiation photolyzes water molecules, driving rapid hydrogen loss to space and gradual planetary desiccation (Cockell et al., 2024; Kasting, 1988).

Geochemically, this elevated thermal state intensifies chemical weathering and mineral leaching (Krissansen-Totton et al., 2021; Léger et al., 2004). Kinetic weathering rates follow the Arrhenius relationship:

$k = A \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{R T}\right)$

Elevated temperatures accelerate the extraction of silicate and trace elements from continental and seafloor rocks (Catling & Kasting, 2017; Kite & Ford, 2018). Intense evaporation strips freshwater systems, driving residual oceans toward saturation in heavy metals, silica ($\text{SiO}_2SiO2\text{SiO}_2SiO2​SiO2SiO2SiO2SiO2​\text{SiO}_2SiO2\text{SiO}_2SiO2​SiO2​SiO2SiO2​SiO2​$), borates, and sulfates (Daswani, 2020; Léger et al., 2004). The combination of thermal stress, high mineral loads, and elevated salinity imposes severe evolutionary constraints, precluding complex multi-tissue organisms and restricting any indigenous ecology to hyperthermophilic single-celled life (Cockell et al., 2024; Hallsworth et al., 2007).

Astrobiological Divergence: Native Abiogenesis Versus Technological Habitation
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The environmental parameters required for the origin of life (_abiogenesis_) differ substantially from those necessary for the survival of complex organisms, and both differ from the requirements for technological human survival (Cockell et al., 2024; Schwieterman et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2025). A planet chemically hostile to the emergence of indigenous biology can nonetheless support long-term technological habitation (Schwieterman et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2025).

Abiogenesis requires specific environmental conditions to support the assembly of primitive metabolic cycles and replicating informational polymers (Cockell et al., 2024; Wong et al., 2025). These include concentrated organic precursors—such as hydrogen cyanide ($\text{HCN}$), formaldehyde ($\text{HCHO}$), and formamide—maintained in localized aqueous systems (Toner & Catling, 2020). Prebiotic chemistry further demands accessible macronutrients, particularly soluble phosphate not locked in insoluble apatite minerals (Toner & Catling, 2020). Thermodynamic driving forces, such as wet-dry cycles on exposed landmasses, are essential to facilitate condensation reactions and polymerize peptides and oligonucleotides (Cockell et al., 2024; Damer & Deamer, 2020). Finally, moderate pH and redox gradients are necessary to establish primitive proton motive forces without degrading exposed catalytic structures (Catling et al., 2010; Daswani, 2020).

Many planetary environments fail these criteria completely (Cockell et al., 2024; Daswani, 2020). In high-pressure ice-sealed waterworlds, the absence of subaerial land eliminates the wet-dry cycles required for nucleotide polymerization, while the ice barrier shuts off access to crustal catalytic surfaces and mineral phosphorus (Kite & Ford, 2018). On thin-atmosphere worlds, surface oxidizing agents destroy organic precursors before self-assembling prebiotic structures can mature (Catling et al., 2010). In hyperalkaline soda systems, the same conditions that concentrate phosphorus also accelerate the alkaline hydrolysis of RNA phosphodiester backbones, preventing genetic replication without protective mechanisms (Russell et al., 2015; Toner & Catling, 2020). Consequently, an exoplanet may remain completely sterile throughout its evolutionary history due to these prebiotic bottlenecks, while remaining physically located within the circumstellar habitable zone (Cockell et al., 2024; Schwieterman et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2025).

For technological human habitation, these prebiotic geochemical constraints are largely irrelevant (Wong et al., 2025). A technological civilization relies on enclosed systems and engineered life-support architectures, utilizing local raw materials through in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) rather than depending on native biospheric cycles (Curr Trends Biol Sci, 2025; Davila et al., 2013). Environmental toxins that prevent native biogenesis can frequently be processed into useful resources (Davila et al., 2013).

Perchlorates ($\text{ClO}_4^-$), which are toxic to both plants and human thyroid function at low concentrations, can be treated using perchlorate-reducing bacteria or synthetic molybdenum catalysts (Curr Trends Biol Sci, 2025; Davila et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2021):

$\text{ClO}_4^- \xrightarrow{\text{perchlorate reductase}} \text{ClO}_2^- \xrightarrow{\text{chlorite dismutase}} \text{Cl}^- + \text{O}_2$

This bioprocess detoxifies local soils and water supplies while generating elemental oxygen for life support and propellant systems (Davila et al., 2013). Chaotropic and hypersaline waters can be processed using multistage nanofiltration and high-pressure reverse osmosis, stripping out divalent cations and restoring water activity to potable levels while generating mineral salts for construction and metallurgical extraction (Davila et al., 2013; Hallsworth et al., 2007). Atmospheres containing elevated levels of carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, and sulfur dioxide can be managed within enclosed habitats using regenerative chemical scrubbing and catalytic Sabatier reactors, which reduce carbon oxides into methane and potable water (Davila et al., 2013; Schwieterman et al., 2019). As a result, a planet classified as barren or hostile to primitive life can remain fully viable for sustained technological settlement, demonstrating that biological habitability and technological habitability represent distinct regimes in planetary science (Cockell et al., 2024; Schwieterman et al., 2019).

Synthesis of Geochemical Landscapes Across the Ecosphere
--------------------------------------------------------

The chemical state of an exoplanet's land, atmosphere, and water is not a simple binary switch governed by stellar irradiance, but an integrated continuum shaped by mantle petrology, atmospheric thickness, thermal balance, and solution thermodynamics (Daswani, 2020). Surface and aquatic toxicity emerges through three fundamental mechanisms: direct chemical poisoning via soft Lewis acid heavy metals, oxychlorines, and volatile chalcogens that disrupt enzymatic active sites (Catling et al., 2010; Cockell et al., 2024; Wadsworth & Cockell, 2017); thermodynamic solvent restriction where extreme chaotropicity and depressed water activity ($a_w < 0.605$) dismantle macromolecular hydration shells (Belilla et al., 2019; Cray et al., 2020; Stevenson et al., 2015); and atmospheric compositional imbalances where elevated carbon dioxide or abiotically accumulated carbon monoxide create conditions toxic to complex aerobic organisms (Schwieterman et al., 2019).

Evaluating habitability requires separating the conditions necessary for de novo abiogenesis from those that define technological human survival (Cockell et al., 2024; Schwieterman et al., 2019). Abiogenesis requires delicate environmental interactions, including accessible phosphorus inventories, subaerial wet-dry cycles, catalytic mineral interfaces, and moderate redox windows (Damer & Deamer, 2020; Russell et al., 2015; Toner & Catling, 2020). These conditions are easily eliminated by deep oceans, thin atmospheres, or extreme thermal states (Catling et al., 2010; Ingersoll, 1969; Kite & Ford, 2018).

Conversely, technological human survival depends on energy availability and raw volatile stocks (Davila et al., 2013). Through chemical engineering, membrane separation, catalytic reduction, and environmental isolation, hostile planetary substrates can be transformed into industrial feedstocks and life-support assets (Davila et al., 2013). Expanding our models beyond simple liquid-water boundaries reveals that the galaxy hosts a diverse spectrum of worlds: environments that are geochemically sterile to native biological emergence, yet fully capable of supporting technological human settlement (Cockell et al., 2024; Daswani, 2020; Schwieterman et al., 2019).
