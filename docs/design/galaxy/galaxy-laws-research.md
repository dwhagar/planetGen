## How to read the tables

Status tags:

- **V** the number or law was read in the paper text (place given). "figure-only" means the paper states the trend but the values are in a plot.
- **partly** abstract only, or quoted by a second paper, or a figure value.
- **D** derived here from verified relations (arithmetic reproducible; the inputs are V). Usable as a rule, but it is this note's arithmetic, not a paper's result.
- **placeholder** my own choice or a recalled figure, labelled so; replace when the research request is answered.
- **not found** no reachable source gave it. The exact request for Boss's list is in the last section (R-numbers).

Arithmetic illustrations use the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation of row 12 with baryonic mass equal to M* (Vf = (M*/47)^(1/4): 89, 181 and 255 km/s at M* = 3e9, 5e10, 2e11). They are not inputs: the chain supplies Vc from the enclosed mass.

## 0. Summary for TODO

1. **The 1.5e9 anchor is wrong.** Deason et al. 2019 (arXiv:1908.02763, abstract, sections 5.3 and 5.4) gives the Milky Way stellar halo as **1.4 ± 0.4e9** solar masses, and puts it near 2 percent of the galaxy, not the 3 percent in the `tuning.py` docstring and in the design note. Mackereth and Bovy 2020 (arXiv:1910.03590, abstract only) give 1.3e9 total and 0.9e9 accreted. The constant is a Milky Way copy in any case; it goes with the law.
2. **There is no measured stellar halo law over 3e9 to 2e11.** The nearest, labelled placeholder, is f = M_halo/M* = 0.05 (M*/5e10)^0.79 with a 0.33 dex draw. The middle (3.8e10 to 8e10) is measured; the slope is from an outer-component stack; the low end is a threefold extrapolation. Request R1.
3. **Dispersions and ages become laws with sources for most components.** Young stars: the gas dispersion. Disk: central σz from Vc (Martinsson 2013). Bulge: from the black hole relation already verified or from Vc/σ0 = 2(1 - B/T). Halo: from the Jeans equation. Clusters and satellites: from the enclosed mass (Wolf 2010). Not sourced: the normalisation of the age-velocity relation for other galaxies (R4) and nuclear populations (R9).
4. **Thick disk:** mass ratio to the thin disk, length ratio and height ratio have laws (Yoachim and Dalcanton 2006), and the youngest thick disk age has a law (Comerón 2021). Two source conflicts are named below; the build needs a choice (defaults proposed).
5. **Satellites and clusters:** a size law in stellar mass (Carlsten 2021, V, with scatter), an occupation draw for the cutoff (Benitez-Llambay and Frenk 2020, V for the edges, width a placeholder), and a cluster system radius law with a conflict between two papers.
6. **Halo metallicity offset:** Harmsen 2017 gives a rough fit and an eye-scaled offset of -0.6 dex; the offset against the host's own centre is about -0.9 dex (D). A physical rule from D'Souza and Bell 2018 is given as the alternative.
7. **Inconsistency in the code itself:** the population scale heights (50/300, 150/300, 1) and the dispersions (15, 25, 50) cannot both hold if height goes as dispersion squared. GEN.401 should take both from one law (see Q4).

## Q6. Reionization cutoff mass and the dwarf end (row 10; GEN.369, GEN.403)

Two cutoffs exist and must not be mixed. (1) The mass below which a halo forms no star at all gates "does a galaxy exist". (2) The mass at which a halo has lost half its baryons says how much gas an existing small galaxy holds. The halo occupation is a property of the z = 0 mass for an isolated field halo and of the peak mass before accretion for a subhalo (GEN.369 draws subhaloes, so it needs the peak-mass version).

## Conflicts between sources (verified wins on contradiction; both are verified where marked)

1. **Where galaxy formation switches on.** Benitez-Llambay and Frenk (field haloes, z = 0 mass): half occupation at 10^9.4, none below 3e8, all above 5e9 (Msun/h). Nadler (peak mass of Milky Way subhaloes): M50 below 8.5e7. Wheeler (FIRE-2): all haloes above 10^8.6 populated. Sawala: luminous fraction below 10 percent at 10 km/s. Fitts: one starless halo of 15 at 1e10. The first, fourth and fifth agree; the second and third imply star formation in lighter haloes. They differ in resolution, in z = 0 against peak mass and in isolated against satellite. Both are verified, so the standing rule does not pick. **Default proposed:** field dwarfs use Benitez-Llambay and Frenk (z = 0 mass); subhaloes use Nadler's erf on the peak mass with M50 at the 95 percent upper limit 8.5e7 and the faintest-satellite bound as a hard floor (Mpeak 1e6). Choice for Boss if he wants otherwise.
2. **Scatter in M* at fixed halo mass in the dwarf range.** Fitts ± 0.5 dex at 1e10; Nadler below 0.19 dex (95 percent, prior driven); Moster 0.15 dex (not valid at the dwarf end). Not resolved. Default: 0.5 dex field, 0.19 dex satellites.
3. **Cluster system size against halo mass.** Hudson and Robison R_e,GCS ∝ R200^2.63; Forbes ∝ R200^1. At Milky Way mass they differ by 2.6 times (3.3 against 8.6 kpc; observed 4.1). At 1e11 the steep law gives 0.44 kpc, smaller than a dwarf's light radius, so it fails if extrapolated down. Default: Hudson and Robison near its range, the dwarf ratio below, the unsourced join in between.
4. **Satellite size against halo size.** Kravtsov's 0.015 R200 (150 pc at 10 kpc) is about 4 times Nadler's fitted 37 pc; Jiang's 0.02 sits with Kravtsov. Kravtsov and Jiang calibrate at 1e8 to 1e11 stellar mass; the faint satellites prefer a lower amplitude. The stellar-mass route (Carlsten) avoids the question.
5. **Thick disc mass ratio at high mass.** Yoachim and Dalcanton 0.06 to 0.14 at Vc = 180 to 255; Comerón 2018 states 0.2 to 1 above 120 km/s. They agree below 120. Comerón's fit has the better point-spread function; Yoachim's is the only formula. Superseded (Boss, 2026-10-17, 12:02 PM EDT, widen and keep both; GEN.532): the mass ratio against Vc is widened between the Yoachim and Dalcanton trend and the Comerón 2018 fit (log10(M_thick/M_thin) = 2.113 - 1.086 log10(Vc), found in VizieR J/A+A/610/A5, gap-filling-plan.md section 4.9), and the height ratio is drawn from 2.5 to 4.2.
6. **Thick disc length against thin.** The code's 0.769 (thick shorter) is Milky Way star counts; Yoachim and Dalcanton find thick longer in all but 5 galaxies, ratio about 1 to 1.2, and Comerón 2018 finds that many up-bending breaks are a thin-thick superposition. The external-galaxy sources agree and disagree with the Milky Way number. Superseded (gap-filling-plan.md, 2026-10-17, 1:16 PM EDT; GEN.532): the length ratio is the Comerón 2018 fit (median 1.46), not 1.15 (Vc/100)^-0.2.
7. **Thick disc ages.** Comerón 2021 predicts 10 Gyr for massive spirals and 4 to 6 below 1e10; lenticulars violate it (measured 4 to 11 Gyr). One formula cannot cover both; the first family is spiral-like, so use the formula with a wide scatter and note the S0 failure.
8. **Spheroid age.** Thomas (light-weighted, 6 to 10 Gyr, depends on mass) against González Delgado (mass-weighted, about 10 Gyr, depends on type). The generator draws star ages, so the mass-weighted quantity is the right one. Default: about 10 Gyr mass-weighted for bulges of early spirals and early types, younger for late types; Thomas's laws as the mass trend.
9. **Halo age.** The code's 10 to 13 Gyr is a Milky Way style; Harmsen 2023 finds massive haloes younger (t90 about 6 Gyr) in a tentative six-point trend.
10. **Halo metallicity baseline.** -0.6 dex against the relation at the halo mass (H17) against about -0.9 against the host centre (computed here). Hydro and semi-analytic models differ in slope (D'Souza and Bell Fig 10).
11. **Halo fractions.** Dragonfly (0.9 percent, light beyond 5 half-mass radii, three non-detections) against GHOSTS (2 to 14 percent). H17 reconciles them as a total halo with limits, but the definitions differ. Hydro runs overpredict by up to ten times.
12. **Specific mass of the cluster system.** η = 2.9e-5 to 6e-5 in four papers (row 9). Even at the dwarf end the Local Volume sits 0.3 dex below Virgo at fixed mass, an environment factor of 2 the generator has no input for.

- **R6. Oldest thick disc age and the first-disc epoch** (GEN.386). Thick-disc stellar age distributions (start and end) against host mass. Comerón 2021 gives only the youngest. Haywood 2013 and Bensby 2014 give the Milky Way only and were not read; the Pinna et al. 2019 MUSE star formation histories (A&A 625 A95, 623 A19) would give the S0 range: please check they are reachable.
- **R7. Radius ratio of metal-poor to metal-rich cluster systems** (GEN.403). R_e(blue)/R_e(red) against galaxy mass: Forbes et al. 2012, Rhode and Zepf 2004, or Kartha et al. 2014 (arXiv:1310.1979, read only qualitatively).
- **R8. Cluster systems at intermediate masses** (GEN.403). Measured system radii for spirals and lower-mass early types, log M* 8.5 to 10.3, to replace the placeholder join: Hargis and Rhode 2014, or Georgiev 2010 half-number radii.
- **R9. Extragalactic cluster half-light radius against mass, cluster ages against host mass, and nuclear populations** (GEN.297, GEN.403, GEN.280). Jordan et al. 2005 (ACS Virgo Cluster Survey half-light radii; the title search found nothing), E-MOSAICS age distributions against host mass (Pfeffer or Kruijssen), and nuclear star cluster ages and dispersions against host mass (Neumayer, Seth and Böker 2020, arXiv:2001.03626, may hold ages; not opened in this pass).
- **R10. Halo age against halo mass with more than six haloes.** Simulation papers with the relation as a fit: Cooper et al. 2010, Deason et al. 2016, D'Souza and Bell 2018b.
- **R11. Dwarf-range stellar-to-halo relation with its scatter.** Which relation Boss wants for haloes of 1e8.5 to 1e10.5: Brook 2014 (row 10), Garrison-Kimmel (slope 1.9 not found in 1701.03792 or 1404.5313), or Behroozi 2019 low-mass fit. Wanted: median log M* against log Mh and the intrinsic scatter at 1e9 and 1e10. Fitts (0.5 dex) and Nadler (below 0.19 dex) conflict.
- **R12. Reionization redshift spread.** Katz et al. 2020 (inhomogeneous reionization, cited by Nadler for M50 about 1e8) and Alvarez 2009 or Busha 2010 (spread of z_re between Milky Way-like regions). Would let the cutoff depend on a per-galaxy z_re.
- **R13. Ultrafaint size relation.** Muñoz et al. 2018 (Sérsic parameters of ultrafaint dwarfs; arXiv id not found by search) or Nadler's catalogue table: r_h against M_V from -2 to -7 with the intrinsic scatter. The Carlsten fit stops at log M* = 5.5.
- **R14. Halo mass law above 1e11 Msun.** An observational accreted halo mass or outer-component fraction against M* for early types, from a method that identifies an accreted halo there (the H17 method does not). None among the papers read.
- **R15. Graus et al. 2019** (occupation fraction, cited by Nadler for the erf form) and Fitts et al. 2018 (occupation scatter): the occupation fraction and its width against halo mass. The Graus text found by search was a different paper.
