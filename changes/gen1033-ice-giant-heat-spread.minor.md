**Headline:** An ice giant can now radiate as little heat as Uranus does, or as much as Neptune does, instead of every one of them getting Neptune's.

### Changed
- **GEN.1033: the spread of ice-giant internal heat.** The cooling law runs through Jupiter and Neptune, so a Uranus-mass giant got Neptune's 0.32 W/m2 where Uranus radiates 0.042 W/m2 (Neptune 0.433). An ice giant now has one of two interiors: mixed (the law as it was) or layered by a stable compositional gradient that stops the convection and leaves a thermal boundary layer, radiating 3 to 30 percent of the law (log-uniform; 0.01 to 0.095 W/m2 at Uranus's mass, inside its measured 0.042 +0.047 -0.042). The chance of a layered interior is one half, from one of the two measured ice giants having one. The cause is the one the thermal evolution models give (a gradient is needed because no adiabatic interior reproduces Uranus's luminosity) and the impact history that mixes or spares it; sources are in the registry (R5094 to R5099, added to the References). The draw is on a stream of the giant's own (its mass), so the one-bar temperature, the helium rain, the air and the habitability all read the same heat and the run's stream does not move. Gas giants, brown dwarfs and rocky bodies are untouched. Stand-ins registered in GEN.725: IGH1 (T4, L0), IGH2 (T2, L1), IGH3 (T6, L0). The row CC14 (the Uranus anomaly of the old law) is retired.
- Generator epoch bump (numbered at merge): about half the ice giants (and rogue ice giants) of 5 to 20 Earth masses get a lower internal heat, so their one-bar temperature, air and habitability score move; every other body is unchanged.
