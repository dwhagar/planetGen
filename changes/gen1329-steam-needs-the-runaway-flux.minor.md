**Headline:** A planet that gets less starlight than the runaway-greenhouse limit no longer ends up as a 2,000 K steam world: 44 of 63 such worlds in a 450-system sample were a fixed-point accident, and Earth-mass worlds in the habitable zone stay oceans now.

### Changed
- GEN.1329: `physics/surface_air.py` gains `Setting.steam_possible` (`_steam_possible`: the body's effective flux, starlight plus its own interior and tidal heat, against `orbits.runaway_greenhouse_flux`) and `MOIST_LIMIT_K` (375 K, the surface of the radiative-convective model's saturated clear-sky air at its last solution before the runaway). A body with water that gets less than the limit has its surface held under 375 K in every step of the fixed point; above the limit nothing changes. Before, a dense hydrogen-rich first step could carry any wet planet past 647 K, where `freeze_out` put the whole ocean in the air (1e10 to 1e11 Pa, held at the 2,000 K guard): 63 of 1,339 rocky planets in 450 generated systems, 44 of them under the limit (median 0.44 of it, the lowest 0.008, 2 W/m2 absorbed), and 8 of 14 Earth-mass bodies in the habitable zone of a Sun-like star (now 191 to 375 K and under 5 bar in all 14). A hot steam air radiates about 280 W/m2 whatever it absorbs (Nakajima, Hayashi and Abe 1992), so such a state does not stand. Generator output changes for those worlds, so it rides a train with an epoch.
- GEN.1329: `test_a_runaway_greenhouse_stops_at_the_ceiling` declares its Earth-like body past the runaway limit (the 2,000 K ceiling is the guard of a body that can hold steam) and asks the ceiling the same question as before.
- GEN.1329: the escape efficiency of the water-loss history (`tuning.ENERGY_LIMITED_EFFICIENCY`, 0.01, Boss's design of 10-23) is not changed here. It takes 12 percent of the water of a K-star planet at 21 times starlight over 13.8 Gyr; at the atmosphere model's 0.15 it would take all of it (the GEN.974 follow-up question).
