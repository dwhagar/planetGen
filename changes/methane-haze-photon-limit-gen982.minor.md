**Headline:** The methane haze of an air can be no thicker than the star's far-ultraviolet light can make it, so a methane world far from its star hazes far less than one near it.

### Changed
- **GEN.982, part 3.** The methane supply of the haze (`aerosols.haze_in`) is the smaller of the diffusion-limited supply and the most methane the star's 91.2 to 200 nm light can break (`photolysis.photon_limited_flux_mol_m2_s`: the column that absorbs the light times the rate per molecule at the body's far-ultraviolet flux and star temperature, or all the methane there is where the air holds less), where it was diffusion-limited only. A body with no star light makes no methane haze. The far-ultraviolet flux at the body (`activity.far_uv_flux_ratio`) and the chromospheric share of it (`activity.far_uv_chromospheric_share`) come from the stellar library; the photolysis rate takes a blend of the Sun's spectrum and the star's photosphere (`photolysis.blended_spectral_ratio`, stand-in SL4), and the light is absorbed in one optical depth of the species' mean UV cross section (stand-in SL5). At Titan's distance the photon limit is Titan's own methane loss (about 6e9 molecules per cm2 per s) and is 100 to 100000 times under the old diffusion supply, so Titan-like hazes come out thinner than before; the Titan-span test is an expected failure until the haze depth per unit of mass flux (the fall term) is reworked.
