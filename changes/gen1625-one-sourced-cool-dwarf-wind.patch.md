**Headline:** A cool dwarf's wind now comes from measured stellar winds, one law for its bubble, the black holes it feeds and the pressure on its planets' magnetic fields; an M dwarf's is no longer 2.8 times the Sun's, and a white dwarf has none.

### Fixed

- GEN.1625: `stellar_wind.wind` scaled every cool dwarf from the Sun by `R^2 L^-0.5 M^-1` (cited to a source that is not about stars: R4756 was a book chapter on the use of force), so an M5 dwarf had 2.8 times the Sun's mass loss and a white dwarf had a wind; and `magnetism.wind_pressure_pa` held a second, different wind (X-ray surface flux to the 1.3 power, capped at 10 for A to K and 1 for dwarfs under 0.4 solar masses). There is now one law (`stellar_wind.coronal_mass_loss_solar`). An A to K dwarf's mass loss is the Sun's times its squared radius times its X-ray surface flux over the Sun's to the 1.29 power ([R5297], 1.34 in [R5298]), the flux held at 1e6 erg/cm^2/s, the dividing line above which the measured winds turn weak. An M dwarf's is 3.3 times the Sun's per unit surface area whatever its flux, the median of the six single M dwarfs measured by [R5296] (an M5 dwarf of 0.2 solar radii has 0.13 of the Sun's, under the 0.2 that Proxima Centauri's limit allows). A white dwarf has none. The speed stays the Sun's 400 km/s scaled by the escape speed. The heliosphere, the winds that feed a black hole and the pressure on a planet's magnetosphere (`magnetism.wind_pressure_pa`: the Earth's times the star's mass loss and speed over the Sun's, no less than the interstellar medium's pressure) all read it; `Star._calculate_heliosphere_radius_static` and `stellar_wind.wind` take the star's `log_lx_lbol`, so the heliosphere is sized after the star's activity. Stand-ins WN5 to WN8 say so; the three exponent constants of the old law and `magnetism.WIND_CAP_SOLAR`, `M_DWARF_WIND_CAP` and `M_DWARF_WIND_MASS` are gone.
