### Added
- **The unconsolidated rocky core of a small icy body makes its tide (GEN.1886).** Choblet 2017 gives the core a tide of its own. With the shell held stiff against a small body's tide (GEN.1877), an Enceladus-like moon's shell made 5.5e8 W and froze through. Its core, a rubble sphere of the radius the mean density gives, of rigidity 1.6e7 Pa and a 1/Q of 0.5 (Choblet's own numbers), makes 1.9e10 W at Enceladus's eccentricity; the heat comes up through the shell like the body's own, so the ocean returns (a shell of 28 km, 20 to 40 km observed) and the body radiates 2.1e10 W against 1.6e10 W observed. The core's k2/Q also adds to the shell's for the spin and the orbit. The rubble gives way to rock with the core's central pressure (Athy compaction, 1e8 Pa), so Europa and Callisto get none. The one number set to a result is the share of an isolated sphere's dissipation the core keeps (0.37, Choblet's 20 GW), stand-in IC8.
