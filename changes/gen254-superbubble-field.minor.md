**Headline:** The galaxy has superbubbles, hot thin cavities with no molecular clouds in them, drawn on the Galaxy Map.

### Changed
- **Molecular clouds were spread evenly through the gas, with no cavities.** About 12% of the plane at the solar circle (more in the arms and the inner disk) now lies inside a superbubble: a cavity 100 to 1,000 pc across (the Local Bubble is one) blown by the supernovae of an OB association, with ionised gas of about a million kelvin and a thousandth of the disk's density. No molecular cloud whose centre is in one is kept. The cavities come from the galaxy's seed and nothing is stored; a galaxy made before this loses its clouds in them on the next `--redo-nebula-field apply`.
- The Galaxy Map draws each cavity as a faint thin shell, a sector page says when its centre is inside one, and a sector's profile names it. `GET /api/galaxy/superbubbles` lists them and a sector's detail carries `superbubble`.
