**Headline:** A system now knows whether its giants scattered each other, and when they did, the cold outer disc gets a hot, excited share.

### Changed
- **Capture windows recorded (GEN.1082).** The planet architecture already threw out the lighter of two neighbouring bodies that ended too close; where both are giants this is now counted as an instability of the system, with the close giant-giant encounters of the reference instability (130, the mean of the three histories in the paper). The count is kept on the system (`StarSystem.capture_windows`, not stored) for the irregular-moon wiring that follows (GEN.1083); the gas window needs no new number because each giant already keeps the lifetime of its circumplanetary disc.
- **The outer disc is excited by an instability.** The scatter step of an outer disc takes how much the giants stirred each other as 0 to 1; it was always 0, so a disc had no hot classical population. A system with a giant-giant instability now gives it 1, so about a fifth of its cold outer disc (a stand-in, 0.4 of the cold share left) is excited into the hot classical population, and its members are drawn on the hotter orbits. Systems without one are as before. About 4 in 100 Sun-like systems have the instability, which is the architecture's own rate and is not tuned.
