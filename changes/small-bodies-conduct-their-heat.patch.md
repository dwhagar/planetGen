A small body whose mantle does not convect now conducts its heat out across the mantle. The one-box interior let none out when the viscosity was past the convecting range, and next to none when the boundary layer would be thicker than the mantle, so a dwarf-planet size belt planet far from its star kept all its radiogenic heat (a Urey ratio from 900 to 20 million and a heat flow near 1e-10 W/m2). The flux is now never under the conduction across the mantle, k (Tm - Ts) / thickness; bodies of 0.004 Earth masses and up, which convect, are unchanged. In 86 belt planets of the first twelve seeds 43 had a Urey ratio over 1.5; none does now (the largest is 1.26, a body still warming).
