**Headline:** Giant planets now keep small captured bodies as irregular moons, drawn from the system's outer disc and from their own feeding zone, and the regular moons no longer take the captured origin as often as a disc moon.

### Changed
- **Irregular moons (GEN.1083).** Once a star's belts stand, each planet gets a second tier of moons above the regular ones: the small bodies it caught in the gas era and in a giant-giant instability from the outer disc, ground down for the age of the star, plus the one massive body a binary fly-by may have exchanged in. Each has a source distance and rock or ice type (the composition follows where in the disc it came from, and the ice it lost on capture) and an orbit that stays inside the planet's stable zone (prograde to 0.4 and retrograde to 0.55 of the Hill radius, outside the regular moons, out of the Kozai band), so the retrograde excess comes from stability. A planet whose zone is too small (a hot Jupiter), a terrestrial planet and a planet of a star with no outer disc keep none. They are kept on the planet and are not stored yet (GEN.1084).
- **The captured origin of a regular moon weighs less the heavier the moon.** A moon draws its origin from the origin table with the captured row weighed by the size law of the survivors, so a moon of 1e20 kg or more is rarely a captured body and a small one is as likely as before.
- **The distance a captured body came from follows the disc.** The source distance of a captured body is drawn by the mass of the disc's solids (the surface density slope of the belt history now feeds the captured composition).
