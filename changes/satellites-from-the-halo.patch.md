### Changed

- Every galaxy used to get the Milky Way's 11 satellite rows, scaled in stellar mass but not in count or distance, so a 3e7 solar mass galaxy held satellites out to 250 kpc and billions of satellite systems. The plan now draws the satellites from the galaxy's seed and halo: the subhalo count law of Boylan-Kolchin 2010 for how many, an occupation draw by peak mass (Nadler 2020), the stellar mass from the chain's stellar-to-halo relation with scatter, the size from Carlsten 2021, the metallicity from Kirby 2013, and an Einasto radial distribution that stops at the host's virial radius (GEN.624). A 3e7 solar mass galaxy now draws about one tiny satellite, a Milky Way mass galaxy about sixty (nine above 1e5 solar masses), and a galaxy without a halo none. The heaviest subhalo drawn is 0.2 of the host halo and no satellite holds more than a quarter of its host's stellar mass (both stand-ins). The Milky Way table remains only as a test reference.
