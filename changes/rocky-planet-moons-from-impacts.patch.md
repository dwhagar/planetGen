### Changed
- **Massive moons of rocky planets from giant impacts (GEN.962).** The moon mass law gave every rocky planet an even chance of moons and then filled its orbits, so rocky planets held 1.5 to 2.0 moons of 1e20 kg and over each against the Solar System's 1 for 4 rocky planets. A rocky planet (one with no row in the hosting gate's satellite-share table, and not a giant) now gets a massive moon from a giant impact in 1 planet in 12 [R5112], of at least 0.006 of its mass (half the Moon's share of the Earth's), and a further one with the same chance, so its moons are few and large; its small moons are the captured ones. Because a massive moon is often denser or lighter than its parent can hold, its mass is drawn again up to 20 times before the slot is given up. In 80 Sun-like systems 8 percent of rocky planets of 0.1 to 1 Earth masses and 4 percent of those over 1 Earth mass hold a massive moon. Giants and small bodies keep their rules. The three values are stand-ins RM1 to RM3 in the stand-in registry (GEN.725).
