**Headline:** A young core-collapse supernova remnant can now host the asteroid field its progenitor's belt became, an expanding ring of rubble that leaves at about 11 km/s and is retired past a light year.

### Added
- **The supernova host rule (GEN.1104).** Every scattered core-collapse remnant draws, from its own seed, a progenitor from the mass windows of its core, whether the star kept a belt for its age, whether the explosion unbound it and whether the remnant is still young enough to host the field (`field_rates.hosted_belt`), and the field's mass from the belt's own grinding for the progenitor's life (`field_mass.unbound_belt_field`, the rocky law because the blast strips the ice). The field is an `AsteroidField` placed at the remnant, shaped as a ring, with its speed, age, largest piece and a sentence of where it came from (GEN.1103's columns); its radius follows from age, speed and the ring. In a probe of 2,000 remnants, 13 percent of the core-collapse ones host one, with a median of about 90 Earth masses (the solids of a 30 to 70 AU belt around a massive star). A Type Ia remnant hosts none. Stand-in rows HF1 and HF2 of section 6.195.
